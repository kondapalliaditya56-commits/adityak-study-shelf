The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: In 1914 the national movement was dull: Extremists crushed, Moderates spent. Tilak and Annie Besant, working outside INC, built two Home Rule Leagues in 1916 to carry educative propaganda from towns to villages. Besant's arrest in 1917 backfired, united Moderates and Extremists and made her INC President, but the Leagues could not become a mass movement and faded, leaving momentum for Gandhi. Alongside, the Lucknow Pact of 1916 joined INC and the Muslim League on joint demands, after a decade of British actions had alienated Indian Muslims.

  1. A dull phase, 1914. Tilak came back from Mandalay in 1914 after six years in prison. Extremists and Revolutionaries had been crushed. Moderates had become irrelevant because their method had failed in 1892 and again with ICA 1909. Self-Government, INC's goal since the 1906 Calcutta Session, had not arrived.
    → So the movement needed new energy and an organisation that could carry it.
  2. Tilak's calculated position. Tilak wanted the Extremists back inside INC, because outside it the British could repress them easily. He declared four things: Swaraj means self-rule within the Empire, violence has hurt the INM, Indians should support the war, and he is loyal to the Crown. The British and the Moderates were pleased.
    → But a united organisation also needed an ally with a base in the South.
  3. Besant joins and the re-entry fight. Annie Besant, an Irish woman inspired by Home Rule in Ireland and head of the Theosophical Society at Madras, joined INC in 1914. She pushed for Extremist re-entry but Pherozshah Mehta blocked it. The 1915 Bombay Session decided to allow re-entry after Mehta died; the actual re-entry came at Lucknow 1916.
    → But INC still would not back Home Rule Leagues, fearing propaganda could slide into violence.
  4. Two Leagues, one movement. Tilak set up the Indian Home Rule League in April 1916, with 6 branches and headquarters at Poona. Besant set up the All India Home Rule League in September 1916, with 200 branches and headquarters at Madras. They cooperated but did not merge, to prevent friction among followers.
    → So the two spread the same message in separate zones.
  5. The message: Swaraj and more. Tilak's rallying cry was 'Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it'. He also demanded vernacular education, linguistic provinces and an end to untouchability. He framed the fight as a clash of national interests, not of religions, so Jinnah, Besant and Tilak could work together.
    → Now the message needed carriers: newspapers.
  6. The press and the Commonwealth idea. Tilak ran Kesari (Marathi) and Maharatta (English). Besant ran New India and Commonweal, from which came the word Commonwealth. She later wrote the Commonwealth of India Bill (1925) for Dominion Status.
    → But the war was straining the masses, and the British grew anxious.
  7. The arrest that backfired, 1917. WWI brought inflation and anger among the masses. Besant was arrested and interned in 1917. Moderates now joined the Leagues, Sir S. Subramania Iyer gave up his knighthood, and Gandhi began a signature campaign. Besant was released and made INC President at the 1917 Calcutta Session.
    → But the Montague Statement and a quarrel over Passive Resistance split the camp.
  8. Montague and the split. The Montague Statement of 1917 was a Divide and Rule tactic: Swaraj would come gradually. Extremists proposed Passive Resistance, meaning boycott plus deliberate law-breaking. Moderates opposed it and Besant, a constitutionalist afraid of anarchy, sided with them.
    → So the Leagues stalled while the mood outside was rising.
  9. Fading, but not for nothing. By 1917-18 the British had repressed the Leagues and they became defunct. They failed to build a mass movement, and Lower Castes in Madras stayed away. Yet 60,000 members joined, new regions woke up, Nehru joined, and a network was left for later phases.
    → Meanwhile a parallel deal had been struck between INC and the League.
  10. The Lucknow Pact, 1916. INC and ML1906 gave joint demands to the British for the first time: separate electorates, an elected majority, 50% Indians in the VEC, broad direct franchise, and early self-government. The League gave up general seats; INC accepted separate electorates and a fixed quota. GOI Act 1919 later hardened this into watertight rolls.
    → Why had the League moved so far from its loyal start?
  11. The Decade of Alienation. Bengal Partition annulled (1911), Italy took Libya (1911), the first Balkan War (1912) and WW1 (1914) pushed the League towards INC. Then the Balfour Declaration (1917) and the Treaty of Sevres (1920) turned the anger into the Khilafat Movement.

In simple words#

  • Home Rule means Indians run domestic affairs, while foreign matters stay with Britain; it is the same idea as Swaraj within the British Empire.
  • The HRL Movement was not an INC initiative: Tilak and Besant launched it, and the method was educative propaganda, not NC or CD.
  • Aim: carry INC's message from urban leaders to rural masses.
  • Two Leagues: Tilak (April 1916, Poona, 6 branches) and Besant (September 1916, Madras, 200 branches), cooperating but not merging.
  • 1917 is the peak: Besant's arrest united Moderates and Extremists, and she became INC President; then Passive Resistance split them.
  • The Lucknow Pact (INC + ML1906) was the first joint demand to the British, built on a separate-electorate compromise.
  • ML1906 drifted from loyalty to unity with INC because of events abroad in 1911-14, and to the Khilafat Movement because of 1917-20 events.

1 · Home Rule League Movement (1916–18)#

Story link

Because the Moderates had failed and the Extremists were crushed, the leaders went outside INC. Ask: whose movement was it, and what was its method?

  • The HRL Movement of 1916-18 was not an INC initiative.
  • It was launched independently by two leaders: Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Annie Besant.
  • Method: educative propaganda, spreading awareness of Home Rule and Swaraj within the British Empire.
  • It was not a programme of Non-Cooperation or Civil Disobedience.
Teacher's board
  • Add to the method: focus on popularising INC among the masses.
  • Direction of travel: urban leaders go to the rural masses.
Handout alert
  • Prelims 2005 alert. Who was a proponent of Fabianism as a movement? Answer (a) Annie Besant. Wrong: (b) AO Hume, (c) Michael Madhusudan Dutt, (d) R Palme Dutt. Slide key: Besant was a Fabian Society member and British socialist; Madhusudan Dutt died 1873, before the Fabian Society (1884); Palme Dutt was a communist.

2 · Context: Why the Movement Was Necessary#

Story link

Because both Extremists and Moderates had run out of road by 1914, the INM needed a new vehicle. Ask: what exactly had failed?

  • 2.1 Tilak returned from Mandalay (Burma) in 1914 after six years of imprisonment; the INM was at a dull phase.
  • 2.2 Extremists and Revolutionaries had been firmly suppressed by the British.
  • Moderates had become irrelevant, not for lack of sincerity but because their approach had failed twice: in 1892 and again in 1909 with the Indian Councils Act (ICA 1909).
  • Those reforms had not delivered Self-Government, the goal INC had set since the 1906 Calcutta Session.
  • 2.3 ICA 1909 gave a non-official majority only in the Provincial Legislative Councils, not in the Imperial Legislative Council at the centre, and even that was not an elected Indian majority in the full sense. So the movement saw ICA 1909 as a disappointment.
Teacher's board
  • Two labels are stressed: Extremists crushed and Moderates irrelevant (failed twice: 1892 and 1909).
Handout alert
  • Handout alert. Handout 2.2 wording ('elected non-official majority') is loose. Use the board of ICA 1909: official majority in ILC; non-officer majority in PLCs, not an elected Indian majority.
Chain: why the British feared the Leagues

3 · Tilak's Strategy#

Story link

Because organisation decides a movement's fate, Tilak wanted the Extremists back inside INC, but without provoking the British. Ask: what did he promise publicly?

  • 3.1 To revive the INM, Tilak wanted the Extremists to re-enter INC, because organisation mattered for any movement.
  • He understood that if Extremists stayed outside INC, the British could repress them more easily. So he took a deliberate, calculated position and declared publicly:
DeclarationWhat Tilak said and how it landed
i) Swaraj definedSwaraj = self-rule within the British Empire; overthrowing British rule is not his goal. The British were pleased.
ii) Violence rejectedViolence has hurt the INM's progress. The British were pleased again; the Moderates too.
iii) Support the warAll Indians should support the British war effort. British pleased.
iv) Loyal to the CrownHe is loyal to the Crown. British pleased. Moderates reacted as if their own sentence had been copied.
  • 3.2 This was a tactical move: it made it hard for the British to repress the Extremists again, and positioned the Extremists as compatible with INC's moderate framework.

Teacher's board
  • A. Goal has two parts: the British allow the HRL movement (board tag: DoI Act 1915) and the Moderates allow re-entry of Extremists into INC (board tag: Surat Split 1907).
  • B. Method (said / clarified): (1) Swaraj is not Purna Swaraj; it is SG within the Empire = Home Rule. (2) Indians should support Britain in WWI: pay taxes yes; no NC, no CD, no violence; join the army ([MG did the same]). (3) Against violence: violence has hurt the INM.
  • Other points: (4) LC-UC unity, with Tilak's untouchability quote; (5) demanded linguistic reorganisation of provinces [INC 1917], e.g. Madras into TN, Ka, Andhra, Kerala; (6) vernacular-educated masses.
  • 'Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it' is Tilak, not MG.
Handout alert
  • Handout alert. The handout does not mention the DoI Act 1915 tag or the INC 1917 adoption of linguistic provinces. Both are the teacher's board points; keep them.
Teacher's board
  • In governance, Govt = service provider and citizens = recipient; they communicate through laws, schemes, rules and orders. One common language gives better governance.
  • If masses are Western educated, the spread of the modern INM rises: modern nation, modern politics.

4 · Annie Besant's Role#

Story link

Because Tilak alone could not move INC, Besant joined from Madras. Ask: who blocked re-entry, and why would INC not back the Leagues?

  • 4.1 In 1914, Annie Besant, head of the Theosophical Society based in Madras, an Irish woman inspired by the Home Rule movement in Ireland, joined INC.
  • She wanted self-rule for India and campaigned inside INC for the re-entry of Extremists, wanting a stronger organisation.
  • Her attempt failed in 1914 because of opposition from Pherozshah Mehta, leader of Bombay's Moderates.
  • 4.2 At the 1915 Bombay Session, INC finally decided to allow re-entry, as P.M. Mehta had died. The actual re-entry happened at the 1916 Lucknow Session.
  • INC still did not support Home Rule Leagues: Moderates did not fully trust the Extremists and feared educative propaganda might slide into Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience or violence.
Teacher's board
  • Besant believed in Fabianism like J. Nehru: socialism in Britain. She was head, not founder, of the Theosophical Society.
  • The 1915 Bombay Session made a decision on re-entry (the board replaces 'allowed' with 'Decision'); the actual re-entry was 1916.
  • Purpose of re-entry: strong united leadership, giving a better chance of SG within the Empire.
Teacher's board
  • Why Moderates did not allow the HRL movement under INC: in an HRL movement there can be a chance of masses becoming violent. (Board context: WWI brought inflation, which hurt economic security.)

9 · Understanding "Passive Resistance": Extremists, Besant, and Gandhi#

Story link

Because the 1917 debate was about law-breaking, Besant parted ways with the Extremists. Ask: what exactly did 'Passive Resistance' include?

  • 9.1 When Extremists proposed 'Passive Resistance' in the HRL era, and earlier in the Swadeshi Movement, they drew from Aurobindo Ghosh. In April 1907 he published seven articles in the daily Bande Mataram, together called 'The Doctrine of Passive Resistance'.
  • 9.2 It was an umbrella term with two linked concepts (flow below).
  • 9.3 In the 1917 debate in INC, the Extremists asked for exactly this: boycotts (NC) and potential law-breaking (CD).
  • 9.4 Besant backed away because she was a constitutionalist. She would agitate, petition and spread propaganda to the edge of legality (which led to her own arrest) but had a deep-seated fear of anarchy. She strictly opposed the law-breaking part, believing it would lead to chaos and mob violence.
  • This divide is why she later broke away from MG when he launched the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920.
Chain: what Passive Resistance meant to Extremists
Teacher's board
  • Underlined by the teacher: 'She was fundamentally a constitutionalist' and 'mob violence', the two keys to Besant's break.

10 · The Gandhian shift#

  • 10.1 For later chapters: when MG took command of the national movement he rejected the term 'Passive Resistance'. To Gandhi both Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience were highly active, fearless moral forces: Satyagraha, never passive.

11 · Overall Impact: Positives & Negatives#

Story link

Because educative propaganda could not become mass action, the Leagues faded; but they left a lot behind. Ask: what failed, and what survived?

Negatives

  • 11.1 By 1917-18 the British had repressed the HRLs; they became defunct, since educative propaganda could not convert itself into a mass movement.
  • 11.2 No mass movement: in Madras, Lower Castes did not support the All India HRL because its leadership was Upper Caste dominated.
  • 11.3 Extremists failed a second time to take over INC; Moderates and Extremists failed to unite on a common programme.

Positives

  • 11.4 60,000 members joined the HRLs.
  • 11.5 It brought Gujarat, Sindh, United Provinces, Bihar and South India more actively into the INM, regions with low participation until then.
  • 11.6 It prepared leadership for the Gandhian struggle: Nehru joined Besant's League through this movement.
  • 11.7 It created an organisational network used in later phases of the INM.
  • 11.8 It permanently decreased the influence of Moderates in INC.
  • 11.9 It was secular in methods and helped Hindu-Muslim unity.

12 · Lucknow Pact (1916)#

Story link

Because INC and the League both wanted reforms after ICA 1909 disappointed, they joined hands at Lucknow. Ask: what did each side give up, and what did it set in motion?

  • 12.1 The Lucknow Pact of 1916 was an agreement between INC (1885) and the Muslim League (ML1906): the first time they gave joint demands to the British for constitutional reforms. ML1906 also supported the HRLs.
  • The rising tension between ML1906 and the British must be read in world history (Section 13).
Teacher's board
  • ICA 1909 board: official majority in the ILC (non-officer majority in PLCs); 60 additional members, 27 elected; ILC = VEC + additional members; 1 Indian in the VEC; first time indirect elections; right to vote (R2V) linked with income (high); Muslims also voted in general / unreserved seats.
  • Legislature tree: officers (nominated) and non-officers; non-officers are nominated or elected; elected are indirect or direct.
  • Stages of the demand: 1885-1906 limited SG; 1906-28 Swaraj; 1928-29 Dominion; 1929 Purna Swaraj. The board links the 1916 demand to this staging.

12.3 The Hindu-Muslim electoral compromise

Chain: the electoral compromise to GOI Act 1919

Assessing the Lucknow Pact

  • Negative 12.4: a major landmark in the Two Nation Theory. By accepting separate electorates INC formally conceded that Hindus and Muslims were separate political communities needing separate representation: a significant and, in hindsight, dangerous concession.
  • Positive 12.5: it led to INC-ML1906 unity, which MG later used in the Khilafat Movement (1919-24) and the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22).
  • It created pressure on the British and contributed directly to the Montague Statement of 1917 and the GOI Act 1919.
  • It also failed the British strategy of Divide and Rule.

Was the HRL Movement an INC initiative?No. Tilak and Besant launched it independently.
What was the HRL method?Educative propaganda on Home Rule and Swaraj; not NC or CD.
Where was Tilak when the INM was dull in 1914?Returning from Mandalay (Burma) after six years' imprisonment.
Why were Moderates irrelevant by 1914?Their approach failed in 1892 and with ICA 1909; Self-Government not delivered.
Tilak's four public declarations?Swaraj within Empire; violence has hurt the INM; support the war; loyal to the Crown (mnemonic LAWS).
Who blocked Extremist re-entry in 1914?Pherozshah Mehta, leader of Bombay's Moderates.
When was re-entry decided and when did it happen?Decided at the 1915 Bombay Session; actual re-entry at the 1916 Lucknow Session.
Why did INC not back the Leagues?Moderates feared propaganda might slide into NC, CD or violence.
Tilak's League: date, zone, branches, HQ?April 1916; Bombay Province except Bombay city, Berar, Karnataka, Central Provinces; 6; Poona.
Besant's League: date, zone, branches, HQ?September 1916; rest of British India; 200; Madras.
Why did the two Leagues not merge?To prevent friction among their followers.
What did the All India HRL become in 1920?Swarajya Sabha, under MG; merged with INC.
Tilak's demands beyond Swaraj?Vernacular education, linguistic provinces, end of untouchability.
Where did Tilak say 'If God tolerates untouchability...'?1918 All India Depressed Classes Conference.
Why did Tilak make less use of Hindu nationalism?The quarrel is over divergent national interests, not because the British are Christians.
Who produced the Lucknow Pact together?Tilak, Jinnah and Besant.
Jinnah's post in the HRL?President of the Bombay branch of Besant's All India HRL.
Tilak's papers and Besant's papers?Kesari and Maharatta; New India and Commonweal.
Who coined 'Commonwealth of Nations' and when?Lord Rosebery, 1884.
What did Besant's Commonwealth of India Bill (1925) propose?Dominion Status for India.
What happened after Besant's 1917 arrest?Moderates joined the HRLs; Sir S. Iyer gave up knighthood; movement energised.
Who was Organising Secretary of the All India HRL?George Arundale, Besant's right hand.
G. Subramania Iyer in one line?Journalist: The Hindu 1878, Swadesamitran 1882, first INC resolution 1885.
Sir S. Subramania Iyer in one line?Judge, Honorary President of the HRL, surrendered knighthood and wrote to Wilson in 1917.
Gandhi's role in 1917?Signature campaign for Besant's release; not a Leaguer; recruited youth for the army.
Besant's post in 1917?INC President at the Calcutta Session (first woman President, teacher).
What is the Montague Statement 1917 in layman's terms?Divide and Rule: British will give Swaraj gradually.
Why did Besant oppose Passive Resistance?She was a constitutionalist who feared anarchy and mob violence from law-breaking.
Two parts of Aurobindo's Passive Resistance (April 1907)?NC (boycott goods, schools, courts, administrative roles) and CD (break oppressive laws, accept punishment).
Three negatives of the HRLs?Repressed by 1917-18; no mass movement (Lower Castes in Madras stayed away); Extremists failed again to take over INC.
Positives of the HRLs?60,000 members; new regions; Nehru; network; Moderates weakened; secular, H-M unity.
Five Lucknow demands?Separate electorates; elected majority; 50% Indians in VEC; broad direct franchise; early self-government.
What did each side concede at Lucknow?ML1906 gave up general seats; INC accepted separate electorates and a fixed seat quota.
Was UAF demanded at Lucknow?No; UAF came with the Motilal Nehru Report 1928.
When was 'Dominion Status' first used as a phrase?Motilal Nehru Report, August 1928.
Negative of the Lucknow Pact?A landmark in the Two Nation Theory: separate electorates conceded.
Phase 1 of the Decade of Alienation?Bengal annulled 1911, Libya 1911, Balkan War 1912, WW1 1914.
Phase 2 of the Decade of Alienation?Balfour Declaration 1917 and Treaty of Sevres 1920.
