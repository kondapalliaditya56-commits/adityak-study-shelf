Chief Minister and State ExecutiveLow priority

The CM is the real executive head of a State. This chapter covers how the CM is appointed (including a hung Assembly and non-legislators), oath and term, the CM's powers, the Governor-CM relationship (Arts. 163, 164, 167), the Council of Ministers (ranks, 91st Amendment, responsibility), the Cabinet and the Chief Secretary.

1 · Overview: nominal head and real head#

In the parliamentary system the Governor is the nominal (de jure) executive head and the Chief Minister is the real (de facto) executive head. The CM does in the State what the Prime Minister does at the Union. Executive power of the State Government is described as resting with the Council of Ministers, but it is the CM who plays the central, commanding role.

Handout alert

This chapter says executive power is "vested in the Council of Ministers", while the Governor chapter says Art. 154 vests it in the Governor, to be exercised through the Council. Use this rule: formally the Governor (Art. 154), in practice the Council under the CM.

Governor

  • Nominal head
  • de jure executive
  • constitutional head of the State

Chief Minister

  • Real head
  • de facto executive
  • head of the government with real authority

2 · Appointment of the Chief Minister#

The Constitution lays down no detailed procedure. Article 164(1): the CM is appointed by the Governor. That is not an unfettered choice; it must follow parliamentary principles and conventions.

Four situations
SituationPractice
1. NormalOne party has an absolute majority: the Governor must appoint its leader; his role is purely formal
2. Hung AssemblyNo clear majority: the Governor uses limited personal discretion, usually inviting the largest party or post-poll coalition; the appointee must prove majority on the floor, generally within a month or the period the Governor specifies. Legitimacy comes from legislative confidence, not the Governor's choice
3. Death of a sitting CMNo immediate successor: the Governor may use individual judgment for continuity. Conventionally the ruling party elects a new leader quickly, whom the Governor must appoint
4. Non-legislator (Art. 164(4))A person from neither House can be appointed but must be elected to the Legislature within six months, else he automatically ceases to hold office
Four situations
NHDN

Normal majority, Hung Assembly, Death of a CM, Non-member: "No Household Disputes Now".

Six-month rule

Non-member CM or minister: 6 months to get elected, else out (Arts. 164(4) and 164(1)(f) in the handout).

3 · Need to prove majority#

The Constitution does not require a person to prove majority before appointment. The Governor may appoint the CM first and ask him to prove majority in the Assembly within a reasonable time. This has been upheld in several precedents; the final authority on majority rests with the Legislature, not the Governor.

B.R. Kapoor Case, 2001
PointHeld
QualificationOne not qualified to contest elections cannot avail of Art. 75(5) and Art. 164(4)
No arbitrary appointmentThese may be appointed: a competent person who has lost an election; a person who could not contest due to other constitutional commitments; experts
No repeatsNo repeat appointment after the six months expire

Hung Assembly path

Election
No party has a clear majority
Invite
Governor invites the largest party or post-poll coalition leader
Appoint
Governor appoints him CM
Floor test
Must prove majority generally within a month (or as the Governor specifies)
Outcome
Legislature, not the Governor, decides if he stays

4 · Membership of either House#

The CM can be a member of either House (Assembly or Council where one exists). Convention picks the CM from the Legislative Assembly, but members of the Legislative Council have also been CMs (the handout names Uttar Pradesh and Bihar).

5 · Oath, term and salary of the CM#

Before entering office the CM takes the oaths of office and secrecy, administered by the Governor.

Oaths
OathContent
Office (Art. 164(3))Bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution; uphold sovereignty and integrity of India; discharge duties faithfully and conscientiously; do right to all manner of people in accordance with the Constitution and the law, without fear or favour, affection or ill will
SecrecyNot to communicate or reveal any matter that comes to his knowledge as a Minister to any unauthorised person, except as needed for the due discharge of his duties
Office oath four
FSDR

Faith and allegiance, Sovereignty and integrity, Duties faithfully, Right to all: "Four Stars Deserve Respect".

Term

  • No fixed term; holds office during the pleasure of the Governor (Art. 164).
  • The pleasure is not arbitrary: it lasts only while the CM enjoys the confidence of the Assembly.
  • With majority support the Governor cannot dismiss him; on losing the House's confidence he must resign or may be dismissed.

Salary and allowances

Set by the State Legislature. In addition to his salary as a Legislature member he gets: sumptuary allowance, free official residence, travelling allowance, and medical and other facilities decided by the State government.

6 · Powers and functions of the CM#

By relation
RelationPowers
Council of MinistersGovernor appoints only persons the CM recommends; allocates and reshuffles portfolios; can ask a minister to resign or advise the Governor to dismiss him; presides over Council meetings and influences decisions; guides, directs, coordinates and controls ministers; his resignation or death collapses the whole Council, while another minister's merely creates a vacancy
Governor (Art. 167)Principal channel between Governor and Council: communicates all decisions on administration and legislative proposals; furnishes information the Governor calls for; submits for Council reconsideration a matter decided by a minister alone if the Governor requires; advises on appointing the Advocate General, SPSC members and State Election Commissioner
LegislatureAdvises on summoning, proroguing and dissolving; can recommend dissolution of the Assembly at any time; announces policies and legislative programmes; keeps the House informed through answers, statements and debates
ExecutiveSupervises the Secretariat headed by the Chief Secretary; oversees senior appointments (Secretaries, Commissioners, department heads); leads the civil service on discipline, efficiency and morale; monitors performance through official channels, party networks and field visits; influences appointments to PSUs, Boards and Commissions
Other roles
RoleDetail
State Planning BoardChairman
Zonal CouncilVice-Chairman by rotation, one year at a time
Inter-State Council and NITI Aayog Governing CouncilEx-officio member (both headed by the PM)
SpokespersonChief spokesperson of the State government
CrisisCrisis manager-in-chief at the political level

Other roles
PZISCPS

Planning Board chair, Zonal Council vice-chair, Inter-State Council member, Spokesperson, Crisis manager, Public grievances, Services head: "People Zealously Issue Small Complaints Proudly Signed".

CM falls = Council falls

CM resigns or dies: the entire Council goes. Any other minister: only a vacancy.

7 · Governor and CM: Arts. 163, 164, 167#

Three Articles
ArticleRule
163Governor acts on the aid and advice of the Council headed by the CM. Two exceptions: when the Constitution requires discretion (e.g. appointing a CM when no party has a majority) and when he performs special responsibilities (e.g. Sixth Schedule areas, reserving Bills for the President)
164(a) CM appointed by the Governor, usually the majority leader; (b) other ministers on the CM's advice; (c) ministers hold office during pleasure, in practice while they have Assembly confidence; (d) Council collectively responsible to the Assembly; (e) Governor administers oaths of office and secrecy; (f) a minister not a Legislature member for six consecutive months ceases to be a minister
167CM communicates Council decisions and legislative proposals; gives information the Governor calls for; submits matters for Council reconsideration if the Governor requires
Three Articles
AAC

Aid and advice (163), Appointment and tenure (164), Communication duties of the CM (167): "All Act Closely".

Legitimacy

Appointments are made in the Governor's name, but political legitimacy rests with the CM.

8 · The State Council of Ministers: composition#

The Constitution sets up the parliamentary system at State level on the Union model. Principles are broadly outlined in Article 163 (status of the Council) and Article 164 (appointment, tenure, responsibility, qualifications, oath, salaries and allowances). The Constitution does not classify ministers into ranks; the tiers are a convention from the British pattern.

Four ranks
RankRole
Chief MinisterHead of the Council and central figure; leader of the majority party or coalition, appointed by the Governor; all others appointed on his advice; allocates portfolios, presides, links Governor and ministers
Cabinet MinistersSenior members who head key departments (Home, Finance, Education, Health, Agriculture, Industries); form the Cabinet, the inner core and supreme decision-making authority; frame policy, introduce Bills, supervise departments; collectively responsible. In practice "Council of Ministers" often means the Cabinet
Ministers of StateMiddle rank. Some hold independent charge of smaller departments (e.g. Tribal Welfare, Small Industries, Tourism) with Cabinet-like functions but outside the core; others are attached to Cabinet Ministers. Not generally in Cabinet meetings unless invited
Deputy MinistersJunior-most; no independent charge; attached to Cabinet Ministers for routine work, piloting Bills, answering questions; usually do not attend Cabinet; often included for political balancing (caste, region, groups)
Four ranks
CCMD

Chief Minister, Cabinet Minister, Minister of State, Deputy Minister: "Cool Cats Meet Daily".

Rank ladder

Head of the Council
Cabinet Ministers

Ministers of State

9 · Appointment, tenure and size of the Council#

Appointment, tenure, oath, pay
TopicRule
AppointmentGovernor formally appoints the CM and ministers, strictly on the CM's advice. Single-party majority: leader becomes CM. Hung Assembly: Governor may invite the largest party or coalition leader to prove majority. Other ministers on the CM's recommendation; the CM assigns portfolios. The Governor has no discretion on other ministers, except rare situations (like hung Assemblies)
TenureNo fixed tenure; during the Governor's pleasure (Art. 164) but exercised only on the CM's advice; in practice while they hold Assembly confidence; a no-confidence vote makes the whole Council resign
OathArt. 164(3) and the Third Schedule: Governor administers oaths of office and secrecy to every minister. Office: uphold the Constitution, protect sovereignty and integrity, discharge duties faithfully, do justice to all without fear or favour. Secrecy: keep matters confidential except when needed for official duties
SalaryDecided by the State Legislature (Art. 164(5)); until it makes a law, the Second Schedule applies; in practice equal to an MLA, plus sumptuary allowance by rank, free residence, travel, medical and other perks
Collective versus individual tenure
TypeRule
CollectiveThe Council is collectively responsible to the Assembly; loss of majority forces the entire Council to step down
IndividualThe CM may advise the Governor to remove any minister who loses his confidence, so individual tenure depends on the CM's will

Size of the Council: 91st Amendment Act, 2003

  • Created to prevent oversized ministries formed for political appeasement.
  • Council strength not more than 15% of the total Assembly strength.
  • Number of ministers including the CM not less than 12.

91st Amendment limits

Ceiling
At most 15% of the Assembly
Floor
At least 12 ministers, including the CM
Aim
Compact, efficient ministries with less burden on the exchequer
Additional information in the handout

Despite the cap, States such as Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal and Orissa have disproportionately large Councils. The 2nd ARC said there should be no blanket 15% norm; Council strength in each State should be linked to the Assembly's strength.

Numbers

15% and 12 (cap and floor). Non-member minister: 6 months.

10 · Collective and individual responsibility#

Responsibility is the cardinal principle of the parliamentary system: the executive stays accountable to the legislature.

Collective (Art. 164(2))

  • Council functions as a team
  • one minister's decision is the Council's
  • a no-confidence vote means the whole ministry including the CM resigns
  • even a motion against a single minister counts as loss of confidence by the whole Council
  • ensures unity and solidarity
  • stops ministers publicly opposing policy while staying in office

Individual

  • Each minister answers for his own department
  • for inefficiency, corruption or policy violation the CM can ask him to resign, and if he refuses advise the Governor to dismiss him
  • accountability without disturbing the whole Council
No legal responsibility
PointDetail
ScopeMinisters are not legally responsible for the Governor's official acts or for advice given under Art. 163
Contrast with BritainBritain requires ministerial countersignature of Crown acts, making ministers liable; India's Constitution does not
ResultMinisters cannot be prosecuted or sued for advice to the Governor or executive acts in his name
NatureAccountability is political, not legal: answerable to the Assembly, not the courts, for policy
EffectGovernor's acts stay valid even if the advice is later questioned politically
Three responsibilities
CIN

Collective, Individual, No legal liability: "Council Is Not sued".

11 · Powers and functions of the Council; Cabinet and committees#

Functions of the Council
SphereWhat it does
ExecutiveImplements laws; supervises administration (police, education, health, agriculture, industries, welfare); formulates policies and programmes per priorities and electoral promises; coordinates ministries
LegislativeIntroduces most Bills, especially Money Bills, which need the Council's prior approval; manages debates and answers questions; stays in power only while it has the majority
AdvisoryAdvises the Governor on almost all powers except discretionary ones: Ordinance-making; appointments (Advocate-General, State Election Commissioner, SPSC members); dissolution of the Assembly on its recommendation
FinancialPrepares and presents the annual budget; ensures revenue collection, expenditure control and financial discipline; no Money Bill or demand for grants without its approval
Four spheres
ELAF

Executive, Legislative, Advisory, Financial: "Every Lion Always Feeds".

The Cabinet

A smaller but most powerful part of the Council, made up only of cabinet-rank ministers; the true centre of authority. It takes the most important political and administrative decisions, formulates policy, is the supreme executive authority, coordinates departments, advises the Governor on crucial matters, manages crises, deals with major legislative and financial matters (including the budget) and has a decisive role in senior appointments.

Cabinet committees

  • Created by the CM according to need, so number, names and composition vary.
  • Standing (permanent) or ad hoc (temporary).
  • They examine issues in detail, sort out complex problems and prepare recommendations; sometimes they decide on their own.
  • The Cabinet as a whole can review and revise their decisions.

12 · Chief Secretary#

The topmost executive officer and head of the State administrative machinery; the senior-most civil servant; principal adviser to the CM and Council on administrative matters; symbolises unity and continuity of State administration.

Position and appointment
PointDetail
CadreGenerally the senior-most IAS officer of the State cadre
Appointed byThe Chief Minister; holds office at the CM's pleasure; formally made in the Governor's name
TenureNo fixed tenure; continuity kept by convention
Handout alert

PYQ 2016 says "The Chief Secretary is appointed by the Governor" (false in the handout's key) and "has a fixed tenure" (false). Key: neither is correct. The appointment is in the Governor's name but made by the CM.

Six functions
FunctionDetail
Principal Adviser to the CMAdvises on all administration and policy implementation; coordinates departments
Head of the State civil servicesExecutive head of the State Secretariat and administrative head of civil services; secretaries report to him
Secretary to the CabinetPrepares agenda, records minutes, ensures decisions are implemented
Centre-State liaisonMain channel with the Union Government, including centrally sponsored schemes
Crisis managerCoordinates relief and administration in emergencies, disasters and law and order crises
Guardian of disciplineEfficiency, integrity and discipline of civil servants; key in transfers and postings of senior officers
Six functions
AHSLCG

Adviser, Head of civil services, Secretary to Cabinet, Liaison, Crisis manager, Guardian of discipline: "All Hands Stay Loyal, Calm, Guided".

Role

A position of both authority and neutrality: balances political directives with bureaucratic objectivity and links the political executive with the permanent civil service.
