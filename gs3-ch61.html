THIS CHAPTER IN 60 SECONDS
  • Jobs: India's new 4 Labour Codes replace 29 laws; they aim for flexibility for firms + protection for workers. Uttar Pradesh shows how (women on night shifts with safeguards).
  • Is growth "jobless"? The teacher's board notes test the claim with the unemployment rate. Verdict: employment elasticity is low, but it is NOT jobless growth; the real problem is mismatch between jobs and skills and voluntary unemployment.
  • Skills: training has spread (8.1% to 34.7% of 15-59 year olds) but marketable skills are weak. Fix: outcomes not numbers, employer ownership, local labour data, credible certificates, skill vouchers, one data loop.
  • Three bridges from classroom to job: apprenticeships (NAPS / NATS), vocational education (school to college) and the PM Internship Scheme.
  • Each bridge has the same pattern in the handout: progress so far → gaps → way forward → conclusion. Learn that pattern and you can write any of them in Mains.
Understand it firstThink of a young person standing on one bank of a river and a job on the other. The Labour Codes make it easier for the firm to hire (build the bridge). Skilling, apprenticeships and internships are the planks. Jobless growth is the question "is the bridge empty?", and the board notes show the bridge is not empty: people are crossing, but the planks do not match the shape of the opposite bank (skills do not match jobs). This picture is a memory aid, not from the handout.

1 · NEW LABOUR CODES: CATALYSING JOB GROWTH
PYQ 2024 · 15m · four Labour Codes: merits, demerits, progressPYQ 2015 · 12.5m · Make in India needs Skill India and radical labour reforms
Understand it firstIndia had 29 central labour laws, many overlapping. A small firm had to deal with many registers, licences and inspectors, so some firms stayed small on purpose to avoid rules. The new Codes bundle the 29 laws into four, simplify the paperwork and still promise protection to workers.
29 central laws → 4 Labour Codes
Code on Wages, 2019
Industrial Relations Code, 2020
Code on Social Security (CSS), 2020
Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020
MNEMONICWISOWages · Industrial relations · Social security · Occupational safety. Read it as "Wages In Secure Offices". (own memory aid, same order as the handout)
Core features
What the Codes give
Formal recognition for gig workers
Mandatory appointment letters for all workers
Portable social security for migrant workers
Equal benefits (including gratuity and leave after one year) for fixed-term and contract workers
A minimum wage, timely payments and a national floor wage
MNEMONICWAGE + PFive features, order does not matter, so the letters are rearranged: Wage floor (minimum wage, timely pay, national floor wage) · Appointment letters · Gig recognition · Equal benefits for fixed-term and contract workers · Portable social security for migrants. Say it: "The Codes secure your WAGE, and Portability follows you." (own memory aid)
Flexibility versus regulation
  • The Codes strike a deliberate balance: operational flexibility for employers (to encourage entrepreneurship and productive employment) while safeguarding social security, minimum wages and fair conditions for workers.
  • States with flexible labour laws show significantly higher employment, fixed capital and output than those with inflexible laws.
Understand it firstFlexibility is the "give" to firms (easier hiring, fewer inspections); regulation is the "give" to workers (wages, insurance, contract). The handout's claim is that the two can go together, and that states that loosened rules saw more jobs, capital and output.
Compliance rationalisation
Making compliance simple
Replace multiple overlapping jurisdictions with simplified compliance
A single pan-India registration / licence / return
A single licence for contract staffing
The punitive "Inspector" becomes "Inspector-cum-Facilitator": guidance, awareness and advice along with enforcement
MNEMONICONE-ONE-ONECompliance in three "ones": one pan-India registration / licence / return · one licence for contract staffing · one friendly Inspector (Inspector-cum-Facilitator), plus overlapping jurisdictions replaced. (own memory aid)

2 · JOBLESS GROWTH: THE TEACHER'S BOARD NOTES
PYQ 2015 · 12.5m · jobless growth: agree?PYQ 2014 · 12.5m · demographic dividend vs employability; where will jobs come fromPYQ 2023 · 15m · structural unemployment and its measurementPYQ 2022 · 15m · labour productivity and job-creating growth
Understand it first"Jobless growth" means the economy grows but jobs do not. The classic test is employment elasticity: if GDP grows fast but jobs grow slowly, elasticity is low. The board notes say India's employment elasticity has been declining, so people call it jobless growth. The class then asks a sharper question: are there really too few jobs? These are the teacher's own handwritten pages; they are reproduced below exactly, and written out in steps first.
Step 1: the claim
Jobless growth occurs when the employment elasticity of the economy is low
In India, employment elasticity has been declining
So it can be said to be jobless growth
Step 2: critical analysis (the unemployment-rate test)
UE rate in India has stagnated around 3-4%
UE rate = No. of UE ÷ Labour force
Board note: LFPR in India ≈ 40%
Take two points of time: labour force 35 (UE₁ = UE / 35) and 70 (UE₂ = UE / 70)
If the rate stays about the same and LFPR stays about the same ⇒ the labour force is growing at the same rate as population
Then UE is rising at the same rate as the labour force ⇒ unemployment grows at the same rate as population ⇒ jobs are growing at the same rate as population
Step 3: the two rates
Rate of job growth = rate of population growth
But rate of job growth « GDP growth ⇒ that gap is employment elasticity (low)
If job growth equals population growth, then the growth is sufficient, boxed as "don't need so much jobs" (the board's words)
Annual surveys of states: number of new jobs > number of people entering the labour force, so "although employment elasticity is low → NOT jobless growth"
ExampleBoard's own numbers: labour force 35 and 70 (units not stated). If the labour force doubles while the unemployment rate stays the same, the number of unemployed also doubles, so the number of employed also doubles. Employment kept pace with the population, so there was no jobs collapse. The "units" and the doubling reading are an aid to the board's two figures; the logic is the board's.
The three-case rule (board, page 2)
If…Then the UE rate…
gR of Jobs ≥ gR of LFstays constant (or falls)
gR of Jobs < gR of LFrises sharply (↑↑↑)
gR of Jobs > gR of LFwill come to zero eventually. The board notes: "this is India's situation but UE rate is NOT zero"
MNEMONICS - S - ZThe three cases in the order of the board: jobs growth ≥ labour-force growth = Steady UE rate; jobs growth < labour-force growth = Surge in UE; jobs growth > labour-force growth = Zero (eventually). Say it: "Steady, Surge, Zero." (own memory aid)
Exam-hall ruleJobs growing faster than labour force would push UR toward zero. India's UR is not zero. Therefore the leftover unemployment is not simply "no jobs"; the board says it is mostly voluntary unemployment.
Voluntary unemployment and the mismatch
Board evidence
Newspaper clippings in the notes: "Aayog member Bibek Debroy sees dramatic rise in voluntary unemployment" (sub-line: backs household plus enterprise surveys for credible job data; the first word of the headline is cut off in the handout); "Economic Survey 2023-24: Only 51% graduates employable"
PM CEA (as written on the board): the problem is "voluntary UE not UE"
Example (DU, 2011 survey): employability of graduates in jobs where the criterion was graduation was only 11%
2017, same survey with the graduation criterion dropped: 43% employable, i.e. 57% not employable
Two pyramids (board figure)
Jobs pyramid and workforce pyramid, each with H (high), M (medium) and L (low) levels
Red arrows labelled "demand" run from the middle of the jobs pyramid to the H and M levels of the workforce pyramid
A blue arrow labelled "aspiration" runs from the workforce's L level to the jobs pyramid's M level, and points down to "voluntary UE"
Reading the figure: people at the lower level aspire to medium jobs, those jobs are limited, and so they stay out of low jobs, which is voluntary unemployment

Challenges in the skilling ecosystem (11)
Challenges (handout order)
1 Supply-driven skilling: programmes chase enrolment, training numbers and certificates; the system rewards how many are trained, not whether they get jobs, stay or earn more
2 Weak foundational readiness: gaps in literacy, numeracy, digital ability, soft skills; vocational training alone cannot fix weak basic employability
3 Industry mismatch: training does not match vacancies, emerging sectors, local investment or workplace equipment
4 Limited employer ownership: employers not deeply involved in curriculum, apprenticeships, assessment; certificates may show attendance, not competence
5 Poor placement and retention support: placement treated as a target, not a service; weak counselling, unrealistic wage expectations, relocation cost, no accommodation, poor follow-up cause early quitting
6 Weak TVET: outdated curricula, inadequately trained instructors, infrastructure gaps, low employer trust
7 Accountability gaps: inflated numbers, weak attendance checks, data gaps, fund delays, low placement outcomes
8 Low employability despite certification: youth employability around 56.3%
9 Low social status of vocational work: weaving, dairy and entry-level manufacturing seen as low-status and low-paying
10 Financing constraints: public funding alone is insufficient; credit constraints, information gaps and uncertain returns discourage learners, employers and providers
11 Fragmented governance: many ministries, schemes and levels of government; without convergence, duplication rises and training is less responsive to local demand
