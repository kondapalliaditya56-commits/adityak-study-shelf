In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The Constitution is borrowed but adapted. The handout lists 11 sources: the Government of India Act 1935 and ten countries.
  • Easy groups:
    • rights and courts = USA
    • government machinery = Britain
    • DPSP = Ireland and Duties = USSR
    • federal mix = Canada + Australia + the 1935 Act
  • Single-feature countries: Weimar (Emergency and suspension of rights), South Africa (amendment procedure, Rajya Sabha by STV), Japan (procedure established by law), France (Republic; Liberty, Equality, Fraternity).
  • Ask 'who gave this?' first, then check the trap list: some features look alike but come from different places.

Key terms

Government of India Act, 1935
Gave the federal scheme, Governor, Public Service Commissions, judiciary, emergency provisions, administrative details and the SC's advisory jurisdiction (Sec. 213).
Prerogative Writs
From Britain, along with parliamentary privileges and the bicameral legislature.
Residuary powers
Powers not listed anywhere; vested in the Centre, as in Canada.
Concurrent List
The shared list, taken from the Australian Constitution.
Joint sitting
Meeting of both Houses of Parliament together; from Australia.
Weimar Constitution
Germany's constitution; source for suspension of Fundamental Rights during Emergency.
Single Transferable Vote
The method for electing Rajya Sabha members, from South Africa.
Procedure established by law
The concept India took from the Japanese Constitution.
Remember it as

USA = FR, judicial review, impeachment, VP. Britain = parliament, cabinet, Rule of Law. Ireland = DPSP. USSR = Duties. Australia = Concurrent List. Canada = residuary powers. Japan = procedure established by law.

2 · Memory tricks for the table#

  • Memory aid (Countries in order):

    • Some Friends Still Joke."
  • Shortcut: Feature-to-country shortcut. Rights and courts = USA (FR, judicial review, impeachment, VP). Government machinery = Britain (parliament, cabinet, Rule of Law, writs). Ideals of the State = Ireland (DPSP) and USSR (duties). Federal mix = Canada + Australia + GoI Act 1935.

From Lakshmikanth · Indian Polity, 8th ed. Ch 4, Salient Features of the Constitution (Table 4.4)
  • Ambedkar proudly said the Constitution was framed after 'ransacking all the known Constitutions of the World'.
  • LK splits the sources by function: structure from the 1935 Act, philosophy (FR from the US, DPSP from Ireland) and politics (cabinet government, executive-legislature relations) from Britain.
  • The 1935 Act is the most profound influence
  • More than half the provisions are identical or close to it, and about 250 provisions were included
  • The 1935 Act gave the Federal Scheme, Judiciary, Governors, Emergency Powers, Public Service Commissions and most administrative details.
  • Ambedkar's reply to the carbon-copy charge:
    • Nothing to be ashamed of in borrowing, no plagiarism, nobody holds patent rights in the fundamental ideas of a Constitution
    • He regretted only that the borrowed 1935 provisions mostly concern administrative detail
  • The justice ideal in the Preamble came from the Russian Revolution (1917) and liberty, equality and fraternity from the French Revolution (1789-1799).
  • India's judicial review is narrower than that of the US because 'procedure established by law' (Japan) is used instead of American 'due process of law'.
  • Lakshmikanth differs slightly:
    • His Britain row also lists legislative procedure, which the handout row does not. Use the handout's list for the class
    • Add this only if your teacher mentions it

Traps#

Trap 1 · Single citizenship The table credits single citizenship to Britain; Chapter 2 contrasts it with US dual citizenship. Rule: for 'source' answer Britain; for 'model contrast' answer the US.
Trap 2 · DPSP and Duties DPSP (Part IV) is Irish, Fundamental Duties (Part IVA) are Soviet; Fundamental Rights are American. Rule: Ireland = Part IV, USSR = Part IVA, USA = Part III.
Trap 3 · South Africa, Japan and Ireland South Africa gave the amendment procedure and Rajya Sabha election by STV; Japan gave procedure established by law; Ireland gave Rajya Sabha nomination and the President's election method. Rule: Rajya Sabha nomination is Ireland, Rajya Sabha election by STV is South Africa.
Trap 4 · Canada versus Australia Canada gave the strong Centre, residuary powers and Governors appointed by the Centre; Australia gave the Concurrent List, freedom of trade and the joint sitting. Rule: residuary = Canada, concurrent = Australia; the 1935 Act is an Act, not a country.
