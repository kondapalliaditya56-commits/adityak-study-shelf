1 · Citizenship IN India · JUS SOLI TO JUS Sanguinis · Citizenship BY Birth · CAA 2019#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: High. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Anti-Defection, Cooperatives, Citizenship module pp. 9-10. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Citizenship (Articles 5-11, Citizenship Act 1955) is "the right to have rights". India moved from jus soli (birth on soil) to jus sanguinis (parents' nationality) in three stages (1950, 1987, 2004). The CAA 2019 gives a faster route (11 to 5 years) to Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis and Christians from Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan who entered on or before 31 Dec 2014, but it does not change birth rules.

Citizenship: Framework

Key point

Citizenship is the legal and political relationship between an individual and the State. It has been described as "the right to have rights".

Constitutional framework

  • Articles 5-11: deal with citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution and empower Parliament to legislate on citizenship.
  • Citizenship Act, 1955:
    • Enacted under Article 11
    • Governs the acquisition and loss of Indian citizenship

Shift FROM JUS SOLI TO JUS Sanguinis

TermMeaning
Jus Soli (Right of Soil)Citizenship based on place of birth.
Jus Sanguinis (Right of Blood)Citizenship based on parents' nationality.
Key point

India has gradually shifted from unconditional citizenship by birth towards citizenship linked to parents' nationality and migration status.

Period of birth in IndiaCitizenship condition
26 January 1950 to before 1 July 1987Citizen by birth, regardless of parents' nationality, subject to statutory exceptions.
1 July 1987 to before 3 December 2004At least one parent must be an Indian citizen.
On or after 3 December 2004Both parents must be Indian citizens, or one must be Indian and the other not an illegal migrant.

Arguments in favour of the stricter rule

  • 1. Illegal Migration: restricts automatic citizenship for children of undocumented migrants.
  • 2. National Security: strengthens scrutiny of claims.
  • 3. Indigenous Identity: addresses demographic concerns, particularly in border States.

Arguments against

  • 1. Statelessness: children of undocumented migrants may lack citizenship in any country.
  • 2. Documentary Burden: may disadvantage women, poor households and illiterate persons.
  • 3. Constitutional Concerns: critics argue stricter requirements weaken an inclusive understanding of citizenship.
  • 4. Discrimination: verification may disproportionately affect vulnerable communities.
Value addition

Important: the CAA, 2019 provides a special citizenship route for specified migrants. It does not change the general rules of citizenship by birth.

Citizenship (amendment) ACT (CAA), 2019

Key point

The CAA, 2019 amended the Citizenship Act, 1955 to provide a faster route to citizenship for specified religious communities from three neighbouring countries.

ProvisionDetails
BeneficiariesHindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis and Christians.
CountriesAfghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan.
Cut-off dateEntered India on or before 31 December 2014, subject to specified exemption conditions.
NaturalisationReduces the required aggregate residence or government-service period from 11 years to 5 years for eligible applicants.
Citizenship statusThose granted citizenship are deemed citizens from their date of entry into India, as provided under Section 6B.
Legal proceedingsPending proceedings concerning illegal migration or citizenship abate upon conferment of citizenship, as provided by the Act.
Territorial exceptionsExcludes specified Sixth Schedule tribal areas and Inner Line Permit areas.
OCI provisionsExpands grounds for cancellation of Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) registration, subject to an opportunity of being heard.
Value addition

Important: eligibility also requires the relevant exemptions under the Passport (Entry into India) Act, 1920 or Foreigners Act, 1946.

Memory aid · HSBJPC

The six communities: Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis, Christians: "How Safe Borders Join People Compassionately" (order is free). (own mnemonic)

Arguments in favour of CAA

  • 1. Humanitarian Relief: protects specified religious minorities facing persecution in three neighbouring countries.
  • 2. Historical Responsibility: supporters connect it with India's Partition legacy (1947).
  • 3. Existing Citizens: does not remove citizenship from existing Indian citizens, including Muslims.
  • 4. Reasonable Classification: the Government argues the selected countries and communities form a distinct category requiring protection.

Arguments against CAA

  • 1. Article 14: critics question religion-based classification under the Right to Equality.
  • 2. Exclusion of Groups: does not cover persecuted groups such as Ahmadiyyas, Hazaras, Rohingyas or Tamil minorities from Sri Lanka.
  • 3. Cut-off Date: the selection of 31 December 2014 has been questioned.
  • 4. Assam Accord: concerns about its interaction with the 25 March 1971 cut-off under Section 6A.
  • 5. Secularism: religion-based differentiation raises concerns about India's secular character.
  • 6. North-East Concerns: indigenous communities fear demographic and cultural consequences.

2 · Illegal Migration · Assam Accord · Section 6A · NRC · NRC vs NPR vs CAA#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: High. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Anti-Defection, Cooperatives, Citizenship module pp. 10-12. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Assam's agitation (1979-85) led to the Assam Accord (1985) and Section 6A with the cut-off of 25 March 1971. On 17 October 2024 the Supreme Court upheld Section 6A 4:1. The Assam NRC (31 Aug 2019) excluded about 19 lakh of 3.30 crore applicants; exclusion does not prove foreign nationality. NRC identifies citizens, NPR lists usual residents, CAA gives a special route to citizenship.

Illegal Migration AND Assam Accord

Key point

Illegal migration from East Pakistan and later Bangladesh became a major political issue in Assam, raising concerns about demographic change, indigenous identity and national security.

  1. 1979-1985
    Assam Agitation: led by the All Assam Students' Union (AASU) against illegal migration.
  2. 1985
    Assam Accord: established 25 March 1971 as the key cut-off date.
  3. Section 6A
    Inserted into the Citizenship Act, 1955, to provide special citizenship provisions for migrants covered by the Assam Accord.

Section 6A: Citizenship Rules FOR Assam

Period of entryCitizenship position
Before 1 January 1966Eligible persons of Indian origin who entered Assam were deemed Indian citizens.
1 January 1966 to before 25 March 1971Specified migrants had to undergo detection and registration procedures. They became entitled to full citizenship rights after the prescribed 10-year period.
On or after 25 March 1971Migrants are not entitled to citizenship protection under Section 6A.
Value addition

Important: the ten-year restriction applies to specified migrants identified under Section 6A, including restrictions on voting rights during that period.

Supreme Court Judgment ON Section 6A (2024)

In Re: Section 6A of the Citizenship Act, 1955 (17 October 2024)

  • The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of Section 6A by a 4:1 majority.
  • 1. Constitutional Validity: falls within Parliament's power to regulate citizenship.
  • 2. Unique Circumstances: Assam's historical and demographic situation justified a distinct framework.
  • 3. Article 14: the classification was held reasonable and not unconstitutional.
  • 4. Cut-off Date: upheld 25 March 1971 as valid, based on Assam's historical circumstances.
  • 5. Cultural Rights: not found to violate the cultural rights of indigenous communities under Article 29.
  • 6. Effective Enforcement: the Court called for stronger implementation of citizenship and immigration laws.
Way forward / key point

Significance: upheld the Assam Accord's citizenship framework, while recognising concerns about illegal migration and indigenous identity.

Number card

  • AASU 1979-85
  • Accord 1985
  • cut-offs 1 Jan 1966 and 25 Mar 1971
  • 10 years
  • judgment 17 Oct 2024, 4:1
  • Article 14 and 29.

National Register OF Citizens (NRC)

Key point

The NRC aims to identify Indian citizens and distinguish them from illegal migrants.

Assam NRC (31 August 2019)Figures
Total applicants3,30,27,661
Included3,11,21,004
Excluded19,06,657 (about 19 lakh)
Value addition

Important: exclusion from the NRC does not automatically establish foreign nationality. Affected persons are entitled to applicable legal remedies.

Arguments in favourConcerns
Citizenship Clarity: helps identify legal citizens.Statelessness: risk of genuine residents being excluded.
National Security: supports identification of illegal migration.Documentation Gaps: old records may be unavailable.
Indigenous Identity: addresses demographic concerns in Assam.Unequal Burden: women, poorer groups and marginalised communities may face difficulties.
Administrative Records: basis for citizenship verification.Legal Delays: disputes may overburden Foreigners Tribunals.

Difference Between NRC, NPR AND CAA

BasisMeaning
NRCRegister intended to identify Indian citizens.
NPR (National Population Register)Register of usual residents, irrespective of citizenship.
CAAProvides a special route to citizenship for specified migrants from three countries.
Value addition

Core concern: debates over their interaction have raised questions about documentation, inclusion, discrimination, privacy and statelessness.

WAY Forward

Five steps

  • 1. Humane Border Management: strengthen border security while protecting fundamental rights.
  • 2. Fair Citizenship Verification: transparent procedures, accessible documentation, legal aid and effective appeals.
  • 3. Prevent Statelessness: protect genuine citizens and long-settled residents from wrongful exclusion.
  • 4. Refugee Protection: consider a comprehensive refugee and asylum framework based on objective protection needs.
  • 5. Constitutional Balance: reconcile national security, indigenous identity, Article 14 and secularism.
Way forward / key point

Conclusion: India's citizenship framework must balance sovereignty and national security with constitutional equality, humanitarian protection and prevention of statelessness.
