10.4 · Hindi Diwas (14 September)#

PrelimsMiscellaneous

Marks adoption of Hindi as official language after the Munshi-Ayyangar Formula.

  • Hindi in Devanagari is Official Language of the Union; the Constitution names no national language. English allowed for 15 years with Parliament empowered to extend; Official Languages Act 1963 continued English beyond 1965; 1967 amendment allowed it indefinitely.

1.2 · Defining Urban Areas in India#

Prelims + MainsPolity

MoHUA told a parliamentary panel that census-town criteria do not capture the scale of urbanisation. The Registrar General of India will keep the existing framework for Census 2027.

  • Statutory towns: notified under law by the State/UT, irrespective of census thresholds; they have municipalities, corporations or cantonment boards.
  • Census towns must meet all three: population at least 5,000; density at least 400 persons/km2; 75% of the male main working population in non-agricultural work. They may stay under rural local governance but count as urban in Census statistics.
  • Measures differ: national definition 31.2% (2011), UN estimate 35.7% (2025); Agglomeration Index 55.3% (2010) lived in urban-like areas; DEGURBA (UN-endorsed, 1 km2 grids, rural-urban continuum).
  • Limits: male-only occupational test excludes women; large revenue-village boundaries lower density; uniform thresholds miss small hill and north-eastern settlements; no commuting or functional linkage test.
  • Effects: hidden urbanisation; governance mismatch (rural bodies facing drainage, waste, land use needs); planning and funding gaps in peri-urban zones; political economy of reclassification (fear of losing rural scheme benefits, new municipal taxes).
Prelims fodder
  • Peri-urban area: rural-urban transition zone, also called rurban.
  • RGI is an office under the MHA.
  • Agglomeration Index was developed for the World Bank World Development Report.
  • Other countries: Canada (1,000 people and 400/km2), USA (2,000 housing units or 5,000 people).
Mains fodder
  • Way forward: review the male-only test and thresholds; use grids, satellite mapping and commuting data; clear procedure for statutory recognition with staff and finance; transition funding, including the Sixteenth Finance Commission Urbanisation Premium; coordinate municipal and panchayat plans.
  • Line to use: India's cities are growing faster than its definitions; an outdated definition is a governance deficit.
  • Keywords: Hidden urbanisation; Census town; Peri-urban belts; Urbanisation Premium

1.4.1 · Appointment of Election Commissioners#

PrelimsPolity

The Supreme Court gave a split verdict on whether challenges to the 2023 Act on appointment of the CEC and ECs should go to a Constitution Bench (at least five judges).

  • The President appoints the CEC and ECs under Article 324(2).
  • ECI is a three-member body (CEC and two ECs). Since 1993 it is multi-member and decides by majority; all ECs have an equal say.
  • A report said two ECs objected to several decisions, questioning the decision-making process.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Article 324(2); multi-member Commission; Constitution Bench; Article 143

1.4.2 · EVM Totaliser#

PrelimsPolity

The Supreme Court asked the Union Government to examine whether totalisers can be used for EVM counting to protect voter secrecy.

  • Now votes are counted polling-station-wise, revealing booth-level voting patterns.
  • A totaliser connects a cluster of EVMs and shows combined candidate-wise results without booth-level results. It protects voter anonymity and reduces intimidation.
  • Rule 59A of the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961 permits mixing of paper ballots, not EVM votes. Totalisers would need an amendment of the Rules (framed under the RP Act, 1951).
  • ECI proposed totalisers in 2008; Law Commission 255th Report (2015) supported them where intimidation is feared; ECI's recent affidavit opposes them for now for lack of a legal framework.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Voter anonymity; Rule 59A; Conduct of Elections Rules 1961

1.4.3 · Registered Unrecognised Political Parties (RUPPs)#

Prelims + MainsPolity

Six Gujarat-based RUPPs reportedly received Rs 1,700 crore in FY 2023-24, more than the Rs 1,480 crore received by five national parties (excluding BJP).

  • RUPPs are registered with ECI under Section 29A of the RP Act, 1951 but not recognised as State or National parties under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968.
  • Benefits: a free symbol from the free list; income-tax exemption; 20 star campaigners.
  • Concerns: only 750 of over 2,800 RUPPs contested the 2024 general election (letter-pad parties); 223% rise in declared income in FY 2022-23 (ADR); delisting is not de-registration; tax evasion and money laundering risk.
  • INC vs Institute of Social Welfare (2002): ECI cannot de-register a party except for fraud in registration, being declared unlawful, etc.
  • Way forward: automatic de-registration after 10 consecutive years of not contesting (Law Commission 255th report); empower ECI to de-register (ECI 2016 memorandum); periodic verification.
Prelims fodder
  • National party (any one): 6% votes in four States plus 4 Lok Sabha seats; 2% of Lok Sabha seats from at least 3 States; State party status in 4 or more States.
  • State party (any one): 6% votes plus 2 seats; 6% in Lok Sabha poll plus 1 seat; 3% of Assembly seats or 3 seats; 1 Lok Sabha seat per 25; 8% votes.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Section 29A; letter-pad parties; De-registration; Electoral transparency

1.4.4 · Use of Regional Languages in High Courts#

Prelims + MainsPolity

The Tamil Nadu Assembly passed a resolution urging the Centre to recognise Tamil, alongside English, as the principal language of the Madras High Court.

  • Article 348(1): proceedings in the Supreme Court and High Courts are in English unless Parliament provides otherwise.
  • Article 348(2): the Governor, with the President's prior consent, may authorise Hindi or another official State language in High Court proceedings (not judgments, decrees and orders).
  • Section 7, Official Languages Act, 1963: Governor with President's consent may authorise the State language for judgments, decrees and orders, besides English.
  • 1965 Cabinet Committee decision: the Chief Justice of India's consent is sought.
  • Hindi is authorised in Rajasthan (1950), Uttar Pradesh (1969), Madhya Pradesh (1971) and Bihar (1972) High Courts. Proposals for Tamil, Gujarati, Bengali and Kannada did not get CJI consent in 2012.
  • Language of subordinate courts is decided by the State Government.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Article 348; Official Languages Act 1963 Section 7

1.4.5 · Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) and State consent#

Prelims + MainsPolity

The Bihar Government expanded the CBI's general consent for specified cases involving Central Government employees, Central PSUs and related private persons. Prior State approval is still needed for State Government employees and State-controlled institutions.

  • CBI was set up in 1963 by an executive resolution; it is not a statutory body. Its powers come from the Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) Act, 1946.
  • Section 5 DSPE Act: Centre may extend CBI powers to a State. Section 6: ordinarily needs the State's consent.
  • General consent lets the CBI investigate covered categories without approval in each case; specific consent is case by case.
  • The Supreme Court and High Courts can order a CBI investigation without State consent.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: General consent; DSPE Act Section 6; federal character

1.4.6 · Forest Clearance and the Forest Rights Act#

Prelims + MainsPolity

The Ministry of Tribal Affairs clarified that the Forest Rights Act (FRA), 2006 does not itself require 100% Gram Sabha consent for Stage-II forest clearance.

  • Forest clearance under the Forest Conservation Act, 1980 has two stages: Stage-I in-principle approval with conditions (compensatory afforestation, levies); Stage-II final approval.
  • After Stage-II, the State/UT can issue the diversion order only after compliance with other laws, including settlement of rights under the FRA.
  • The Forest (Conservation) Rules, 2022 removed the requirement of Gram Sabha consent before Central approval. The 2023 Rules allow Central approval before settlement of rights; actual diversion needs prior settlement.
  • Gram Sabha role under FRA: verifies claims; free, informed consent for relocation from critical wildlife habitats; protects forests and heritage; can pass a resolution against violators after 60 days' notice to the State Level Monitoring Committee.
  • FRA 2006 (Ministry of Tribal Affairs) recognises rights of forest-dwelling STs and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers: individual rights; community rights (grazing, fishing, PVTG habitat, Community Forest Resource, traditional knowledge); minor forest produce rights. Committees: Sub-Divisional, District and State Level Monitoring.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Stage-I and Stage-II clearance; Community Forest Resource rights; Gram Sabha consent
PYQ connect

2013 Prelims: Gram Sabha initiates the process of determining individual or community forest rights under FRA.

1.5.1 · Article 142 (quick update)#

Prelims + MainsPolity

The Supreme Court invoked Article 142 to quash FIRs against students in the NEET-UG 2026 protests. Relief did not cover persons with serious criminal antecedents.

  • Article 142(1): SC may pass any decree or order necessary for complete justice in a pending case; enforceable throughout India. Extraordinary and discretionary; available only to the SC.
  • Article 142(2): secure attendance, order production of documents, investigate or punish contempt.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Complete justice; Article 142

2019 Prelims: Article 142 powers are not constrained by laws made by Parliament.

1.5.2 · New Guidelines for Film Certification, 2026#

Prelims + MainsPolity

The I&B Ministry notified new film certification guidelines under the Cinematograph Act, 1952, superseding the 1991 notification.

  • CBFC (under the 1952 Act) certifies films.
  • Provisions: no glorification or justification of violence; no abuse or ridicule of persons with disabilities; sexual violence against women to be avoided.
  • Age-based certification retained: U/A 7+, U/A 13+, U/A 16+. Others: A (adults only), Specialised (restricted to a profession or class).

  • Keywords: CBFC; U/A categories; Article 19(1)(a) and 19(2)

1.5.3 · Tulu language#

PrelimsPolity

Karnataka Cabinet gave Tulu the status of second additional administrative language for Dakshina Kannada and Udupi.

  • Article 345: a State legislature may adopt one or more languages in use in the State for official purposes.
  • Tulu is not among the 22 languages in the Eighth Schedule.
  • Dravidian family language; historic Tigalari script (from medieval Grantha); now written in Kannada script; core region Tulunadu (coastal Karnataka and Kasaragod in Kerala).
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Article 345; Eighth Schedule

1.5.5 · 1951 baseline for NRC in Manipur#

PrelimsPolity

The Manipur government told the Assembly that 1951 would be the proposed baseline for updating the NRC.

  • An NRC/NRIC needs the Union Government's authorisation. Section 14A of the Citizenship Act, 1955 empowers the Centre to register citizens and maintain the NRIC (Citizenship (Registration of Citizens and Issue of National Identity Cards) Rules).
  • Census (Census Act, 1948; Union List) counts the population. NPR records all usual residents, citizens and non-citizens. NRC contains only citizens after verification.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Section 14A; NRIC; NPR

4.2 · Maternity rights: Rakhi Bisht v. Union of India (2026)#

Prelims + MainsSociety & Social Justice

Delhi High Court held that availing maternity leave cannot diminish a woman's professional status or career prospects; cited Articles 14, 15, 21, 42 and the Code on Social Security, 2020.

  • Principles: return to same or substantially equivalent role (pay, status, responsibilities, authority, promotion); protection covers duties, reporting, appraisal; explain reasons and alternatives before rejoining if original role is unavailable; no retaliation; Court noted a gap in post-maternity reintegration and told the Centre to frame safeguards within six months.
  • Code on Social Security 2020 subsumed the Maternity Benefit Act 1961. Applies to factories, mines, plantations and shops/establishments with 10+ employees; eligibility 80 days of work in preceding 12 months.
  • Leave: 26 weeks for fewer than two surviving children (up to 8 weeks before delivery); 12 weeks for two or more (up to 6 weeks before); adoptive and commissioning mothers 12 weeks; crèche for 50+ employees with four visits daily.
  • Challenges: Time Use Survey 2024 - 41% of women (15-59) did unpaid caregiving vs 21.4% of men; women 140 min/day vs men 74; employer-cost bias; weak reintegration; informal-sector exclusion (PLFS 2025: 63.1% of female non-agricultural workers in proprietary/partnership enterprises); weak crèches and enforcement.
  • Way forward: shared parental leave (ILO C156); socialise costs (ILO C183); phased return, flexible work, lactation support; PMMVY; strengthen enforcement.
  • Connecting news: Tamil Nadu announced 365 days of maternity leave for state employees for a third child (up from 12 weeks). Central Civil Services (Leave) Rules 1972 give male employees 15 days paternity leave; no statutory private-sector paternity leave. Hamsaanandini Nanduri (2026) urged recognising paternity leave as social security.
Prelims fodder
  • CEDAW 1979 Article 11: non-discrimination in employment. ILO C183 (2000) Maternity Protection; ILO C156 (1981) Workers with Family Responsibilities; India has not ratified either.
  • Cases: MCD v. Female Workers (Muster Roll) 2000; Deepika Singh v. CAT 2022; Hamsaanandini Nanduri v. Union of India 2026 (removed three-month adoption limit).
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: motherhood penalty, substantive equality, career continuity, reproductive autonomy, care redistribution, workplace reintegration.
PYQ connect

2019 Prelims: Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act 2017 provisions (crèche visits, leave for two children).

Read with: Fundamental Rights · Directive Principles
