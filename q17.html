2006PYQ 6(Prelims 2006) — Charter 1833 + GOI 1858

Consider the following statements: 1. The Charter Act, 1833 abolished East India Company monopoly of Indian trade. 2. Under the Government of India Act, 1858, the British Parliament abolished the East India Company altogether and undertook the responsibility of ruling India directly.

Answer: (B)

(b) ✓ — alert (Prelims 2006 two-statement): Statement 1 WRONG — Charter Act 1833 ended EIC's remaining commercial monopolies (Tea + Opium + China), but the EIC's monopoly over Indian trade itself had already been ended by Charter Act 1813. So statement 1's claim that 1833 abolished EIC's Indian trade monopoly is technically wrong (1813 did that). Statement 2 CORRECT — under GOI Act 1858, EIC rule was replaced by direct rule of the British Crown; EIC was formally dissolved in 1874. Hence only 2.

Why others wrong: (a) statement 1 wrongly credits 1833 for ending EIC's Indian-trade monopoly which was actually 1813's act; (c) statement 1 is wrong (1813 did it, not 1833); (d) statement 2 is correct (Crown takeover under GOI Act 1858 is textbook fact).

Memory hook: 1813 ended India-trade monopoly; 1833 ended residual tea+opium+China; 1858 abolished EIC altogether.

Answer: (A)

Statement 2 CORRECT — Proclamation placed Indian administration under the British Crown. Statement 3 WRONG — Proclamation had nothing to do with regulating EIC's trade; by 1858 EIC's commercial function had already been ended (Charter 1833) and EIC itself was being dissolved. 2019PYQ 9(Prelims 2019) — Charter Act of 1813

Consider the following statements about 'The Charter Act of 1813': 1. It ended the trade monopoly of the East India Company in India except for trade in tea and trade with China. 2. It asserted the sovereignty of the British crown over the Indian territories held by the Company. 3. The revenues of India were now controlled by the British Parliament.

Answer: (A)

(a) ✓ — alert (Prelims 2019 trap on Charter 1813): Statement 1 CORRECT — Charter Act 1813 ended EIC's monopoly over India trade except tea, opium and China. Statement 2 CORRECT — Charter Act 1813 re-clarified undoubted sovereignty of Crown over Indian territorial possessions. Statement 3 WRONG — control of Indian revenues by the British Parliament was NOT a Charter 1813 provision; the supervisory power over Civil, Military and Revenue government of British territorial possessions was given to the Board of Control under Pitt's India Act 1784, and revenues of British India were placed under Parliament's control only by the Charter Act 1853. Hence 1 and 2 only.

Why others wrong: (b) statement 3 is wrong (revenue control was Pitt's Act 1784 / Charter 1853, not Charter 1813); (c) statement 2 is correct (Crown sovereignty re-asserted in 1813), so excluding it is wrong; (d) statement 3 is wrong.

Memory hook: Charter 1813 = end India-trade monopoly (tea+opium+China retained) + Crown sovereignty re-clarified + missionaries + ₹1 lakh education; revenue control NOT here.

2023PYQ 10(Prelims 2023) — Act that Designated GG/Bengal as GG/India

By which Act was the Governor General of Bengal designated as the Governor General of India?

Answer: (D)

(d) ✓ — alert (Prelims 2023 — By which Act was GG of Bengal designated GG of India): The Charter Act 1833 elevated GG(Bengal)-in-Council to GG(India)-in-Council. Bentinck28-35 was the holder of that re-designated office. Hence (d) Charter Act 1833.

Why others wrong: (a) Regulating Act 1773 only created the office of GG/Bengal, it did not redesignate it as GG/India; (b) Pitt's India Act 1784 created the Board of Control and Double Government, it did not redesignate the GG; (c) Charter Act 1793 increased GG's power over his council and over Bombay/Madras Governors but did not redesignate GG.

Memory hook: 1773 made GG/Bengal; 1833 promoted him to GG/India; Bentinck28-35 = first GG/India.

FYQ 2

Consider the following statements regarding Pitt's India Act of 1784: 1. It created the Board of Control as the supreme political authority over the East India Company. 2. It merged the offices of Governor-General and Commander-in-Chief into a single post. 3. It declared that conquest in India was repugnant to the wish, policy and honour of the British nation. Which of the statements is/are correct?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — Statements 1 and 3 are correct: BoC was created in 1784 to supervise EIC's political affairs, and the Act stated conquest was repugnant to the wish, policy and honour of the nation. Statement 2 is wrong — Pitt 1784 made CIC an ex-officio ORDINARY member of GG's council (one of 3 seats), but the offices of GG and CIC were NOT merged into a single post.

Memory hook: Pitt 1784 = BoC + 'conquest repugnant' + CIC as ex-officio ordinary member of GG's council (NOT a merger of offices).

FYQ 15

Chapter-derived Mains practice with the demand, guiding themes and a conclusion.

FYQ 1 Trace the evolution of the EIC-British State relationship from the Regulating Act 1773 to the GOI Act 1858.Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words

Demand: Show the sequential tightening of British State control over EIC across the major Acts.

Guiding themes:

1. Regulating Act 1773 — Parliamentary oversight initiated; GG of Bengal Presidency created (see 'Regulating Act of 1773').

2. Pitt's India Act 1784 — Board of Control created in London (see 'Pitt's India Act / East India Company Act (1784)').

3. Charter Act 1813 — trade monopoly ended (except Tea/Opium/China) (see 'Charter Act (1813)').

4. Charter Act 1833 — EIC commercial functions ended; GG/India + Law Commission (see 'Charter Act (1833) / St. Helena Act').

5. Charter Act 1853 — Indian Civil Service via open competition (see 'Charter Act (1853)').

6. GOI Act 1858 — EIC abolished, Crown direct rule (see 'Government of India Act (1858) / Act for Better Government of India').

7. Conclusion: each Act narrowed EIC autonomy until total abolition in 1858.

FYQ 2 Discuss the constitutional significance of Queen Victoria's Proclamation of 1858.Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words

Demand: Identify the Proclamation's main promises + their political significance for British-Indian relations.

Guiding themes:

1. End of EIC rule → direct Crown rule via GOI 1858 (see 'Government of India Act (1858) / Act for Better Government of India').

2. Proclamation explicitly ended the Doctrine of Lapse (see 'Government of India Act (1858) / Act for Better Government of India').

3. Pledged respect for treaties and rights of Indian rulers — reversal of Dalhousie48-56's annexationist policy.

4. Promised religious tolerance and non-interference in Indian customs — direct acknowledgement that religious interference had been a major 1857 grievance.

5. GOI 1858 introduced the Policy of Subordinate Union — implemented through Paramountcy — replacing Mughal sovereignty over Princely States ('Government of India Act (1858) / Act for Better Government of India' #2).

6. Laid the legal and political foundation for the 89 years of Crown Rule until 1947.
