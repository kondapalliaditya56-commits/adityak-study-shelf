1 · Origin and Purpose#

GST was a major overhaul of India's indirect tax architecture, enabled by the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016. A nationwide GST needed continuous coordination between the Union and the States, both to design uniform rates and rules and to manage collection and administration.

  • To give that coordination a permanent institution, the Amendment created the Goods and Services Tax Council.
  • Article 279A(1): the President had to constitute the Council within 60 days of the commencement of the Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016.

2 · Composition (Article 279A(2))#

Members of the GST Council
ClauseMember
aThe Union Finance Minister (Chairperson)
bThe Union Minister of State in charge of Revenue or Finance
cThe Minister in charge of Finance or Taxation, or any other Minister nominated by each State Government
dAny person nominated by the Governor of the State where there is a proclamation of emergency under Article 356
  • The State Ministers elect among themselves a Vice-Chairperson from time to time.
  • The Chairperson of the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) is a permanent invitee with no vote, present at all proceedings.

3 · Working#

Working is governed by Article 279A and the procedures the Council follows. Meetings are its decision-making forum.

Quorum and voting
PointRule
QuorumOne-half of the total members must be present
DecisionAdopted at a meeting by a majority of not less than three-fourths of the weighted votes of the members present and voting
Centre's weightOne-third of the total weighted votes
States' weightAll State Governments together carry the remaining two-thirds
ResultA proposal succeeds only when combined weighted support reaches the 75% threshold
Voting sum

1/3 Centre + 2/3 States = 100. Pass at 3/4 = 75. The Centre alone cannot pass anything, and the States together cannot pass anything without Centre support, since two-thirds is less than three-fourths.

Safeguards for validity

No act or proceeding of the Council is invalid merely because of:

  • a vacancy in, or defect in, the constitution of the Council;
  • an irregularity in the appointment of a Member; or
  • a procedural irregularity that does not affect the merits of the case.

Procedure and Secretariat

  • The Council determines its own procedure (agenda-setting, deliberation, follow-up).
  • It works through its Secretariat in New Delhi to operationalise decisions and follow up with Union and State administrations.
Safeguards
VIP

Vacancy or defect in constitution, Irregular appointment, Procedural irregularity (not affecting merits): "Very Important Proceedings".

4 · Institutional Purpose and Vision#

The Council is the central mechanism steering India's unified indirect tax regime. Its reason for being is a harmonised GST architecture and a single national market through sustained Union-State cooperation. It is the principal forum where policy choices affecting both levels are negotiated and converted into a common framework (see Article 279-A).

Vision

  • Exemplary cooperative federalism
  • the highest standard-setting federal body for GST
  • decisions taken collectively
  • parity of participation, sustained consultation and mutual accommodation

Mission

  • Evolve a GST structure through wide consultation of experts, administrators and elected representatives
  • use information technology so processes are digital, transparent and user-friendly
  • reduce compliance costs and raise efficiency
  • Its procedural role institutionalises predictability: rate revisions, classification disputes and administrative guidelines go through established, consultative procedures, not ad hoc decisions.

5 · Functions (Article 279A(4))#

Under Article 279A(4) the Council makes recommendations to the Union and the States on GST matters.

Functions of the GST Council
FunctionWhat it does
Rationalisation of taxesRecommends which existing taxes, cesses and surcharges of the Centre, States and local bodies are subsumed into GST; replaces many indirect levies with a single destination-based tax and reduces cascading
Tax coverageRecommends which goods and services come under GST and which are exempted
Model GST laws and principlesFrames model laws; uniform principles of levy and collection; rules for apportioning tax on inter-state trade or commerce; principles for place of supply
Thresholds and ratesTurnover threshold below which small taxpayers are exempt; GST rates including floor rates with bands; special temporary rates during natural calamities or disasters
Special provisions for certain StatesRecommendations for specified States (see list below)
Other mattersAny other GST-related matter it deems necessary

The Council is the apex body for tax harmonisation: consolidation of taxes, legal coherence, fiscal fairness and cooperative federalism, in the spirit of Article 279-A.

6 · Other Responsibilities and Powers#

Derived from Article 279A, these tasks help the Union and States implement GST smoothly.

1. GST on petroleum products

  • The Council recommends the date from which GST may be levied on specified petroleum products: petroleum crude, high-speed diesel, motor spirit (petrol), natural gas, aviation turbine fuel.
  • These commodities are currently kept outside GST and are brought in when the Centre and States reach consensus, to limit revenue disruption and price instability.

2. Dispute resolution mechanism

The Council sets up an institutional mechanism to adjudicate disputes about its recommendations or their execution.

Three types of disputes
TypeBetweenExample in handout
(a)The Centre and one or more StatesDisagreement over apportionment of tax revenues
(b)The Centre (and certain States) on one side and other States on the otherDiverse fiscal interests
(c)Two or more StatesDifferences in interpreting place of supply or rate classification
  • The aim is to resolve conflicts within the GST framework without judicial delays.

3. Compensation to States

  • The Council recommends a compensation framework for States to offset potential revenue loss from GST.
  • Under Article 279A(5) and Section 18 of the 101st Amendment Act, 2016, the Council proposed compensation for five years from the date of GST rollout.
  • Parliament then enacted the Goods and Services Tax (Compensation to States) Act, 2017, providing bi-monthly compensation from the GST Compensation Cess on specified luxury and sin goods.

Recall sheet#

  1. GST: 101st Amendment Act 2016; GST Council in Art. 279A, constituted by the President within 60 days.
  2. Members: Union Finance Minister (Chair), Union MoS Revenue or Finance, one State Minister per State, Governor's nominee where Art. 356 applies.
  3. State Ministers elect the Vice-Chairperson; CBIC Chairperson is a permanent non-voting invitee.
  4. Quorum one-half of members; decision by three-fourths of weighted votes; Centre one-third, States two-thirds.
  5. Validity not affected by vacancy, irregular appointment, or harmless procedural irregularity.
  6. Functions (Art. 279A(4)): taxes subsumed, coverage, model laws and place of supply, threshold and rates, special States, other matters.
  7. Eleven States get special provisions: eight North-East plus Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand and Jammu & Kashmir.
  8. Petroleum: crude, HSD, petrol, natural gas, ATF stay outside GST until the Council recommends a date.
  9. Disputes of three types; compensation five years; Compensation Act 2017; bi-monthly from the GST Compensation Cess.
