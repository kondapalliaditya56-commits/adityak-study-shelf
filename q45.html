2000PYQ 1(Prelims 2000)

The Indian National Army (INA) came into existence in 1943 in

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — After Singapore fell (February 1942), Captain Mohan Singh conceived the INA from Indian PoWs and 40,000 PoWs in Singapore joined; the First Division was formed in September 1942. At Singapore again, in July 1943, Netaji took over the INA and went on to proclaim the Provisional Government. Date trap: the INA existed from 1942; 1943 was only a change of leadership.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Japan was the patron power and R.B. Bose's place of exile, but the army itself was raised at Singapore from the PoWs handed over there.

(B) ✗ — Rangoon (Burma) became an INA headquarters only after the Provisional Government of October 1943; the force originated at Singapore.

(D) ✗ — Malaya was the first of the three territories Japan overran in early 1942 - context, not the birthplace.

Memory hook: Singapore falls (February 1942) - and the INA rises from its PoW camps.

2005PYQ 2(Prelims 2005)

Consider the following statements
1. Lord Mountbatten was the viceroy, when Shimla Conference took place.
2. Indian Navy Revolt, 1946 took place when the Indian sailors in the Royal Indian Navy (RIN) at Bombay and Karachi rose against the government.
Which of the statement(s) given above is/are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Statement 2 is correct: the mutiny began on HMIS Talwar at Bombay and at Karachi HMIS Hindustan rose with it (February 1946). Statement 1 fails: the Shimla Conference of June 1945 met under Viceroy Wavell (1943-47); Mountbatten replaced Wavell only in 1947. Teacher's board: '1945, Wavell 43-47' - statement 1 F, statement 2 T.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Reversed: statement 1 is the false one, while RIN sailors did rise at Bombay and Karachi.

(C) ✗ — Statement 1 fails (Shimla Conference sat under Wavell), so 'both' overcounts.

(D) ✗ — Statement 2 stands: RIN sailors rose at Bombay and Karachi in February 1946, so 'neither' fails.

Memory hook: Talwar at Bombay, Hindustan at Karachi - February 1946.

2008PYQ 3(Prelims 2008)

Which one of the following suggested the reconstitution of the Viceroy's Executive Council in which all the portfolios including that of war members were to be held by the Indian leaders?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — The Wavell Plan, placed before the Simla Conference of June 1945, proposed reconstituting the Viceroy's Executive Council with all portfolios, the War Member's included, in Indian hands. All INC leaders were released in June 1945 precisely to attend it. Teacher's board: Simon 1927 F, Cripps 1942 F, Cabinet Mission 1946 F.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The Simon Commission (1927) recommended provincial autonomy with 'no change at the Centre' - the opposite of Indianising the Executive Council.

(C) ✗ — Cripps (1942) offered no such Indianisation: Defence stayed with the Viceroy and there was to be no dilution of the Viceroy's powers; talks broke down on that point.

(D) ✗ — The Cabinet Mission came later, in 1946, to settle constitution-making, not to reconstitute the wartime Executive Council.

Memory hook: All portfolios, War Member included -> Simla Conference, June 1945.

2008PYQ 4(Prelims 2008)

During the Indian Freedom Struggle, who of the following raised an army called 'Free Indian Legion'?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Subhash Chandra Bose raised the Free Indian Legion in Germany, from Indian PoWs, in the same phase as his Azad Hind Radio broadcasts of 1942. He reached Singapore in July 1943, took over the INA and gave the call 'Delhi Chalo'. Rash Behari Bose, in exile in Japan since the Ghadar days, joined the INA effort.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Lala Hardayal's theatre was the Ghadar Movement of 1913-15, a generation before the Free Indian Legion.

(B) ✗ — R.B. Bose's INA role was caretaker leadership in early 1943, handed to Netaji at Singapore; the Legion of Germany was Netaji's.

(D) ✗ — No army-raising is recorded for Savarkar; the Free Indian Legion belongs to Netaji's Germany phase.

Memory hook: Free Indian Legion = Germany = Netaji.

2017PYQ 5(Prelims 2017)

With reference to Indian freedom struggle, consider the following events :
1. Mutiny in Royal Indian Navy
2. Quit India Movement launched
3. Second Round Table Conference
What is the correct chronological sequence of the above events ?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — The 2nd Round Table Conference sat September-December 1931; the Quit India Movement was launched on 9 August 1942 at Bombay; the Naval Ratings Mutiny broke out in February 1946 (Talwar at Bombay, Hindustan at Karachi). Order: 3-2-1.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Reverses the line completely: puts the 1946 mutiny first and the 1931 RTC last.

(B) ✗ — Starts with Quit India (1942) and ends with the 2nd RTC, but the RTC (1931) came first of the three.

(D) ✗ — Places the Naval Mutiny (February 1946) before Quit India (August 1942) - impossible.

Memory hook: 1931 -> 1942 -> 1946.

2021PYQ 6(Prelims 2021)

In the context of Colonial India, Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Kumar Sehgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon are remembered as

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — The most famous INA trial was held at the Red Fort in November 1945. Shah Nawaz Khan (a close aide of Netaji), Prem Sehgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon were the accused, a Muslim, a Hindu and a Sikh, remembered as INA officers. They were captured by the British when the INA was defeated.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The Swadeshi and Boycott Movement belongs to 1905-11, an earlier generation.

(B) ✗ — The Interim Government of 1946 had a different cast; in 1945-46 these three men were on trial at the Red Fort.

(C) ✗ — The Drafting Committee belongs to the Constituent Assembly's constitution-writing work, not the INA trials.

Memory hook: Red Fort, November 1945 - three INA officers, three faiths.

FYQ 2

Consider the following statements about the Bengal Famine of 1943:
1. It was caused primarily by a failure of crops in Bengal.
2. Food supplies from India were diverted to Europe to feed British soldiers and European civilians.
3. The Japanese occupation of Burma stopped food imports that Bengal had been receiving.
4. Viceroy Linlithgow's request for food and a ban on food exports was denied by Prime Minister Churchill.
Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Statements 2, 3 and 4 hold: food diverted to Europe, Burmese imports cut off after Japan occupied Burma (March 1942), and Churchill denied Linlithgow's request and export ban. Statement 1 is false: it was a man-made famine, not a crop failure; roughly 5 million Indians died.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Includes false statement 1 and omits true statement 4.

(C) ✗ — Includes false statement 1 and omits true statement 2.

(D) ✗ — Includes false statement 1: the famine was man-made, not a crop failure.

FYQ 3

(A) ✓ — Bose was released in January 1941 after a hunger strike and put under house arrest in Calcutta, escaped disguised as an Afghan (via Afghanistan and USSR), reached Germany in March 1941, set up Azad Hind Radio there in 1942, and reached Singapore in July 1943: 2, 1, 3, 4.

FYQ 1 The Indian National Army (1942-45) was militarily defeated but politically transformative. Examine. (15 marks, 250 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine' + 'defeated but transformative' - separate the military outcome from the political consequence.

Suggested intro (~25 words): The INA never captured an Indian city; it failed at Imphal-Kohima (1944) and Netaji's reported death came in August 1945. Yet its political effect far exceeded its military record.

1. The military record: Mohan Singh conceived it Feb 1942 (First Division Sept 1942, 40,000 PoWs); R.B. Bose early 1943; Netaji July 1943 and the Provisional Government on 21 Oct 1943. The 1944 Kohima-Imphal campaign with Japan failed, and INA soldiers were short-changed on weapons and supplies. Japan surrendered 15 Aug 1945. [Companion adds: monsoon and supply collapse, retreat by August 1944 - not in the handout.]

2. The trials transformed perception: Red Fort, Nov 1945 - Khan, Sehgal, Dhillon (Muslim, Hindu, Sikh); defence by Desai, Sapru, Nehru, Asaf Ali; mass upsurge across all sections, maximum unity in the national movement, started by the people; INC election campaign, INA Relief Committee; officials openly contributed.

3. Demonstration effect: Naval Ratings Mutiny, Feb 1946 - sailors drew inspiration from the INA, carried Netaji's pictures and raised INA flags; with RIAF strikes it shattered the 'martial races' myth.

4. Political accelerant: INA agitations plus the mutiny convinced the British that the army could no longer be trusted and transfer of power could not be postponed; Cabinet Mission 1946, Mountbatten 1947 followed.

Closing verdict: small military legacy, large political legacy - the INA broke the loyalist-military framework, hastened the transfer of power and became the symbolic anchor of post-1945 nationalism. [Companion adds: 1972 recognition of INA men as freedom fighters - not in the handout.]

FLAGS: the companion says Bose 'died in August 1945'; handout/teacher: an alleged plane crash at Taihoku (18 Aug 1945) reportedly killed him, still controversial - written as 'reported death' here. Companion's 'sentence remission' claim is not in the handout.

FYQ 2 The Bengal Famine of 1943 was not a natural disaster but an administrative & policy failure. Examine. (10 marks, 150 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine' + 'not natural but administrative' - trace causes beyond crop failure.

Suggested intro (~25 words): About 5 million died in 1943; it was not a crop failure but a man-made famine of diverted food, lost imports, poor logistics and speculation. FLAG: the companion says 2-3 million; handout and teacher say roughly 5 million, used here.

1. Policy diversion: food from India, including Bengal, was diverted to Europe to feed British soldiers and European civilians - army and Europeans prioritised over Indian lives; Churchill denied Linlithgow's request for food and ban on exports from Bengal; little relief.

2. War shocks and mismanagement: Japan occupied Burma (March 1942) and imports to Bengal stopped entirely; government managed logistics and supply chains poorly; war-driven speculation put prices beyond ordinary people.

3. Entitlement collapse (Amartya Sen): wages collapsed, speculative prices soared, procurement drained rural markets - a failure of access, not supply. Companion extras not in the handout: 'denial policy' (boat and rice-stock removal on the coast), press censorship, Calcutta hoarding, Allied food offers refused.

4. Official response: the Famine Inquiry Commission (1945, Sir John Woodhead) largely blamed natural factors and supply disruption and was criticised for not holding the administration accountable.

Closing verdict: a policy famine; it bred deep and lasting distrust of British rule, shifted British opinion, and deepened the agrarian crisis that fed the CPI-led Tebhaga movement (1946-47). FLAG: the companion's Churchill 'breed like rabbits' remark is not in the handout and is omitted; the handout has only his reported query about whether Gandhi was still alive.

PYQ 1 Mains PYQ (Guiding Theme)In what ways did the naval mutiny prove to be the last nail in the coffin of British colonial aspirations in India?

FLAG: the companion gives only the question for this Mains PYQ; the answer below is built from the handout (Secs 3-4) and the finished chapter notes.

Demand of the question: 'In what ways' - show the specific channels through which the February 1946 mutiny ended the British ability to hold India.

Suggested intro: February 1946, HMIS Talwar, Bombay - ratings struck during the INA agitations; within days it spread to 78 ships, 20 shore establishments and about 20,000 sailors, and the instrument of British rule, Indian armed forces, cracked.

1. INA spillover: the Red Fort trials (Nov 1945) produced unmatched sympathy; the ratings had contributed money for INA men and felt direct identification with the cause.

2. Grievances and politicisation: fear of unemployment after the war, poor service conditions and racial pay discrimination (cf. 1857), inspiration from the INA - coordinated, not isolated.

3. Symbols and unity: tricolour hoisted with INA, INC and Muslim League flags; 'Quit India' written on ships; trucks with pictures of Netaji through Bombay; CPI support and a Bombay workers' strike; Aruna Asaf Ali's leadership; RIAF sympathy strikes at Karachi.

4. The myth and the edifice: the 'martial races' myth cultivated since 1857 shattered; the Raj rested on using Indian soldiers against Indians - once that instrument cracked visibly, the game was over.

5. Timing: widely seen as a key factor convincing the British that transfer of power could not be postponed.

Balance: the mutiny ended by repression and the withdrawal of INC and League support (Patel and Jinnah calmed the ratings; Gandhi and INC saw a truce period); the ratings were not recruited after independence - so 'last nail', not sole cause.

Closing: the mutiny was less a battle than a signal - the last reliable instrument of control was in doubt, and the transfer of power followed.
