2000PYQ 1(Prelims 2000)

The Indian National Army (INA) came into existence in 1943 in

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Singapore: Following the fall of Singapore in February 1942, Captain Mohan Singh conceived the idea of the INA by organising Indian PoWs... 40,000 PoWs in Singapore joined — and it was at Singapore in July 1943 that Netaji took over the INA and went on to proclaim the Provisional Government. (See the ⚠ Prelims Trap in on the 1942-vs-1943 date.)

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Japan was the patron power and the place of R.B. Bose's exile, but the army itself was raised at Singapore from the Indian PoWs handed over there.

(B) ✗ — Rangoon (Burma) became an INA headquarters only after the Provisional Government was set up in October 1943; the force originated at Singapore.

(D) ✗ — Malaya was the first of the three territories Japan overran in early 1942 — context, not birthplace: the PoW regrouping that created the INA happened at Singapore. (Note: INA was an armed force formed by Indian nationalists in 1942 in South-East Asia during World War II. The aim of the army was to secure Indian Independence with Japanese assistance. Initially formed in 1942 under Captain Mohan Singh, it was later revived in 1943 under Subhas Chandra Bose.)

2005PYQ 2(Prelims 2005)

Consider the following statements 1. Lord Mountbatten was the viceroy, when Shimla Conference took place. 2. Indian Navy Revolt, 1946 took place when the Indian sailors in the Royal Indian Navy (RIN) at Bombay and Karachi rose against the government. Which of the statement(s) given above is/are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Statement 2 is recorded: the mutiny began on HMIS Talwar at Bombay, and at Karachi the HMIS Hindustan rose with it. Statement 1 fails: the Simla Conference of June 1945 belongs to Wavell's viceroyalty (1943–47) — Mountbatten replaced Wavell only in 1947. Hence only 2.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Reversed: statement 1 is the false one — Wavell, not Mountbatten, was Viceroy when the Simla Conference met in June 1945 — while the RIN revolt at Bombay and Karachi is the recorded fact.

(C) ✗ — Statement 1 fails — the Simla Conference sat under Wavell (1943–47); Mountbatten's viceroyalty came in 1947 — so 'both' overcounts.

(D) ✗ — Statement 2 stands: sailors of the Royal Indian Navy rose at Bombay and Karachi in February 1946 — so 'neither' fails. (Note: Lord Wavell was the Governor General of India during 1943-1947 and June, 1945, Shimla Conference begins on 28th February, it was due to the efforts of Vallabhbhai Patel, the ratings surrendered.)

2008PYQ 3(Prelims 2008)

Which one of the following suggested the reconstitution of the Viceroy's Executive Council in which all the portfolios including that of war members were to be held by the Indian leaders?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — The Wavell Plan, placed before the Simla Conference of June 1945, proposed reconstituting the Viceroy's Executive Council with all portfolios — including the War Member's — in Indian hands. All INC leaders were released in June 1945 precisely to attend this conference.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The Simon Commission's recorded recommendations were provincial autonomy with "No change at the Centre" — the opposite of Indianising the Viceroy's Executive Council.

(C) ✗ — Cripps offered no such Indianisation: Defence stayed with the Viceroy and there was to be "no dilution of the Viceroy's powers over ministers" — the talks broke down on exactly that point.

(D) ✗ — The Cabinet Mission came later, in 1946, to settle the constitution-making question — not to reconstitute the wartime Executive Council. (Note: The plan suggested the reconstitution of the Viceroy's Executive Council. Different Communities were to get their due share in the council and parity was reserved for cast Hindus and Muslims.)

2017PYQ 5(Prelims 2017)

With reference to Indian freedom struggle, consider the following events: 1. Mutiny in Royal Indian Navy 2. Quit India Movement launched 3. Second Round Table Conference What is the correct chronological sequence of the above events?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Chronology from the record: the 2nd Round Table Conference sat September–December 1931; the Quit India Movement was launched in August 1942; the Naval Ratings Mutiny came in February 1946. Order: 3 – 2 – 1.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Code 1-2-3 reverses the line completely: it puts the 1946 mutiny first and the 1931 RTC last.

(B) ✗ — Code 2-1-3 starts with Quit India (1942) and ends with the 2nd RTC — but the RTC (1931) came first of the three.

(D) ✗ — Code 3-1-2 places the Naval Mutiny (February 1946) before Quit India (August 1942) — impossible. (Note: Mutiny of Royal Indian Navy broke in February, 1946 at Bombay on Talwar ship and in Karachi at Hindustan ship. Quit India Movement is launched on 9th August, 1942 at Bombay.)

2021PYQ 6(Prelims 2021)

In the context of Colonial India, Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Kumar Sehgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon are remembered as

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — The most famous trial was held at the Red Fort: Shah Nawaz Khan (a close aide of Netaji), Prem Sehgal, and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon were the accused. They are remembered as officers of the INA.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The Swadeshi and Boycott Movement belongs to 1905–11 — a different generation; the trio's record is the Red Fort INA trial of November 1945.

(B) ✗ — The Interim Government of 1946 carries a different cast; in 1945–46 these three men were standing trial at the Red Fort.

(C) ✗ — The Drafting Committee belongs to the Constituent Assembly's constitution-writing work; the recorded fame of Khan, Sehgal and Dhillon is as the three INA accused — Muslim, Hindu and Sikh — whose trial united the country. (Note: Major General Shah Nawaz Khan, Colonel Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon and Colonel Prem Kumar Sehgal were the officers of the Indian National Army (INA). They were captured by the British when INA was defeated.)

Chapter-derived Mains practice with the demand, guiding themes and a conclusion.

FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words The Indian National Army (1942-45) was militarily defeated but politically transformative. Examine.

Demand of the question: "Examine" + "defeated but transformative" demands distinguishing military outcome from political consequence.

Suggested intro (~25 words): INA never captured an Indian city; its forces retreated from Imphal-Kohima by August 1944; Bose died in August 1945. Yet INA's political effect was disproportionate to its military record. Body guidance — 4 themes:

1. The military record (see 'Indian National Army / Azad Hind Fauj (1942–1945)'). INA reached Imphal & Kohima in March-April 1944 with Japanese support but couldn't sustain — British counter-offensive + monsoon + supply collapse forced retreat. By August 1944, INA was in administrative retreat; Japan's defeat in August 1945 ended INA's external base.

2. The Trials transformed perception (see 'INA Trials and INA Agitations (November 1945 – 1946)'). Red Fort Trials Nov 1945 made INA the focal point of post-war nationalism. The three defendants (Hindu/Muslim/Sikh) symbolised inter-community unity; their defence panel (Bhulabhai Desai + Nehru + Sapru + Asaf Ali) made the trials INC's political theatre. Public agitation forced sentence remission — establishing INA cadres as nationalist heroes, not traitors.

3. The military demonstration effect (see 'The Naval Ratings Mutiny (February 1946)'). RIN Mutiny February 1946 — sailors explicitly invoked INA inspiration. The Mutiny showed the British that even loyalist military forces could not be trusted indefinitely. This is the most direct evidence of INA's transformative effect on Indian military thinking.

4. The political accelerant. INA + Trials + RIN Mutiny convinced post-war Labour government that holding India militarily was unsustainable. Cabinet Mission 1946, Mountbatten 1947, & rapid transfer-of-power followed. Without the INA's political legacy, the timetable would have been different.

Closing verdict: INA's military legacy is small. Its political legacy — disrupting the British loyalist-military framework, accelerating transfer-of-power decisions, providing the symbolic anchor of post-1945 nationalism — is large. The legitimacy of the INA's struggle was vindicated by India's 1972 official recognition as "Freedom Fighters".

Revise: 'INA founding + Azad Hind Government + Imphal-Kohima' + 'Red Fort Trials' + 'RIN Mutiny'.

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words The Bengal Famine of 1943 was not a natural disaster but an administrative & policy failure. Examine.

Demand of the question: "Examine" + "not natural but administrative" demands tracing causes beyond food production failure.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Death toll 2-3 million. Bengal had near-normal rice production in 1942-43. The famine was not caused by absolute food shortage — but by entitlement collapse, hoarding & British policy. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Bengal Famine (1943)'):

1. Entitlement Collapse. Amartya Sen's analysis: rural workers, fisherfolk, artisans lost income because wartime urban inflation diverted purchasing power. Their entitlement to food collapsed even though absolute food availability did not. The poor starved not because food was absent but because they couldn't buy it.

2. British "Denial Policy". Japanese threat from Burma prompted British "denial policy" in coastal Bengal — destruction of boats (denying transport to a potential Japanese invasion), removal of rice stocks from coastal areas. The boats were used by Bengali fisherfolk; their livelihoods collapsed.

3. War-time Procurement & Churchill's Hand. British military procured rice for Indian army + war supplies. Churchill diverted food shipments away from Bengal even when Australia & Canada offered food aid. Press censorship hid the famine; Calcutta's commercial elite hoarded rice for speculation. Administrative response was slow & inadequate.

Closing verdict: Bengal 1943 was a policy famine — produced by war-time British administrative decisions, racial indifference (Churchill's "Indians breed like rabbits" remark), & speculative hoarding. Natural causes were marginal; political causes were primary.

Revise: 'Bengal Famine'.
