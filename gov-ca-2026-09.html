2.7 · Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Amendment Rules, 2026#

Prelims + MainsEconomy
The Department of Consumer Affairs notified amendments to the 2020 E-Commerce Rules; in force from January 2027.
300 millionNeed29%Need
● Complaintsacknowledged in 48 hours and resolved in a month (continues); consumer gets a copy of the recorded complaint; every e-commerce entity must become a National Consumer Helpline (NCH) convergence partner.
● Search and listingsno manipulation of search results; main ranking factors explained in order of importance; sponsored listings clearly marked.
●Price cuts must show the prior price, meaning the lowest price in the preceding 30 days.
● Dark patternsfollow the 2023 Guidelines; yearly self-audit and a prominently displayed compliance certificate.
● Disclosurescountry of origin, best-before date, return-shipping cost, delivery and payment terms; seller GSTIN or MSME registration. Express consent needed for promotional use of consumer information.
★ Needonline shoppers about 290-300 million; about 29% of NCH complaints in 2025 were e-commerce (5.11 lakh of 17.72 lakh).
⚠ Issuesself-audit may not be enough; enforcement at scale; overlap with competition, data protection (DPDP Act 2023, Rules 2025) and legal metrology; burden on small businesses.
➜ Way forwardCCPA audit standards and continuous compliance; automated and risk-based supervision; coordination among CCPA, CCI, MeitY and Legal Metrology.
Prelims fodder
● Marketplace modelplatform facilitates transactions; inventory model: entity owns and sells the stock.
● CCPASection 10, Consumer Protection Act, 2019; set up 2020, New Delhi.
●CCI Market Study (2020) flagged platform neutrality, price parity, deep discounts.
●EU also uses a 30-day lowest-price benchmark.

2.8 · DILRMP 3.0 (2026-31)#

Prelims + MainsEconomy
The Ministry of Rural Development issued Operational Guidelines for DILRMP 3.0 (2026-2031).
Rs 565.50 croreHistory99.90%Achievements97%Achievements60%Need
● HistoryNLRMP (2008-16) from merging CLR and SRA&ULR; DILRMP (2016-26) as a Central Sector Scheme with Bhu-Aadhaar (ULPIN) and NGDRS. DILRMP 3.0: Central Sector Scheme, outlay Rs 565.50 crore over FY 2026-27 to 2030-31, run by the Department of Land Resources; shift from digitisation to an integrated, interoperable GIS-based Digital Public Infrastructure.
✔ Achievements99.90% Records of Rights digitised, 97% cadastral maps, 99% Sub-Registrar Offices computerised.
● PillarsLand Stack (federated, links cadastral maps, RoR, registration and court cases through APIs); Bhu-Aadhaar (14-digit geospatial ID for every parcel); Registration Seva Kendras (75 high-footfall SROs, like Passport Seva Kendras); NAKSHA (urban survey, urban property cards).
★ Needover 60% of civil litigation is land related and takes about 20 years; fragmented databases; unclear titles delay projects; small farmers lack verifiable titles for loans.
⚠ Challengesdigitally signed RoRs not legally valid in all States; presumptive titling, not guaranteed title; legacy errors; NAKSHA still at pilot stage.
➜ Way forwardlegal validity for digital RoRs; conclusive titling (Rajan committee on Financial Sector Reforms; NITI Aayog draft Model Land Titling Act with Land Titling Authority and Appellate Tribunals); verify legacy records; auto-triggered mutation after registration.
Prelims fodder
●SVAMITVA (2020, Ministry of Panchayati Raj): property cards for village abadi areas. Cadastral map: large-scale map of land parcel boundaries and ownership. Mutation: updating land records after sale, inheritance, partition or court order.

1.1 · FCRA Amendment Bill, 2026#

Prelims + MainsPolity
The FCRA Amendment Bill, 2026 has been referred to the Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC).
●FCRA governs how individuals, associations, NGOs, trusts and companies receive and use money, securities or articles from outside India. Administered by the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA).
● Two routesRegistration (valid 5 years, must be renewed) and Prior Permission (one-time receivers, for a specific purpose and source).
● Prohibited recipientselection candidates; journalists, editors and owners of registered newspapers; judges and government servants; MPs and MLAs; political parties and office-bearers; organisations of a political nature.
● Bill addsa certificate ceases if not renewed before expiry, no renewal application was made, or renewal was denied.
●A Designated Authority handles vesting, supervision, management and disposal of foreign-contribution assets once a certificate ends. Provisional vesting (returned if registration is restored); permanent vesting if not restored, used for public purposes, sale proceeds go to the Consolidated Fund of India.
●The Authority must preserve the religious character of places of worship and cannot convert or secularise them.
● Penaltymaximum jail term cut to one year (from five). Prior approval of the Central Government is needed to start an investigation.
Prelims fodder
● Vestinglegal process of earning permanent, unconditional ownership of an asset.
● FCRA vs FEMAFCRA is with MHA (donations, grants); FEMA is with Ministry of Finance/RBI (commercial flows, FDI).
●NGOs may register as Societies (1860 Act), Trusts (Indian Trusts Act, 1882) or Section 8 Companies (Companies Act, 2013).
Mains fodder
★ Significancecurbs foreign-funded interference; fills the legal gap on assets after a certificate ends; aligns with the Jan Vishwas approach; supports FATF commitments.
⚠ Concernspermanent loss of NGO assets on a missed deadline; vesting can reach assets even if the NGO now uses domestic funds; assets under prior permission excluded; no hearing or appeal against denial of renewal; prior Central approval dilutes State police powers.
➜ Way forwardgrace period and judicial approval before permanent vesting; audited exit; consistent treatment; digital compliance support; risk-based regulation with graded penalties (FATF).
● Noel Harper v. Union of India (2022)SC upheld the 2020 amendments; receiving foreign contribution is not an absolute right. S. Vijay Kumar Committee (2017) favoured lighter regulation; Second ARC (2008) said decentralise FCRA implementation.
🔑 KeywordsDesignated AuthorityAsset VestingDue ProcessJudicial OversightCivil Society AccountabilityJan Vishwas

1.3 · FSSAI's Food Safety Push#

Prelims + MainsPolity
In 3S and Our Health Society v. Union of India the Supreme Court reserved its order on FSSAI's proposed Front-of-Pack Nutrition Labelling (FoPL). It questioned the one-year voluntary compliance period.
2%Initiatives30.7%Why stronger regulation27.3%Why stronger regulation
● InitiativesLabelling and Display Regulations, 2020; FoSCoS (digital licensing and compliance platform); trans-fat limit of 2% by weight; Food Safety on Wheels; FoSTaC training; Hygiene Rating Scheme.
★ Why stronger regulationNFHS-6 shows 30.7% of women and 27.3% of men (15-49) overweight or obese; high blood sugar in 17.8% of women and 20.9% of men; rising processed food; online food delivery growth; over 2.23 lakh samples tested in 2025-26, more than 40,000 non-conforming.
⚠ Concernsweak enforcement (12,780 of 33,405 milk samples non-conforming in 2024-25); CAG found state labs lacked facilities; informal food sector; misleading advertising of HFSS foods (FSSAI banned "ORS" on sugary drinks not following the WHO formula).
➜ Way forwardrisk-based inspections (RBIS); transparent nutrient profiling; NABL labs and 305 Food Safety on Wheels units in 35 States/UTs (March 2026); mandatory, simple FoPL warnings in regional languages; interoperable monitoring.
Prelims fodder
● FSSAIFood Safety and Standards Act, 2006; Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
● InitiativesEat Right India, Clean Street Food Hub, Diet4Life.
●India's National Codex Contact Point (Codex Alimentarius, joint FAO-WHO).
●Chile and Mexico show mandatory warning labels aid reformulation.
Mains fodder
●Shift from reactive enforcement to preventive, consumer-oriented regulation.
🔑 KeywordsNutrient profilingRisk-based regulationBehavioural nudgesNutrition literacy

1.4.5 · Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) and State consent#

Prelims + MainsPolity
The Bihar Government expanded the CBI's general consent for specified cases involving Central Government employees, Central PSUs and related private persons. Prior State approval is still needed for State Government employees and State-controlled institutions.
●CBI was set up in 1963 by an executive resolution; it is not a statutory body. Its powers come from the Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) Act, 1946.
● Section 5 DSPE ActCentre may extend CBI powers to a State. Section 6: ordinarily needs the State's consent.
●General consent lets the CBI investigate covered categories without approval in each case; specific consent is case by case.
●The Supreme Court and High Courts can order a CBI investigation without State consent.
🔑 KeywordsGeneral consentDSPE Act Section 6federal character
