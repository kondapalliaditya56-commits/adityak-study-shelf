2.7 · Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Amendment Rules, 2026#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

The Department of Consumer Affairs notified amendments to the 2020 E-Commerce Rules; in force from January 2027.

  • Complaints: acknowledged in 48 hours and resolved in a month (continues); consumer gets a copy of the recorded complaint; every e-commerce entity must become a National Consumer Helpline (NCH) convergence partner.
  • Search and listings: no manipulation of search results; main ranking factors explained in order of importance; sponsored listings clearly marked.
  • Price cuts must show the prior price, meaning the lowest price in the preceding 30 days.
  • Dark patterns: follow the 2023 Guidelines; yearly self-audit and a prominently displayed compliance certificate.
  • Disclosures: country of origin, best-before date, return-shipping cost, delivery and payment terms; seller GSTIN or MSME registration. Express consent needed for promotional use of consumer information.
  • Need: online shoppers about 290-300 million; about 29% of NCH complaints in 2025 were e-commerce (5.11 lakh of 17.72 lakh).
  • Issues: self-audit may not be enough; enforcement at scale; overlap with competition, data protection (DPDP Act 2023, Rules 2025) and legal metrology; burden on small businesses.
  • Way forward: CCPA audit standards and continuous compliance; automated and risk-based supervision; coordination among CCPA, CCI, MeitY and Legal Metrology.
Prelims fodder
  • Marketplace model: platform facilitates transactions; inventory model: entity owns and sells the stock.
  • CCPA: Section 10, Consumer Protection Act, 2019; set up 2020, New Delhi.
  • CCI Market Study (2020) flagged platform neutrality, price parity, deep discounts.
  • EU also uses a 30-day lowest-price benchmark.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Dark patterns; Choice architecture; Information asymmetry; Platform accountability

2.8 · DILRMP 3.0 (2026-31)#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

The Ministry of Rural Development issued Operational Guidelines for DILRMP 3.0 (2026-2031).

  • History: NLRMP (2008-16) from merging CLR and SRA&ULR; DILRMP (2016-26) as a Central Sector Scheme with Bhu-Aadhaar (ULPIN) and NGDRS. DILRMP 3.0: Central Sector Scheme, outlay Rs 565.50 crore over FY 2026-27 to 2030-31, run by the Department of Land Resources; shift from digitisation to an integrated, interoperable GIS-based Digital Public Infrastructure.
  • Achievements: 99.90% Records of Rights digitised, 97% cadastral maps, 99% Sub-Registrar Offices computerised.
  • Pillars: Land Stack (federated, links cadastral maps, RoR, registration and court cases through APIs); Bhu-Aadhaar (14-digit geospatial ID for every parcel); Registration Seva Kendras (75 high-footfall SROs, like Passport Seva Kendras); NAKSHA (urban survey, urban property cards).
  • Need: over 60% of civil litigation is land related and takes about 20 years; fragmented databases; unclear titles delay projects; small farmers lack verifiable titles for loans.
  • Challenges: digitally signed RoRs not legally valid in all States; presumptive titling, not guaranteed title; legacy errors; NAKSHA still at pilot stage.
  • Way forward: legal validity for digital RoRs; conclusive titling (Rajan committee on Financial Sector Reforms; NITI Aayog draft Model Land Titling Act with Land Titling Authority and Appellate Tribunals); verify legacy records; auto-triggered mutation after registration.
Prelims fodder
  • SVAMITVA (2020, Ministry of Panchayati Raj): property cards for village abadi areas. Cadastral map: large-scale map of land parcel boundaries and ownership. Mutation: updating land records after sale, inheritance, partition or court order.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Bhu-Aadhaar (ULPIN); Land Stack; Digital Public Infrastructure; Conclusive titling

1.1 · FCRA Amendment Bill, 2026#

Prelims + MainsPolity

The FCRA Amendment Bill, 2026 has been referred to the Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC).

  • FCRA governs how individuals, associations, NGOs, trusts and companies receive and use money, securities or articles from outside India. Administered by the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA).
  • Two routes: Registration (valid 5 years, must be renewed) and Prior Permission (one-time receivers, for a specific purpose and source).
  • Prohibited recipients: election candidates; journalists, editors and owners of registered newspapers; judges and government servants; MPs and MLAs; political parties and office-bearers; organisations of a political nature.
  • Bill adds: a certificate ceases if not renewed before expiry, no renewal application was made, or renewal was denied.
  • A Designated Authority handles vesting, supervision, management and disposal of foreign-contribution assets once a certificate ends. Provisional vesting (returned if registration is restored); permanent vesting if not restored, used for public purposes, sale proceeds go to the Consolidated Fund of India.
  • The Authority must preserve the religious character of places of worship and cannot convert or secularise them.
  • Penalty: maximum jail term cut to one year (from five). Prior approval of the Central Government is needed to start an investigation.
Prelims fodder
  • Vesting: legal process of earning permanent, unconditional ownership of an asset.
  • FCRA vs FEMA: FCRA is with MHA (donations, grants); FEMA is with Ministry of Finance/RBI (commercial flows, FDI).
  • NGOs may register as Societies (1860 Act), Trusts (Indian Trusts Act, 1882) or Section 8 Companies (Companies Act, 2013).
Mains fodder
  • Significance: curbs foreign-funded interference; fills the legal gap on assets after a certificate ends; aligns with the Jan Vishwas approach; supports FATF commitments.
  • Concerns: permanent loss of NGO assets on a missed deadline; vesting can reach assets even if the NGO now uses domestic funds; assets under prior permission excluded; no hearing or appeal against denial of renewal; prior Central approval dilutes State police powers.
  • Way forward: grace period and judicial approval before permanent vesting; audited exit; consistent treatment; digital compliance support; risk-based regulation with graded penalties (FATF).
  • Noel Harper v. Union of India (2022): SC upheld the 2020 amendments; receiving foreign contribution is not an absolute right. S. Vijay Kumar Committee (2017) favoured lighter regulation; Second ARC (2008) said decentralise FCRA implementation.
  • Keywords: Designated Authority; Asset Vesting; Due Process; Judicial Oversight; Civil Society Accountability; Jan Vishwas

1.3 · FSSAI's Food Safety Push#

Prelims + MainsPolity

In 3S and Our Health Society v. Union of India the Supreme Court reserved its order on FSSAI's proposed Front-of-Pack Nutrition Labelling (FoPL). It questioned the one-year voluntary compliance period.

  • Initiatives: Labelling and Display Regulations, 2020; FoSCoS (digital licensing and compliance platform); trans-fat limit of 2% by weight; Food Safety on Wheels; FoSTaC training; Hygiene Rating Scheme.
  • Why stronger regulation: NFHS-6 shows 30.7% of women and 27.3% of men (15-49) overweight or obese; high blood sugar in 17.8% of women and 20.9% of men; rising processed food; online food delivery growth; over 2.23 lakh samples tested in 2025-26, more than 40,000 non-conforming.
  • Concerns: weak enforcement (12,780 of 33,405 milk samples non-conforming in 2024-25); CAG found state labs lacked facilities; informal food sector; misleading advertising of HFSS foods (FSSAI banned "ORS" on sugary drinks not following the WHO formula).
  • Way forward: risk-based inspections (RBIS); transparent nutrient profiling; NABL labs and 305 Food Safety on Wheels units in 35 States/UTs (March 2026); mandatory, simple FoPL warnings in regional languages; interoperable monitoring.
Prelims fodder
  • FSSAI: Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006; Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
  • Initiatives: Eat Right India, Clean Street Food Hub, Diet4Life.
  • India's National Codex Contact Point (Codex Alimentarius, joint FAO-WHO).
  • Chile and Mexico show mandatory warning labels aid reformulation.
Mains fodder
  • Shift from reactive enforcement to preventive, consumer-oriented regulation.
  • Keywords: Nutrient profiling; Risk-based regulation; Behavioural nudges; Nutrition literacy
PYQ connect

2018 Prelims: FSS Act 2006 replaced the PFA Act 1954; FSSAI is not under the DGHS.

1.4.5 · Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) and State consent#

Prelims + MainsPolity

The Bihar Government expanded the CBI's general consent for specified cases involving Central Government employees, Central PSUs and related private persons. Prior State approval is still needed for State Government employees and State-controlled institutions.

  • CBI was set up in 1963 by an executive resolution; it is not a statutory body. Its powers come from the Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) Act, 1946.
  • Section 5 DSPE Act: Centre may extend CBI powers to a State. Section 6: ordinarily needs the State's consent.
  • General consent lets the CBI investigate covered categories without approval in each case; specific consent is case by case.
  • The Supreme Court and High Courts can order a CBI investigation without State consent.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: General consent; DSPE Act Section 6; federal character

1.5.2 · New Guidelines for Film Certification, 2026#

Prelims + MainsPolity

The I&B Ministry notified new film certification guidelines under the Cinematograph Act, 1952, superseding the 1991 notification.

  • CBFC (under the 1952 Act) certifies films.
  • Provisions: no glorification or justification of violence; no abuse or ridicule of persons with disabilities; sexual violence against women to be avoided.
  • Age-based certification retained: U/A 7+, U/A 13+, U/A 16+. Others: A (adults only), Specialised (restricted to a profession or class).
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: CBFC; U/A categories; Article 19(1)(a) and 19(2)
