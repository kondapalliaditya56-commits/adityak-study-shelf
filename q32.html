1998PYQ 1(Prelims 1998)

Who was the leader of the Ghadar Party?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — In 1913 Lala Hardayal, professor of Sanskrit and Indian philosophy in San Francisco, set up the weekly The Ghadr. The party HQ in San Francisco was called Yugantar Ashram (same name as the Bengal secret society). Note: original name Pacific Coast Hindustani Association; Sohan Singh Bhakna was founding President, Hardayal co-founder and moving spirit.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Bhagat Singh belongs to the LATER revolutionary generation. The First Lahore Conspiracy Case (1915, Ghadar) is separate from the Second (1929-30, Bhagat Singh and HSRA).

(C) ✗ — Tilak's arena was Extremist politics inside the INC, not the NRI revolutionary party of San Francisco.

(D) ✗ — Savarkar's society was Abhinav Bharat, run from India House, London - a separate stream from the Ghadar Party.

Memory hook: San Francisco professor with a weekly = Lala Hardayal.

2005PYQ 2(Prelims 2005)

What was Komagata Maru?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Canada's rule let in only Indians making an unbroken direct journey from India. Baba Gurdit Singh chartered the Japanese ship Komagata Maru from Hong Kong in early 1914 to challenge it; it reached Vancouver on 23 May 1914, and the government did not allow it to land.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Not a party and no Taiwan link: it was the SHIP chartered to challenge Canada's racist immigration rules.

(B) ✗ — Not a person: it was the vessel carrying 376 mostly Punjabi Sikh passengers to Vancouver.

(D) ✗ — Nothing Chinese about it: the voyage ran Hong Kong to Vancouver and ended at Budge Budge near Calcutta, where police firing killed about 20 passengers.

Memory hook: Komagata Maru = a SHIP, Hong Kong to Vancouver, 376 aboard.

2005PYQ 3(Prelims 2005)

Where were the Ghadar revolutionaries, who became active during the outbreak of the World War I based?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Thousands of Ghadarite NRIs returned to India from North America to trigger a massive sepoy mutiny in Punjab and ignite civil rebellion. Their headquarters, Yugantar Ashram, was at San Francisco on the US Pacific coast.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Central America never figures in Ghadar geography; the base was the Pacific coast of NORTH America.

(C) ✗ — 'West America' is not a region; the party's home, San Francisco, is in North America.

(D) ✗ — South America had no Ghadar presence; returning Ghadarites sailed from NORTH America for the Punjab mutiny plan.

Memory hook: Yugantar Ashram, San Francisco -> North America.

2014PYQ 4(Prelims 2014)

The Ghadr (Ghadar) was a

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — The Ghadar Movement was a global conspiracy spanning San Francisco, Berlin, Bengal and Punjab, but the party itself was set up by NRIs in the US with HQ at Yugantar Ashram, San Francisco. 'Ghadr' means revolt or rebellion against British rule, organised overseas by Indian immigrants to Canada and the USA.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — Singapore was a mutiny SITE in the wartime conspiracies, not the party's base.

(C) ✗ — Berlin was one node (the German connection); the party's own HQ was in San Francisco.

(D) ✗ — Tashkent and communism belong to M.N. Roy's later story; Ghadar was a secular-republican revolutionary movement run from San Francisco.

Memory hook: Ghadr = revolt; HQ = Yugantar Ashram, San Francisco.

2022PYQ 5(Prelims 2022)

Who were actively associated with the Ghadar Party?
1. Barindra Kumar Ghosh
2. Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee
3. Rash Behari Bose

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — Only Rash Behari Bose was actively associated with the Ghadar effort: he operated in Lahore to contact the sepoys for the mutiny planned for 21 February 1915. Barindra Ghosh belonged to Bengal's Anushilan-Jugantar stream; Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee to the later HRA generation.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Includes both non-Ghadarites and omits the one genuine Ghadar coordinator, Rash Behari Bose.

(B) ✗ — Chatterjee's organisation was the 1920s HRA, not the Ghadar Party; also drops Rash Behari Bose.

(C) ✗ — Right to include Rash Behari Bose, wrong to add Barindra Ghosh, whose field was the Bengal samiti network.

FYQ 1

Who was the first President of the Ghadar Party, established by NRIs in the United States in 1913?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — When NRIs set up the Ghadar Party in the US in 1913, Sohan Singh Bhakna became its first President; its San Francisco headquarters was Yugantar Ashram, echoing the Bengal secret society.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — Hardayal was the Sanskrit and philosophy professor who set up the weekly The Ghadr in 1913, but not first President.

(C) ✗ — Baba Gurdit Singh chartered the Komagata Maru in 1914; no role in founding the party.

(D) ✗ — Rash Behari Bose contacted sepoys in Lahore in 1915 and later escaped to Japan; not the founding President.

Memory hook: Bhakna chairs, Hardayal writes - first seat in San Francisco goes to the Punjabi farmer-ex-soldier.

FYQ 2

2. Its method had two stages - a press campaign overseas, then a return to India to repeat a 1857-style revolt.
3. It was primarily led by elite Bengali zamindars.
Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the aim was a secular Republic and the method was two-stage (overseas press campaign, then physical return to cause army disaffection and civil rebellion). Statement 3 is wrong: it was a movement of Punjabi NRIs and Bengal revolutionaries drawn from farmers, labourers, students and ex-soldiers.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Wrongly includes statement 3 and drops the two-stage plan.

(C) ✗ — Wrongly includes statement 3 and drops the secular republic goal.

(D) ✗ — Wrongly accepts statement 3.

Memory hook: Build the fire abroad, light it inside India - secular republic, not zamindar drawing-room.

FYQ 4

(D) ✓ — In 1912, a year before the Ghadar Party was formally set up, Lala Hardayal established the Bakunin Institute in California, calling it a 'monastery of anarchism' and marrying European anarchist strategy to Punjabi NRI grievances.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Bhakna was first President of the party but did not set up the institute.

(B) ✗ — Rash Behari Bose worked inside India contacting sepoys in Lahore in 1915.

Memory hook: Hardayal in 1912 builds the anarchist monastery - California gets a Bakunin chapel.

FYQ 5

Which Russian thinker, identified as a founding figure of anarchism, supplied the Ghadar Movement with the key concept of "Propaganda of the Deed"?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Michael Bakunin (1814-1876), Russian founding figure of anarchism, argued that all States are instruments of oppression and must be destroyed; the concept he supplied was 'Propaganda of the Deed' - armed acts ignite the masses more powerfully than speeches.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — Marx supplied the linked idea of a Stateless Society, but is not credited with 'Propaganda of the Deed'.

(C) ✗ — Lenin is not named in connection with the Ghadar ideological framework.

(D) ✗ — Kropotkin is not mentioned for this movement.

Memory hook: Bakunin says destroy the State - Hardayal turns the slogan into smuggled rifles.

FYQ 6

The Ghadr newspaper carried on its front page an expose of British imperialism with which exact title?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Every front page of The Ghadr carried 'Angrezi Raj ka kachcha Chithha', an expose of British imperialism, and the masthead carried the date of the 1857 revolt to reinforce the inspiration.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Indian War of Independence (1909) was Savarkar's book, which the paper published in parts; not the front-page title.

(C) ✗ — Ghadr Di Goonj was the title of the patriotic poems carried in the paper.

(D) ✗ — Amra Morbo, Jagat Jagbe was Bagha Jatin's rallying cry in Bengal.

Memory hook: Front page = 'kachcha Chithha', masthead = 1857 - every issue puts the Raj on the dock.

FYQ 9

Which Canadian rule, in force since 1908, did the Komagata Maru voyage specifically seek to challenge?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Since 1908 Canada allowed only Indians making a direct (continuous) journey from India to enter. The Komagata Maru, chartered from Hong Kong by Baba Gurdit Singh in 1914, was an explicit challenge to this racist rule.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — The record describes a 'direct journey' condition, not a blanket Asian ban.

(C) ✗ — No English literacy test is mentioned.

(D) ✗ — No gold-sovereign poll tax is mentioned.

Memory hook: Only an unbroken voyage from India counted - the Hong Kong stop made the ship 'illegal'.

FYQ 10

(B) ✓ — While Hardayal supplied the ideology from abroad, inside Bengal it was Jatindranath Mukherjee, Bagha Jatin (Jatin the Tiger), who reorganised the shattered network and unified Jugantar factions. His rallying cry: 'We shall die to awaken the nation'.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — B.K. Ghosh set up Jugantar in 1906 but did not lead the Ghadar-era reorganisation; the cry is not his.

(C) ✗ — Aurobindo Ghosh is not credited with this reorganisation.

(D) ✗ — Rash Behari Bose worked in Lahore on sepoy disaffection, not as Bengal organiser.

Memory hook: Tiger of Bengal stitches Jugantar back together - 'we die, the nation wakes.'

FYQ 1 "Chapter - Ghadar Movement (1913-15)": examine the major themes covered in this chapter, with specific reference to its key sections including "The Ghadar Movement" and "Three Plots: Global Dimensions of the Ghadar Conspiracy". (250 words)

Demand of the question: trace the chapter's arc from the Ghadar Movement (ideology, organisation) through the Komagata Maru pivot to the Three Plots, using dates, personalities and organisations from the chapter only.

Suggested intro (verdict): Ghadar was the first organised attempt to use the Indian diaspora for armed, secular-republican revolution - global in reach, brave in vision, but crushed by British intelligence and the absence of mass support.

1. The Ghadar Movement: Ghadar Party founded 1913 in San Francisco (HQ Yugantar Ashram; Sohan Singh Bhakna first President, Lala Hardayal moving spirit). Goal: secular Republic. Method in two stages: press campaign abroad, then return to India for army disaffection and civil rebellion. Bakunin's 'Propaganda of the Deed'; Bakunin Institute 1912; The Ghadr weekly (front page 'Angrezi Raj ka kachcha Chithha'); Free Hindustan (1907, Tarak Nath Das), Swadesh Sevak (1909, G.D. Kumar).

2. Mid-chapter pivot - Komagata Maru 1914: Baba Gurdit Singh's charter from Hong Kong, 376 passengers, Canada's 1908 direct-journey rule, forced out July 1914, Budge Budge riot in September with about 20 killed - anger turbocharged and Ghadarites began returning.

3. Three Plots: Plot 1 Punjab Mutiny (RB Bose, 21 Feb 1915, crushed by intelligence); Plot 2 Pacific Arms Plot (SS Maverick, 30,000 rifles, April-July 1915); Plot 3 Christmas Day Plot (Calcutta, Bagha Jatin; Rodda heist August 1914; Balasore, September 1915). Hindu-German (Zimmermann) conspiracy ties San Francisco, Berlin, Bengal, Punjab.

4. Failure and legacy: Defence of India Act (March 1915), First Lahore Conspiracy Case (April-September 1915), Sarabha hanged at 19 (November 1915); a lost generation, yet inspiration for Bhagat Singh.

Closing: Balasore silenced a particular, very brave vision of what India could be - secular, radical, diasporic.

FYQ 2 Discuss the historical significance of the events covered in "Chapter - Ghadar Movement (1913-15)" for the broader trajectory of the Indian National Movement. Use specific examples from chapter notes. (150 words)

Demand of the question: 3-4 chapter examples, each framed as cause -> consequence, ending with the chapter's closing takeaway as verdict.

Suggested intro: Ghadar was perhaps the first sustained revolutionary current explicitly and consciously secular, shaping the later revolutionary generation.

1. Komagata Maru (1914) -> Canada's racist direct-journey rule exposed Britain's treatment of 'British subjects' -> anger, Budge Budge firing, return of Ghadarites.

2. Sarabha hanged at 19 (Nov 1915) -> martyr-hero; Bhagat Singh kept his photograph -> inspired the next generation (HSRA, Second Lahore Conspiracy Case 1929-30).

3. Defence of India Act 1915 and First Lahore Conspiracy Case -> detention without trial, special tribunals, no appeal -> template for later repression (Rowlatt Acts 1919).

4. First organised use of the diaspora for armed revolution -> showed 1857's spirit had not died; secular Hindu-Muslim-Sikh unity rejected religious identity as the basis of the nation.

Closing takeaway: Balasore was not just a military failure - it silenced a brave, radical vision of India, but that vision outlived the defeat.
