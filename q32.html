FYQ 1

Who was the first President of the Ghadar Party, established by NRIs in the United States in 1913?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — When the Ghadar Party was set up by NRIs in the US in 1913, Sohan Singh Bhakna became its first President; its headquarters in San Francisco was called Yugantar Ashram, echoing the Bengal secret society.

Why others wrong: (b) Lala Hardayal was a Sanskrit & Indian philosophy professor in San Francisco who set up the weekly The Ghadr in 1913, but he was not the first President of the party; (c) Baba Gurdit Singh chartered the Komagata Maru in 1914, he had no role in founding the party; (d) Rash Behari Bose contacted sepoys in Lahore in 1915 and later escaped to Japan, he was not the founding President.

Memory hook: Bhakna chairs, Hardayal writes — first seat in San Francisco goes to the Punjabi farmer-ex-soldier.

FYQ 2

Consider the following statements about the Ghadar Movement: 1. Its declared goal was a secular Republic of India i.e. Purna Swaraj. 2. Its method had two stages — a press campaign overseas, then a return to India to repeat a 1857-style revolt. 3. It was primarily led by elite Bengali zamindars. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The Ghadar Movement aimed at a secular Republic of India i.e. Purna Swaraj, and used a two-stage method — first a press campaign overseas, then a physical return to India to cause disaffection in the army plus a civil rebellion. Statement 3 is wrong — it was a movement of Punjabi NRIs and Bengal revolutionaries, drawing members from farmers, labourers, students and ex-soldiers, not Bengali zamindars.

Why others wrong: (a) wrongly includes Statement 3 and drops the two-stage plan; (c) wrongly includes Statement 3 and drops the secular republic goal; (d) wrongly accepts

Statement 3.

Memory hook: Build the fire abroad, light it inside India — secular republic, not zamindar drawing-room.

FYQ 3

Which newspaper, published from Vancouver in 1907 by Tarak Nath Das, exposed the irony of Britain sending Indians to Fiji plantations while barring their entry into North America?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — In 1907, Tarak Nath Das launched Free Hindustan from Vancouver to highlight the plantation-versus-migration irony — Britain sent Indians as plantation labourers to Fiji yet barred their entry into Canada and the US.

Why others wrong: (a) Swadesh Sevak was G.D. Kumar's Gurmukhi weekly from Canada in 1909, not Tarak Nath Das's 1907 paper; (b) The Ghadr was Lala Hardayal's weekly from San Francisco, launched in 1913; (d) Yugantar was a Bengal secret-society publication, not a Vancouver Indian-NRI paper.

Memory hook: Tarak in Vancouver, paper named Free Hindustan — plantation in Fiji, no land in Canada.

FYQ 4

The Bakunin Institute, described by its founder as a "monastery of anarchism," was set up in California in 1912 by which leader?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(D) ✓ — In 1912, a year before the Ghadar Party was formally set up, Lala Hardayal established the Bakunin Institute in California and described it as a "monastery of anarchism," marrying European anarchist strategy to Punjabi NRI grievances.

Why others wrong: (a) Sohan Singh Bhakna was the first President of the Ghadar Party but did not set up the Bakunin Institute; (b) Rash Behari Bose worked inside India contacting sepoys in Lahore in 1915, he was not the institute's founder; (c) Bagha Jatin reorganised the Jugantar network in Bengal, he had no role in the California institute.

Memory hook: Hardayal in 1912 builds the anarchist monastery — California gets a Bakunin chapel.

FYQ 5

Which Russian thinker, identified as a founding figure of anarchism, supplied the Ghadar Movement with the key concept of "Propaganda of the Deed"?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — Michael Bakunin (1814–1876), a Russian thinker and founding figure of anarchism, argued that all States are instruments of oppression and must be destroyed; the key anarchist concept he supplied to the Ghadar Movement was "Propaganda of the Deed" — armed acts ignite the masses more powerfully than any speech.

Why others wrong: (b) Karl Marx supplied the linked idea of a Stateless Society but is not credited by the record with the "Propaganda of the Deed" formulation; (c) Vladimir Lenin is not named by the record in connection with the Ghadar ideological framework; (d) Peter Kropotkin is not mentioned for this movement.

Memory hook: Bakunin says destroy the State — Hardayal turns the slogan into smuggled rifles.

FYQ 6

The Ghadr newspaper carried on its front page an exposé of British imperialism with which exact title?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — Every front page of The Ghadr newspaper carried "Angrezi Raj ka kachcha Chithha" — an exposé of British imperialism — and the masthead carried the date of the 1857 revolt to reinforce the inspiration.

Why others wrong: (a) Indian War of Independence (1909) was Veer Savarkar's book, which the paper published in parts — it was not the front-page exposé title; (c) Ghadr Di Goonj was the title of the patriotic poems carried in the paper, not the exposé heading; (d) Amra Morbo, Jagat Jagbe was Bagha Jatin's rallying cry in Bengal, not a front-page headline.

Memory hook: Front page = "kachcha Chithha", masthead = 1857 — every issue puts the Raj on the dock.

FYQ 9

Which Canadian rule, in force since 1908, did the Komagata Maru voyage specifically seek to challenge?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — Canada had restricted Indian entry since 1908 by allowing only those who made a direct (continuous) journey from India to enter; the Komagata Maru, chartered by Baba Gurdit Singh in 1914 from Hong Kong, was an explicit challenge to this racist "direct journey" rule.

Why others wrong: (b) the record describes a "direct journey" condition, not a blanket Asian-immigration ban; (c) no English literacy test is mentioned; (d) no gold-sovereign poll tax is mentioned.

Memory hook: Only an unbroken voyage from India counted — Hong Kong stop made the ship "illegal."

FYQ 10

Who reorganised the shattered revolutionary network across Bengal during the Ghadar period and is remembered by the rallying cry "Amra morbo, jagat jagbe"?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — While Hardayal supplied the ideological framework from abroad, inside Bengal it was Jatindranath Mukherjee — universally known as Bagha Jatin (Jatin the Tiger) — who reorganised the shattered revolutionary network and unified Jugantar factions; his rallying cry was "Amra morbo, jagat jagbe" — "We shall die to awaken the nation".

Why others wrong: (a) Barindra Kumar Ghosh (B.K. Ghosh) had set up the Jugantar Party in 1906 but did not lead its Ghadar-era reorganisation, and the rallying cry is not attributed to him; (c) Aurobindo Ghosh, B.K. Ghosh's elder brother, is not credited by the record with this reorganisation; (d) Rash Behari Bose worked in Lahore on sepoy disaffection — he was not the Bengal organiser.

Memory hook: Tiger of Bengal stitches Jugantar back together — "we die, the nation wakes."
