In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • A political party is a voluntary, organised group that seeks public power by lawful means. The Constitution does not define parties
  • Art. 19(1)(c) (form associations) lets them exist
  • Parties are classed by ideology:
    • Radical = left, Liberal = centre, Reactionary and Conservative = right (CPI/CPM left, INC centre, BJP right of centre). By system: one, two or multi-party
    • India is multi-party
  • The ECI (Art. 324) registers parties under Sec. 29A of the RP Act, 1951 and recognises them as National, State or Registered-Unrecognised, using the Symbols Order, 1968.
  • Recognised parties get a reserved symbol, free airtime on Doordarshan and AIR, and access to electoral rolls. Others pick a free symbol.
  • Regional parties have a limited footprint, rest on local identity, and now decide coalitions. They check the Centre and widen choice, but can put regional interests above national ones.

Key terms

Political party
Voluntary, organised group that seeks and uses public power by lawful, constitutional means
Congress system
Long one-party dominance of the Congress, later weakened by regional and new national parties
Reserved symbol
Symbol kept exclusively for the candidates of recognised national or state parties
Free symbol
Symbol from an ECI list for registered-unrecognised parties and independents
Registered-Unrecognised party
Registered under Sec. 29A but failing the criteria; contests with free symbols and has no privileges
National party
Recognised by the ECI across the country; any one of three routes (6% in 4 states plus 4 LS seats; 2% LS seats from 3 states; State party in 4 states)
State (regional) party
Recognised in a state by any one of five routes; its reserved symbol works only in that state
Offshoot party
Regional party formed by a split from a national party; inherits its structure and cadres
Remember it as

Art. 19(1)(c) lets parties form; Art. 324 + RP Act 1951 (Sec. 29A) + 1968 Symbols Order recognise them; National = 4 states and 4 seats / 2% (11 of 543) / State party in 4 states.

1 · Meaning and ideological types#

  • Political parties are voluntary, organised groupings of persons around shared political beliefs who seek to acquire and exercise public power by lawful, constitutional means. They aggregate interests, formulate public policies and give citizens distinct choices at elections. They also claim to work for the national interest while following programmes that reflect their ideology and organisational priorities.

Four types by ideological orientation

ConservativeFavour the status quo; incremental change, not radical transformationRight

3 · Constitutional underpinning#

  • The enabling base for parties is the fundamental freedom to form associations and unions, Article 19(1)(c). It lets citizens organise political parties and take part collectively in democracy.
  • Key point: The Constitution does not itself define or regulate parties exhaustively. Article 19(1)(c) only creates the enabling environment in which parties operate and contest public office. Recognition and functioning are governed by the Representation of the People Act, 1951 and the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968, both administered by the Election Commission of India under Article 324 (see Section 5).
  • Memory aid (Legal base): Art. 19(1)(c) (association), RP Act 1951 (registration under Sec. 29A), ECI under Art. 324 (recognition and symbols): "All Recognised Entities".

4 · Features of the Indian party system#

  • Parties are organised, voluntary associations that aggregate interests, mobilise voters, frame programmes and offer alternatives. Eight features are stressed.

Eight features

  • Plurality and multi-party character: vast territory, social and cultural diversity, universal adult franchise and the political process produced very many parties. Left, centre and right coexist with communal and non-communal formations. The result is often hung legislatures and coalitions at the Centre and in States.
  • Long single-party dominance and its decline: called the one-party dominance or the "Congress system". It weakened as regional formations and new national parties emerged, giving a progressively competitive multiparty order.
  • Weak ideological identities: except a few clearly ideological formations, parties have broad commitments (democracy, secularism, social justice) and overlap in policy. Mobilisation is driven by electoral considerations and issue positions more than deep ideology.
  • Leader-centred organisation: parties are identified with a prominent leader whose stature eclipses ideology. They are known by leaders' names or images rather than manifestos.
  • Identity bases: many parties formed on religion, caste, language or culture, unlike Western parties built on socio-economic programmes. They pursue sectional interests and can divert attention from broader public policy.
  • Rise of regional formations: first state-level, many became pivotal nationally in coalitions. They reshaped centre-state dynamics and national coalition patterns.
  • Factionalism, splits and instability: defections, splits, mergers and fragmentation, driven by ambition and pragmatism, cause instability, realignments and offshoots of major parties.
  • Challenge of effective opposition:
    • A robust opposition checks the executive and is a potential alternative government. India has struggled to sustain a cohesive national opposition
    • It has been fragmented and united only intermittently
  • Memory aid (Eight features): Plurality, Congress system, Weak ideology, Leader-centred, Identity, Regional rise, Factionalism, Opposition weak: "Parties Continue With Leaders Invoking Regional Factions, Opposition weak".

6 · Classification and recognition by the ECI#

  • The Constitution does not explicitly mention political parties. Their functioning and recognition are governed by the Representation of the People Act, 1951 and the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968, both administered by the Election Commission of India under Article 324.

How a party gets its status

  • Register: Under Section 29A of the RP Act, 1951
  • ECI evaluates performance: After elections, the Commission classifies the party
  • Status given: National Party, State Party or Registered-Unrecognised Party
How a party gets its status

Why recognition matters

  • Exclusive use of a reserved election symbol.
  • Free airtime on state-owned media (Doordarshan and All India Radio) during elections.
  • Access to electoral rolls and voter data for campaigns.
  • Enhanced status and visibility in the electoral arena.

Two kinds of symbols

KindFor whom
ReservedRecognised national or state parties for their candidates, exclusively
FreeCandidates of registered-unrecognised parties or independents (from a list notified by the ECI)
  • A national party uses its symbol across the country
  • A state party uses its reserved symbol only in the state or states where it is recognised

National Party: any ONE of three

RouteCondition
Vote share + LS seatsAt least 6% of valid votes in any four or more states in a Lok Sabha or State Assembly general election, and at least four LS seats from any state or states
Seats in Lok SabhaAt least 2% of total LS seats (11 of 543), elected from at least three different states
State party statusRecognised as a State Party in at least four states
  • These ensure broad geographical presence and electoral strength across India.

State Party: any ONE of five

No.Condition
16% of valid votes in the state's Assembly election and at least 2 Assembly seats
26% of valid votes in the Lok Sabha election from that state and at least 1 LS seat from the state
3At least 3% of total Assembly seats or 3 seats, whichever is higher
41 LS seat for every 25 seats (or any fraction) allotted to that state
5At least 8% of total valid votes in the state in either an LS or Assembly election
  • Memory aid (National route numbers):
    • Six percent in four states with four seats, Two percent (11 seats) from three states, State party in four states: "Six To Succeed"
    • For State parties remember 6, 3, 8 and 1-per-25
  • Shortcut: Number shortcut. National = 4s (four states, four seats, four-state recognition) with 2% = 11 of 543 as the odd route. State = 6% twice, 3% seats, 1-per-25, 8% votes.

Registered-Unrecognised

  • Registered under Section 29A but failing the criteria. They can contest elections but get no reserved symbol and none of the recognised parties' privileges
  • They choose from the free symbols

Changing dynamics

  • Status is re-assessed after each general election and the ECI revises the list.

  • National versus regional: National parties (All-India Parties) have a pan-India vision and presence. State parties, commonly called regional parties, represent specific states or regions. Both play a pivotal role in coalition politics and federal balance.

7 · Regional parties in the party system#

  • Regional parties operate mainly in sub-national spaces but influence local, state and national governance. They articulate region-specific interests, mobilise local identities and contest state and municipal elections, so that diversity is represented in democracy.
  • In coalition politics their importance has risen. Without a single-party majority at the Centre they are often pivotal partners
  • Their support can decide formation, durability and policy orientation of national governments
  • Within States, strong regional parties shape administrative priorities, legislative agendas and centre-state bargaining over resources and implementation.

Three interlinked roles

Roles drawn from local constituencies

  • Aggregate: Regional demands become coherent political programmes
  • Intermediate: They link local societies with higher tiers of government
  • Negotiate: Leverage in multi-party coalitions, influencing national policy
  • Result: deeper democratic representation within states and a balance of regional interests in the federal polity.
  • Memory aid (Three roles): Aggregate, Intermediary, Negotiate: "All Issues Negotiated".

Also in the handout

  • In the contemporary era of coalition politics, the strategic importance of regional parties has increased markedly.

8 · Character, types and causes of regional parties#

  • Regional parties get strength from concentrated social bases and claims grounded in local culture, language, ethnicity or community interests. Their main aim is to mobilise local constituencies, take up region-specific issues and capture power at state level to influence policy, resources and centre-state relations.

Salient characteristics

  • Limited territorial footprint: one state or clearly defined region.
  • Identity linked to cultural, religious, linguistic or ethnic communities
  • Demands flow from historic, economic or social particularities
  • Exploit local grievances or primordial loyalties: language, caste, religion, regional pride.
  • Appeal built on local development, identity protection and often claims for greater regional autonomy within the federation.
  • Programmes aim at securing state-level power rather than an immediate nationwide presence.

Four types

Typology of regional parties

Culture- or ethnicity-basedAnchored in regional cultural or ethnic identities (linguistic, religious or historic community claims)
Regionally rooted with pan-India outlookState base, all-India perspective, occasional national alliances; strength stays regional
Offshoots of national partiesCreated by splits from larger national parties; inherit structures and cadres, then evolve distinct regional programmes
Personalised or leader-centredBuilt around a charismatic individual; frequently short-lived or dependent on the founder's popularity
  • This typology explains why some regional parties are durable and others transient or episodic.

  • The 'Congress system' (one-party dominance) is the phrase of the analyst Rajni Kothari. Its dominance has been falling since 1967, with the rise of regional parties and of Janata (1977), Janata Dal (1989) and the BJP (1991).

  • Weak ideology:
    • Except the BJP and the two communist parties (CPI, CPM), other parties have no clear ideology
    • Almost all say they favour democracy, secularism, socialism and Gandhism, and are driven by power capture. It is said that India has 'political personalities rather than political parties'
  • Defections gained greater currency after the fourth general elections (1967). An effective, organised national Opposition has appeared only 'in flashes' since the first general elections.
  • Party-system examples:
    • One-party = former communist countries such as the USSR and East Europe
    • Two-party = USA (Democratic, Republican) and Britain (Conservative, Labour)
    • Multi-party = France, Switzerland, Italy

2% of LS seats (11 of 543) from at least three states.
National Party route 3?
Recognised as State Party in at least four states.
State Party criteria (five)?
6% + 2 Assembly seats; 6% in LS + 1 LS seat; 3% of seats or 3 seats; 1 LS seat per 25 seats; 8% of valid votes.
Numbers of recognised parties in 1952 and before 2019?
1952: 14 national and 39 state. Before 2019: 7 national and 52 state.
What is the "Congress system"?
Prolonged one-party dominance that later weakened as regional and new national parties emerged.
Name some features of the Indian party system.
Multi-party; Congress system; weak ideology; leader-centred; identity-based; regional rise; factionalism; weak opposition.
Four types of regional parties?
Culture/ethnicity-based; regionally rooted with pan-India outlook; offshoots of national parties; personalised or leader-centred.
Three roles of regional parties from local constituencies?
Aggregate regional demands; act as intermediaries; negotiate leverage in coalitions.
Regional leaders named as examples?
M. G. Ramachandran (Tamil Nadu) and N. T. Rama Rao (Andhra Pradesh).
Which regional role concerns Governors?
Exposing misuse of constitutional offices: appointment or dismissal of Chief Ministers and misuse of ordinance powers.
Six limits of regional parties?
Narrow priorities; identity politics; inter-state obstacles; corruption and nepotism; populism and fiscal strain; disproportionate coalition influence.
Who founded INC, CPI, DMK, BSP?
A. O. Hume (1885); M. N. Roy (1925); C. N. Annadurai (1949); Kanshi Ram (1984).
Who founded BJP, AAP, TRS, NPP?
Vajpayee and Advani (1980); Arvind Kejriwal (2012); K. Chandrashekar Rao (2001); P. A. Sangma (2013).
Parties formed in 1999?
PDP, JD(U), JD(S) and NCP.

Traps#

Trap 1 · National versus State party numbers National routes: 6% in four states plus four LS seats; 2% of LS seats (11 of 543) from three states; State party in four states. State routes: 6% plus 2 Assembly seats; 6% LS plus 1 LS seat; 3% seats or 3 seats; 1 LS seat per 25; 8% votes. Rule: National = 4s plus the 2%-from-3-states route; State = 6, 6, 3, 1-per-25, 8.
Trap 2 · Radical is left, not right Radical parties want a new order and sit on the left; liberal in the centre; reactionary and conservative on the right. Rule: radical = left, liberal = centre, the two old-order types = right.
Trap 3 · Parties are not in the Constitution Only Art. 19(1)(c) gives the freedom to form associations; recognition comes from the RP Act 1951, the 1968 Symbols Order and the ECI under Art. 324. Rule: Constitution = right to form; law + ECI = recognition.
Trap 4 · Reserved versus free symbols and the 1952 numbers Reserved symbols go to recognised parties; free symbols to registered-unrecognised parties and independents. In 1952 there were 14 national and 39 state parties; before 2019, 7 national and 52 state. Rule: recognised = reserved; others = free. National parties shrank from 14 to 7; state parties grew from 39 to 52.
