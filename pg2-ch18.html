1 · Office OF THE Governor · President's RULE · Article 356 · Bommai · Financial Emergency#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Centre State Relations handout pp. 18-23. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • The Governor is an appointed constitutional head and a Union-State link, which makes the office contested. Art 356 was often misused until Bommai (1994) made proclamations reviewable. Commissions prefer reforming, not abolishing, the Governor.

Constitutional Position

ArticleProvision
153a Governor for each State; one person can serve two or more
155appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal
156holds office during the pleasure of the President; normal term 5 years
157Indian citizen, 35 years
158not an MP/MLA, no office of profit
159oath before the High Court Chief Justice or senior-most judge

Why appointed

  • avoid dual authority with the CM
  • parliamentary system needs a constitutional head
  • national integration link
  • avoids cost of elections for a nominal post

Discretion

Art 163

  • Ministers aid and advise except where the Governor must act in discretion
  • Discretion must have a constitutional basis and is subject to review

Constitutional discretion

  • reserving Bills (Art 200)
  • Administrator of a UT (Art 239)
  • Sixth Schedule, Arts 371-371J roles

Situational discretion

  • hung Assembly: whom to invite
  • floor test on credible doubt
  • alternative government
  • report for Art 356

Issues WITH THE Office

Issues

  • Political appointees
  • Doctrine of pleasure insecurity
  • interference: CM choice, summoning Assemblies
  • delay in assent
  • controversial 356 reports
  • two competing centres of authority
Note

Counter: Governors have real duties in hung Assemblies, breakdowns and constitutionally sensitive Bills; not all intervention is misuse.

B. P. Singhal v. Union (2010)

  • Governor can be removed without public reasons, but not arbitrarily or capriciously
  • A change of government at the Centre alone is not enough

Hung Assembly

  • Risk of favouring one side and encouraging defections
  • Principle: floor test
  • Reform: objective conventions and prompt floor tests

Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker (2016)

  • Art 174 summon, prorogue, dissolve:
    • Governor generally acts on ministerial advice
    • Cannot interfere in legislative proceedings over political disagreement

President's RULE (article 356)

Procedure and effect

  • When State government cannot be carried on per the Constitution
  • On Governor's report or other information
  • Parliament approves within two months
  • lasts 6 months, extendable to a maximum of 3 years (conditions beyond one year)
  • President takes executive functions
  • Ministry usually ceases
  • Parliament exercises legislative power
  • Assembly suspended or dissolved
  • High Court powers cannot be taken over

Misuse

  • dismissal for political difference
  • Governors' reports with political motives
  • majority tested outside the Assembly
  • erosion of federalism. Examples:
    • Kerala 1959
    • 1977 and 1980 dismissals after Centre changed
    • 1992 after Babri Masjid
Way forward / key point

S. R. Bommai v. Union (1994), nine judges: federalism is Basic Structure; proclamation judicially reviewable; majority to be tested on the floor; satisfaction must rest on relevant material; courts may restore a dismissed government and Assembly.

Rameshwar Prasad v. Union (2006)

  • Dissolution of the Bihar Assembly (2005) held unconstitutional
  • Reports cannot rest on unsupported assumptions

Financial Emergency (Art 360)

  • if financial stability or credit of India is threatened
  • Parliament approves within two months
  • Continues until revoked
  • Never proclaimed
  • Union may direct financial propriety, salary reductions, reserving Money/Financial Bills

Reforms

BodyRecommendations
Sarkariaeminent person from outside the State, detached from politics, not ruling-party politician in opposition State; consult CM; keep 5-year tenure; Art 356 last resort, warn first, prefer floor test
Punchhifixed 5 years; delete "during the pleasure of the President" from Art 156; impeachment-type removal by State Legislature; define discretion; limit Chancellor-role controversy; six months to decide reserved Bills; localised emergency instead of whole State
NCRWC 2002appointment consulting PM, Home Minister, Lok Sabha Speaker and the State CM; security of tenure; neutrality

Abolish

  • colonial legacy
  • political interference
  • unaccountable
  • unneeded discretion

Retain

  • Union-State link
  • neutral umpire in hung Assemblies
  • sensitive/tribal regions
  • continuity
Way forward / key point

Preferred route: reform, not abolition: consultative appointment, security of tenure, limited discretion, no delay in Bills, less conflict over universities, 356 as exception.

2 · Fiscal Federalism · Divisible POOL · CESS · Finance Commission · 15th AND 16th FC#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Centre State Relations handout pp. 23-26. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • The Union holds major revenues while States hold major spending, so transfers are needed. The Finance Commission (Art 280) recommends vertical and horizontal devolution. 15th FC: 41%; 16th FC (Panagariya, 2026-31) kept 41% and added a GDP-contribution criterion.

Fiscal Imbalance AND Transfers

Vertical imbalance

  • Union has revenue powers, States have spending duties
  • dependence on transfers

Horizontal imbalance

  • States differ in income, population, geography
  • Poorer States need more
  • FC equalises

Divisible pool (Art 270)

  • shareable Union taxes excluding cesses, surcharges, certain separately assigned taxes, and collection costs
  • a 41% share does not mean 41% of the Centre's gross tax revenue

Cess and surcharge (Art 271)

  • surcharge:
    • Extra on an existing tax
    • Cess: tax for a specific purpose
  • Proceeds generally outside the pool, so the Union keeps them
  • Rising reliance is a major grievance

Grants-in-aid

  • Art 275: grants per the Finance Commission
  • Art 282: grants for any public purpose, even outside legislative competence
  • excess conditional/discretionary transfers reduce autonomy

Finance Commission (article 280)

Set-up

  • Constituted by the President every five years
  • Chairperson + four members
  • FC (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1951:
    • Chair with public affairs experience
    • Members from law, finance and accounts, financial administration, economics

Functions

  • vertical devolution (Union-States share of pool)
  • horizontal devolution (among States)
  • grants-in-aid principles
  • supplement resources of Panchayats and Municipalities (based on SFC reports)
  • other matters referred by the President
VerticalHorizontal
pool split between Union and States as a group; fixes vertical imbalanceStates' share split among States; fixes horizontal imbalance; income distance, population, area, forest cover

15th FC (2021-26), N. K. Singh

  • 41% (down from 42% because J&K became a Union Territory)
  • criteria: 2011 population, income distance, area, forest and ecology, demographic performance, tax effort
  • revenue deficit grants for 17 States
  • Local body grants
  • Disaster corpus about Rs 1.6 lakh crore
  • Sectoral and performance grants

16th FC (2026-31), Dr Arvind Panagariya

  • Report submitted 17 Nov 2025
  • Tabled 1 Feb 2026
  • 41% retained
  • Union accepted
  • Union Budget 2026-27 provides Rs 1.4 lakh crore FC grants

Population (2011)15%17.5%

Note

Reading the shift: income distance still highest; population weight up; demographic performance down; GDP contribution newly rewards economic output; debate over relative State shares continues.

Other 16th FC points

  • dropped separate revenue-deficit, sector-specific and State-specific grants
  • local body grants: basic and performance-linked
  • disaster corpus Rs 2,04,401 crore (incl. States' contribution)
  • transparent accounting of off-budget borrowings
  • Union fiscal deficit 3.5% of GDP by 2030-31
  • State limit 3% of GSDP a year

3 · GST Council · NITI Aayog · Special Category Status · Fiscal Issues · Commissions#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Centre State Relations handout pp. 26-31. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • GST (101st Amendment, 1 July 2017) is shared taxation run by the GST Council, where neither side can win alone. NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission as a think tank. Special Category Status has no constitutional basis and has faded since 2015. Fiscal friction: cesses, CSS, borrowing limits, off-budget debt.

GST AND Federalism

Framework

  • 101st Amendment 2016
  • In force 1 July 2017
  • Art 246A GST law-making by both
  • Art 269A IGST on inter-State supplies
  • Art 279A GST Council

GST Council

  • Chair:
    • Union Finance Minister
    • Member: Union MoS for Revenue/Finance
    • Members: State Finance/Taxation Ministers
  • votes:
    • Union one-third, States collectively two-thirds
    • Decisions need three-fourths of weighted votes of members present and voting, so neither side can decide alone

Benefits

  • cooperative decisions
  • common national market
  • harmonised rates
  • IGST sharing framework

Issues

  • loss of State tax autonomy
  • dependence on Council
  • unequal revenue capacity
  • Union influence through its voting weight
Note

Union of India v. Mohit Minerals (2022): GST Council recommendations are not binding on Parliament and State Legislatures; both have legislative power over GST; cooperative federalism by dialogue.

NITI Aayog

Basics

  • Set up 1 January 2015, replacing the Planning Commission
  • Non-constitutional, non-statutory think tank
  • Cooperative and competitive federalism
  • Bottom-up planning
  • Research
  • Aspirational Districts Programme
BasisPlanning CommissionNITI Aayog
Set up19502015
Naturecentral planning bodypolicy think tank
Approachtop-downbottom-up consultation
RoleFive-Year Plans, resource allocationadvice, monitoring, coordination
Fundsinfluenced Plan transfersdoes not allocate Plan funds
Federal approachrelatively centralisedcooperative and competitive

Limits

  • no financial authority
  • advisory
  • limited State influence
  • overlap with ministries and other forums

Special Category Status (SCS)

Basics

  • Created in the planning framework
  • No constitutional or statutory basis
  • criteria: hilly terrain, low density or sizeable tribal population, strategic border location, backwardness, non-viable finances
  • benefits: preferential Normal Central Assistance, 90:10 grant-loan, extra scheme support, tax concessions

After 2015

  • Planning Commission replaced
  • Normal Central Assistance ended
  • 14th FC raised untied devolution
  • SCS lost most meaning
  • different from Arts 371-371J
  • Raghuram Rajan Committee 2013:
    • Composite Development Index
    • States as relatively developed / less developed / least developed
  • demands continue

Way forward

  • predictable devolution and untied grants
  • rationalise cess and surcharge
  • horizontal equity
  • reform CSS
  • coordinate FC, GST Council, NITI, SFCs
  • responsible borrowing
  • bring off-budget debt into reporting
  • strengthen local governments

4 · Inter-state Relations · Water Disputes · Boundaries · Language · Articles 371-371J#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Centre State Relations handout pp. 31-36. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Inter-State ties rest on Arts 131, 261, 262, 263, 301-307 and 3. Water disputes (Cauvery, Krishna, Ravi-Beas, Mahadayi, Mahanadi) and boundary disputes (Maharashtra-Karnataka, Assam-Meghalaya, Assam-Arunachal) need better data, faster tribunals and Zonal Councils. Asymmetry runs through Arts 371-371J and the Sixth Schedule.

Constitutional Framework

Water Disputes

Laws

  • Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956:
    • Tribunals on Union reference after failed talks
    • Published awards bind
  • River Boards Act 1956: boards for inter-State rivers, never effectively operationalised

Cauvery

  • Tribunal award 2007
  • 2018 Supreme Court modified allocation, retaining 740 TMC ft in a normal year
  • implementation bodies: CWMA and CWRC

Problems

  • tribunal delay
  • weak enforcement
  • politicisation
  • poor data sharing
  • fragmented project planning
  • climate stress
Note

Inter-State River Water Disputes (Amendment) Bill 2019 (proposals only): Disputes Resolution Committee, a single permanent tribunal with benches, time limits, data management. Not an operating system yet.

Way forward

  • early mediation
  • time-bound adjudication
  • transparent flow data
  • river-basin management
  • climate adaptation
  • enforcement of awards

  • Art 3:

  • Art 131 for legal-rights disputes

Sixth Schedule

  • Autonomous District and Regional Councils in tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram
  • Local administration, customs, land

Significance

  • protects diversity
  • regional autonomy
  • national integration
  • conflict management

Challenges

  • demands for similar benefits
  • administrative complexity
  • special status does not ensure development
  • identity sensitivity

5 · Challenges TO Federalism · Emerging Issues · Reforms · Commissions · Assessment#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Centre State Relations handout pp. 36-42. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Challenges are political, legislative, administrative, financial and institutional. New ones are delimitation, climate, public health, migration and weak local government. Four commissions (Rajamannar, Sarkaria, NCRWC, Punchhi) shape reform: restraint, consultation, fair finance and strong forums.

FIVE Layers OF Challenge

LayerIssues

Legislativebroad entries (Farm Laws); Art 254 dominance; thin consultation; Art 200-201 delays; Art 253 treaties
AdministrativeArts 256-257; AIS disputes; CBI general consent withdrawn by States; BSF jurisdiction; Union ministries in State subjects
Financialvertical imbalance; cesses; CSS; GST autonomy loss; Art 293 borrowing
Institutionalrare ISC meetings; non-binding advice; weak follow-up; no general pre-legislative consultation; overlapping forums
Memory aid · PLAFI

Five layers: Political · Legislative · Administrative · Financial · Institutional. (own mnemonic)

Emerging Challenges

Delimitation

  • Arts 81, 82, 170
  • 42nd Amendment 1976: froze seat allocation on 1971 Census
  • 84th Amendment 2001: freeze until first Census after 2026
  • 87th Amendment 2003: boundaries readjusted on 2001 Census, no change in State allocation
  • issues: population vs demographic performance, regional balance, bargaining power, equal population per seat, link to women's reservation (106th Amendment)
Note

131st Amendment Bill 2026: introduced 16 April 2026; defeated in Lok Sabha on 17 April 2026. Proposed 550 to 850 seats (815 States, 35 UTs), Census by legislation, easier women's quota. (current affairs; verify)

Climate and environmental federalism

  • Shared pollution, disasters, unequal vulnerability, thin State resources
  • Example Delhi-NCR air
  • Fix: coordination, river-basin and airshed planning, funding, flexibility

Public health federalism

  • Health is a State subject
  • COVID-19 showed need for coordination
  • Unequal capacity
  • Uniform guidelines
  • Fix: cooperative planning, surveillance, funding

Migration

  • jobs for locals, linguistic tension, benefit portability, inter-State labour coordination

Local self-government

  • 73rd and 74th Amendments 1992
  • Incomplete devolution of functions, funds, functionaries
  • Financial dependence
  • Weak State Finance Commissions
  • Parallel agencies. Federalism also needs decentralisation below the State

Reform Agenda

Memory aid · RSNP

Commissions in order: Rajamannar 1969 · Sarkaria 1983 · NCRWC 2002 · Punchhi 2007. (own mnemonic)

Overall Assessment

Strengths

  • national integration
  • regional autonomy
  • accommodation of diversity (reorganisation, asymmetry)
  • judicial protection (Basic Structure)
  • cooperative institutions (FC, GST Council, ISC, NITI)

Weaknesses

  • political centralisation
  • fiscal dependence
  • weak consultation
  • water/boundary/resource disputes
  • incomplete decentralisation
Way forward / key point

The aim is not to weaken the Union or give States unrestricted powers, but a balance through restraint, cooperative institutions, fiscal fairness, political neutrality, decentralisation and mutual trust.
