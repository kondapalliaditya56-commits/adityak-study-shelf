3.1 · US-China relations: the Washington summit#

Prelims + MainsInternational Relations

The Chinese president made a state visit to Washington; both sides agreed to build a "constructive relationship of strategic stability" based on respect, fairness and reciprocity.

  • Timeline: early 2025 tariffs raised sharply; 2025 Geneva talks cut tariffs for a trial period and China showed leverage with rare-earth export controls; Busan meeting suspended the controls and cut US tariffs; May 2026 Beijing set up Boards of Trade and Investment and the "strategic stability" formula.
  • Outcomes: 30-for-30 (favourable tariff treatment for $30 billion of non-sensitive goods each way); Busan truce extended to January 2027; Boards of Trade and Investment operational; working group on farm market access; dialogue on AI risks and an AI-incident channel; China export controls on two fentanyl precursors; militaries to conclude a crisis-communication memorandum (not yet signed); support for each other's G20 and APEC hosting; Iran must never have a nuclear weapon; opposed tolls on international waterways.
  • Unresolved: Taiwan (China asks the US to "oppose" independence, the US "does not support" it; about $14 billion arms package on hold); short truce; chip export controls; trade imbalances and overcapacity; rare-earth supply with no agreed figures.
  • Why rivalry persists: power and order, Taiwan and South China Sea, subsidies and trade, AI and chips, critical minerals (China refines about 91% of magnet rare earths, IEA), BRI vs AIIB-type institutions. Why they cooperate: interdependence, escalation risk, costs of tariffs, selective easing (January 2026 licences for some AI chips), mutual leverage ("mutually assured disruption").
  • India: rivalry raises India's value as a partner and China+1 destination; a US-China bargain could lower it and cheaper Chinese goods may hit Indian exporters; critical-mineral exposure; pressure on strategic autonomy.
  • Way forward: raise own value; convert China+1 into investment (logistics, value addition, electronics, semiconductors); diversify trade and inputs (National Critical Mineral Mission); build technology capacity (India Semiconductor Mission); strengthen maritime partnerships (Quad, ASEAN).
Prelims fodder
  • APEC: established 1989, 21 member economies, Secretariat in Singapore; India is not a member.
  • Three Joint Communiques: 1972 (Shanghai), 1979 (diplomatic relations), 1982 (arms sales to Taiwan). Taiwan Relations Act (1979).
  • Four parts of strategic stability (Wang Yi): positive, healthy, constant, lasting.
  • De-risking vs decoupling. Weaponised interdependence (Farrell and Newman, 2019). Thucydides Trap (Graham Allison). G2.
Mains fodder
  • Conclusion: Washington marks stabilisation, not settlement; India must stay valuable either way through resilience, capability and strategic autonomy.
  • Keywords: Strategic stability; Managed rivalry; China+1; Critical minerals; Strategic autonomy

3.2 · 18th BRICS Summit: New Delhi Declaration#

Prelims + MainsInternational Relations

India hosted the 18th BRICS Summit at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, in September 2026; the New Delhi Declaration was adopted by consensus. 20 years of BRICS; India's fourth chairship (2012, 2016, 2021, 2026). Theme: Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability.

  • Political: bigger role for developing countries in the UNSC (China and Russia restated support for India's and Brazil's aspirations; Africa's position under the Ezulwini Consensus); condemned the April 2025 Pahalgam attack, zero tolerance, early adoption of CCIT; West Asia "deep concern", maximum restraint, two-state solution on 1967 borders and full UN membership for Palestine.
  • Economic: opposed unilateral tariffs and non-tariff barriers including CBAM; restore WTO dispute settlement; IMF quota reform under the Diriyah Guiding Principles; BRICS Payment Task Force on interoperability and local-currency settlement (no common currency); India-led BRICS Risk Lab at GIFT City; Jaipur Consensus on invoice discounting for MSMEs; BRICS Grain Exchange discussions; BRICS AGRIN set up.
  • Technology and health: BRICS Digital Public Infrastructure Repository (India's proposal); Leaders' Statement on Global Governance of AI; BRICS Mission for Healthy Lifestyle (2026-29) with NIMHANS Bengaluru as Centre of Excellence; early warning sub-working group for infectious diseases. China hosts the 19th summit in 2027.
  • Limits: the declaration does not name the US on tariffs, does not name countries in the West Asia conflict, and omits Ukraine.
  • India's gains: multi-alignment and strategic autonomy; bridging role (foreign ministers had failed to agree in May but leaders reached consensus though Iran and UAE are on opposite sides); China's president in India for the first time since 2019; PM's 3R agenda (Representation, Responsiveness, Rule-making) and "pyramid of privilege to platform of partnership".
  • Sidelines: India-China talked of a "fair, reasonable and mutually acceptable" boundary resolution and India raised the trade deficit (about USD 112 billion in FY26; trade about USD 151 billion); India-Russia reaffirmed USD 100 billion trade by 2030; Iran-UAE held their highest-level meeting since the 2026 war began.
  • Limits of BRICS: Iran-UAE and Egypt-Ethiopia (GERD) divisions; vague consensus language (Rio 2025 condemned strikes on Iran, New Delhi 2026 only "deep concern"); China's outsized weight; payment linkage needs compatible systems; no permanent secretariat so implementation gap.
  • Way forward: consolidate before expanding; Troika-based continuity and a digital decision tracker; payment interoperability over a common currency; insulate food, health, digital, climate cooperation; "non-Western, not anti-Western"; IBSA coordination.
Prelims fodder
  • Evolution: BRIC coined 2001 (Jim O'Neill); first foreign ministers' meeting 2006; first summit 2009 Yekaterinburg; South Africa joined 2011; 2024 Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE joined and the partner category was created at Kazan; 2025 Indonesia joined. India's 2026 roster counts Saudi Arabia among 11 members, though Saudi Arabia calls itself an "invited country" without formal ratification.
  • Partner countries (10): Belarus, Bolivia, Cuba, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Nigeria, Thailand, Uganda, Uzbekistan, Vietnam.
  • NDB: agreed at Fortaleza (2014), HQ Shanghai, President Dilma Rousseff. CRA: USD 100 billion liquidity arrangement. Ezulwini Consensus (2005): two permanent seats with veto and five non-permanent seats for Africa. CCIT: proposed by India in 1996, still not adopted. Members hold nearly half of world population and about 40% of global GDP (PPP).
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Multi-alignment; Reformed multilateralism; Global South; Payment interoperability; Implementation gap

3.3 · 26th SCO Summit: Bishkek Declaration#

Prelims + MainsInternational Relations

26th SCO Heads of State Council met in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan; SCO's 25th anniversary; Bishkek Declaration adopted. Theme: "25 Years of the SCO: Together for Sustainable Peace, Development and Prosperity". Pakistan took the 2026-27 chair and hosts the 2027 summit.

  • Members (10): Belarus, China, India, Iran, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Pakistan, Russia, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan.
  • Security: terrorism condemned "in all its forms", double standards rejected, CCIT supported; independent, neutral, peaceful Afghanistan with inclusive government; strikes on Iran condemned; just settlement of Palestine question; anti-drug programmes to 2030.
  • Institutions: Protocol amending the Charter makes English an official language; regulations for the Universal Centre for Countering Security Challenges and Threats and the Executive Committee of the SCO Anti-Drug Centre.
  • Economy: Economic Development Strategy and Energy Cooperation Strategy to 2030; transport corridors, smart customs; SCO Development Bank progressing among interested members; road map to raise national-currency share in settlements; IMF/World Bank reform; opposed unilateral coercive measures.
  • Environment and tech: Memorandum on combating climate change; Initiative on Biodiversity Conservation with Joint Action Plan 2026-31; AI memorandum; emergency assistance statement. People-to-people: Civilisational Dialogue Forum in India (autumn 2026); Lahore SCO Tourism and Cultural Capital 2026-27.
  • India at Bishkek: SCO vision of Security, Connectivity, Opportunity; "dismantle the entire ecosystem of terrorist financing, recruitment, radicalisation and safe havens"; "terrorism can never be a strategic asset for anyone"; connectivity must respect sovereignty and territorial integrity (CPEC through PoJK); met Russia, Iran, Kyrgyzstan, Armenia; informal interaction with China's President.
  • Importance for India: multi-alignment and strategic autonomy; Connect Central Asia; counter-terror cooperation via RATS; Afghanistan stability; Chabahar and INSTC; shaping agenda (English, Civilisational Dialogue Forum, Start-up and Young Scientists forums).
  • Limits: India-Pakistan and India-China differences; declaration rejects double standards but names no attack; BRI disagreement; China's weight; Pakistan's chairship; consensus slows decisions, implementation gap.
  • Way forward: use RATS effectively; operationalise the new security bodies; turn connectivity into trade (Chabahar-INSTC); use English and Indian initiatives to widen role; keep working-level engagement with Pakistan chair; track delivery through the Secretariat.
Prelims fodder
  • Shanghai Five (1996) became SCO (2001); Charter signed 2002. India and Pakistan joined 2017, Iran 2023, Belarus 2024.
  • Secretariat in Beijing; RATS permanent body in Tashkent against the "three evils": terrorism, separatism, extremism.
  • Bishkek attendees: Mongolia (observer); dialogue partners Armenia, Azerbaijan, Egypt, Laos, Türkiye.
  • Shanghai Spirit: mutual trust, mutual benefit, equality, consultation, respect for cultural diversity, common development. Green Technology Corridor programme 2026-30.
Mains fodder
  • Quote (PM, Bishkek 2026): "We must transform our shared geography into shared opportunities."
  • Keywords: multi-alignment, Eurasian engagement, regional integration, consensus-building, implementation gap.

3.4 · India and Central Asia#

Prelims + MainsInternational Relations

The PM visited Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan and attended the 26th SCO Summit.

  • Uzbekistan: ties upgraded from Strategic Partnership to Comprehensive Strategic Partnership; trade target US$5 billion by 2030; cooperation to conserve Buddhist sites Fayaz Tepa and Kara Tepa in Termez; US$1 million grant for afforestation in the Aral Sea region. Kyrgyzstan: Strategic Partnership reaffirmed.
  • Trade: merchandise trade US$1.72 billion (2023). Uzbekistan about 45% of India's exports to the region; Kazakhstan supplied 64% of imports. India exports pharma, electrical equipment, machinery; imports mineral fuels, fertilisers, inorganic chemicals, ores. New BITs signed with Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan.
  • Significance: energy (Central Asia holds over 35 billion barrels proven oil; TAPI to tap Galkynysh field); uranium (Kazakhstan world's largest producer); crossroads location; pharma market (Uzbekistan imports about 30% of drugs from India); RATS in Tashkent; all five support India's permanent UNSC seat.
  • Challenges: no direct land access (Pakistan, Afghanistan); China dominance and BRI; Chabahar/Iran route disruption and US sanctions; TAPI incomplete; second India-Central Asia Summit not held; withdrawal from Tajikistan's Ayni airbase.
  • Way forward: accelerate INSTC and Ashgabat Agreement; critical minerals via KABIL (as in Australia, Argentina, Chile); share DPI (UPI, Aadhaar); convene the overdue second summit.
Prelims fodder
  • All five states are landlocked; Uzbekistan is doubly landlocked (only other: Liechtenstein).
  • Ashgabat Agreement: multimodal transport and transit pact linking Central Asia with the Persian Gulf. INSTC: initiated by India, Russia, Iran in 2000.
  • Mechanisms: India-Central Asia Business Council, Digital Partnership Forum, Rare Earth Forum. Exercises: KAZIND (Kazakhstan), DUSTLIK (Uzbekistan).
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Connect Central Asia policy, Chabahar, INSTC, TAPI, counter-terror cooperation.

3.5 · India-Mercosur: expanding the PTA#

Prelims + MainsInternational Relations

In September 2026 India and Mercosur announced negotiations to expand their Preferential Trade Agreement.

  • Mercosur: Southern Common Market, Treaty of Asunción 1991; Common External Tariff; members Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay, Bolivia; Venezuela suspended.
  • Evolution: 2004 PTA signed, 2009 implemented, 2026 expansion talks. Trade grew from about $10.6 billion (2020) to $20.84 billion (2025). Indian exports: refined petroleum, chemicals, pharma, engineering, vehicles, textiles; imports: crude oil, soybean and sunflower oils, gold, sugar. Brazil is the main partner.
  • Existing PTA covers limited goods: India gives preferences on 450 tariff lines, Mercosur on 452.
  • Opportunities: export diversification (medicines, chemicals, machinery); critical minerals (KABIL 2024 deal for five lithium blocks in Argentina); biofuels (Global Biofuels Alliance founders India and Brazil); food security; pharma investment; South-South cooperation, DPI sharing.
  • Challenges: narrow product list; non-tariff barriers (drug approvals, plant health); logistics costs; competing domestic sensitivities; supply shocks (Argentina 2023 drought).
  • Way forward: expand market access with safeguards; ease trade (customs, approvals; 2026 India-Brazil medicines agreement); shipping links, EXIM Bank São Paulo office; production partnerships; use the PTA Joint Administrative Committee.
Prelims fodder
  • Mercosur Secretariat: Montevideo; Protocol of Ouro Preto (1994) set institutions. Associate members: Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Guyana, Suriname.
  • IBSA Dialogue Forum 2003 (Brasilia Declaration): India, Brazil, South Africa.
  • SPS Agreement (food safety, animal and plant health) and TBT Agreement (technical regulations) are the WTO frameworks for non-tariff measures.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: open regionalism, South-South cooperation, trade complementarity, supply-chain resilience, strategic diversification.

3.6.1 · UNGA "Correct the Map" resolution#

PrelimsInternational Relations

UNGA adopted the "Correct the Map" resolution promoting maps that show continents' relative sizes accurately. Led by Togo for African countries with the African Union.

  • Vote: 164 in favour, US against, six abstentions. Non-binding; encourages equal-area projections such as Equal Earth. India voted in favour.
  • Why: Mercator enlarges regions near poles; Africa (about 30.3 million sq km) looks similar to Greenland (2.16 million sq km). Colonial-era mapping conventions shape perceptions.
  • Mercator: conformal cylindrical, 1569, for navigation, preserves angles and shapes. Equal Earth: equal-area pseudocylindrical, introduced 2018, preserves relative areas.
Prelims fodder
  • Every flat map distorts area, shape, direction or distance.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Map projection; Equal Earth; Mercator

3.6.2 · US-Denmark-Greenland defence agreement#

Prelims + MainsInternational Relations

The US, Denmark and Greenland signed an agreement strengthening Greenland and Arctic defence; Greenland is an autonomous territory of Denmark.

  • Affirms Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to self-determination; amends the 1951 US-Denmark Defense Agreement (under NATO).
  • Provisions: modernise and expand Pituffik Space Base; two new Defence Areas at Narsarsuaq (south) and Mestersvig (east); extensive US access and movement rights including territorial waters; non-NATO states barred from military installations or persistent presence.
  • Importance: location between North America and Europe; ice sheet second largest after Antarctica (full melt about 7 m sea-level rise); Northwest Passage and Transpolar Sea Route opening; 25 of the 34 minerals on the EU critical raw materials list.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Arctic; Pituffik; Critical raw materials

3.6.3 · Indus Waters Treaty: Court of Arbitration ruling#

Prelims + MainsInternational Relations

The Court of Arbitration (secretariat: Permanent Court of Arbitration) held that the IWT remains fully in force despite India's abeyance decision, and imposed temporary limits on specified construction at the Ratle Hydroelectric Project. India rejected the award as illegally constituted and without jurisdiction over its sovereign decisions.

  • IWT 1960: India and Pakistan, World Bank facilitated and signatory. Eastern rivers Ravi, Beas, Sutlej to India; Western rivers Indus, Jhelum, Chenab mainly to Pakistan, India keeps irrigation and hydropower uses.
  • Permanent Indus Commission (one commissioner each). Dispute ladder: Commission, then Neutral Expert, then Court of Arbitration. Modification or termination only by a duly ratified treaty.
  • After the 2025 Pahalgam attack India held the treaty in abeyance.
  • CoA view: treaty cannot be unilaterally suspended or ended; Court has jurisdiction; Ratle limits until Neutral Expert decides. India: abeyance continues; Court constituted contrary to the treaty; rulings have no legal effect.
Mains fodder
  • Link with water diplomacy and sovereignty versus treaty obligation arguments.
  • Keywords: Abeyance; Court of Arbitration; Ratle

3.7 · IR quick updates#

PrelimsInternational Relations

Short items from the September 2026 current affairs.

  • Sohar Port (Oman): deep-sea port on the Gulf of Oman outside the Strait of Hormuz, with Sohar Freezone; Oman invited Indian firms to invest as Hormuz disruptions hit Gulf trade.
  • 1991 India-Pakistan Agreement on Advance Notice on Military Exercises, Manoeuvres and Troop Movements: a CBM; Article 10 says naval ships and submarines must not come within 3 nautical miles in international waters. India protested after an Indian Navy-Pakistani vessel collision in the Arabian Sea.
  • Enemy state clauses (UN Charter Art. 53 and 107): Japan at the 81st UNGA asked for their removal. Amendment needs two-thirds of UNGA and ratification by two-thirds of members including all five P5.
  • Partners for Multilateralism (P4M): launched at its first summit; India co-sponsored with EU, Australia, Barbados, Brazil, Canada, Kenya; defends UN Charter, reform of institutions.
  • L.69 Group: 40+ developing countries seeking comprehensive UNSC reform; named after draft resolution A/61/L.69 (2007, 61st UNGA). EAM co-chaired the consultation with Saint Lucia.
  • FIPIC: India with 14 Pacific Island Countries since 2014; summits 2014 Suva, 2015 Jaipur, 2023 Port Moresby; 12-Point Action Plan (2023); second Foreign Ministers' meeting held in New York.
  • Visegrad Group (V4): informal Central European platform of four countries; first India-V4 foreign ministers' meeting at the 81st UNGA; backed India's permanent UNSC seat.
  • First International Space Summit, Paris: 90 countries and about 1,100 space companies; declarations on debris, orbital safety; EU IRIS² reaffirmed; ISRO-ESA cooperation extended to 2032.

3.8 · High-level visits: Belgium and Luxembourg#

PrelimsInternational Relations

Belgian PM's first India visit: aim to double bilateral trade in five years, India-Belgium Investment Fast-Track Mechanism, CBI-Belgian Federal Police MoU on organised crime and cybercrime. Luxembourg's Deputy PM also visited.

  • Belgium (Brussels): hosts European Commission, European Council, Council of the EU and NATO HQ; euthanasia legal since 2002, extended to minors of any age in 2014; early adopter of compulsory voting.

3.9 · Conflict areas in news#

PrelimsInternational Relations

West Asia and East Asia flashpoints in September 2026.

  • East Sea (Sea of Japan): North Korea fired a projectile; semi-enclosed western Pacific sea; warm Tsushima Current meets cold Liman and North Korea currents giving fishing grounds; names: Japan "Sea of Japan", South Korea "East Sea", North Korea "East Sea of Korea".
  • Perim Island (in the Bab el-Mandeb strait) and Greater and Lesser Hanish Islands (Red Sea): captured by the Houthis; drone attacks forced a temporary shutdown of Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline to Yanbu.
  • Ahmad al-Jaber (Kuwait) and Al Minhad (UAE) air bases: targeted by Iran; used by national and allied forces including the US.
