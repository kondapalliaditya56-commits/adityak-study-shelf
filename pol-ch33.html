In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The Finance Commission (FC) is a quasi-judicial constitutional body under Article 280. The President sets it up every fifth year or earlier.
  • It has a Chairman plus four members, who may be reappointed. Parliament sets their qualifications. The Chairman has public affairs experience
  • Members are a High Court judge, or experts in government finance, financial administration, or economics
  • It recommends tax sharing between the Union and States (and among States), principles of grants-in-aid, measures to boost State funds for Panchayats and Municipalities, and any matter the President refers.
  • Its advice is not binding on the Union Government, yet it is authoritative
  • The FC is the balancing wheel of fiscal federalism. NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015
  • The 15th FC (chair N.K. Singh) fixed the States' share at 41% of central taxes and covered 2021-26. The 16th FC is chaired by Arvind Panagariya.

Key terms

Article 280
Creates the Finance Commission; constituted by the President every fifth year
Vertical devolution
Share of central taxes going to the States as a whole (41% under the 15th FC)
Horizontal distribution
How the States' share is split among individual States
Grants-in-aid
Money from the Consolidated Fund of India to States; FC recommends the principles
Quasi-judicial
Body that decides by fair procedure and may set its own procedure, like a court
Balancing wheel
The FC's role in keeping Centre-State finances in balance
Revenue deficit grants
15th FC gave them to 17 States at first (11 under the 14th FC), reduced to 6 by 2025-26
SDRF
State Disaster Response Fund; corpus of Rs 1.6 lakh crore, with Centre's share Rs 1.2 lakh crore
Remember it as

Art. 280, every 5th year, Chairman + 4 members, advice not binding; 15th FC = 41%, chair N.K. Singh.

1 · Overview and Duties#

  • Article 280 establishes the Finance Commission as a quasi-judicial constitutional body. The President constitutes it every fifth year, or sooner if he thinks it necessary. It is the constitutional mechanism to examine and advise on fiscal matters between the Union and the States.

Duties: recommendations to the President

Four duties

No.Recommendation
1Distribution of net tax proceeds between the Union and the States, and allocation among individual States
2Principles for grants-in-aid to States from the Consolidated Fund of India
3Measures to augment State Consolidated Funds so as to support Panchayats and Municipalities, based on State Finance Commission recommendations
4Any other fiscal matter referred by the President in the interest of sound finance
  • The Commission can determine its own procedure and exercise powers granted by Parliament.
  • Memory aid (Four duties): Tax proceeds sharing, Grants-in-aid principles, Measures for State funds (local bodies), Other matters from the President: "Ten Good Men Operate".

2 · Composition#

  • The Commission is a Chairman plus four other members, all appointed by presidential order under Article 280. Members serve for the period in the order and may be reappointed.
  • Parliament lays down the qualifications and selection procedure
  • Statutory rules specify the background
  • The Chairman is normally a person with experience in public affairs.

Profiles for the four other members (one or more)

No.Profile
1A Judge of a High Court, or someone qualified for such appointment
2A person with specialised knowledge of government finance and accounts
3A person with substantial experience in financial administration
4An expert with special knowledge of economics
  • Memory aid (Four member profiles): Judge of High Court, Accounts expertise, Financial administration, Economics: "Just Ask Finance Experts".
  • Why this mix: The rules combine constitutional legitimacy with the technical expertise needed for balanced recommendations on fiscal federalism.

Also in the handout

  • The four other members are drawn from expert backgrounds and are selected from among persons who satisfy one or more of the following profiles: 1.

4 · Advisory Role and the Planning Commission#

  • Although a constitutional body under Article 280, its recommendations carry no binding legal force. The Union government keeps the final discretion on whether and how to implement the transfers. The outputs guide fiscal relations but create no enforceable rights for beneficiary States.

Binding or authoritative?

  • Not binding:
    • Union keeps final discretion
    • No enforceable rights for States
  • Authoritative:
    • Quasi-judicial character and expected neutrality
    • Dr. P. V. Rajamannar, Chairman of the Fourth FC, said the Union should not reject recommendations except for very compelling reasons

Balancing wheel

  • The Finance Commission is the "balancing wheel" of India's fiscal federal structure, ensuring equitable distribution of shared revenues.
  • For many decades up to 2014 its influence was diluted: the Planning Commission, a non-constitutional and non-statutory entity, overlapped with it in Centre-State fiscal transfers (as noted by Rajamannar).
  • This overlap drew criticism because a body with no constitutional sanction overshadowed the Finance Commission's constitutionally guaranteed role.
  • In 2015 the Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India), meant to restore clarity of roles and reinforce the Finance Commission in cooperative federalism.

Overlap and repair

  • Finance Commission: constitutional role under Art. 280
  • Planning Commission overlaps:
    • Non-constitutional, non-statutory
    • Diluted the FC up to 2014
  • NITI Aayog replaces it in 2015: to restore clarity and strengthen cooperative federalism

5 · The 15th Finance Commission#

  • It was constituted for the award period 2021-2026, but because of delays and contentious Terms of Reference (ToR) it first submitted an interim report for 2020-21.

Why the ToR was contested

  • Shift of the population base year from the 1971 Census to the 2011 Census. Southern and progressive states opposed it, saying it penalises successful population control.
  • It was asked to review continuation of revenue deficit grants and to assess the Centre's fiscal situation after higher tax devolution earlier.
  • It was asked to integrate performance-based conditionalities: tax effort, demographic control, power sector reforms, ease of doing business, Swachh Bharat outcomes.
  • These conditions raised fears of central overreach and micromanagement of state governance.
  • It finally gave a six-year award period, unlike the usual five, for stability and to address the fiscal shock of COVID-19.
Handout alert

Handout alert: The handout says the award period is 2021-2026 and also a six-year period. Counting the interim report year 2020-21 gives six years. Use: interim 2020-21, main award 2021-2026, six years in all.

From Lakshmikanth · Indian Polity, 8th ed. Ch 46, Finance Commission
  • Article 281 (in the LK table) deals with the recommendations of the Finance Commission
  • The report goes to the President, who lays it before both Houses of Parliament with an explanatory memorandum on action taken
  • Parliament specified member qualifications through the Finance Commission Act, 1951.
  • The third function (supplementing State funds for Panchayats and Municipalities) was added by the 73rd and 74th Amendments of 1992.
  • Till 1960 the FC also suggested grants to Assam, Bihar, Odisha and West Bengal in place of a share of export duty on jute
  • These grants were temporary, for ten years from the Constitution's start
  • Fifteen commissions had been formed as per LK. Chairmen in order: Neogy (1st), Santhanam, Chanda, Rajamannar (4th), Mahavir Tyagi, Brahmananda Reddy, Shelat, Chavan, Salve (9th), Pant (10th), then the 11th to 15th.
  • Appointed and report years: 1st (1951, 1952), 4th (1964, 1965), 10th (1992, 1994), 12th (2002, 2004), 14th (2013, 2014), 15th (2017; reports 2019 and 2020). The 14th covered 2015-20 and the 15th 2020-26.
  • The 15th FC gave two reports: the first on 5 December 2019 for 2020-21, and the second on 9 November 2020 for 2021-22 to 2025-26.
  • D.D. Basu is quoted: nowhere does the Constitution say the FC's advice binds the Government of India or creates a legal right for States.
  • Lakshmikanth differs:

Traps#

Trap 1 · Advisory but weighty The Union Government need not accept the FC's recommendations, and States gain no legal right to the money. Yet Dr. Rajamannar said they should not be rejected without very compelling reasons. Rule: not binding, but authoritative.
Trap 2 · FC versus Planning Commission Until 2014 the non-constitutional Planning Commission overlapped with the FC in Centre-State transfers. It was replaced by NITI Aayog in 2015. Rule: FC = constitutional; Planning Commission = neither constitutional nor statutory.
Trap 3 · 1971 versus 2011 Census The 15th FC's Terms of Reference moved the population base from the 1971 Census to the 2011 Census; southern States objected that this penalised population control. Rule: ToR change = 1971 to 2011; the objection came from States with good population control.
Trap 4 · Five years or six FCs are normally for five years, but the 15th FC gave a six-year award (2020-21 interim, then 2021-26) because of the COVID shock. Rule: 15th FC is the six-year exception.
