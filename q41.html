1995PYQ 1(Prelims 1995)

In 1930, Mahatma Gandhi started Civil Disobedience Movement from

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — The Dandi March began in March 1930 — MG marched from the Sabarmati Ashram (set up in 1917) to the Dandi coast to break the salt law. The starting point of CDM was the Sabarmati Ashram.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The march set out from Sabarmati. Sevagram plays no role in the CDM launch.

(B) ✗ — Dandi was the destination, not the start: MG marched from the Sabarmati Ashram to the Dandi coast, where the salt law was broken. The stem asks where the movement started from.

(D) ✗ — Wardha does not figure in the CDM launch route on record — the march ran from Sabarmati to Dandi. (Note: Mahatma Gandhi's Dandi March was significant in terms of breaking the salt law and beginning of Civil Disobedience movement in 1930.)

1996PYQ 2(Prelims 1996)

The meeting of Indian and British political leaders during 1930-32 in London has often been referred to as the first, second and third round table conferences. It would be incorrect to refer to them as such because

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — The London meetings were, in effect, one continuing exercise. INC rejected the RTC (three RTCs were held — in 1930, 1931, and 1932). It was one conference held in three sessions — not three separate conferences — which is why the 'first/second/third' naming is strictly incorrect.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — A true fact, but the wrong reason: INC did skip the 1st RTC and the 3rd RTC — yet who attended does not change whether the meetings were one conference or three. The naming flaw lies in the session structure, option (d).

(B) ✗ — Also broadly true — the non-INC delegates spoke for sectional interests (the minorities at the 2nd RTC each pressed their own electorate demands) — but representativeness is not what makes the 'three conferences' label incorrect; the session structure is.

(C) ✗ — No Labour withdrawal is recorded — Labour PM Ramsay MacDonald stayed engaged throughout, even announcing the Communal Award in August 1932. (Note: It was a conference held in three sessions and not of three separate conferences. It was based only on Simon Commission Report and further course of action.)

1997PYQ 3(Prelims 1997)

The Poona Pact which was signed between the British Government and Mahatma Gandhi in 1934 provided for

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — Separate electorates were removed, but as compensation, the number of reserved seats was more than doubled. The Depressed Classes would contest in joint electorates with reserved seats — exactly what this option states.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The Poona Pact dealt with how the Depressed Classes would be represented; dominion status was a constitutional-goal question fought over the Nehru Report and at the RTCs, not a Pact provision.

(B) ✗ — Muslims already had separate electorates — Other minorities had already had separate electorates since the GOI Act 1919; the Pact was about the Depressed Classes and did not touch Muslim electorates.

(C) ✗ — The trap option: separate electorates for the Depressed Classes were the Communal Award's method, which the Pact rejected — replacing it with joint electorates plus enlarged reservation (71 to 147 seats). Picking (c) reverses what the Pact did. (Note: Poona Pact was signed between Dr B.R. Ambedkar and Mahatma Gandhi in 1934 giving separate electorates to Dalit for electing members of State Legislative Assemblies in British India.)

1997PYQ 4(Prelims 1997)

Withdrawl of a movement C. The Chauri-Chaura incident 3. Communal electorates D. The Dandi March 4. Communal outbreaks 5. Illegal manufacture of salt

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — A-3 (Morley-Minto Reforms → Communal electorates), B-1 (Simon Commission → Country-wide agitation), C-2 (Chauri-Chaura → Withdrawal of NCM), D-5 (Dandi March → Illegal manufacture of salt). The match codes give (d) A-3 B-1 C-2 D-5.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Code A-3 B-4 C-5 D-2 fails at the second pair: the Simon Commission produced country-wide agitation (List II-1), not communal outbreaks — its all-British composition triggered nationwide protest.

(B) ✗ — Code A-4 B-1 C-2 D-3 fails at both ends: Morley-Minto's result was communal electorates (List II-3), not communal outbreaks; and the Dandi March's result was illegal manufacture of salt (List II-5), not communal electorates.

(C) ✗ — Code A-2 B-3 C-1 D-5 fails immediately: the Morley-Minto Reforms did not cause withdrawal of a movement — their result was communal electorates (List II-3); withdrawal of a movement belongs to Chauri-Chaura. (Note: Morley Minto Reforms of 1909 introduced first time separate electorates for Muslim category known as communal electorate. Simon Commission group appointed in November 1927 by the British conservative government.)

2005PYQ 10(Prelims 2005)

(D) ✗ — Overcounts by including statement 2: the record account of the Poona Pact lists seat enlargement and joint electorates, not provisions for local bodies or civil services. (Note: First Round Table Conference was held on 12th November, 1930 to 19th January, 1931. First Round Table Conference was presided by the head of Labour party Ramsay MacDonald.)

FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words The Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-34) differed structurally from the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22) in its goal, method & social base. Examine.

Demand of the question: "Examine" + "differed structurally" demands a three-axis comparison (goal/method/social base). Body must show what made CDM a distinct mobilisation, not a repeat.

Suggested intro (~25 words): NCM (1920-22) aimed at "Swaraj within the Empire" through withdrawal of cooperation. CDM (1930-34) aimed at Purna Swaraj through active defiance of unjust laws — a different conception of struggle entirely. Body guidance — 4 themes:

1. The goal-shift (see 'The INC Lahore Session, December 1929'). NCM accepted Dominion Status as adequate; CDM was anchored to Purna Swaraj after the 1929 Lahore Resolution. The goal moved from "more autonomy within Empire" to "complete independence from Empire".

2. The method-shift (see 'Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM), 1930–34'). NCM was withdrawal-based — boycott institutions, refuse cooperation. CDM was defiance-based — actively break unjust laws (salt, forest, revenue, customs). The Salt March symbolised this shift.

3. The social base-shift (see 'NWFP: The Khudai Khidmatgars and the Qissa Khwani Bazaar Massacre' and 'Implementation: 2nd Stage (May 1930 – March 1931)'). NCM was middle-class led with peasant participation. CDM mobilised broader rural & gender bases — Khudai Khidmatgars in NWFP, peasant Salt-breaking from Tanjore coast to Vedaranyam, large-scale women's participation (Sarojini Naidu at Dharasana, Kasturba Gandhi, Kamla Devi).

4. The structural conclusion — sustained 4 years (see 'CDM 2nd Phase, September 1932–1934'). NCM lasted 2 years & withdrew after Chauri Chaura. CDM sustained 4 years (1930-34) through two phases, surviving Bhagat Singh's hanging & Gandhi's arrests. CDM's longer endurance built deeper INC institutional infrastructure.

Closing verdict: CDM was not NCM-2.0. The Purna Swaraj goal, the defiance method & the broader social base made it the first truly nationwide mass movement aimed at complete independence — a precursor to Quit India 1942.

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words The Gandhi-Irwin Pact (March 1931) was the first formal political compromise between the INC & the British Crown — but it was attacked from within the INC. Examine why.

Demand of the question: "Examine why attacked from within" demands tracing the internal INC objections, not the pact's content.

Suggested intro (~25 words): The Gandhi-Irwin Pact ended CDM's 1st phase. Within the INC, it was seen as a strategic surrender — particularly by the Independence-for-India-League wing. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Gandhi-Irwin Pact 1931 (Delhi Pact, 5 March 1931)'):

1. The substance gap (see 'Gandhi-Irwin Pact 1931 (Delhi Pact, 5 March 1931)'). The Pact released CDM prisoners but excluded those convicted of violence — meaning Bhagat Singh & Saunders-murder convicts. This was unacceptable to large parts of the Indian public. Gandhi's failure to extract a commutation of Bhagat Singh's death sentence was held against him.

2. The Bhagat Singh execution & Karachi (see 'Karachi Special Session of INC, March 1931'). Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev & Rajguru were hanged 23 March 1931, just 18 days after the Pact. The INC Karachi Session (held just after) passed a resolution condemning the execution — implicitly criticising the Pact's failure to prevent it. Patel's presidency could not fully contain this.

3. The deeper critique (see 'Gandhi-Irwin Pact 1931 (Delhi Pact, 5 March 1931)'). The Pact framed the INC as the British Government's bargaining counterpart — acknowledging the INC's representative status. Critics within (Nehru, Bose) feared this legitimised the British framework rather than dismantling it. The 2nd RTC of September 1931 — to which the Pact obligated Gandhi — produced no progress, confirming the critique.

Closing verdict: The Pact was both a tactical win (release of CDM prisoners, salt-tax relief, INC recognition) & a strategic compromise (no movement on Purna Swaraj, Bhagat Singh's execution). Its mixed legacy made it the most-debated Gandhian negotiation of the 1930s.

Revise: 'Gandhi-Irwin Pact' + 'Karachi Session'.
