1 · Constitutional Morality#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Polity handout pp. 12-13 · current affairs: verify dates. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Constitutional morality is deep commitment to constitutional values even when popular sentiment opposes them. Know Ambedkar's idea, how it differs from public morality, the Navtej and Sabarimala judgments, and the debate on whether courts should use it.

Meaning AND Ambedkar's IDEA

Value addition

Constitutional morality is a deep commitment to the values of the Constitution: liberty, equality, fraternity, justice and dignity, even when popular sentiment runs against them.

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar's conception

  • meant accepting plural opinions, rejecting coercion, keeping the executive answerable to elected representatives, and ensuring no single person or institution claims absolute authority
  • Not a natural sentiment in India
  • It had to be cultivated
  • a Constitution can be silently subverted not by changing its text but by distorting how it is administered
  • courts have built on this: protecting minority rights, limiting majoritarianism, keeping all branches in check
Public moralityConstitutional morality
rooted in majority beliefs, traditions, customsrooted in constitutional principles (liberty, equality, fraternity, justice)
can be majoritarian, regressive, discriminatoryinclusive, rights-based, progressive
subordinate when in conflictprevails when in conflict

Significance

  • protects individual and minority rights from majority pressure
  • check on the highest functionaries: ministers, judges, citizens
  • keeps the spirit of the Constitution alive beyond its text
  • gives courts a tool to interpret rights expansively
Memory aid · PCKI

Significance: Protects minorities · Checks the high · Keeps the spirit alive · Interprets rights expansively. (own mnemonic)

Judicial Application

Landmark judgments

  • Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018): SC decriminalised consensual same-sex relations between adults (Section 377 IPC)
  • Indian Young Lawyers Association v. State of Kerala (2018): the Sabarimala judgment on women's entry into the temple
  • in both, constitutional morality must prevail over public or social morality when they conflict with constitutional rights and values

THE Debate

Criticism

  • April 2026: SG Tushar Mehta in the Sabarimala review argued it should be discarded as a ground of judicial review: "vague and subjective", absent from the text, letting courts strike down actions without clear basis
  • 2018, K. K. Venugopal (then AG): "very dangerous", could make the SC a third chamber of Parliament

Counter view: Upendra Baxi

  • not a judicial invention: embedded in the Preamble, Part III and Part IVA
  • three ideas:
    • Equality, liberty, fraternity
    • Respect for diversity
    • Constitution in letter and spirit
  • Protects the vulnerable when majority opinion is hostile
  • Vagueness is inherent in all principles and courts use it through reasoned judgments
Note

Recent (verify): a nine-judge Constitution Bench reserved judgment on the Sabarimala reference on 14 May 2026. Handout view: it is a shared responsibility of legislature, executive and judiciary; discarding it would leave the constitutional spirit vulnerable to the majoritarianism it was designed to check.

2 · THE Constitution AS A Living Document · Article 21#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Polity handout pp. 13-15 · 2025 case: verify. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • A living constitution evolves without losing its core identity. Know the seven features that make ours living, five reasons it must be, and the five-stage journey of Article 21 from Gopalan to digital access.

Concept AND Features

Value addition

The Indian Constitution is not a frozen text; it is a living instrument with capabilities of enormous dynamism. A living constitution evolves with time without losing its core identity: an ongoing project responding to social, economic and political realities.

What makes it living

  • Article 368:
    • Amendment power
    • Over a hundred amendments since 1950
  • amendment procedures:
    • Simple majority, special majority, special majority with State ratification: flexible yet stable
    • Simple-majority changes are made outside Article 368
  • Basic Structure Doctrine: Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) keeps amendments within limits
  • Judicial Review: supported by Articles 13, 32, 136 and 226 (32 and 226 writ jurisdiction; 136 special leave to appeal)
  • open-textured language: esp. Articles 14, 19, 21
  • Directive Principles: guideposts to gradually realise socio-economic goals
  • constitutional conventions develop over time
Memory aid · ABJODDC

Seven features: Art 368 · Basic structure · Judicial review · Open texture · DPSP · Different amendment procedures · Conventions. (own mnemonic)

Why it is necessary for India

  • social: gender equality, LGBTQ rights, dismantling caste hierarchies
  • economic: 1991 liberalisation, privatisation, globalisation forced reinterpretation of socialist provisions
  • technological: internet, mobile, data, AI, digital identity raise questions on privacy, speech, surveillance
  • federal evolution: coalition politics, cooperative and competitive federalism
  • global engagement: treaties, climate agreements, human rights covenants
Memory aid · SETFG

Five drivers: Social · Economic · Technological · Federal · Global. (own mnemonic)

Note

Limit: living does not mean it can mean anything anyone wants. It is bound by the values of the Preamble, the limits of the Basic Structure and the discipline of constitutional methods.

Article 21: THE Living Constitution IN Action

  1. 1950
    A. K. Gopalan: narrow reading; restrictive personal liberty; rights as separate compartments; "procedure established by law" without fairness test
  2. 1978
    Maneka Gandhi: progressive view; procedure must be fair, just and reasonable; opened a whole new world of rights
  3. PILs
    unstated rights read into Art 21 (see list below)
  4. 2017
    Puttaswamy: nine-judge bench, unanimous: privacy intrinsic to life and liberty (physical, informational, decisional); overruled ADM Jabalpur (1976)
  5. Now
    dignity in death, partner choice, transgender rights, personality rights, digital access

Rights read into Article 21 (Stage 3)

  • livelihood: Olga Tellis (1985)
  • free legal aid: Hussainara Khatoon (1979)
  • clean environment: M. C. Mehta cases
  • education: led to Article 21A by the 86th Amendment, 2002
  • health: Paschim Banga Khet Mazdoor Samity
  • shelter: Chameli Singh
  • speedy trial and protection against custodial violence
Memory aid · L-FEEHSS

Stage-3 rights: Livelihood (Olga Tellis) · Free legal aid (Hussainara) · Environment (M. C. Mehta) · Education (21A) · Health (Paschim Banga) · Shelter (Chameli Singh) · Speedy trial. (own mnemonic)

Stage 5: continuing expansion

  • Right to die with dignity (passive euthanasia)
  • Right to choose a partner
  • Rights of transgender persons
  • Personality rights (e.g. Jackie Shroff case)
  • right to digital access: Pragya Prasun v. Union of India (2025): an intrinsic component of the right to life and liberty under Article 21 (verify)

Ambedkar's Insight

Way forward / key point

The Constitution is not a mere lawyer's document but a vehicle of life, and its spirit is the spirit of the age. "However good a Constitution may be, if those who are implementing it are bad, it will prove to be bad. However bad a Constitution may be, if those implementing it are good, it will prove to be good." It lives only when institutions and citizens give it life in every generation.
