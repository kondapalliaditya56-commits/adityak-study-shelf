1998PYQ 1(Prelims 1998)

Assertion [A]: The Congress rejected the Cripps proposals.
Reason [R]: The Cripps Mission consisted solely of whites.

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Both parts are true. INC did reject the proposals, wanting Purna Swaraj, not Dominion Status, and the Mission was an all-British team (Cripps plus senior British officials). But composition was not the reason for rejection. The recorded grounds were Dominion Status instead of Purna Swaraj, the Princes' nominees in the Constituent Assembly, and the right to secede. So R is true but does not explain A. (Teacher's slide: T, T, link crossed out.)

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Fails the explanation test: the rejection grounds were constitutional (Dominion Status, secession rights, unelected Princely-State nominees), not the Mission's all-British composition.

(C) ✗ — R is not false: the Mission was led by Cripps with senior British officials as the other members, no Indian on the team.

(D) ✗ — A is not false: INC rejected the offer, and MG dismissed it as a 'post-dated cheque'.

1999PYQ 2(Prelims 1999)

Which Indian nationalist leader looked upon a war between Germany and Britain as a godsent opportunity which would enable Indians to exploit the situation to their advantage?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Netaji Bose took the view opposite to Gandhi's: Britain's weakness was India's opportunity, and the moment called for an immediate Civil Disobedience Movement. That is precisely the 'godsent opportunity' position.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — No such view is recorded for Rajagopalachari; most INC leaders privately favoured an Allied victory.

(B) ✗ — Jinnah's wartime opening was political, not military: ML1906 used the INC's confrontation with the British to pass the Lahore Resolution (March 1940).

(D) ✗ — Nehru's position was the opposite of adventurism: no cooperation without Purna Swaraj, but also no mass movement during the war itself.

Chapter-derived Mains practice with the demand, guiding themes and a conclusion.

FYQ 1 WWII fundamentally transformed the Indian National Movement, from a colonial reformist struggle into a movement that could no longer be contained by negotiation. Examine. (15 marks, 250 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine' + 'fundamentally transformed' demands tracing the structural shift WWII caused.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Before September 1939, the INM operated within the constitutional reformist framework of GOI 1935. By August 1942 (Quit India), it had broken out of that framework entirely.

1. The Linlithgow declaration: the Viceroy's unilateral war declaration without consulting elected Indian governments destroyed the legitimacy of the GOI 1935 federal-cooperation framework. The Congress Ministries' resignation ended a key British strategy: using elected Indian governments as legitimating instruments.

2. Wardha, August Offer, Individual Satyagraha: each negotiation revealed the gap. Indians wanted a post-war independence commitment plus immediate representative government; Britain offered post-war Dominion Status plus a minority-veto framework. Cumulative rejection created a constituency for radical action.

3. The Cripps failure: sent under pressure (US and USSR pressure on Churchill; Burma/Singapore reverses), Cripps confirmed that no further constitutional advance was possible during the war. Gandhi's 'post-dated cheque' phrase captured this. The failure was the immediate trigger for Quit India.

4. The structural transformation: WWII forced the INM to choose between negotiating within the British framework (which had repeatedly failed) or moving outside it. Quit India was the first INM action explicitly aimed at making British rule administratively unworkable, not at extracting reforms.

Closing verdict: WWII collapsed the negotiation framework. The trajectory Sep 1939 (resignations), Aug 1940 (Offer rejection), Oct 1940 (Individual Satyagraha), Mar 1942 (Cripps failure), Aug 1942 (Quit India) was a one-way transformation. The pre-war reformist INM could not have produced Quit India; the post-WWII INM could not return to GOI 1935 negotiation.

Revise: 1939 Where Did the INC Stand @WW II (resignations + Wardha), August Offer, Individual Satyagraha, Cripps.

FYQ 2 Examine the reasons why the Cripps Mission (March 1942) failed despite the British sending their most India-friendly negotiator. (10 marks, 150 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine reasons' + 'despite India-friendly' demands explaining why even a sympathetic Cripps could not bridge the gap.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Sir Stafford Cripps was a Labour-Left politician with public sympathy for Indian independence. His Mission still failed because the substance of what Britain could offer fell below what India would accept.

1. The substance gap: Cripps offered Dominion Status after the war plus a post-war Constituent Assembly. INC wanted post-war independence plus immediate representative government with Indians in the Defence portfolio. The two were structurally incompatible regardless of Cripps's personal goodwill.

2. The provincial opt-out, the Pakistan corridor: any province or Princely State could opt out of the Union and form its own dominion. INC saw this as conceding Pakistan by stealth; ML saw it as still inadequate (Pakistan not explicit). Both opposed the same clause for opposite reasons.

3. Cripps's lack of authority: his role was to convey the War Cabinet's pre-decided position, and the Viceroy kept Defence ('take it or leave it'). Gandhi's 'post-dated cheque' captured the dual problem: Britain's commitment was time-distant, and its military position was deteriorating (Burma lost, Singapore fallen).

Closing verdict: Cripps failed because the negotiator was India-friendly but the brief was not. Substance, not personality, drove the failure. The collapse was the immediate trigger for Quit India (August 1942).

Revise: Cripps Mission.
