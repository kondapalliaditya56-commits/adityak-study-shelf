1998PYQ 1(Prelims 1998)

Assertion [A] The Congress rejected the cripps proposals. Reason [R] The Cripps Mission consisted solely of whites.

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Both parts are true: INC did reject the proposals — wanting Purna Swaraj, not Dominion Status — and the Mission was an all-British team: Cripps plus "senior British officials". But the composition was not the reason for rejection — the recorded grounds were Dominion Status instead of Purna Swaraj, the Princes' nominees in the Constituent Assembly, and the right to secede. Hence R is true but does not explain A.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Fails on the explanation test: the recorded rejection grounds are constitutional — Dominion Status, secession rights, unelected Princely-State nominees — not the Mission's all-British composition.

(C) ✗ — R is not false: the Mission was led by Cripps with "senior British officials" as the other members — no Indian on the team.

(D) ✗ — A is not false: INC rejected the offer — MG dismissed it as a "post-dated cheque".

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — Jinnah's recorded wartime opportunity was political, not military: ML1906 used INC's confrontation with the British to pass the Lahore Resolution demanding Pakistan in March

  • The war-as-opportunity stance is Bose's.

(D) ✗ — Nehru's recorded position was the opposite of adventurism: no cooperation without Purna Swaraj, but also "no mass movement during the war itself". (Note: The deteriorate condition of Europe during the Second World War made many advocated to involved in colonial India, the sense of complete freedom. An important aspect of the Cripps Mission of 1942 was

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Provisions: (@Swaraj) Dominion Status after the war, plus Indian control over foreign policy. An Indian Union with Dominion Status soon after the war was the heart of the offer.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Reversed: far from making accession compulsory, the offer's most explosive provision allowed any Province or Princely State that disagreed with the new constitution NOT to join the Union and to frame its own constitution.

(C) ✗ — Full sovereign status was never on the table: the offer stopped at Dominion Status — "still Dominion Status, not Purna Swaraj".

(D) ✗ — Reversed again: instead of one constitution binding all provinces, a dissenting Province or State could refuse the framed constitution and write its own — the 576-entities danger. (Note: An important aspect of Cripps Mission, under the leadership of Stafford Cripps was the creation of Indian union with dominion status, very soon after the war.)

2008PYQ 4(Prelims 2008)

Assertion (A) The Congress Ministries in all the provinces resigned in the year 1939. Reason (R) The Congress did not accept the decision of the Viceroy to declare war against Germany in the context of the II World War. Codes

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Both Assertion and Reason are true, and R IS the correct explanation of A. Viceroy Linlithgow declared India a co-belligerent in WWII (Sept 1939) without consulting Indian leaders. The Congress Working Committee at the Wardha Meet (Sept-Oct 1939) directed all Congress-led provincial ministries to resign in protest. The resignation (Assertion) was directly caused by Congress's refusal to accept the unilateral war declaration (Reason).

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — The explanation IS direct: the Viceroy's unilateral war declaration led straight to the Wardha directive that "Congress ministries in the Provinces were directed to resign". R correctly explains A.

(C) ✗ — R is not false: Britain declared India a belligerent "without consent" and the CWC refused to support the war effort.

(D) ✗ — A is not false: the resignation of the Congress ministries is recorded — it "shook the British" into making the August Offer. (Note: Indian Viceroy Lord Linlithgow declared India against Germany in II World War dated 17th October, 1939. The Indian National Congress Working Committee on 23rd October, 1939, decided not to support the war effort.)

2009PYQ 6(Prelims 2009)

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Statement 2 is recorded: a post-war Constituent Assembly "composed solely of Indians" was a Cripps provision. Statement 1 fails: the offer was Dominion Status, "not Purna Swaraj". Hence only 2.

Wrong options:

2010PYQ 8(Prelims 2010)

Who among the following were official Congress negotiators with Cripps Mission?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Nehru and Maulana Azad. Azad was the INC's serving President through the war years, and Nehru was the leader most invested in a negotiated path — INC's December 1941 offer of war support had been made "overriding the individual objections of both Nehru & MG", and his stated line was negotiation without a wartime mass movement. They negotiated for the party with Cripps.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — MG stood apart from the negotiation: the December 1941 decision to engage had overridden his objections, and his verdict on the offer — a "post-dated cheque" — was that of a critic, not an official negotiator.

(B) ✗ — Neither Kripalani nor Rajagopalachari is recorded as negotiating with Cripps; the negotiating pair credited in this Answer Key is Nehru and Azad (see the ✓ entry above).

(D) ✗ — Rajendra Prasad and Rafi Ahmed Kidwai have no recorded role in the Cripps talks; the official pair was Nehru and Azad (see the ✓ entry above). (Note: The Cripps Mission was an attempt in late March, 1942 by the British Government to secure Indian cooperation and support for their efforts in World War II. The Mission was headed by Sir Stafford Cripps.)

2022PYQ 11(Prelims 2022)

Proposals of Cripps Mission: 1. Constituent Assembly with nominated members. 2. Provinces right to separate agreement.

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Statement 2 is recorded: a Province that did not accept the framed constitution could stay out of the Union and frame its own constitution or set up its own union within the British Empire — i.e. a separate arrangement with Britain. Statement 1 fails: provincial members of the Constituent Assembly were to be ELECTED from the PLAs — only the Princely States sent unelected nominees. Hence 2 only.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Reversed: statement 1 is the flawed one — PLA members were elected, not nominated — while statement 2 is the recorded secession option.

(C) ✗ — Statement 1 fails on the word 'nominated': only Princely-State representatives were nominees; provincial members came by election from the PLAs.

(D) ✗ — Statement 2 stands: the right of a dissenting Province to stay out and frame its own arrangement is recorded — so 'neither' fails.

Chapter-derived Mains practice with the demand, guiding themes and a conclusion.

FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words WWII fundamentally transformed the Indian National Movement — from a colonial reformist struggle into a movement that could no longer be contained by negotiation. Examine.

Demand of the question: "Examine" + "fundamentally transformed" demands tracing the structural shift WWII caused.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Before September 1939, the INM operated within the constitutional reformist framework of GOI 1935. By August 1942 (Quit India), it had broken out of that framework entirely. Body guidance — 4 themes:

1. The Linlithgow declaration (see '1939: Where Did the INC Stand @WW II?'). Viceroy's unilateral war declaration without consulting elected Indian governments destroyed the legitimacy of the GOI 1935 federal-cooperation framework. The Congress Ministries' resignation (Oct 1939) ended a key British strategy — using elected Indian governments as legitimating instruments.

2. Wardha → August Offer → Individual Satyagraha (see '1939 Wardha Meet of the Congress Working Committee'). Each negotiation revealed the gap: Indians wanted post-war independence commitment + immediate representative government; Britain offered Dominion Status (post-war) + minority veto framework. Cumulative rejection created a constituency for radical action.

3. The Cripps failure (see 'Cripps Mission (1942)'). Sent under pressure (Singapore fall + American pressure on Churchill), Cripps confirmed that no further constitutional advance was possible during the war. Gandhi's "post-dated cheque" phrase captured this. Cripps's failure was the immediate trigger for Quit India.

4. The structural transformation. WWII forced the INM to choose: negotiate within the British framework (which had repeatedly failed) or move outside it. By August 1942, the choice was made — Quit India was the first INM action that explicitly aimed at making British rule administratively unworkable, not at extracting reforms.

Closing verdict: WWII collapsed the negotiation framework. The trajectory Sep-1939 (resignations) → Aug-1940 (Offer rejection) → Oct-1940 (Individual Satyagraha) → Mar-1942 (Cripps failure) → Aug-1942 (Quit India) was a one-way transformation. The pre-war reformist INM could not have produced Quit India; the post-WWII INM could not return to GOI 1935 negotiation.

Revise: '1939: Where Did the INC Stand @WW II?'-3 (Resignations + Wardha) + 'August Offer (AO1940) by Linlithgow36-43'-5 (August Offer) + 'Individual Satyagraha' + 'Cripps'.

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words Examine the reasons why the Cripps Mission (March 1942) failed despite the British sending their most India-friendly negotiator.

Demand of the question: "Examine reasons" + "despite India-friendly" demands explaining why even a sympathetic Cripps couldn't bridge the gap.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Sir Stafford Cripps was a Labour-Left politician with public sympathy for Indian independence. His Mission still failed — because the substance of what Britain could offer fell below what India would accept. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Cripps Mission (1942)'):

1. The Substance Gap. Cripps offered Dominion Status after the war + post-war Constituent Assembly. INC wanted post-war independence + immediate representative government with Indians in the Defence portfolio. The two were structurally incompatible regardless of Cripps's personal goodwill.

2. The Provincial Opt-Out — the Pakistan corridor. The Proposals allowed any province or Princely State to opt out of the Indian Union & form its own dominion. INC saw this as conceding Pakistan by stealth; ML saw it as still inadequate (Pakistan not explicit). Both opposed the same clause for opposite reasons.

3. Cripps's lack of authority. Cripps had no authority to negotiate the offer; his role was to convey Churchill's War Cabinet's pre-decided position. Gandhi's "post-dated cheque on a crashing bank" captured the dual problem — Britain's commitment (post-war) was time-distant, & British military position (Singapore fallen February 1942) was deteriorating.

Closing verdict: Cripps failed because the negotiator was India-friendly but the brief was not. Substance, not personality, drove the failure. The Mission's collapse was the immediate trigger for Quit India (August 1942).

Revise: 'Cripps Mission'.
