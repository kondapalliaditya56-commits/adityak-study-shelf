Part 2 · Europeans arrive and the Company conquers

Chapters 8–14

8The Advent of EuropeansAsia had what Europe wanted: spices from Indonesia, textiles from India, silk and tea from China. Arabs and the traders of Genoa and Venice carried them, and the Ottoman capture of Constantinople (1453) made Europe look for a new sea route.DoneClass slides9Carnatic Wars (1746-63)The EIC was a profit-making company, yet by 1857 it ruled 63% of India's territory and 78% of its population. The 1661 Charter gave it forts, troops, war and peace and courts; 1676 gave it a mint.DoneClass slides10Battle of Plassey 1757 and Battle of Buxar 1764The EIC began as a trader with trading privileges (the Farmans, above all the 1717 one) that its officials abused through Dastaks and private trade.DoneClass slides11Subsidiary Alliance of Wellesley 98-05 (1798-1805)Richard Wellesley 98-05 was joined in India by his two brothers: Arthur (Royal Army, not an EIC officer) and Henry (administration and diplomacy).DoneClass slides12Anglo-Mysore Wars (1766-99) + Travancore + CarnaticHyder Ali 61-82 and Tipu 82-99 ran Mysore on Military Fiscalism and a trading State, so the EIC and four neighbours (Marathas, Hyderabad, Travancore, Carnatic) all felt threatened.DoneClass slides13Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775-1819) till 1848The three wars in one line each: 1st (1775-82) began in factionalism at Surat, ended at Salbai in a military alliance; 2nd (1803-05) began at Bassein 1802, the Peshwa's SA; 3rd (1817-19) came from the breach of the SA under the Policy of Paramountcy.DoneClass slides14Dalhousie (1848-56): Peak of ExpansionDalhousie 48-56 used the Policy of Paramountcy (1813-58) in three ways: the Doctrine of Lapse, wars (Punjab 1849, Pegu 1852-53) and territory in lieu of subsidy (Berar 1853).DoneClass slides

Part 3 · Company rule: ideology, economy, administration, society

Chapters 15–19

15British Imperial IdeologyThe White Man's Burden is the roof: Western Europe was superior, so the British had a duty to civilise the world. The ideas under it changed with the EIC's confidence.DoneClass slides16Land Revenue Systems (1765-1885)Under the Mughals the Khudkasht (the cultivator) held customary rights and a patta (a written record of what he owed); the Zamindar only collected on commission and the King alone was the legal owner.DoneClass slides17Constitutional History of EIC's British India (1773-1858)The Br State let the EIC rule as a Company State, but from 1773 it regulated only the EIC's political (CMR) work, because trade is private business and territory is a State matter.DoneClass slides18EIC Administration: Judiciary, Civil Services, PoliceJudiciary: before 1772 Bengal had two ladders (civil and criminal). Hastings 73-85 put the DC as civil judge (1772) and the Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774). Cornwallis 86-93's Code of 1793 took judicial power from the DC, because PS1793 zamindars needed a fair court, and made Anglo-Muhammadan law.DoneClass slides
