Anglo-Mysore Wars (1766-99) + Travancore + Carnatic

Exam lens#

Prelims hooks

  • The four wars with years, result and treaty: 1st 1767-69 won by Mysore (Madras 1769); 2nd 1780-84 tie (Mangalore 1784); 3rd 1790-92 lost (Seringapatam 1792); 4th 1799 lost (Wodeyars restored under an SA)
  • Prelims 2001: Tipu 82-99 sent delegations to France, Turkey and Egypt; Prelims 2004 (partial): Tipu 82-99 died in the 4th war (1799) at Seringapatam, not the 3rd, so only statement 3 is true
  • Pollilur, September 1780: iron-cased rockets against Colonel Baillie, tested in Prelims
  • Triple Confederacy 1780-82 (Nana Fadnis) against Triple Alliance (1766-67 and 1790); Warren Hastings 73-85's two moves (Guntur back, Salbai May 1782)
  • The sums: 35 lakh to the Marathas, 20 lakh to the Nizam, 3 crore and two sons in 1792; what Mysore lost in 1792 (Malabar, Dindigul, Baramahal)
  • Dhondia Wagh 1800; Carnatic annexed 1801; Travancore: Treaty of Perpetual Friendship 1795, Resident 1800, SA 1805, Kundara Proclamation January 1809

Mains hooks

  • 'Why Indian States lost?' (150 or 250 words, the teacher's focus): factionalism, war defeats, internal revolts; many States with a territory motive means rivalry; the EIC's better leadership and army
  • Why Mysore lost: no stable alliance, French naval gap, Military Fiscalism over-extension, Seringapatam open to siege
  • Travancore as the worked example of how an SA is extracted and why it breeds revolt; how the south was consolidated by 1809

Prelims: few questions, so hold the wars, treaties and numbers. Mains: the one question to prepare is the overall 'Why Indian States lost?'.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: Mysore built a war machine and a trading State that frightened every neighbour, so the EIC kept building coalitions against it; across four wars Mysore went from winning to losing, and with its fall the Carnatic was annexed and Travancore was pulled into a Subsidiary Alliance, completing the south by 1809.

  1. A State that frightened everyone. Hyder Ali 61-82 and Tipu 82-99 followed Military Fiscalism: the goal was a stronger military and the tool was maximising revenue. France gave arms and support in exchange for trade concessions, and Tipu ran a State-owned trading company and put an embargo on pepper, cardamom and sandalwood. The teacher's four anxious neighbours were the Marathas, Hyderabad, Travancore and the Carnatic.
    → So in 1766 the EIC and its friends moved.
  2. 1766: the first alliance. Hyder Ali 61-82 had a modern arsenal at Dindigul Fort (1755) and in 1766 annexed Malabar and Calicut. The same year, under the Treaty of Hyderabad, the EIC promised to help Hyderabad invade Mysore and received the Northern Sarkars (4 of 5 districts, Guntur kept) for Rs 7 lakh a year. The Marathas joined, giving the triple alliance of EIC, Hyderabad and Marathas.
    → But Hyder knew how to buy a coalition apart.
  3. The bribe and the gates of Madras. In 1767 Hyder paid the Marathas Rs 35 lakh to exit, and gave the Nizam Rs 20 lakh plus a promise of the Carnatic Nawabi for his son. Hyderabad made peace with the EIC in 1768. In 1769 Hyder reached the gates of Madras while the Bombay navy took coastal Mysore (Mangalore). The EIC signed the Treaty of Madras: territories returned and a defensive alliance. The teacher marks the 1st war as won by Mysore.
    → But a defensive alliance is only as good as who attacked whom.
  4. A promise broken. In 1771 the Marathas attacked Mysore and the EIC refused its 1769 duty, so the alliance was a paper one. France then joined the American War (1778) and in 1779 the EIC seized the French port of Mahe, under Mysore's protection and Hyder's arms route.
    → Hyder struck back in 1780.
  5. Pollilur and the Confederacy. Hyder invaded the Carnatic and took Arcot. At Pollilur (September 1780) his iron-cased rockets annihilated Colonel Baillie's infantry, one of the worst defeats ever suffered by a European army in India. Nana Fadnis, the first Indian statesman to grasp the EIC's empire ambitions, built the Triple Confederacy of Marathas, Mysore and Hyderabad (1780-82); Hyderabad joined over Guntur and the unpaid 7 lakh.
    → Warren Hastings answered with diplomacy.
  6. Hastings splits the coalition. Warren Hastings 73-85 returned Guntur to Hyderabad and signed the Treaty of Salbai (May 1782) with the Marathas, ending the 1st Anglo-Maratha War. Mysore was left isolated. Hyder died of cancer in December 1782 and Tipu 82-99 continued, until the Treaty of Mangalore (1784): a stalemate, territories returned.
    → Tipu kept growing, and the EIC found a pretext.
  7. The Travancore trigger, 1790-92. Tipu 82-99 attacked Travancore, a formal EIC ally (1789), and Cornwallis 86-93 used it to declare war. The EIC formed a Triple Alliance with the Marathas and Hyderabad in 1790, and Mysore faced a three-front campaign. By the Treaty of Seringapatam (1792) Tipu lost half his Kingdom (Malabar, Dindigul, Baramahal), paid 3 crore and gave two sons as hostages. The teacher: notice the encirclement by the EIC.
    → Seven years later came the last war.
  8. Napoleon and the fall, 1799. Tipu 82-99's talks with Napoleon gave Wellesley 98-05 his pretext in London. Hyderabad had to fight because of its SA of 1798, the Peshwa sent no army on Nana Fadnis's advice, and Arthur Wellesley, with his Royal regiment, was among the key commanders at the siege. Tipu died defending Seringapatam. Kanara and Coimbatore went to the EIC and Hyderabad, and the Wodeyars returned under an SA with five-year-old Krishnaraja III.
    → So why did Mysore lose?
  9. Four reasons and one answer. Mysore had no stable alliance (its external ally was weak and its Indian neighbours were rivals), a French naval gap (France was busy in 1776-83 and 1792-1804 and its navy could not match Britain's), Military Fiscalism over-extension (the war fine drained the treasury: the teacher's BUT), and a Seringapatam that three coalition armies could surround. Cornwallis 86-93 and Wellesley 98-05 judged it too strong for Indirect Rule: it fought well but largely alone. The teacher's Mains skeleton generalises this to why Indian States lost: factionalism, war defeats and fear of neighbours, internal revolts.
    → After the fall came the clean-up.
  10. Clean-up in the south. Dhondia Wagh, a former prisoner of Tipu 82-99, raised a mounted force from soldiers disbanded under the SA, raided the Mysore-Maratha borderlands (1799-1800) as 'King of Two Worlds', and was killed in 1800 by Colonel Arthur Wellesley. In 1801 Wellesley 98-05 annexed the Carnatic, whose Nawabs had borrowed from private EIC officials against their Jagirs (the Carnatic Debts), citing financial maladministration and secret contact with Tipu. It became direct EIC territory, not an SA State.
    → One southern State was still outside: Travancore.
  11. Travancore pays for help. Marthanda Varma 29-58 had built the State and beaten the Dutch at Colachel (1741), and Rama Varma 58-98 kept the EIC alliance as a hedge against Mysore, which triggered Tipu's 1789 attack. Travancore signed a Treaty of Perpetual Friendship in 1795, took a British Resident in 1800, and in 1805 signed a proper SA with Wellesley 98-05, extracted in exchange for crushing a revolt by its own Nair troops over unpaid salary.
    → The price proved unbearable.
  12. Kundara, 1809. Velu Thampi Dalawa, Prime Minister 1802-09, led an armed revolt against heavy subsidy demands and constant interference, and in January 1809 issued the Kundara Proclamation calling all Travancoreans to rise. It was crushed within months and he died that year. With the Cochin SA of 1809, the EIC was paramount from the Krishna to Kanyakumari, setting up the 2nd and 3rd Anglo-Maratha Wars.

1 · Why was Mysore such a serious threat?#

Story link

Because Mysore built a war machine and a trading State, it frightened the EIC and every neighbour. Ask: what is the goal and what is the tool of Military Fiscalism?

  • Hyder Ali 61-82 and then Tipu 82-99 made Mysore more than a State trying to survive: it tried to dominate the trade, the coastline and the politics of South India.
  • That made it a threat to every neighbouring power, and to the EIC in two special ways.

1.1 Military Fiscalism

  • Mysore followed Military Fiscalism: the primary goal of maximising State revenue was to fund an ever-stronger military.
  • (The EIC had followed the same model since 1765 to build its own strong military.)
  • Under Hyder Ali 61-82 and Tipu 82-99, Mysore built a centralised State with a modern military and a navy, with French assistance.
  • France gave arms and technical support in exchange for trade concessions, to counter EIC trade.
  • The French connection made both the EIC and the neighbouring Indian States even more anxious.
Teacher's board
  • Military Fiscalism is read as two words: Military = GOAL, Fiscalism = TOOL (he underlined TOOL).
  • The teacher's frame for the whole chapter: each player has goals (the box: TRADE + T for territory) and efficient tools to achieve them.
  • For Mysore the goal is military strength
  • The tool is revenue maximisation
  • Chapter 7's board links Mysore to the Vijayanagar empire (1336-1646), so "VN" most likely means Vijayanagar, but that is a reading, not his words (Chapter 7).
  • Under all three he wrote "Anxiety/RIVALRY @ Ma, Hyd, Tr, Ca" (Marathas, Hyderabad, Travancore, Carnatic): the four neighbours who feared Mysore.

1.2 Mercantilist trading State

  • Tipu 82-99 set up a State-owned trading company that entered international trade directly.
  • Mysore was acting as a rival Mercantilist State, exactly as the EIC did.
  • Two mercantilist powers competing over the same trade routes and coastal markets cannot coexist easily.
  • In the late 1780s he put a trade embargo on exports of pepper, cardamom and sandalwood from ports under Mysore's control, and barred local merchants from dealing with British traders entirely.
  • Malabar was a critical source of pepper for the EIC and had been under Mysore's control since 1766.
  • He brought in his own Mauludi calendar: a new lunisolar calendar based on the Prophet's birth era, introduced to replace the Islamic Hijri calendar.
  • He sent delegations to France, Turkey (the Ottoman Empire) and Egypt to seek anti-British alliances.
Key takeaway
  • Takeaway: the EIC-Mysore rivalry was not just a military conflict.
  • It was a clash between two entities following the same model (Military Fiscalism plus Mercantilism) in the same geography. Only one could win.
  • The EIC's diplomatic advantage: Mysore's ambitions also threatened every neighbouring State, so coalitions against Mysore were easy to build.

2 · 1st Anglo-Mysore War (1767-69): the EIC lost?#

Story link

Because the threat was real, the EIC and Hyderabad moved in 1766 and Hyder broke the coalition. Ask: who was paid how much?

2.1 Reason

  • Even before Hyder Ali 61-82 came to power, he had begun modernising his army with French help (the French were present in Mahe).
  • In 1755 he set up a modern arsenal (arms factory) at Dindigul Fort.
  • This continued friendship threatened the EIC.
  • 1766: Hyder Ali 61-82 annexed Malabar (the slide adds Calicut), important for the spice trade.
  • The same year, the EIC promised to help Hyderabad invade Mysore, so that the EIC would get the Northern Sarkars (important to connect Bengal with Madras).
  • (Recall: in 1766 Hyderabad gave the EIC the Northern Sarkars in return for protection against Mysore and the Marathas.)
  • The Marathas also saw Mysore as a rival and joined.
  • So the EIC crafted a triple alliance of EIC, Hyderabad and Marathas against Mysore.
  • (It was formed twice; the 2nd time in 1790.)
Same three powers, opposite sides

  • 1768: Hyderabad feared a counter-invasion of Hyderabad itself by the EIC and exited by making peace with the EIC.
  • 1769: Hyder Ali 61-82 had outmanoeuvred the EIC forces and marched to the gates of Madras.

2.3 Result: the Treaty of Madras (1769)

  • The EIC, fearing the destruction of Madras, was forced to sign the Treaty of Madras (1769).
  • Both sides returned conquered territories and agreed to a defensive military alliance: each promised to help the other if attacked by a third party.
  • This was a PoRF arrangement only on paper (PoRF = Policy of Ring Fence, Chapter 10): a fake alliance in practice, as the very next war showed.
Teacher's board

  • The 1769 treaty was a defensive alliance, so he separated the two kinds.
  • Offensive alliance: valid even when one member is the aggressor (Pact of Steel 1939, Germany and Italy).
  • Defensive alliance: valid only when a member is the victim of aggression (NATO 1949, SEATO 1954, Baghdad Pact 1955, later CENTO 1958).
  • His example: Pakistan was a member of these defensive blocs, but in the 1965 and 1971 wars Pakistan was the aggressor, so the pacts did not bind the others.
  • Keep the rule: a defensive clause is only as good as the question "who attacked whom".

3 · 2nd Anglo-Mysore War (1780-84): how the EIC broke the Mysore-Marathas-Hyderabad alliance#

Story link

Because the 1769 alliance was empty and Mahe was seized, Hyder struck again and a Confederacy formed. Ask: how did Hastings split it?

3.1 Reason for war

  • 1771: the Marathas attacked Mysore, and the EIC refused to honour its 1769 treaty obligation to come to Mysore's defence.
  • The final trigger: France entered the American War of Independence (1776-83) against Britain in 1778, and the EIC seized the French port of Mahe in Malabar in 1779.
  • Mahe was under Mysore's military protection, and through it Hyder Ali 61-82 was importing French arms and ammunition.
Teacher's board
  • He drew Mahe as the supply route: France to Mahe to Mysore.
  • Seizing Mahe cut the arms line, so Hyder 61-82 attacked.
  • The teacher also gave the world frame: the American Revolution runs 1765-83, in two phases: violent protest 1765-76 and the American War of Independence 1776-83.
  • France joined that war in 1778
  • So a war in America opens a war in India (the teacher tags the 2nd war "Fr FACTOR")

3.2 Events

  • Hyder Ali 61-82 invaded Carnatic (an EIC ally) and captured Arcot (the capital).
  • He then inflicted one of the worst defeats ever suffered by a European army in India at the Battle of Pollilur (September 1780): Colonel Baillie's disciplined infantry formations were annihilated, Hyder Ali 61-82's troops using iron-cased rockets to devastating effect.
  • Pollilur was a shock to the EIC.

9 · The Carnatic annexed (1801)#

Story link

Because the Carnatic was a debtor to EIC officials, annexation was the end of a long squeeze. Ask: what were the two charges?

9.1 The Carnatic Debts

10 · Travancore: what happens when the SA breaks its own promises?#

Story link

Because Travancore hedged with the EIC against Mysore, it paid later. Ask: what did the EIC get for crushing a mutiny?

10.1 Travancore before the SA

  • Travancore's modern State was built in the 18th century by Marthanda Varma 29-58, who decisively defeated the Dutch at the Battle of Colachel (1741) and reorganised the kingdom into a centralised revenue-based State.
  • His successor Rama Varma 58-98 maintained the EIC alliance as a hedge against Mysore, relevant during Tipu 82-99's late-1780s expansion and embargo on Travancore's pepper exports (section 1).
  • Rama Varma 58-98's EIC alliance was the trigger for Tipu 82-99's attack on Travancore in 1789, which Cornwallis 86-93 used as the pretext for the 3rd Anglo-Mysore War (section 4).
  • After Rama Varma 58-98's death in 1798, the kingdom entered fiscal and political strain.

10.2 British Resident stationed (1800), Subsidiary Alliance signed (1805)

  • Travancore signed a "Treaty of Perpetual Friendship" with the EIC in 1795 (in Rama Varma 58-98's reign): they agreed to pay for a British force to protect against Tipu 82-99.
  • In 1800 a British Resident was stationed in Travancore's court (the handout's note: can you see the imprint of ToA 1765, Chapter 10?).
  • In 1805 Travancore signed a proper Subsidiary Alliance with Wellesley 98-05.
  • The EIC extracted it in exchange for helping the king crush a revolt by his own Nair troops, who had mutinied over non-payment of salary.
  • The State lost sovereignty on the standard SA template (see the SA terms in Chapter 11).

What does the teacher expect from this chapter?Prelims: less Qs. Mains: focus on the overall "Why Indian States lost?" (150 or 250 words).
Mains PYQ (teacher's focus): Why did Indian States lose to the EIC? (150 or 250 words)Intro: States were many, motive territory, so rivalry and no stable alliance; the EIC had better leadership and army. Body: (1) Factionalism: triple alliances 1766-67 and 1790; Hastings 73-85 broke the Confederacy by settling each partner. (2) War defeats and fear of neighbours: Mysore's losses in 1792 and 1799; Hyderabad's first SA (1798). (3) Internal revolts: Travancore's Nair mutiny and Kundara 1809; Dhondia Wagh. Closing: Mysore fought well but alone; the EIC paramount from the Krishna to Kanyakumari by 1809.
Practice Mains (book FYQ 1): Why did Mysore lose the four Anglo-Mysore Wars despite being modernised?Intro: Mysore modernised (centralised State, army, navy with French help) but was a Mercantilist rival that fought largely alone. Body: no stable alliance (aggressive expansion plus EIC diplomacy, Hastings 73-85's Guntur and Salbai moves); French naval gap (France busy in 1776-83 and 1792-1804); Military Fiscalism over-extension (3 crore fine, EIC richer); Seringapatam surrounded by a three-way coalition. Closing: it fought well but alone.
Practice Mains (book FYQ 2): How did the SA and direct annexation complete southern consolidation (1799-1809)?Intro: where a State accepted protection the EIC used an SA, where it did not it annexed. Body: Mysore 1799 (Wodeyars under an SA; Kanara and Coimbatore shared by the EIC and Hyderabad); Carnatic 1801 (Debts, annexed outright); Travancore 1805 (SA for crushing the Nair mutiny; Resident from 1800); Kundara 1809 crushed; Cochin SA. Closing: paramount from the Krishna to Kanyakumari by 1809.
Military Fiscalism: which word is goal, which is tool?Military = goal (an ever-stronger army); Fiscalism = tool (maximise State revenue to fund it). The EIC followed the same model since 1765.
What did Tipu 82-99's embargo cover, and why did it matter?Pepper, cardamom and sandalwood from Mysore's ports (late 1780s); local merchants barred from British traders. Malabar, under Mysore since 1766, was the EIC's critical pepper source. The teacher links it to the 3rd war (1790-92).
Where did Tipu 82-99 send delegations?France, Turkey (Ottoman Empire) and Egypt, for anti-British alliances (Prelims 2001: embassies on modern lines).
Which four neighbours feared Mysore (teacher's Ma, Hyd, Tr, Ca)?Marathas, Hyderabad, Travancore, Carnatic.
What happened in 1766 (Treaty of Hyderabad)?Hyderabad gave the EIC the Northern Sarkars (4 of 5 districts; Guntur not handed over) for protection against Mysore and the Marathas; the EIC to pay 7 lakh a year; the Sarkars linked Bengal with Madras. Hyder Ali 61-82 annexed Malabar and Calicut the same year.
Triple Alliance versus Triple Confederacy?Alliance: EIC + Hyderabad + Marathas against Mysore (1766-67 and 1790). Confederacy (1780-82): Marathas + Mysore + Hyderabad against the EIC, built by Nana Fadnis.
How did Hyder 61-82 break the first triple alliance?1767: paid the Marathas 35 lakh to exit; allied with Hyderabad (20 lakh to the Nizam, promise of Carnatic's Nawabi for his son, joint attack on Carnatic). 1768: Hyderabad made peace with the EIC. 1769: he reached the gates of Madras.
Treaty of Madras 1769: terms and weakness?Both sides returned conquered territories and agreed to a defensive military alliance; on paper only: in 1771 the EIC refused to defend Mysore against the Marathas.
Offensive versus defensive alliance (teacher)?Offensive: valid even when a member is the aggressor (Pact of Steel 1939). Defensive: valid only when a member is the victim (NATO 1949, SEATO 1954, Baghdad Pact 1955 [CENTO 1958]); Pakistan was a member but the aggressor in 1965 and 1971.
Triggers of the 2nd Anglo-Mysore War?1771 EIC refused its 1769 duty when the Marathas attacked Mysore; France joined the American War in 1778; the EIC seized the French port of Mahe (Mysore's protection, Hyder's arms route) in 1779.
Pollilur (September 1780): what and why tested?Colonel Baillie's infantry annihilated by Hyder Ali 61-82's iron-cased rockets, one of the worst defeats of a European army in India; tested in Prelims (rocket-warfare innovation).
Who was Nana Fadnis?De-facto ruler and top minister in the Peshwa's council; the first Indian statesman to understand the EIC's empire ambitions (teacher's "PRE"); built the Triple Confederacy 1780-82 and kept the Marathas out later.
Why did Hyderabad join the Confederacy?The EIC had illegally occupied Guntur (one of 5 Northern Sarkars districts, not handed over in 1766) and stopped the annual 7 lakh.
How did Warren Hastings 73-85 break the Confederacy?Returned Guntur to Hyderabad and signed the Treaty of Salbai (May 1782) with the Marathas, ending the 1st Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82); Mysore was left alone.
Result of the 2nd war?Hyder 61-82 died of cancer in December 1782; Tipu 82-99 continued; Treaty of Mangalore 1784: stalemate, both returned territories (teacher: Tie).
Reason and treaty of the 3rd war?Tipu 82-99 attacked Travancore (an EIC ally; 1789); Cornwallis 86-93 and the 1790 Triple Alliance. Treaty of Seringapatam 1792: half the Kingdom (Malabar, Dindigul, Baramahal), 3 crore, two sons as hostages.
How did the 4th war begin and end?Tipu 82-99's talks with Napoleon gave Wellesley 98-05 a pretext; Hyderabad fought under the SA of 1798; the Peshwa sent no army (Nana Fadnis); Arthur Wellesley (Royal regiment) at the siege; Tipu 82-99 died defending Seringapatam (1799).
What happened to Mysore in 1799?Kanara, Coimbatore and others divided between the EIC and Hyderabad; the central core to the Wodeyars with five-year-old Krishnaraja III under an SA (1799); the Peshwa refused an offer tied to an SA.
Why did Mysore lose (four reasons)?No stable alliance; French naval gap; Military Fiscalism over-extension (3 crore fine, EIC richer: "BUT"); Seringapatam open to siege. Verdict: fought well, fought largely alone.
Who was Dhondia Wagh?A former prisoner of Tipu 82-99 who raised a mounted force of demobilised Mysore soldiers, raided the Mysore-Maratha borders (1799-1800) as "King of Two Worlds"; killed 1800 by Colonel Arthur Wellesley; the EIC formally supreme in the south by 1801.
Tipu 82-99's religious policy in one line?Patronage in the Mysore heartland (Sringeri Sharada Math, temple grants, yajnas) and coercion in Malabar (Nairs, Christians) and Coorg 1785 (Kodavas): secularism was a tactic, not a value.
How did the Carnatic Debts work?Nawabs borrowed from private EIC officials at high interest, pledging Carnatic's Jagirs; the officials collected the land revenue as interest.
Why and when was the Carnatic annexed?1801, six years after Md Ali 52-95's death (1795), by Wellesley 98-05: (financial) maladministration and secret contact with Tipu 82-99; direct EIC territory, not an SA.
Travancore before the SA?Marthanda Varma 29-58 built the centralised revenue State and beat the Dutch at Colachel (1741); Rama Varma 58-98 kept the EIC alliance as a hedge against Mysore, which triggered Tipu 82-99's 1789 attack.
Travancore's road to the SA?1795 Treaty of Perpetual Friendship (pay for a British force); 1800 Resident in the court; 1805 SA, extracted for helping crush the Nair troops' salary mutiny.
Velu Thampi Dalawa and the Kundara Proclamation?PM 1802-09; led an armed revolt against subsidy demands and interference (a breach of the SA's non-interference promise); Kundara Proclamation January 1809 called all Travancoreans to rise; crushed within months; he died that year.
How was the south consolidated by 1809?1799 Wodeyar SA; 1801 Carnatic annexed; 1805 Travancore SA; 1809 Cochin SA and Kundara crushed: the EIC paramount from the Krishna to Kanyakumari, setting up the 2nd and 3rd Anglo-Maratha Wars.
Prelims 2004 in one line?(b) Only 3: Abdali beat the Marathas in 1761 (Lodi lost in 1526 to Babur 26-30); Tipu 82-99 died in the 4th war (1799).
