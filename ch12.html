1 · Why was Mysore such a serious threat?#

Hyder Ali 61-82 and then Tipu 82-99 made Mysore more than a State trying to survive: it tried to dominate the trade, the coastline and the politics of South India. That made it a threat to every neighbouring power, and to the EIC in two special ways.

1.1 Military Fiscalism

  • Mysore followed Military Fiscalism: the primary goal of maximising State revenue was to fund an ever-stronger military.
  • (The EIC had followed the same model since 1765 to build its own strong military.)
  • Under Hyder Ali 61-82 and Tipu 82-99, Mysore built a centralised State with a modern military and a navy, with French assistance. France gave arms and technical support in exchange for trade concessions, to counter EIC trade.
  • The French connection made both the EIC and the neighbouring Indian States even more anxious.

1.2 Mercantilist trading State

  • Tipu 82-99 set up a State-owned trading company that entered international trade directly. Mysore was acting as a rival Mercantilist State, exactly as the EIC did. Two mercantilist powers competing over the same trade routes and coastal markets cannot coexist easily.
  • In the late 1780s he put a trade embargo on exports of pepper, cardamom and sandalwood from ports under Mysore's control, and barred local merchants from dealing with British traders entirely. Malabar was a critical source of pepper for the EIC and had been under Mysore's control since 1766.
  • He brought in his own Mauludi calendar: a new lunisolar calendar based on the Prophet's birth era, introduced to replace the Islamic Hijri calendar.
  • He sent delegations to France, Turkey (the Ottoman Empire) and Egypt to seek anti-British alliances.
Remember

Takeaway: the EIC-Mysore rivalry was not just a military conflict. It was a clash between two entities following the same model (Military Fiscalism plus Mercantilism) in the same geography. Only one could win. The EIC's diplomatic advantage: Mysore's ambitions also threatened every neighbouring State, so coalitions against Mysore were easy to build.

2 · 1st Anglo-Mysore War (1767-69): the EIC lost?#

2.1 Reason

  • Even before Hyder Ali 61-82 came to power, he had begun modernising his army with French help (the French were present in Mahe). In 1755 he set up a modern arsenal (arms factory) at Dindigul Fort. This continued friendship threatened the EIC.
  • 1766: Hyder Ali 61-82 annexed Malabar (the slide adds Calicut), important for the spice trade.
  • The same year, the EIC promised to help Hyderabad invade Mysore, so that the EIC would get the Northern Sarkars (important to connect Bengal with Madras). (Recall: in 1766 Hyderabad gave the EIC the Northern Sarkars in return for protection against Mysore and the Marathas.)
  • The Marathas also saw Mysore as a rival and joined. So the EIC crafted a triple alliance of EIC, Hyderabad and Marathas against Mysore. (It was formed twice; the 2nd time in 1790.)
Teacher's board

Beside Malabar and Calicut: "[not Part of Travancore]." The 1766 annexation took Malabar and Calicut, not Travancore (Chapter 7 says the same: Travancore itself was not taken). His map line from Madras through Andhra to Bengal shows why the EIC wanted the Northern Sarkars: a land link between its two Presidencies. He added "+ 7 lakh Rs p.a. to HYD": what the EIC promised to pay Hyderabad for the Sarkars. The arrow "3rd A-My [1790-92]" under the triple-alliance line marks the second time it was formed.

Memory hook

1766: four given, Guntur kept, seven lakh promised.

Decode: 1766 Treaty of Hyderabad: the Northern Sarkars go to the EIC (4 of 5 districts handed over; Guntur was not), the EIC promises 7 lakh rupees a year and military help to Hyderabad against Mysore. The EIC wanted Andhra to join Bengal and Madras.

Why it sticks: Guntur is the fifth finger held back; it comes back to bite in 1780.

Memory hook

Decode: Triple Alliance = EIC + Hyderabad + Marathas against Mysore, formed 1766-67 and again 1790. Triple Confederacy (1780-82) = Marathas + Mysore + Hyderabad against the EIC, built by Nana Fadnis. Same three Indian powers, opposite sides.

Why it sticks: C for Confederacy and Company-opponent, A for Alliance and Allied-with-Company.

2.2 Events

Hyder Ali 61-82 broke the coalition through skilled diplomacy:

  • 1767: he paid the Marathas Rs 35 lakh to exit the war.
  • Then he allied with Hyderabad: he gave the Nizam Rs 20 lakh, promised to make the Nizam's son Nawab of Carnatic, and agreed to a joint future attack on Carnatic (it was under Md Ali 52-95, an ally of the EIC).
  • 1768: Hyderabad feared a counter-invasion of Hyderabad itself by the EIC and exited by making peace with the EIC.
  • 1769: Hyder Ali 61-82 had outmanoeuvred the EIC forces and marched to the gates of Madras. Meanwhile the EIC navy from Bombay captured coastal areas of Mysore, e.g. Mangalore.

2.3 Result: the Treaty of Madras (1769)

  • The EIC, fearing the destruction of Madras, was forced to sign the Treaty of Madras (1769).
  • Both sides returned conquered territories and agreed to a defensive military alliance: each promised to help the other if attacked by a third party.
  • This was a PoRF arrangement only on paper (PoRF = Policy of Ring Fence, Chapter 10): a fake alliance in practice, as the very next war showed.
Teacher's board

Why the teacher stopped to teach "military alliance". The 1769 treaty was a defensive alliance, so he separated the two kinds. Offensive alliance: valid even when one member is the aggressor (Pact of Steel 1939, Germany and Italy). Defensive alliance: valid only when a member is the victim of aggression (NATO 1949, SEATO 1954, Baghdad Pact 1955, later CENTO 1958). His example: Pakistan was a member of these defensive blocs, but in the 1965 and 1971 wars Pakistan was the aggressor, so the pacts did not bind the others. Keep the rule: a defensive clause is only as good as the question "who attacked whom". (The next slide, 33, is a small board with "wp" and "ep" and a circled 1971, crossed out: a side sketch with no new facts.)

3 · 2nd Anglo-Mysore War (1780-84): how the EIC broke the Mysore-Marathas-Hyderabad alliance#

3.1 Reason for war

  • 1771: the Marathas attacked Mysore, and the EIC refused to honour its 1769 treaty obligation to come to Mysore's defence.
  • The final trigger: France entered the American War of Independence (1776-83) against Britain in 1778, and the EIC seized the French port of Mahe in Malabar in 1779. Mahe was under Mysore's military protection, and through it Hyder Ali 61-82 was importing French arms and ammunition.
Teacher's board

He drew Mahe as the supply route: France to Mahe to Mysore. Seizing Mahe cut the arms line, so Hyder 61-82 attacked. The teacher also gave the world frame: the American Revolution runs 1765-83, in two phases: violent protest 1765-76 and the American War of Independence 1776-83. France joined that war in 1778; so a war in America opens a war in India (the teacher tags the 2nd war "Fr FACTOR").

Memory hook

71 refused, 78 France, 79 Mahe, 80 war.

Decode: 1771 Marathas attack Mysore, the EIC refuses its 1769 duty. 1778 France enters the American War (1776-83). 1779 the EIC seizes French Mahe (under Mysore's protection; Hyder 61-82's arms route). 1780 Hyder Ali 61-82 invades Carnatic. The American Revolution: protest 1765-76, war 1776-83.

Why it sticks: four steps up the years 71, 78, 79, 80, the last three almost consecutive.

3.2 Events

  • Hyder Ali 61-82 invaded Carnatic (an EIC ally) and captured Arcot (the capital).
  • He then inflicted one of the worst defeats ever suffered by a European army in India at the Battle of Pollilur (September 1780): Colonel Baillie's disciplined infantry formations were annihilated, Hyder Ali 61-82's troops using iron-cased rockets to devastating effect. Pollilur was a shock to the EIC.

The Triple Confederacy (1780-82): an alliance of Marathas, Mysore and Hyderabad against the EIC, established by Nana Fadnis, the de-facto ruler and most important minister in the Peshwa's council.

  • Hyderabad joined because the EIC had illegally occupied Guntur (one of the 5 districts of the Northern Sarkars, not handed over in 1766) and had stopped paying the annual 7 lakh rupees agreed for ceding the Northern Sarkars.

1771 the EIC refuses to help, 1779 it seizes Mahe, 1780 Pollilur: Baillie burns to iron rockets; Nana's Triple Confederacy (My-Ma-Hy) falls to Hastings 73-85's Guntur back, Salbai signed; Mangalore 1784, a tie.

1789 Tipu 82-99 hits Travancore, Cornwallis 86-93 rebuilds the Triple Alliance, and Seringapatam 1792 costs Half, 3, 2.

1799 Napoleon letters, Wellesley 98-05, a bound Hyderabad, Arthur Wellesley at the siege: Tipu 82-99 falls; a five-year-old Wodeyar rules under an SA. Mysore lost for four NOs (ally, fleet, funds, fortress).

Dhondia 99-00, then 01 Carnatic (Loan, Collateral, Interest; Money and Mole), 05 Travancore (pay the army, pay the price), 09 Kundara: the EIC from the Krishna to Kanyakumari.

Decode: 1766, 1769, 1780, 1784, 1792, 1799, 1801, 1805, 1809.

Recall sheet#

Read once, hide it, then rebuild the chapter from memory.

  1. Teacher's priority: Prelims less Qs; Mains focus "Why Indian States lost?" (150 or 250 words): Factionalism, War defeats (fear of neighbours: Hyderabad), Internal revolts (Travancore); multiple States with a territory motive = rivalry; the EIC's better leadership and army.
  2. Mysore threat: Military Fiscalism (Military = goal, Fiscalism = tool; the EIC did the same since 1765); French arms and a navy for trade concessions; Tipu 82-99's State trading company (Mercantilist State).
  3. Tipu 82-99's late-1780s embargo on pepper, cardamom and sandalwood; barred merchants from British traders; Malabar (Mysore's since 1766) was the EIC's pepper source; Mauludi lunisolar calendar; delegations to France, Turkey and Egypt (Prelims 2001: Tipu 82-99).
  4. 1st war 1767-69: Hyder Ali 61-82 annexed Malabar and Calicut (1766); Treaty of Hyderabad 1766 (EIC gets Northern Sarkars, 4 of 5 districts, Guntur not handed over; EIC to pay 7 lakh a year); triple alliance EIC + Hyderabad + Marathas (again in 1790).
  5. Hyder 61-82 paid the Marathas 35 lakh and the Nizam 20 lakh (plus promise of Nawabi of Carnatic for the Nizam's son); Hyderabad made peace in 1768; 1769 at the gates of Madras; Treaty of Madras 1769 returned territories and a defensive alliance (a farce).
  6. Offensive versus defensive alliance (teacher): Pact of Steel 1939; NATO 1949, SEATO 1954, Baghdad Pact 1955 [CENTO 1958]; Pakistan was a member but the aggressor in 1965 and 1971.
  7. 2nd war 1780-84: 1771 EIC refused to defend Mysore; France in the American War 1778; EIC seized Mahe 1779; Hyder 61-82 took Arcot; Pollilur Sep 1780 (Colonel Baillie, iron-cased rockets; tested in Prelims).
  8. Triple Confederacy 1780-82: Marathas + Mysore + Hyderabad against the EIC, built by Nana Fadnis (1st Indian statesman to grasp the EIC's empire ambitions: circled PRE); Hyderabad joined over Guntur and the unpaid 7 lakh.
  9. Warren Hastings 73-85: Guntur back to Hyderabad, Treaty of Salbai May 1782; Hyder 61-82 died December 1782; Tipu 82-99 continued; Treaty of Mangalore 1784: stalemate, status quo.
  10. 3rd war 1790-92: Tipu 82-99 attacked Travancore (1789); Cornwallis 86-93; Triple Alliance 1790; Treaty of Seringapatam 1792: half the Kingdom (Malabar, Dindigul, Baramahal), 3 crore, two sons as hostages. Teacher: encirclement by the EIC.
  11. 4th war 1799: Napoleon letters and Wellesley 98-05; Hyderabad fought because of the SA of 1798; Peshwa Baji Rao II 96-18 sent no army (Nana Fadnis); Arthur Wellesley's Royal regiment; Tipu 82-99 died defending Seringapatam (Prelims 2004: the 4th, not the 3rd).
  12. After 1799: Kanara and Coimbatore divided between the EIC and Hyderabad; Wodeyars restored with five-year-old Krishnaraja III under an SA (1799); the Peshwa refused a territory-for-SA offer.
  13. Why Mysore lost: no stable alliance (external ally weak, domestic rivalry), French naval gap, Military Fiscalism over-extension (3 crore fine; BUT), Seringapatam open to siege; it fought well but largely alone.
  14. Dhondia Wagh: ex-prisoner of Tipu 82-99, mounted force of demobilised soldiers (SA disbanded the army), "King of Two Worlds", killed 1800 by Colonel Arthur Wellesley; the EIC formally supreme in the south by 1801.
  15. Tipu 82-99's religion: patronage in the heartland (Sringeri Sharada Math, temple grants, yajnas); coercion in Malabar (Nairs, Christians) and Coorg 1785 (Kodavas); secularism a tactic, not a value; King of Coorg allied with the EIC 1790.
  16. Carnatic: Md Ali 52-95 Wallajah 52-95; Carnatic Debts (loans from private EIC officials, Jagirs as collateral, revenue as interest); annexed 1801 under Wellesley 98-05 for (financial) maladministration and secret contact with Tipu 82-99; direct territory, not an SA.
  17. Travancore: Marthanda Varma 29-58 (Colachel 1741), Rama Varma 58-98 (EIC hedge; 1789 attack); Treaty of Perpetual Friendship 1795; Resident 1800; SA 1805 for crushing the Nair mutiny; Velu Thampi Dalawa PM 1802-09; Kundara Proclamation January 1809; Cochin SA 1809; EIC paramount from the Krishna to Kanyakumari.
