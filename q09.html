1995PYQ 1Battle of Wandiwash

Which one of the following pairs is correctly matched?

Answer: (B)

Why (b) is correct: The Battle of Wandiwash was fought in 1760 between the French and the East India Company. It was the decisive battle of the 3rd Carnatic War. The British, led by Eyre Coote, defeated the French, led by Count de Lally. You will find this in 'Why Did France Lose 3rd Carnatic War (1756-63)?'.

Why the other options are wrong:

  • (a) Battle of Buxar (1764) was fought between Mir Qasim — not Mir Jafar — & the EIC. Mir Jafar was the EIC's man at Plassey (1757); by Buxar he had been replaced by Mir Qasim, who then fought the EIC.
  • (c) Battle of Chilianwala (1849) was fought between Dalhousie48-56's EIC army and the Sikhs — not the Marathas. It was part of the 2nd Anglo-Sikh War.
  • (d) Battle of Kharda (1795) was fought between the Nizam of Hyderabad and the Marathas — not the East India Company.
1996PYQ 2Dupleix as the first European to take part in Indian princely

quarrels Prelims 1996 — Who among the following were the first European to initiate the policy of taking part in the quarrels of Indian princes with a view to acquire territories?

Answer: (B)

Why (b) is correct: Dupleix42-54 invented the model of empire building in India. His blueprint was simple: put your chosen candidate on the throne, station your army at his expense, take territory as payment for the army, and sit in the royal court as the real power behind the throne. He first used this model in the 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54) — backing Muzaffar Jung50-51 in Hyderabad and Chanda Sahib49-52 in Carnatic. You will find this in 'The 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54) — How Did Dupleix Invent the Blueprint for Empire in India?' (Phase 1 — French Victory (1749–51). The Subsidiary Alliance framework — often credited to Wellesley98-05 after 1798 — was operationally pioneered by Dupleix42-54 in Hyderabad in 1751. The EIC was the refiner, not the inventor.

Why the other options are wrong:

  • (a) Robert Clive57-67 came later. He used and refined Dupleix42-54's model in Bengal (Plassey 1757, Buxar 1764) — but he did not invent it.
  • (c) Afonso de Albuquerque was a 16th-century Portuguese conqueror who took Goa in
  • He belonged to a much earlier era of direct conquest — not the "back-a-claimant" model Dupleix42-54 invented.
  • (d) Warren Hastings was a later EIC Governor-General (1772–85). Like Clive57-67, he was a refiner of the Dupleix42-54 model — not the originator.

FYQ 2

With reference to the Wars for Trade, consider the following statements: 1. The Anglo-Mughal War of 1686–90 — also called Child's War — was named after Josiah Child, who was Governor of Bombay and "General" of all EIC forces in India, and John Child, who was Chairman of the Court of Directors of EIC in London. 2. In February 1690, Aurangzeb58-07 pardoned the EIC in return for Rs 1.5 lakh compensation, despite the EIC having been the aggressor that seized Hugli and attacked Mughal ships in the Arabian Sea. 3. The 1691 "Farman" — granting the EIC duty-free trade in Bengal in exchange for Rs 3,000 per annum — was technically a Parwana issued by the Subahdar of Bengal under Aurangzeb58-07's approval, not an Imperial Farman. 4. Aurangzeb58-07 chose not to abolish the EIC because its trade brought forex/gold into India, which he needed for his own constant warfare. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(b) 2, 3 and 4 only. Statement (1) is the trap — roles SWAPPED. Josiah Child was Chairman of the Court of Directors of EIC in London; John Child was Governor of Bombay & "General" of all EIC forces in India. The statement reverses the two — same surname, easy swap under exam pressure. Statements (2), (3), (4) are correct — verbatim from: EIC seized Hugli + attacked Mughal ships in Arabian Sea, Feb 1690 pardon for Rs 1.5 lakh; 1691 Farman technically a Parwana issued by Subahdar of Bengal under Aurangzeb58-07's approval, Rs 3,000 per annum for duty-free trade in Bengal; Aurangzeb58-07's economic-utility motive — forex/gold for war.

FYQ 3

With reference to the reasons for the Carnatic Wars, consider the following statements: 1. The Carnatic Wars (1746–63) were fundamentally an extension of the Anglo-French rivalry in Europe brought to Indian soil. 2. The two European companies in southern India — French EIC at Pondicherry and English EIC at Madras — found themselves on opposing sides whenever London and Paris went to war. 3. The Carnatic region was politically fragmented, with weak Mughal authority and rival claimants to the Nawabship of Carnatic — making it open ground for European interference. 4. All three Carnatic Wars were fought entirely on Indian soil with no European war as the trigger. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(b) 1, 2 and 3 only. Statement (4) is the trap — all three Carnatic Wars were extensions of European wars: 1st Carnatic War = War of the Austrian Succession (1740–48); 3rd Carnatic War = Seven Years' War (1756–63). Only the 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54) was fought primarily on Indian terms (the succession disputes), but even that was framed by the Anglo-French rivalry.

FYQ 4

Which one of the following correctly describes the Battle of Adyar / St Thome (1746)?

Answer: (B)

(b)

Why (b) is correct: At the Battle of Adyar / St Thome (1746), a small French force of ~700 French-trained Indian sepoys and ~300 French soldiers completely destroyed the Nawab of Carnatic’s 10,000-strong traditional cavalry. The French victory rested on three things — superior artillery, tactical discipline (infantry squares), and regular cash salaries. This was the first proof in India that a small, disciplined, European-trained infantry with modern artillery could crush a vastly larger traditional Indian army.

(a) ✗ — The battle was fought between the French and the Nawab of Carnatic, not Bengal, and it concerned southern India, not Surat. (c) ✗ — Clive57-67 and Dupleix42-54 never fought each other directly; Clive came into the picture only from the 2nd Carnatic War (Phase 2). (d) ✗ — Adyar / St Thome was a land battle on the north bank of the River Adyar, not a naval engagement. *Memory hook: 300 + 700 destroyed 10,000 — the first time European-trained infantry beat a traditional Indian cavalry on Indian soil.*

FYQ 5

Which one of the following correctly describes the Treaty of Aix-La-Chapelle (1748)?

Answer: (B)

(b) (Consequence — Treaty of Aix-La-Chapelle (1748)

Why (b) is correct: The Treaty of Aix-La-Chapelle (1748) ended the War of the Austrian Succession in Europe and, by extension, the 1st Carnatic War in India. Under its terms, Madras (which the French had captured in 1746) was restored to the British in exchange for Louisbourg in North America — a global swap in which European powers prized Atlantic territory over Indian holdings.

(a) ✗ — The treaty did not divide the Carnatic; Dupleix42-54 was not recognised as supreme authority. (c) ✗ — the treaty was a European-level agreement signed at Aix-La-Chapelle, not a local one at Pondicherry. (d) ✗ — the Northern Sarkars were not part of this treaty; the British kept Madras after the swap. *Memory hook: Madras swapped for Louisbourg — the Atlantic mattered more to Europe than India in 1748.*

FYQ 6

With reference to the succession disputes that triggered the 2nd Carnatic War, consider the following statements: 1. In Hyderabad, after Nizam-ul-Mulk24-48 died in 1748, his son Nasir Jung48-50 and his grandson Muzaffar Jung50-51 claimed the throne; the British backed Nasir Jung48-50 and the French backed Muzaffar Jung50-51. 2. In Carnatic, Md Ali Wallajah52-95 (the incumbent Nawab Anwaruddin's son) was backed by the British, while the rival claimant Chanda Sahib49-52 was backed by the French. 3. The same two European powers — British and French — backed opposing sides in both succession disputes. 4. Both succession disputes were settled peacefully through Mughal imperial mediation, without military intervention. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(b) 1, 2 and 3 only. Statement (4) is the trap — both succession disputes triggered the 2nd Carnatic War, a major military intervention. Mughal mediation did not happen; the Mughal Emperor was already weak by 1748.

FYQ 7

Which one of the following correctly describes Phase 1 of the 2nd Carnatic War (1749–51)?

Answer: (B)

(b) (Phase 1 — French Victory (1749–51)

Why (b) is correct: Phase 1 of the 2nd Carnatic War (1749–51) saw French ascendancy. At the Battle of Ambur (1749) Anwaruddin (Nawab of Carnatic) was killed and Chanda Sahib49-52 was installed as Nawab. In Hyderabad, Nasir Jung48-50 was killed in December 1750 and Muzaffar Jung50-51 was made Nizam; Muzaffar Jung was killed in February 1751, after which the French installed Salabat Jung51-62 as Nizam — a French puppet. During this phase, Dupleix42-54 invented the Subsidiary Alliance blueprint: back a claimant, station your army at his expense, take territory as payment for the army, and sit in court as the real power.

(a) ✗ — Clive57-67 captured Arcot in Phase 2 (not Pondicherry, not in Phase 1); Md Ali Wallajah52-95 was installed in Phase 2. (c) ✗ — the Treaty of Pondicherry was signed in 1754, after Phase 2. (d) ✗ — Phase 1 had decisive French victories at Ambur and in Hyderabad, not a stalemate. *Memory hook: Phase 1 = Dupleix’s blueprint invented; Phase 2 = Clive copies it.*

FYQ 9

Which one of the following correctly describes the Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)?

Answer: (B)

(b) (Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)

Why (b) is correct: The Treaty of Pondicherry (1754) ended the 2nd Carnatic War. Under its terms both sides agreed not to interfere in the internal politics of Indian states, and Dupleix42-54 was recalled to France. Paris saw Dupleix as the architect of an unprofitable adventure that had cost the Crown without delivering returns. The treaty was a French diplomatic loss because it rolled back Dupleix’s gains and ended his blueprint at the State level — even as the EIC quietly kept copying it.

(a) ✗ — The treaty installed nobody; it just rolled back both sides’ gains. (c) ✗ — there was no permanent French/British division of Carnatic. (d) ✗ — the treaty was signed at Pondicherry, not Madras; Dupleix42-54 negotiated for the French side, not Clive57-67. *Memory hook: France recalled Dupleix — abandoned his blueprint; EIC kept using it.*

Statement (4) is the trap — Wandiwash was French vs EIC, not EIC vs Mughal Empire. ⚠ Alert — Prelims 1995 trap: UPSC asks "match the battle to who fought it" and swaps opponents to fool half-memory. FYQ 12

Which one of the following correctly describes why the EIC won the Carnatic Wars (1746–48, 49–54, 56–63)?

Answer: (B)

(b)

Why (b) is correct: The EIC won the Carnatic Wars for several connected reasons. France starved Dupleix42-54 of reinforcements to preserve its 1748 Treaty gains. Dupleix’s recall after the 2nd Carnatic War broke French strategic continuity, while Robert Clive57-67 and Eyre Coote provided British leadership across all three wars. British capitalism was mature and could finance long wars, while French feudalism plus revolutionary disruption (1789) starved the French EIC of resources. British naval supremacy (which led directly to the Industrial Revolution 1750–1850) made coastal warfare British-favoured.

(a) ✗ — The EIC did not have the largest army in India; it won via leadership continuity + finance + navy, not by sheer numbers. (c) ✗ — the Mughal Emperor played no role in the Carnatic; the Carnatic was a southern theatre. (d) ✗ — the Marathas did not coordinate an attack on the French; they had their own theatre. *Memory hook: Money + Navy + Leadership continuity — the EIC’s three structural advantages over the French.*

FYQ 13

Which one of the following correctly describes the Treaty of Paris (1763)?

Answer: (B)

(b) (Treaty of Paris (1763)

Why (b) is correct: The Treaty of Paris (1763) ended the global Seven Years’ War and, by extension, the 3rd Carnatic War in India. Under its terms, the French got back their factories and Pondicherry but lost their political ambitions in India — they could not fortify Pondicherry or maintain a political establishment. After 1763 the French were no longer a serious empire-building rival to the British in India.

(a) ✗ — Mughal authority was not ended by this treaty; EIC sovereignty over Bengal came via the Diwani grant of 1765, not Paris 1763. (c) ✗ — there was no India-division along the Eastern Ghats. (d) ✗ — the treaty was signed in Paris, not Pondicherry; France received Pondicherry back as a trading post only. *Memory hook: France got the factories back — but lost the empire.* Battle of Plassey 1757 & Battle of Buxar 1764

FYQ 1 How did Dupleix invent the blueprint for empire in India during the 2nd Carnatic War? 150 words · 10 marks "During the 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54), Dupleix invented the blueprint for European empire-building in India. Examine the components of that blueprint and their long-term significance."

Demand of the question: Identify Dupleix’s strategic innovations during the 2nd Carnatic War + show how those moves became a model the EIC later copied at Plassey 1757 and during the Subsidiary Alliance era.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Dupleix’s blueprint had four parts — intervene in succession disputes, back a puppet, secure revenue districts, and use trained Indian troops officered by Europeans. The EIC later copied all four.

Guiding themes (pick 4-5 for a 150-word answer):

1. Intervention in succession disputes — Dupleix exploited Hyderabad and Carnatic succession quarrels (see 'The 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54) — How Did Dupleix Invent the Blueprint for Empire in India?').

2. Puppet rulers — Muzaffar Jung in Hyderabad, Chanda Sahib in Carnatic; European-backed candidates who paid back in cash, jagirs, and trade privileges (see 'The 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54) — How Did Dupleix Invent the Blueprint for Empire in India?').

3. Revenue districts (the Northern Sarkars / Circars) — the French secured territorial revenue from Hyderabad as the price of backing Muzaffar Jung; this was the first European territorial revenue grant in India (see 'The 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54) — How Did Dupleix Invent the Blueprint for Empire in India?').

4. Sepoys — Indian foot-soldiers trained in European drill and officered by Europeans; the original model of the EIC sepoy army (see 'The 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54) — How Did Dupleix Invent the Blueprint for Empire in India?').

5. EIC copies the blueprint — Clive at Plassey 1757 (puppet Mir Jafar + Bengal revenue + sepoy army) was applying exactly Dupleix’s 1749 model on a much bigger canvas (see 'The 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54) — How Did Dupleix Invent the Blueprint for Empire in India?' and 'The 3rd Carnatic War (1756–63)').

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): France won the methodology of empire — and lost the empire. The EIC copied Dupleix’s blueprint and built British India.

Revise: 'The 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54) — How Did Dupleix Invent the Blueprint for Empire in India?' + 'The 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54) — How Did Dupleix Invent the Blueprint for Empire in India?' + 'The 3rd Carnatic War (1756–63)'.

FYQ 2 Why did the French lose the Carnatic Wars and the EIC win? 150 words · 10 marks "Both the French and the English fought three Carnatic Wars in India between 1746 and 1763. The French lost and the English won. Examine the reasons."

Demand of the question: Cover BOTH the French weaknesses (why France lost) AND the EIC strengths (why EIC won). Conclude with the long-term consequence — the Treaty of Paris

1763.

Suggested intro (~25 words): The French lost the Carnatic Wars because home-government support was weaker, the French navy was outclassed, and finance was thinner; the EIC won on all three counts.

Guiding themes (pick 4-5 for a 150-word answer):

1. Home-government support — the British State (Royal Navy + Parliament) backed the EIC; the French monarchy under-funded the French Compagnie (see 'The 3rd Carnatic War (1756–63)').

2. Naval power — the Royal Navy controlled the Atlantic and Indian Ocean sea-lanes; the French lost control of reinforcement routes after 1758 (see 'The 3rd Carnatic War (1756–63)').

3. Financial depth — EIC raised capital through joint-stock + Bank of England credit; French Compagnie was a State monopoly with thinner pockets (see 'The 3rd Carnatic War (1756–63)').

4. Bengal annexation 1757 — Plassey gave the EIC Bengal revenues, funding the Carnatic wars without drawing on London. France had no such windfall (see 'The 3rd Carnatic War (1756–63)').

5. Treaty of Paris 1763 — ended the global Seven Years War + the Indian Carnatic War; France retained Pondicherry as a non-fortified trading post; EIC dominance in India was now uncontested (see 'The 3rd Carnatic War (1756–63)').

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): The Carnatic Wars were lost on the seas + in the treasury, not on the battlefield alone. The EIC won India because Britain backed it.

Revise: 'The 3rd Carnatic War (1756–63)' + 'The 3rd Carnatic War (1756–63)'.
