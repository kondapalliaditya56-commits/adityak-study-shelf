1 · Source and the Articles of Part IV#

The DPSP sit in Part IV, Articles 36 to 51. They are the Constitution's philosophy of social and economic justice and guide the State in making laws and policies.

  • Main source: the Irish Constitution of 1937, which itself took such principles from the Spanish Constitution.
  • Other influences: Baldwin's ideas of the welfare state and Gandhiji's vision of socio-economic justice.

2 · Nature of the Directive Principles#

Five ideas define what the DPSP are.

1. Guiding ideals for the State

They are constitutional instructions or recommendations to the legislative, executive and administrative organs. By Art. 36, "State" means the same as in Part III: Central and State governments, legislatures, local bodies and public authorities.

2. Resemblance to the Instrument of Instructions

  • They resemble the Instrument of Instructions of the Government of India Act, 1935.
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar compared them to those colonial instructions, but in independent India they are addressed to the legislature and executive.
  • The Governor-General once received directions from the British government; DPSP now give democratic directions to Indian legislatures.

3. Comprehensive socio-economic programme

  • A holistic economic, social and political programme for a modern democratic state.
  • Aim: secure the Preamble's justice, liberty, equality and fraternity.
  • Vision: a welfare state, not the colonial police state. Equitable distribution of wealth and protection of workers show this.

4. Non-justiciable but fundamental

  • Courts cannot enforce them.
  • Yet Art. 37 declares them fundamental to governance and makes it the State's duty to apply them in policy-making.

5. Role in constitutional interpretation

  • Courts use them to judge the reasonableness and constitutionality of laws.
  • A law that furthers a DPSP may be upheld even if challenged under Fundamental Rights.
  • Example in the handout: in land reform cases the Supreme Court upheld laws because they gave effect to Directive Principles promoting social justice.

1935 Act

  • Directions from the British government to the Governor-General

DPSP

  • Democratic directions to the legislature and executive of independent India

3 · Classification of the Directive Principles#

The Constitution does not classify them. On content and orientation they fall into three groups: Socialistic, Gandhian, Liberal-Intellectual.

Socialistic (social and economic equality, welfare state)

  • Art. 38 welfare state: secure a social order based on justice; minimise inequalities.
  • Art. 39 equitable livelihood and distribution: adequate means of livelihood, no concentration of wealth, equal pay for equal work, protection of workers and children.
  • Art. 39A equal justice and free legal aid, so economic barriers do not deny justice.
  • Art. 41 right to work, education, assistance: social security in unemployment, sickness, old age, disability.
  • Art. 42 humane work conditions and maternity relief.
  • Art. 43 living wage, decent standard of life, cultural opportunities.
  • Art. 43A workers' participation in management.
  • Art. 47 nutrition, living standards, public health.

Gandhian (simple living, self-reliant villages, moral development)

  • Art. 40 village panchayats as units of self-government.
  • Art. 43 cottage industries: rural and cottage industries on an individual or cooperative basis.
  • Art. 43B cooperative societies: autonomous and democratic functioning.
  • Art. 46 educational and economic promotion of SCs, STs and weaker sections.
  • Art. 47 prohibition of harmful drugs and drinks.
  • Art. 48 cow protection: no slaughter of cows, calves and draught animals; improve their breeds.

Liberal-Intellectual (liberal democracy, modern and globally responsible state)

  • Art. 44 Uniform Civil Code across India.
  • Art. 45 early childhood care and education (the group list says free education up to six years).
  • Art. 48 modern agriculture and animal husbandry on scientific and modern lines.
  • Art. 48A environment, forests and wildlife.
  • Art. 49 monuments and places of national importance.
  • Art. 50 separation of judiciary and executive, so the judiciary is independent in administration.
  • Art. 51 international peace, respect for international law, settlement of disputes by arbitration.

4 · Evolution through amendments#

Amendments have expanded and modified the Directives, showing the Constitution adapts to national needs.

42nd Amendment Act, 1976

The most comprehensive enlargement; four changes for social justice, workers' rights and environment.

Four 42nd changes
CLWE

Children (Art. 39 widened: healthy development, physical and moral well-being), Legal aid (new 39A), Workers in management (new 43A), Environment (new 48A): "Clever Lawyers Win Elections."

  • Art. 48A brought ecological concerns into the Constitution for the first time.
  • 39A: access to justice must not depend on economic capacity.
  • 43A: constitutional backing to industrial democracy.

5 · Constitutional status of the Directive Principles#

They are guiding values, not enforceable rights. The split between justiciable and non-justiciable rights was recommended by Sir B.N. Rau (Constitutional Advisor) and accepted by the Drafting Committee. FRs (Part III) are enforceable; DPSP (Part IV) are not.

Article 37

"The provisions contained in this Part shall not be enforceable by any court, but the principles therein laid down are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the country, and it shall be the duty of the State to apply these principles in making laws." Not legally binding, but a moral and political obligation.

Constituent Assembly voices

  • Alladi Krishna Swamy Ayyar: no responsible ministry could "light-heartedly" ignore the DPSP, since public opinion and political accountability would compel them.
  • Dr. Ambedkar: a government that ignores them answers to the people at elections.

Why the framers made them non-justiciable

Three pragmatic reasons

1
At Independence the country lacked the financial resources for the extensive programme
2
Socio-economic diversity and regional backwardness made uniform enforcement hard
3
The new State, burdened with integration, poverty and development, needed flexibility on order, timing and manner, free of litigation

The real sanction is not courts but public opinion, democratic processes and political responsibility. The DPSP are a moral compass towards the Preamble's ideals.

Sanction one-liner

Court cannot enforce; the voter can. Money, diversity, flexibility = the three reasons.

6 · Significance of the Directive Principles#

Fifteen points in the handout, grouped for recall.

Moral and ideological frame

  • Fundamental to governance: the moral and ideological framework within which the State operates.
  • Life-giving provisions (L.M. Singhvi); M.C. Chagla: full implementation would make India a true welfare state.
  • Conscience of the Constitution (Granville Austin); instrument of social revolution.
  • Moral and educative value (B.N. Rau): a reminder to governments.
  • Instrument of Instructions (M.C. Setalvad): guidance to build a just social and economic order.
  • Reinforce the Preamble: justice, liberty, equality, fraternity.

Economic democracy

  • Ambedkar: the foundation of economic democracy alongside political democracy, so liberty and equality are substantive, not formal.
  • They balance political democracy with economic justice, so democracy touches everyday life.
  • Supplement to FRs: FRs give political freedoms; DPSP recognise social and economic rights for human dignity.

Law, policy and politics

  • Guidance for judiciary in judging reasonableness and constitutionality of laws.
  • Background for legislation and policy.
  • Stability and continuity: commitment stays constant when governments change.
  • Accountability tool for the Opposition.
  • Standard for evaluating government performance: citizens can use them as benchmarks.
  • Common political manifesto: a constitutional guide, philosopher and friend for any government.
Who said what
SCAR

Singhvi = life-giving, Chagla = true welfare state, Austin = conscience, Rau = moral and educative (Setalvad = instrument of instructions, Ambedkar = economic democracy): "Some Critics Adore Rights."

8 · Fundamental Rights versus Directive Principles#

Justiciable FRs and non-justiciable DPSP have clashed since 1950. Art. 37 makes DPSP morally binding, but only FRs can be enforced.

The case chain

Champakam Dorairajan 1951
Court: FRs prevail over DPSP; DPSP must conform and be subordinate. Parliament may amend FRs to give effect to DPSP
Parliament acts
First Amendment (1951), Fourth (1955), Seventeenth (1964) operationalise socio-economic directives
Golaknath 1967
Parliament cannot amend or abridge FRs, which are sacrosanct. Deadlock: DPSP cannot be implemented if they encroach on FRs
24th and 25th Amendments 1971
24th restores Parliament's power to amend FRs. 25th inserts Art. 31C
Kesavananda Bharati 1973
Strikes down the second part of 31C (judicial review is a basic feature). Upholds the first part: limited primacy of Art. 39(b) and (c) over Arts. 14, 19 and 31
42nd Amendment 1976
Extends the first clause of 31C to laws for all DPSP
Minerva Mills 1980
Strikes that expansion down. DPSP again subordinate to FRs, though Arts. 14 and 19 are subject to Art. 39(b) and (c)
Art. 31C as inserted by the 25th Amendment

(i) A law to implement the socialist DPSP in Art. 39(b) and 39(c) is not invalid for contravening Arts. 14, 19 or 31. (ii) No court can question such a law on the ground that it does not conform to that policy.

Case chain
CGAKFM

Champakam, Golaknath, Amendments 24th-25th, Kesavananda, Forty-second, Minerva Mills: "Cats Get Angry, Kick Fat Mice."

  • The 44th Amendment (1978) abolished Art. 31, removing the constitutional right to property.
  • Minerva Mills called FRs and DPSP "two wheels of a chariot": the Constitution rests on a delicate balance, neither gets absolute precedence, and DPSP goals must be pursued without undermining FRs.
  • Today: FRs hold precedence, but Parliament may amend FRs to give effect to DPSP, provided the basic structure is preserved.
Fundamental Rights versus Directive Principles
AspectFundamental RightsDirective Principles
NatureRestrictive: stop the State from infringing individual freedomsPrescriptive: direct the State to take positive measures for welfare
EnforceabilityLegally enforceable; citizens go directly to courtsNot enforceable in court; depends on political will
PurposePolitical freedom and a democratic framework; safeguard individual libertySocial and economic justice; overall welfare
SanctionLegal sanctions; judicial remedies and penaltiesOnly moral and political sanctions
FocusIndividual rights and personal libertyCollective welfare; a just and equitable society
Legislation neededAutomatically operationalNeed enabling legislation or policy measures

Restrain versus push

FR = restrictive, DP = prescriptive. FR: court, legal sanction, self-operating. DP: voter, moral sanction, needs a law.

The 42nd Amendment appears twice: once as the source of four DPSP changes (1976, Section 4) and once as the extension of Art. 31C (Section 8). Both are in the handout and both are true; keep them separate.
