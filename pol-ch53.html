1 · What a cooperative is, and why it matters#

A Cooperative is an autonomous association of persons united voluntarily to meet common economic, social and cultural needs through a jointly owned and democratically controlled enterprise.

  • Philosophy: "Each for all and all for each."
  • Identity: in 2021 the Government created the Ministry of Cooperation to realise "Sahkar se Samriddhi" (Prosperity through Cooperation).
  • As India moves towards a $5 trillion economy, cooperatives are meant to keep growth inclusive.
Rationale and objectives
ObjectiveWhat it means
Credit accessibilityLow-interest loans through PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Societies)
Market linkagesEliminate middlemen: "fair price" for producers, "affordable price" for consumers
Economic growthLinkages from local PACS to national level generate employment and strengthen the rural economy
InclusivityEmpower women, ST/SC and small-scale artisans
Democratic governanceOne member, one vote

In a country of small-holding farmers and fragmented producers, individual bargaining power is low; cooperatives give the economies of scale to compete with large corporations.

Five objectives
CMEID

Credit, Market linkages, Economic growth, Inclusivity, Democratic governance: "Cooperatives Make Everyone's Income Dependable."

2 · Features and historical evolution#

Features of cooperative societies
FeatureMeaning
Voluntary and open membershipAvailable to all without discrimination of gender, social, racial, political or religious affiliation
Democratic member controlControlled by members who set policies; "One Member, One Vote"
Economic participationMembers contribute equitably to capital and receive a fair return
Limited liabilityGenerally restricted to the capital they contributed
Service over profitPrimary objective is services to members, not profit for shareholders
Five features
VDELS

Voluntary membership, Democratic control, Economic participation, Limited liability, Service over profit: "Very Democratic, Equally Local Service."

Historical evolution (the handout says "three distinct phases")

Timeline of the movement

1904
First Cooperative Credit Societies Act, on the recommendation of the Sir Edward Law Committee
1919
Cooperation became a Provincial Subject under the Montague-Chelmsford Reforms
Post-Independence
Cooperatives integrated into rural development strategy under the Five-Year Plans
White Revolution (1970s)
Operation Flood, led by Dr. Verghese Kurien, made India the world's largest milk producer via the Amul model
2011
97th Constitutional Amendment gave constitutional status
2021
Ministry of Cooperation created

Union

  • Parliament governs Multi-State Cooperative Societies (MSCS) operating in more than one state
  • MSCS Act, 2002 is the primary law
  • Amendment Act, 2022 introduced the Co-operative Election Authority to oversee board elections for transparency

State

  • State legislatures govern other cooperative societies through their own state acts

The Ministry of Cooperation (2021) gives a separate administrative, legal and policy framework, taking over these duties from the Ministry of Agriculture.

Handout alert

The handout says the movement evolved through three distinct phases but numbers only two (Pre-Independence and Post-Independence). The items after them (97th Amendment, MSCS Act and 2022 Amendment, Ministry of Cooperation 2021) are the later constitutional and institutional stage; treat them as the third phase when asked.

3 · The 97th Amendment and Part IX-B#

The 97th Constitutional Amendment Act of 2011 is the "Magna Carta" of the sector. It made three changes:

Three critical changes
ChangeDetail
Fundamental RightAdded "cooperative societies" to Article 19(1)(c) (right to form)
Directive PrincipleInserted Article 43-B: State to promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning and professional management
New Part IX-BArticles 243-ZH to 243-ZT, titled "The Co-operative Societies", for democratic and economically sound functioning

State List Entry 32

  • Cooperatives confined to a single state
  • supervised by the Registrar of Cooperative Societies (RCS) of the state

Union List Entry 44

  • Multi-State Cooperative Societies (MSCS) operating in more than one state
Key provisions of Part IX-B
ArticleSubjectProvision
243ZIIncorporationState legislatures make laws for incorporation, regulation and winding-up on the basis of voluntary formation, democratic control and autonomous functioning
243ZJBoard compositionMaximum 21 directors; functional directors are board members but excluded from the count of 21. Reservations: 1 seat for SC or ST; 2 seats for Women on every board having members from these categories. Term: 5 years from election for elected members and office bearers. Co-opted members: max 2 with expertise in banking, management or finance; they cannot vote in elections or be elected office bearers
243ZKElection controlSuperintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and elections vest in a body provided by the State Legislature; elections must be held before the current term expires
243ZLSupersession or suspensionMaximum 6 months; allowed for default, negligence, stalemate or failure to conduct elections. Restriction: a board cannot be superseded if there is no Government shareholding, loan, financial assistance or guarantee
243ZMAudit of accountsAudited at least once every financial year by an auditor or firm from a state-approved panel; audit completed within 6 months of year end
243ZNGeneral body meetingsAnnual general body meeting within 6 months of close of the financial year
243ZOMember rightsRight to access books, information and accounts; state provides for cooperative education and training
243ZPFiling of returnsWithin 6 months of year end: annual report, audited accounts, surplus disposal plan, list of by-law amendments
243ZQOffences and penaltiesFalse returns, disobeying summons, employers failing to pay deducted amounts, failing to hand over records, corrupt election practices
243ZRMulti-State societiesProvisions apply, but references to "State Legislature or Government" read as "Parliament or Central Government"
243ZSUnion TerritoriesProvisions apply unless the President directs otherwise by notification
243ZTContinuance of lawsInconsistent existing laws stayed in force for only one year after the 97th Amendment Act (2011) unless amended sooner
Three changes
FDP

Fundamental Right 19(1)(c), DPSP 43-B, Part IX-B: "Free Democratic Partnerships."

Numbers of Part IX-B

21 directors, 5 years, 2 co-opted, 1 SC/ST seat, 2 women seats, 6 months for supersession, audit, AGM and returns. The only "one year" is 243ZT.

Article ladder

ZI incorporation, ZJ board, ZK election, ZL supersession, ZM audit, ZN general body, ZO member rights, ZP returns, ZQ offences, ZR multi-state, ZS Union Territories, ZT continuance of laws. The suffix letters run I to T in this order.

Six months
SAAR

Supersession, Audit, AGM, Returns: "Six Accounts Are Reviewed": all four capped at six months.

5 · Recent developments (2024-25)#

National Cooperative Policy 2025

Unveiled in mid-2025, replacing the 2002 version. The 3-3-3 target: triple the cooperative sector's GDP contribution by 2034. Mission pillars: strengthening foundations, tech-adoption and expansion into new sectors (Green Energy, Tourism, Insurance). Model Cooperative Village: 5 villages per tehsil as "Centers of Excellence".

6 · Challenges and way forward#

Challenges
ChallengeDetail
Regional imbalancesGujarat and Maharashtra have robust ecosystems; large agrarian states like Uttar Pradesh and Bihar struggle with dormant or poorly managed societies
Trust and certification hurdlesFor NCOL: lack of affordable, widespread district-level testing labs makes it hard for small farmers to prove "organic" status to global buyers
Professional management gapMany PACS lack expertise for modern supply chains, digital accounting and international trade compliance
Competition with corporate giantsNational cooperatives now compete with private MNCs in seeds and FMCG; price competitiveness versus high farmer margins
Regulatory complexityCoordination between the Multi-State Cooperative Act (Union) and various State Acts is a bureaucratic challenge for nationwide scaling

Way forward (roadmap to 2030)

The National Cooperative Policy 2025 addresses these gaps through what the handout calls six strategic pillars.

  • "Bharat" branding: strengthen Bharat Organics and Bharat Beej brands for uniform identity and trust.
  • Digital integration (the "Cooperative Stack"): a national digital database for real-time tracking of procurement and payments; integration with ONDC so local societies sell directly to consumers globally.
  • Expansion to new sectors: Renewable Energy (solar cooperatives), Health (cooperative pharmacies), Services (Sahkar Taxi aggregators).
  • Infrastructure creation: organic testing labs in every district; a decentralised grain storage network at PACS level to reduce post-harvest losses.
  • Educational support: Tribhuvan Cooperative University to train professional managers.
Handout alert

The handout says six strategic pillars but lists five bullets above. Do not invent a sixth; recall the five listed. Also the roadmap is headed "to 2030" while the GDP-tripling target is 2034 and the vision is 2047.

Vision

The evolution is from a "government-aided" sector to a "market-driven" powerhouse. Aim: triple the cooperative sector's GDP share by 2034; by 2047, at least one professional cooperative in every village, making the sector the "second engine" of the Indian economy, prioritising individual self-respect and equitable profit-sharing over corporate consolidation.

Challenges five
RTPCR

Regional imbalance, Trust and certification, Professional management gap, Competition, Regulatory complexity: "Rural Traders Prefer Clear Rules."
