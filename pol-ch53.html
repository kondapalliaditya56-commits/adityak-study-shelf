In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • A cooperative is a voluntary, jointly owned and democratically run group: 'Each for all and all for each'. The Ministry of Cooperation was created in 2021 ('Sahkar se Samriddhi').
  • The 97th Amendment (2011) gave constitutional status: the right to form societies in Art. 19(1)(c), the directive principle Art. 43-B, and Part IX-B (Arts. 243-ZH to 243-ZT).
  • State List Entry 32 covers cooperatives in one state (watched by the state Registrar). Union List Entry 44 covers multi-state societies, under the MSCS Act, 2002 (amended 2022: Co-operative Election Authority).
  • Board rules: at most 21 directors, 1 SC/ST seat and 2 women seats, a 5-year term and up to 2 co-opted experts who cannot vote in elections.
  • Supersession can last at most 6 months
  • Audit, the annual general body meeting and returns each fall due within 6 months of the financial year end
  • The National Cooperative Policy 2025 (replacing the 2002 policy) aims to triple the sector's share of GDP by 2034.

Key terms

1 · What a cooperative is, and why it matters#

  • A Cooperative is an autonomous association of persons united voluntarily to meet common economic, social and cultural needs through a jointly owned and democratically controlled enterprise.
  • Philosophy: "Each for all and all for each."
  • Identity: in 2021 the Government created the Ministry of Cooperation to realise "Sahkar se Samriddhi" (Prosperity through Cooperation).
  • As India moves towards a $5 trillion economy, cooperatives are meant to keep growth inclusive.

Rationale and objectives

ObjectiveWhat it means
Credit accessibilityLow-interest loans through PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Societies)
Market linkagesEliminate middlemen: "fair price" for producers, "affordable price" for consumers
Economic growthLinkages from local PACS to national level generate employment and strengthen the rural economy
InclusivityEmpower women, ST/SC and small-scale artisans
Democratic governanceOne member, one vote
  • In a country of small-holding farmers and fragmented producers, individual bargaining power is low
  • Cooperatives give the economies of scale to compete with large corporations
  • Memory aid (Five objectives): Credit, Market linkages, Economic growth, Inclusivity, Democratic governance: "Cooperatives Make Everyone's Income Dependable."

Historical evolution (the handout says "three distinct phases")

Timeline of the movement

  • 1904: First Cooperative Credit Societies Act, on the recommendation of the Sir Edward Law Committee
  • 1919: Cooperation became a Provincial Subject under the Montague-Chelmsford Reforms
  • Post-Independence: Cooperatives integrated into rural development strategy under the Five-Year Plans
  • White Revolution (1970s): Operation Flood, led by Dr. Verghese Kurien, made India the world's largest milk producer via the Amul model
  • 2011: 97th Constitutional Amendment gave constitutional status
  • 2021: Ministry of Cooperation created
Timeline of the movement

Union and State power

  • Union:
    • Parliament governs Multi-State Cooperative Societies (MSCS) operating in more than one state
    • MSCS Act, 2002 is the primary law
    • Amendment Act, 2022 introduced the Co-operative Election Authority to oversee board elections for transparency
  • State: State legislatures govern other cooperative societies through their own state acts
  • The Ministry of Cooperation (2021) gives a separate administrative, legal and policy framework, taking over these duties from the Ministry of Agriculture.
Current affairs · Aug 202697th Amendment and Part IXB97th Amendment added cooperatives to Article 19(1)(c), Article 43B and Part IXB. Rajendra N. Shah (2021) struck Part IXB for State cooperatives, it survives for multi-State ones.

3 · The 97th Amendment and Part IX-B#

  • The 97th Constitutional Amendment Act of 2011 is the "Magna Carta" of the sector. It made three changes:

Three critical changes

ChangeDetail
Fundamental RightAdded "cooperative societies" to Article 19(1)(c) (right to form)
Directive PrincipleInserted Article 43-B: State to promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning and professional management
New Part IX-BArticles 243-ZH to 243-ZT, titled "The Co-operative Societies", for democratic and economically sound functioning

Seventh Schedule entries

  • State List Entry 32:
    • Cooperatives confined to a single state
    • Supervised by the Registrar of Cooperative Societies (RCS) of the state
  • Union List Entry 44: Multi-State Cooperative Societies (MSCS) operating in more than one state
Current affairs · Aug 2026NCDC Amendment Act 2026NCDC may now lend directly to any cooperative, take equity in single-State cooperatives, share credit data with RBI and banks. Federal friction: cooperatives are State List Entry 32.

Key provisions of Part IX-B

ArticleSubjectProvision
243ZIIncorporationState legislatures make laws for incorporation, regulation and winding-up on the basis of voluntary formation, democratic control and autonomous functioning
243ZJBoard compositionMaximum 21 directors; functional directors are board members but excluded from the count of 21. Reservations: 1 seat for SC or ST; 2 seats for Women on every board having members from these categories. Term: 5 years from election for elected members and office bearers. Co-opted members: max 2 with expertise in banking, management or finance; they cannot vote in elections or be elected office bearers
243ZKElection controlSuperintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and elections vest in a body provided by the State Legislature; elections must be held before the current term expires
243ZLSupersession or suspensionMaximum 6 months; allowed for default, negligence, stalemate or failure to conduct elections. Restriction: a board cannot be superseded if there is no Government shareholding, loan, financial assistance or guarantee
243ZMAudit of accountsAudited at least once every financial year by an auditor or firm from a state-approved panel; audit completed within 6 months of year end
243ZNGeneral body meetingsAnnual general body meeting within 6 months of close of the financial year
243ZOMember rightsRight to access books, information and accounts; state provides for cooperative education and training
243ZPFiling of returnsWithin 6 months of year end: annual report, audited accounts, surplus disposal plan, list of by-law amendments
243ZQOffences and penaltiesFalse returns, disobeying summons, employers failing to pay deducted amounts, failing to hand over records, corrupt election practices
243ZRMulti-State societiesProvisions apply, but references to "State Legislature or Government" read as "Parliament or Central Government"
243ZSUnion TerritoriesProvisions apply unless the President directs otherwise by notification
243ZTContinuance of lawsInconsistent existing laws stayed in force for only one year after the 97th Amendment Act (2011) unless amended sooner
  • Memory aid (Three changes): Fundamental Right 19(1)(c), DPSP 43-B, Part IX-B: "Free Democratic Partnerships."
  • Shortcut: Numbers of Part IX-B. 21 directors, 5 years, 2 co-opted, 1 SC/ST seat, 2 women seats, 6 months for supersession, audit, AGM and returns. The only "one year" is 243ZT.
  • Shortcut: Article ladder. ZI incorporation, ZJ board, ZK election, ZL supersession, ZM audit, ZN general body, ZO member rights, ZP returns, ZQ offences, ZR multi-state, ZS Union Territories, ZT continuance of laws. The suffix letters run I to T in this order.
  • Memory aid (Six months): Supersession, Audit, AGM, Returns: "Six Accounts Are Reviewed": all four capped at six months.

6 · Challenges and way forward#

ChallengeDetail
Regional imbalancesGujarat and Maharashtra have robust ecosystems; large agrarian states like Uttar Pradesh and Bihar struggle with dormant or poorly managed societies
Trust and certification hurdlesFor NCOL: lack of affordable, widespread district-level testing labs makes it hard for small farmers to prove "organic" status to global buyers
Professional management gapMany PACS lack expertise for modern supply chains, digital accounting and international trade compliance
Competition with corporate giantsNational cooperatives now compete with private MNCs in seeds and FMCG; price competitiveness versus high farmer margins
Regulatory complexityCoordination between the Multi-State Cooperative Act (Union) and various State Acts is a bureaucratic challenge for nationwide scaling

Way forward (roadmap to 2030)

  • The National Cooperative Policy 2025 addresses these gaps through what the handout calls six strategic pillars.
  • "Bharat" branding: strengthen Bharat Organics and Bharat Beej brands for uniform identity and trust.
  • Digital integration (the "Cooperative Stack"):
    • A national digital database for real-time tracking of procurement and payments
    • Integration with ONDC so local societies sell directly to consumers globally
  • Expansion to new sectors: Renewable Energy (solar cooperatives), Health (cooperative pharmacies), Services (Sahkar Taxi aggregators).
  • Infrastructure creation:
    • Organic testing labs in every district
    • A decentralised grain storage network at PACS level to reduce post-harvest losses
  • Educational support: Tribhuvan Cooperative University to train professional managers.

From Lakshmikanth · Indian Polity, 8th ed. Ch 73, Co-operative Societies
  • Art. 43-B says the State shall endeavour to promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of cooperative societies.
  • Art. 243-ZI says the law should rest on four principles: voluntary formation, democratic member-control, member-economic participation and autonomous functioning. Art. 243-ZH holds the definitions: 'board' is the governing body and 'office bearer' includes president, vice-president, chairperson, secretary and treasurer.
  • Supersession grounds are five: persistent default, negligence, acts prejudicial to the society or members, stalemate in the board, and failure of the election body to hold elections. For cooperative banks other than multi-state ones the limit is one year. The administrator must hold elections within six months.
  • Audit: the auditor is appointed by the general body from a panel approved by the State Government, and the audit report of an apex cooperative society is laid before the State Legislature.
  • Returns also include a declaration about the date of the general body meeting and conduct of elections. An offence is an employer failing to pay a society the amount deducted from an employee within 14 days.
  • Why the amendment: elections were postponed indefinitely, administrators stayed in charge for long, and management was not professional. The 97th Amendment commenced on 15 February 2012 and states were asked to amend their Acts before 14 February 2013. Existing inconsistent laws continued only until amended or one year, whichever is less.
  • Rajendra N. Shah v. Union of India (Gujarat High Court, 2013):
    • Part IX-B was held ultra vires because the states had not ratified it under Art. 368
    • The changes to Art. 19(1)(c) and Art. 43-B were not affected
  • Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah (Supreme Court, 2021):
    • It upheld the High Court except that it did not strike down all of Part IX-B
    • Part IX-B is operative only for multi-state cooperative societies, in states and Union Territories
  • Lakshmikanth differs:
    • How far Part IX-B applies. The handout presents Part IX-B as general law for all cooperatives
    • Lakshmikanth reports the 2021 ruling limiting it to multi-state societies. For the exam give the handout's provisions and add this ruling

Traps#

Trap 1 · Entry 32 versus Entry 44 State List Entry 32 is for cooperatives within one state; Union List Entry 44 is for multi-state societies. Rule: one state = 32 (State List); many states = 44 (Union List).
Trap 2 · Counting the 21 directors The maximum of 21 does not count functional directors, and the co-opted members (at most 2) cannot vote in elections or become office bearers. Rule: 21 directors excludes functional directors; co-opted members have no vote in elections.
Trap 3 · Two things from 2002 The MSCS Act is from 2002. The National Cooperative Policy 2025 replaces the 2002 policy, a different document. Rule: MSCS Act 2002 is a law; the 2002 policy is the one replaced in 2025.
Trap 4 · When a board cannot be superseded A board cannot be superseded if there is no Government shareholding, loan, financial help or guarantee. Even otherwise the limit is 6 months. Rule: no government money = no supersession; at most 6 months.
