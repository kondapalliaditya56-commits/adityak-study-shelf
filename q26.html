2000PYQ 1(Prelims 2000)

Assertion [A] The basic weakness of the early nationalist movement lay in its narrow social base. Reason [R] If fought for the narrow interests of the social groups which joined it.

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — A is true: the record calls uneven representation and the total exclusion of non-elites "the most serious structural weakness of the early INC". R is false: although its MEMBERS were elites, the movement's demands went well beyond elite interests — decrease of Land Revenue, end of the Salt Tax, repeal of the Arms Act 1878, trial by jury. A true + R false = (c).

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Fails twice over: R is false — the demand list in (land-revenue cuts, salt-tax abolition, Arms Act repeal) served the common people, not merely the groups that joined — so R can neither be true nor "explain" A.

(B) ✗ — Also requires R to be TRUE — but the record demand list contradicts R: a movement fighting for lower land revenue and an end to the salt tax was not fighting only for "the narrow interests of the social groups which joined it."

(D) ✗ — Inverts the truth values: A is exactly what — a narrow, elite, Presidency-dominated social base — while R is the false statement.

PYQ 1 Mains — Moderates: Failure to Carry Conviction (See Chapter 'Weaknesses of

the Moderates and the INC' — Negatives of Moderate Methods)

Mains · 10 marks · 150 words "Why did the 'Moderates' fail to carry conviction with the nation about their proclaimed ideology and political goals by the end of the nineteenth century?"

Themes to bring out:

1. Limited political mobilisation — Moderates confined activity to educated middle class and professional elite; never reached peasants, workers, women.

2. Constitutional methods alone — petitions, memorials, prayers; British indifference exposed by 1892 (ICA1892's indirect-election fraud).

3. Narrow social base — anglicised vocabulary, English-medium meetings; alienated rural India.

4. Ideological vacuum — refused to demand independence; "Home Rule within the Empire" rang hollow after Bengal Famine 1900 & Partition 1905.

5. Failure of results — three decades of agitation produced ICA 1892 (a sham), Lord Curzon's Partition (1905), and the cultural humiliation of being called "microscopic minority" by Dufferin.

Closing line: by the late 1890s the next generation (Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai) had concluded Moderate constitutionalism could not deliver — Extremism + Swadeshi were the answer.

FYQ 1

Which two Western philosophical traditions formed the intellectual foundation of the Moderates in the INC?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(c) ✓ — Per, the Moderates were shaped by Utilitarianism (greatest good for the greatest number through law and good governance) and Liberalism (individual rights, rule of law, constitutional governance). Together these underwrote their faith in gradual constitutional reform.

Why others wrong: (a) Romanticism/Nationalism — not the intellectual foundation at issue; (b) Socialism/Marxism — never embraced; Moderates supported Capitalism and Industrialisation on British lines; (d) Conservatism/Mercantilism — opposite of the liberal-utilitarian framework they actually held.

Memory hook: Two L-shaped pillars hold up the Moderate roof — Liberalism left, utiLitarianism right.

FYQ 2

Who, as the first President of the INC, declared that the Congress was "loyal to the British and well-wishers of the British"?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(c) ✓ — Per, the first President of the INC, W.C. Bonnerjee, captured the Moderate stance by declaring the INC was "loyal to the British & well-wishers of the British."

Why others wrong: (a) Naoroji — Drain of Wealth theorist, not the source of this declaration;

(b) Banerjea — coined "Un-British Rule," a related but distinct phrase; (d) Gokhale — defended the Moderates in 1913 but did not author this opening declaration.

Memory hook: Bonnerjee opened the bonhomie — "loyal & well-wishers" was his line.

FYQ 3

Surendranath Banerjea's famous phrase "Un-British Rule" was meant to describe:

Answer: (C)

(C)

(c) ✓ — Per, "Un-British Rule" meant the gap between Britain's own stated liberal values and its actual conduct in India; Moderates wanted that gap removed, not British rule itself.

Why others wrong: (a) Indian rule copying British institutions — not the meaning; (b) Rule without Indian collaborators — chapter does not define the phrase this way; (d) Princely States — outside the scope of the phrase, which targeted British conduct in British India.

Memory hook: "Un-British" = Britain not living up to Britain's own brochure.

FYQ 4

Consider the following statements about the Moderates' goal: 1. Their ultimate vision was full independence from Britain. 2. They sought Self-Government on the model of Canada or Australia. 3. They believed the Indian masses were not yet ready for full self-rule. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — Per and, the Moderates' ultimate vision was Self-Government like Canada or Australia (dominion status), not independence; and they held that Indians, lacking widespread Western education, were not yet ready for full self-rule. Statement 1 is therefore wrong.

Why others wrong: (a) includes wrong Statement 1; (c) includes wrong Statement 1 and omits correct Statement 2; (d) includes wrong Statement 1.

Memory hook: Canada-Australia dream, not flag-independence scream.

FYQ 5

With reference to the Moderates' demand for Indianisation of services, consider the following statements: 1. They demanded that the Civil Services examination be held simultaneously in India and Britain. 2. They demanded that the upper age limit for the examination be raised to 23. 3. They demanded immediate appointment of Indians to officer rank in the military. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(d) ✓ — Per.4 lists all three: simultaneous Civil Services exam in India and Britain, raising the age limit to 23, and the demand for Indians at officer rank in the military (which was rejected as the British could not imagine taking orders from Indians).

Why others wrong: (a), (b), (c) each omit at least one of the three demands explicitly listed under Indianisation in.

Memory hook: Exam-here, age-23, officers-too — Moderates asked, British said no.

FYQ 6

Which of the following demands by the Moderates was framed as a "Right to Bear Arms for self-defence" rather than as a call to violence?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — Per.8, the Moderates demanded repeal of the Arms Act 1878 (which had disarmed ordinary Indians) and framed it as a Right to Bear Arms for self-defence, not as a call to violence.

Why others wrong: (a) Trial by Jury — concerned the right to be judged by peers, unrelated to bearing arms; (c) Volunteer Army — demand to open recruitment beyond "Martial Races", not a self-defence framing; (d) Assam indentured labour — labour-exploitation demand, no arms element.

Memory hook: Arms Act 1878 repealed — for self-defence, not war-call.

FYQ 7

Why did the Moderates demand the abolition of the India Council?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(c) ✓ — Per.1, the Moderates wanted abolition of the 15-member India Council sitting in London around the Secretary of State because lobbying one Secretary of State was easier than lobbying fifteen members of a Council.

Why others wrong: (a) Indian Princes — Council was a British advisory body, not princely-dominated; (b) Drain of revenue — this was not the abolition rationale; (d) Blocking Indianisation — not cited; the rationale was decision-making efficiency.

Memory hook: Lobby one, not lobby fifteen.

FYQ 8

Consider the following statements about the social composition of the INC in the period 1892-1905: 1. Hindus made up about 90% of members. 2. Muslims made up about 6.5% of members. 3. Brahmins alone constituted roughly 40% of Hindu membership. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(d) ✓ — Per, all three figures are stated verbatim: Hindus 90%, Muslims about 6.5%, Brahmins roughly 40% within the Hindu share.

Why others wrong: (a), (b), (c) each omit at least one of the three figures given for 1892-1905 composition.

Memory hook: 90-6.5-40 — the lopsided ledger of early INC.

FYQ 9

Who became the first Muslim President of the INC, at the Madras session of 1887?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(a) ✓ — Per, Bombay's Badruddin Tyabji became the first Muslim President of the INC at the Madras session of 1887, and was cited as a notable exception to otherwise low Muslim participation.

Why others wrong: (b) Syed Ahmed Khan — opposed the INC; (c) Aga Khan — associated with the 1906 Muslim deputation, not early INC presidency; (d) Jinnah — entered politics much later and was not president in 1887.

Memory hook: Tyabji of Bombay, Madras chair 1887 — the early Muslim exception.

FYQ 10

Why did the Moderates oppose the Factories Acts and the Indian Mines Act 1901?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — Per, the Moderates argued that British textile manufacturers in Lancashire were lobbying for factory regulations in India so that stricter rules would raise Indian production costs and reduce the competitiveness of Indian textiles, keeping India a supplier of raw materials rather than a manufacturing economy.

Why others wrong: (a) Coverage of Indian-owned factories — not the stated reason; (c) Trade union recognition — the Acts did not turn on this; (d) Working age vs household affordability — this was not the Moderate rationale.

Memory hook: Lancashire's lobby, not labour's welfare — that was the British game.

FYQ 12

Why did the Moderates support extending the Permanent Settlement of 1793 outside Bengal?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — Per.1, the Moderates wanted PS1793 extended outside Bengal because it prevented the State from raising Land Revenue and thus protected Zamindars (the class many INC members came from) from higher taxation.

Why others wrong: (a) Famine relief — opposite intent; PS1793 capped revenue, not raised it;

(c) Ryotwari to landlord conversion — not the stated logic; (d) Centralising revenue — PS1793 fixed revenue, the opposite of arbitrary central control.

Memory hook: Fix the rent, freeze the Zamindar's fear.

FYQ 15

Consider the following statements explaining why the Moderates' methods failed to produce results: 1. They were a narrow demographic, roughly 90% Hindu and drawn overwhelmingly from lawyer and landlord classes. 2. They distrusted the masses and refused to mobilise them, fearing popular agitation would spiral out of control. 3. The British government had legally banned petitions from political associations. Answer: (A)

(A)

(a) ✓ — Per, the two structural problems that reinforced each other were a narrow demographic (90% Hindu, lawyer/landlord-dominated) and a fundamental distrust of the masses (a refusal to mobilise them for fear of uncontrolled agitation).
