Exam lens#

Prelims hooks

  • Key names: Lord Cross (SoS) and Lansdowne 88-94 (Viceroy, 1888-1894); before him Lytton 76-80, Ripon 80-84, Dufferin 84-88 (teacher's board)
  • Nomination on the basis of recommendation, not election (the word 'election' avoided); two tiers: Local Bodies to PLC, then non-official PLC members and the Bengal Chamber of Commerce to ILC; the trap 'Municipalities recommended directly to the ILC' is wrong
  • ILC additional members 10 to 16 (1861: 6 to 12), at least half non-officials; official majority from 1861 to 1909; first non-officers' and elected majority in GOI Act 1919
  • VEC unchanged from 1874: 6 Ordinary Members (Finance added 1861 as C5, Public Works added 1874 as C6)
  • Powers: questions (first time, 6 days' notice, no discussion, no supplementaries); Budget (first time discussed, no vote, no amendment); no resolutions; ordinance power unchanged (6 months)
  • Federalism: Governor no longer needs the Viceroy's sanction to nominate non-officers to the PLC; Aitchison Commission (CCS to ICS, UCS to PCS) is NOT a provision of the Act; Swaraj at Calcutta 1906

Mains hooks

  • Why the Moderates failed to carry conviction by the end of the 19th century: ICA 1892 as proof (Chapter 26 Mains PYQ; year not in the bank)
  • Practice: examine the major themes of ICA 1892 (250 words) and its significance for the national movement (150 words)
  • Nomination not election; talk without a vote (TWR); the Act as the catalyst for Extremism

The British gave just enough to show they had listened and too little to share power; the hollow Act was the soil in which Extremism grew.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: The Moderates asked politely (3P) and got a shortlist, not an election: bigger councils, talk without a vote, an unchanged executive and an official majority, plus one small federal step. The hollow Act proved that petitions could not buy real power, so the Moderates raised their demand to Swaraj and the Drain Theory, and a new generation turned to the street.

  1. Seven years of polite asking. From 1885 to 1892 the Moderates used the 3P method (Prayer, Protest in Press, Petitions): carefully reasoned arguments for constitutional reform. The reply was the Indian Councils Act 1892, the Lord Cross Act, passed with Lord Cross as Secretary of State and Lansdowne 88-94 as Viceroy. It was a result of their struggle and proof of its failure.
    → But what did the Act actually give?
  2. A shortlist called an election. The most significant feature was indirectly nominated members in the ILC and the PLCs, the first time something like representative membership appeared. But the word 'election' was deliberately left out: the mechanism was nomination on the basis of recommendation. In the teacher's example, if 10 seats were to be filled, names of 50 were asked and the Viceroy or Governor chose 10.
    → So who sent the names, and to whom?
  3. Names climb the stairs. Tier 1: District Boards, Municipalities, Universities, Trade Associations and Zamindars sent names to the Governor, who nominated the non-official PLC members. Tier 2: the non-official PLC members and the Bengal Chamber of Commerce sent names to the Viceroy, who nominated the non-official ILC members. Gokhale and Pherozeshah Mehta were among those who served; Municipalities never recommended straight to the ILC.
    → Now, how big was the room?
  4. A bigger room, the same majority. The ILC's additional members rose to 10 to 16 (6 to 12 in 1861), and at least half had to be non-officials. But the VEC plus the official additional members always outnumbered the non-officials: like a board where the promoters always hold the vote, the independents may speak but cannot win.
    → And what about the inner cabinet?
  5. The inner cabinet stays put. The VEC did not change at all: GG + C-in-C + six Ordinary Members holding Home, Revenue/Agriculture, Law, Military, Finance (added 1861) and Public Works (added 1874). The ILC is the VEC plus the additional members: two boxes, one inside the other.
    → So what could the new members actually do?
  6. Ask and browse, never vote. For the first time members could ask questions (6 days' notice, no discussion, no supplementaries) and discuss the Budget (no vote, no amendment): Taxation without Representation, the 1776 slogan, with a faint echo. They could not bring resolutions or demand a vote on a government resolution, the government could legislate over them, the Viceroy could veto any Bill, and the 6-month ordinance power was unchanged.
    → Did the provinces gain anything?
  7. One small step for the provinces. The Governor no longer needed the Viceroy's approval to nominate non-officers to the PLC: the first relaxation of the Viceroy's grip since 1861. Rule-making powers, prior approval of certain bills, the single budget and the power to create provinces and appoint Lieutenant-Governors stayed as before.
    → Meanwhile a separate reform was happening in the services.
  8. A reform from the same years, but not the Act. On the Aitchison Commission's recommendations (1886-88), in Lansdowne 88-94's time, the Covenanted Civil Services became the ICS, the Uncovenanted Civil Services became the PCS, and the Statutory Civil Services were abolished. These were administrative orders, not provisions of ICA 1892: a classic exam trap.
    → So how did the Moderates do overall?
  9. The scorecard: one yes, six no. Indianisation of Civil Services and Military, a non-official majority, an elected majority, proper indirect election, powerful legislatures and constitutionalism were all refused. Only expansion of the ILC and PLCs scored a yes, and a dissatisfied one. The word 'election' did not make it into the text.
    → Disappointed, the Moderates changed the ask.
  10. Raising the ceiling. The Moderates began asking for self-government on the lines of Australia (1901) and Canada (1867): the Dominion model. Swaraj, self-government within the Empire (Home Rule), became the INC goal at Calcutta in 1906. They also developed the Drain Theory: the political demand and the economic critique became the twin pillars of their politics.
    → But the method itself was in doubt.
  11. The soil for Extremism. If prayers and petitions could produce only this hollow Act (a Budget talk with no vote, questions with no follow-up, no elections, no non-official majority), the method was bankrupt. Belief in British benevolence cracked; street protest and mass politics took root, with the Bengal Partition of 1905 as the catalyst.

In simple words#

  • ICA 1892 (the Lord Cross Act; Viceroy Lansdowne 88-94) was the Moderates' reward for years of 3P (Prayer, Protest in Press, Petitions): a result of their struggle and proof of its failure.
  • Members came by nomination on the basis of recommendation, not election: bodies sent lists of names and the Governor or Viceroy made the final pick, in two tiers (Local Bodies to PLC; PLC members and the Bengal Chamber of Commerce to ILC).
  • The ILC grew to 10 to 16 additional members, but the official majority was guaranteed and the VEC (GG + C-in-C + 6 Ordinary Members) did not change.
  • Indians got questions and Budget discussion for the first time, but no follow-up, no vote, no resolutions
  • the Governor no longer needed the Viceroy's sanction for his nominations: one small federal step
  • The Aitchison civil service changes (CCS to ICS, UCS to PCS) were a separate order, not part of the Act
  • The scorecard reads 1 yes, 6 no.
  • The Moderates raised the demand to Dominion-style Swaraj (Calcutta 1906) and the Drain Theory
  • the failure of petitions opened the way for Extremism.

2 · The "indirect election" that was not called an election (handout Sec. 3)#

Story link

Because the British wanted to look generous without sharing power, they invented 'nomination on recommendation'. Ask: why is a list from which someone else picks not an election?

  • The most significant feature of ICA 1892: indirectly nominated members in the ILC (Imperial Legislative Council) and the PLCs (Provincial Legislative Councils).
  • This was the first time something resembling representative membership appeared in these councils.
  • But nominated, not elected. The word "election" was deliberately omitted from the Act.
  • The legal mechanism was strictly "nomination on the basis of recommendation." It was selection, not election.
  • Notable Indians who served under this framework: Gokhale and Pherozeshah Mehta, names that would dominate Moderate politics for the next two decades.
Teacher's board
  • 1861: the first GRAIN of popular character. 1892: SOME FORM of representative membership.
  • One step up the ladder (Chapter 25: 1861 non-officers, 1909 proper indirect election, 1919 direct election).
Teacher's board
  • Proper election = the choice of the voters is FINAL.
  • In ICA 1892 "Final" was missing.
  • Example:
    • 10 seats to fill
    • The bodies send a list of 50 names
    • The Viceroy or Governor picks 10 from the 50 = nominated.
  • In a 100% proper indirect election the chain would be: people elect local body members, local body members elect the Legislators of the province from among themselves, and the Legislators elect the next level from among themselves.
  • In a proper election the choice of voters is final.
  • Under ICA 1892 neither condition held: the recommending bodies only sent names, and the final pick was the Viceroy's or Governor's.
  • The "election" was really a shortlist.
Teacher's board
  • Proper indirect election = the elected representative is not directly chosen by the people.
  • Today's example:
    • People (direct) elect MLAs
    • MLAs elect Rajya Sabha members (indirect).
  • The 1892 chain: People, Local Bodies [Direct], PLCs [indirect] (Provinces), ILC [indirect] (Centre), with "elect" at each step.
Teacher's board
  • Local Bodies are four kinds: Urban LBs (Municipalities), Rural LBs (Panchayats), Business Bodies, Others.
  • District Boards = the rural local body (Panchayats).
  • Business Bodies = Trade Associations and the Bengal Chamber of Commerce.

2.1 How it worked: two levels (handout Sec. 3.1 and 3.2)

  • For the PLCs: the Governors nominated non-official members on the recommendation of District Boards, Municipalities, Universities, Trade Associations and Zamindars.
  • For the ILC: the Viceroy nominated non-officials on the recommendation of the non-official members of the PLCs and the Bengal Chamber of Commerce, not directly from local bodies.

The two tiers of "nomination on recommendation"

  • People elect (directly) the Local Bodies (Panchayats, Municipalities, Business Bodies, Others)
  • Tier 1: District Boards, Municipalities, Universities, Trade Associations and Zamindars send a list of names to the Governor
  • Governor nominates some from the list: the non-official PLC members
  • Tier 2: the non-official PLC members + the Bengal Chamber of Commerce send a list of names to the Viceroy
  • Viceroy nominates some from the lists: the non-official ILC members
  • Choice of voters not final at both tiers: the lists only recommend
Teacher's board
  • PLC non-official members = "[* Politicians]" who then recommend the ILC non-officials.
  • The Viceroy or Governor nominates only some names from the list: "Choice of voter not Final" (at both PLC and ILC levels).
Handout alert
  • Exam Trap. "ILC members under ICA 1892 were directly recommended by Municipalities" is WRONG.
  • Municipalities fed the PLCs
  • The PLCs fed the ILC. Two separate tiers.
  • Direct recommendation to the ILC came only from the PLCs (their non-official members) and the Bengal Chamber of Commerce.

4 · The Viceroy's Executive Council: no change (handout Sec. 5)#

Story link

Because the legislature grew, the executive had to be kept fixed. Ask: which box (VEC or ILC) changed in 1892?

  • Whenever a question mentions the Indian Councils Acts (1861, 1892, 1909), split the government into two boxes: Box A, the Executive Council (VEC): the inner cabinet = GG + C-in-C + Ordinary Members. Box B, the Legislative Council (ILC): Box A plus the "Additional Members" (a mix of officials and non-officials).

  • Half of these must be non-officials.
  • Box A: "[Law itself says they will be member]" (Ordinary Members are members by law).
  • The C-in-C was "sent by CoD" and is the "extra-ordinary member" (Chapter 25: appointed by the Court of Directors).

5 · What the members could do (handout Sec. 6)#

Story link

Because the room had new members, ask what they could do with the floor. Ask: which two powers were firsts, and which two were refused?

Teacher's board
  • Gained for the first time (starred): Questions and Budget.
  • Not gained: Resolutions and Bills ("[Official Majority]"; "Non officers = inconsequential").
  • Beside Questions: "C1 to C6" with a "not equal" sign.

5.1 Questions

  • For the first time, non-official members could ask questions to the executive (the VEC), a genuine new right.
  • But with strict conditions: 6 days' prior notice was required, there could be no discussion of the answers, and no supplementary questions were allowed. You could ask. You could not follow up.

5.2 Resolutions

  • Opposition members (the non-official, non-officer members, since the executive under the Viceroy was made up entirely of officers) could not bring any resolution before the council, nor demand a vote on a government resolution.
  • Resolution = a formally declared intent of policy: a motion that the council endorses a direction.
Got and did not get: ICA 1892

5.3 Bills and legislation

  • The government could make laws without the ILC or PLCs.
  • The official majority meant that on every bill, Indian non-official members were outvoted.
  • Their presence was advisory, not decisive: the legislative function was recommendatory, not mandatory.
  • The Viceroy could veto any Bill.
  • Ordinance power: no change. It remained as under ICA 1861: a 6-month emergency instrument.
  • The council was recommendatory because of the official majority and the Viceroy's absolute veto, not because of any new bypass mechanism.

5.4 Budget

  • The Budget could be discussed for the first time, another genuine new right.
  • But members could neither vote on it nor amend it.
  • "Taxation without Representation" (TWR) was the slogan that sparked the American Revolution (1776).
  • Here Indians had a faint echo of representation: they could watch the Budget and speak to it, with zero power to change it.

6 · Federalism: one small step (handout Sec. 7)#

Story link

Because the Centre kept every key, the one small change stands out. Ask: which of BRASS's keys was handed back?

  • ICA 1892 made one notable change in Centre-Province relations: the Governor no longer needed the Viceroy's approval to nominate Non-Officers to the PLC.
  • Until then (ICA 1861), even though Governors recommended names for their own Provincial Councils, the Viceroy's sanction was required. ICA 1892 ended this requirement.
  • A small but genuine decentralisation: the first relaxation of the Viceroy's iron grip over provincial council composition since 1861.
  • All other controls stayed exactly as under ICA 1861:
    • Rule-making powers
    • Prior approval for certain bills
    • A single unified budget with no separate provincial budget
    • The power to create new provinces and to appoint Lieutenant-Governors.
Teacher's board
  • A feature is federal if it shifts power from the Centre to the Provinces.
  • Scale:
    • Unitary = Centre 80% or more, Provinces 20% or less
    • Federal = about 50:50
    • Confederal = Centre 20% or less, Provinces 80% or more.
  • "No mention of ppl in Topic of Federalism":
    • People belong to Democracy
    • federalism is the law about Centre and Province
  • So the Governor's freedom from the Viceroy's sanction counts as a small federal step.

8 · A separate administrative reform of the same era (handout Sec. 9)#

Story link

Because 1892 also saw civil service renaming, the exam sets a trap. Ask: was any of this written in the Councils Act?

Handout alert
  • Classic exam trap: keep these separate from the Act.
  • Under Lansdowne 88-94, on the Aitchison Commission (1886-88) recommendations, these civil-services changes were made as administrative orders, not provisions of ICA 1892.
  • If listed under "provisions of ICA 1892": not a provision.
  • Covenanted Civil Services (CCS) renamed ICS.
  • Uncovenanted Civil Services (UCS) renamed Provincial Civil Services (PCS).
  • Statutory Civil Services (created 1878): abolished.
  • The "federal logic": Centre and province now had their own civil service cadre (ICS at the Centre, PCS in the Province).

10 · What the Moderates did next (handout Sec. 11)#

Story link

Because the Act disappointed them, the Moderates changed the ask. Ask: how do Dominion status, Swaraj and the Drain Theory fit together?

  • Disappointed by ICA 1892, the Moderates raised their ceiling of demand.
  • They began asking for Self-Government on the lines of Australia and Canada: the Dominion model, full self-governance within the British Empire without breaking from it.
  • Their argument:
    • Australia got self-government as a Dominion in 1901
    • Canada in 1867. Both were majority white-settler colonies.
  • If those colonies could govern themselves, why not a civilisation far older and more complex than either?
  • This officially became the goal of the INC at the 1906 Calcutta session, named Swaraj: Self-Government within the Empire, also called Home Rule.
  • Alongside, the Moderates developed and propagated the Drain Theory: the economic argument that British rule was systematically extracting India's wealth to Britain.
  • Political demand for Swaraj and economic critique through the Drain Theory became the twin pillars of Moderate politics in the years that followed.
Teacher's board
  • The Dominion demand (Australia and Canada) and Swaraj (1906 Calcutta) are the same idea.
  • Dominion status = "Self-Government within Br Empire" = Swaraj = Home Rule.
  • (Chapter 27: the Drain Theory in detail.)

11 · Legacy: the catalyst for Extremism (handout Sec. 12)#

Story link

Because the Moderates had played by every rule and still got a hollow Act, the method broke. Ask: what grew in the soil the Act left?

  • ICA 1892 matters not only for what it contained, but for what it proved.
  • Six demands, and one partially met.
  • The Moderates had played by every British rule, used every permissible channel and mustered every rational argument.
  • The Act they received was hollow: expanded councils with no real power, a budget discussion with no vote, questions with no follow-up.
  • This was the moment the belief in British benevolence began to crack.
  • If "prayers and petitions" could produce only this, the method itself was bankrupt.
  • The disillusionment seeded by ICA 1892 was the soil in which Extremism would take root in the decade that followed.

Recall sheet#

Read the question, answer in your head, then tap to check.

Who are the key names of ICA 1892?Lord Cross (SoS) and Lansdowne 88-94 (Viceroy and GG, 1888 to 1894); hence the Lord Cross Act.
Which Viceroys came just before Lansdowne 88-94 (the teacher's board)?Lytton 76-80, Ripon 80-84, Dufferin 84-88 (L-R-D-L: Little Rabbits Dig Lawns).
Why is ICA 1892 called both a result and a proof of failure?It resulted from the Moderates' 1885-92 struggle, but gave just enough to show the British had listened and too little to concede power: proof that 3P failed.
What is 3P?The teacher's label for the Moderates' method: Prayer, Protest in Press, Petitions (carefully reasoned arguments for constitutional reform).
What was the most significant feature of ICA 1892?Indirectly nominated members in the ILC and PLCs: the first time some form of representative membership; nominated, not elected.
Which word did the Act deliberately avoid?"Election." The mechanism was "nomination on the basis of recommendation": selection, not election.
What is a proper election, according to the teacher?One where the choice of the voters is final. In 1892 "final" was missing: e.g. 50 names asked for 10 seats and the Viceroy or Governor chose 10.
Which bodies recommended names to the Governor for the PLCs?District Boards, Municipalities, Universities, Trade Associations and Zamindars.
Which bodies recommended names to the Viceroy for the ILC?The non-official members of the PLCs and the Bengal Chamber of Commerce, not local bodies directly.
Trap: did Municipalities recommend directly to the ILC?No. Municipalities fed the PLCs; the PLCs fed the ILC (two tiers, L-P-I).
Which prominent Moderates served on the councils under this framework?Gokhale and Pherozeshah Mehta.
What kinds of local bodies did the teacher list?Urban (Municipalities), Rural (Panchayats), Business Bodies, Others.
How many additional members did the ILC have?10 to 16 (1861: 6 to 12), at least half non-officials (1861: 3 to 6).
Why was the official majority guaranteed?VEC plus the official additional members always outnumbered the non-official additional members; the Viceroy also held the veto.
What is Box A and Box B?Box A = the Executive Council (VEC) = GG + C-in-C + Ordinary Members; Box B = the Legislative Council (ILC) = VEC + additional members.
What were the six portfolios of the Ordinary Members?Home, Revenue/Agriculture, Law, Military, Finance (1861, C5), Public Works (1874, C6). Mnemonic: Harsh Rulers Love Money, Fund Projects.
Teacher's ladder of the VEC?Pre-1861: GG + CIC + C1 to C4; 1861: C1 to C5; 1874: C1 to C6; 1892: no change.
What were the conditions on questions?First time allowed; 6 days' notice; no discussion of the answers; no supplementary questions.
What could the opposition not do about resolutions?Bring any resolution before the ILC, or demand a vote on a Government resolution. (Resolution = a formally declared intent of policy.)
What was the ILC's role in legislation?Advisory/recommendatory, not decisive; the government could legislate without the non-officials; the Viceroy could veto any Bill.
What happened to the ordinance power?No change: the 6-month emergency ordinance of ICA 1861.
What new right on the Budget, and its limit?First time it could be discussed; members could neither vote on nor amend it (Taxation without Representation).
Who used the slogan Taxation without Representation, and when?It sparked the American Revolution (1776); in 1892 Indians had only a faint echo of representation.
What federal change did ICA 1892 make?The Governor no longer needed the Viceroy's approval to nominate non-officers to the PLC (BRASS loses one S).
What stayed unchanged in Centre-Province relations?Rule-making powers, prior approval for certain bills, the single unified budget, the power to create new provinces and appoint Lt Governors.
Teacher's federal scale?Unitary: Centre 80% or more; federal: about 50:50; confederal: Centre 20% or less. A federal feature shifts power from Centre to Provinces.
Which Aitchison Commission changes fell in this era, and are they in the Act?Aitchison 1886-88: CCS to ICS, UCS to PCS, Statutory CS abolished. They were administrative orders, not provisions of ICA 1892.
What does the scorecard show?Indianisation no; non-official majority no; elected majority no; proper indirect election no; expansion yes (dissatisfactory); powerful legislatures no; constitutionalism no: 1 yes, 6 no.
What did the Moderates demand after ICA 1892?Dominion-style Self-Government on the lines of Australia (1901) and Canada (1867); Swaraj = Self-Government within the Empire = Home Rule, the INC goal at Calcutta 1906.
What was the second pillar of Moderate politics after 1892?The Drain Theory: British rule systematically extracted India's wealth to Britain. Swaraj and the Drain were the twin pillars.
Why is ICA 1892 a catalyst for Extremism?It showed prayers and petitions yielding only a hollow Act (no vote, no follow-up, no elections, no non-official majority); faith in British benevolence cracked and street politics followed, with Partition 1905 as the spark.
What did the teacher tie to "some form" and "GRAIN"?1861 = the first grain of popular character; 1892 = some form of representative membership; one step up the ladder to 1909 (proper indirect) and 1919 (direct).
Mains (Chapter 26): Why did the Moderates fail to carry conviction by the end of the 19th century? Skeleton?Intro: Moderates used 3P and a narrow base. Body: weak claim of representing Indians, no trust in the masses, weak demands, weak methods; ICA 1892 as proof (a hollow Act, no election, no vote). Closing: the method was bankrupt, the soil for Extremism.
