In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Delimitation means redrawing the boundaries of Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies after each Census so that each seat has roughly equal population: one person, one vote, one value.
  • The Delimitation Commission is statutory, not constitutional: Parliament passes a Delimitation Act (1952, 1962, 1972, 2002) and the Commission is formed under it. Four commissions so far.
  • Its orders, once notified by the President, have the force of law and cannot be challenged in any court
  • They are laid before Parliament only for information
  • The 84th Amendment (2001) froze the number of seats on the 1971 Census till the first Census after 2026. The Fourth Commission (2002, Justice Kuldip Singh) used the 2001 Census and its orders took effect in 2008
  • The 2009 elections used them for 499 of 543 seats
  • A 2020 Commission (Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai) finally worked only on Jammu and Kashmir, reporting in May 2022: 90 Assembly seats, 9 ST seats for the first time and 7 SC seats.

1 · Meaning and rationale#

  • Delimitation is the act of redrawing boundaries of parliamentary and legislative assembly constituencies so that citizens have fair and equal representation. It keeps constituencies in line with population changes recorded in each Census. The task lies with an independent, high-powered statutory body, the Delimitation Commission, constituted under successive Delimitation Acts of Parliament. Once the President publishes its orders they have the force of law and cannot be challenged in any court.
  • Constitutional ideal: One person, one vote, one value: each elected representative speaks for an approximately equal number of citizens.

Rationale for delimitation

  • 1. Ensures electoral equality: parity in the value of each citizen's vote by equalising constituency populations. 2. Reflects demographic shifts: adjusts for migration, urbanisation and population growth. 3. Guarantees fair representation: prevents over- or under-representation of regions. 4. Strengthens democracy: periodic revision builds public trust. 5. Prevents gerrymandering: independent and non-partisan, so boundary drawing stays neutral.
  • Memory aid (Five reasons): Equal vote, Demographic shifts, Fair representation, Strengthens democracy, Prevents gerrymandering: "Every District Fights Some Politics".

2 · Constitutional framework#

  • Delimitation is guided by Articles 81, 82, 170, 330 and 332.

Articles

ArticleSubject
Article 81Fixes representation of States in the Lok Sabha based on population
Article 82Readjustment of constituencies after each Census through a Parliamentary Act
Article 170Delimitation for State Legislative Assemblies
Article 330Reservation of seats for SCs and STs (Lok Sabha)
Article 332Reservation of seats for SCs and STs (State Assemblies)
  • Memory aid (Articles): 81 = Lok Sabha seats, 82 = Readjustment, 170 = Assemblies, 330 and 332 = SC/ST reservation: "Let Real Articles Speak".

84th Amendment Act, 2001

  • Froze the total number of Lok Sabha and Assembly seats (based on the 1971 Census) till the first Census after 2026.
  • The 84th and 87th Amendments froze seat numbers on the 1971 Census but allowed reallocation and redrawing on 2001 data.
  • Shortcut: Article shortcut. 81 = Lok Sabha seats. 82 = readjustment by Act. 170 = Assemblies. 330 and 332 = SC/ST reservation (LS and Assemblies).

5 · Functions, principles and procedure#

  • 1. Redraw boundaries: demarcate parliamentary and assembly constituencies on the latest Census for balanced representation. 2. Reservation for SCs and STs: decide reserved constituencies under Articles 330 and 332 in proportion to their population in each State. 3. Population parity: nearly equal population across constituencies within a State ("one person, one vote, one value"). 4. Administrative integrity: avoid arbitrary division of districts, tehsils or panchayats and keep natural administrative boundaries. 5. Transparency and consultation: draft proposals published in the Gazette and local newspapers, inviting objections and suggestions before finalising. 6. Coordination with the Election Commission for implementing final orders.

Guiding principles

PrincipleExplanation
Equal representationNearly equal population per constituency, so uniform vote value
Geographical contiguityCompact and continuous, no disconnected territories
Administrative convenienceDistrict and block boundaries kept intact as far as practicable
Physical and natural featuresTerrain, rivers, transport links and population density considered
Public participationCitizens, local bodies and parties may object and suggest during hearings
Reservation paritySC and ST seats proportionate to demographic presence per Census
  • Memory aid (Six principles): Equal representation, Geographical contiguity, Administrative convenience, Physical features, Public participation, Reservation parity: "Even Good Advice Pushes People's Rights".

Working procedure

How a Commission works

  • Initiation: Begins after the Census and enactment of a new Delimitation Act
  • Data compilation: Census data and administrative maps collected
  • Drafting of proposals: Preliminary constituency outlines prepared and made public
  • Public consultation: Hearings on suggestions from citizens and parties
  • Finalisation and notification: Final orders in the Gazette, notified by the President, with force of law

6 · Implementation and the Fourth Commission (2002)#

Legal effect

  • Once notified by the President the orders have force of law and cannot be modified by Parliament, State Legislatures or any court.
  • Under the Delimitation Act the decisions are final and non-justiciable, ensuring independence from political influence.
  • Orders are laid before Parliament and State Legislatures for information only, not approval. They take effect from the date in the Presidential Notification.

Implementation of the Fourth Commission

AspectDetails
ChairpersonJustice Kuldip Singh (Retd.), Supreme Court
Report submitted2007
Implementation notified2008

  • Memory aid (Fourth Commission dates): Report 2007, Notified 2008, First poll 2009: "Run Now, Finish".

Major outcomes of the 2008 implementation

  • Equalised representation: balanced populations, reducing rural-urban variation.
  • Urban representation enhanced: rapidly growing regions like Delhi, Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu gained fairer representation.
  • Administrative coherence: boundaries aligned better with district and tehsil borders.
  • Reservation adjustments: SC and ST seats re-identified on 2001 Census data.
  • Electoral legitimacy: uniform vote value upholding political equality.

Monitoring and coordination

  • Election Commission: implements new constituency maps during electoral roll revision and elections.
  • State governments: logistical readiness, updating administrative divisions, field support.
  • Central oversight: Ministry of Law and Justice oversees the process and notifies clarifications or corrections.

Limitations and deferral

  • 1. The 2002 Commission's recommendations were not applied to certain North-Eastern States owing to internal disturbances and demographic sensitivities. 2. The 2020 Commission was set up to address the pending States and Jammu and Kashmir (after the Reorganisation Act, 2019).

  • A Delimitation Commission is also called a Boundary Commission. The Supreme Court in R.C. Poudyal v. Union of India (1993) noted that votes in a less populated constituency are worth more than votes in a more populated one.
  • The orders come into force on a date fixed by the President and are laid before the Lok Sabha and the State Legislative Assembly concerned, but they cannot be modified.
  • Effect of the 84th (2001) and 87th (2003) Amendments:
    • The number of Lok Sabha and Assembly seats stays on the 1971 Census till the first Census after 2026
    • SC and ST seats are reworked on the 2001 Census
    • Each state is redelimited on 2001 data and the new boundaries stay frozen till the first Census after 2026
    • Each constituency should have about the same population within a state
  • The Fourth Commission's task covered all states except the erstwhile state of Jammu and Kashmir. The Delimitation Act, 2002 was amended in 2003, 2008 and 2016.
  • In 2008 the Government deferred delimitation in four North-Eastern states and cancelled the final order for Jharkhand. In February 2020 the Government rescinded the 2008 deferral notifications after the security situation improved, and set up the 2020 Commission in March 2020.
  • Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai was first appointed for one year or till further orders. The term was extended one year (till March 2022) and then by two months (till May 2022). The final J&K order is dated 5 May 2022
  • The report is called 'Delimited Landscape of Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir'
  • Before 2019, J&K Assembly seats were delimited under the J&K Constitution in 1957, 1966, 1975 and 1995 (the last on the 1981 Census). The old Assembly had 111 seats including 4 in Ladakh, with 24 reserved for Pakistan-occupied J&K.
  • The J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019 fixed 114 directly elected seats, 24 of them to remain vacant for Pakistan-occupied J&K, giving 90 usable seats (a gain of 7). It allows the Lieutenant Governor to nominate 2 women if they are under-represented. The 2022 exercise used the 2011 Census.
  • Persons displaced from Pakistan-occupied J&K are those who left in 1947-48, 1965 and 1971. The Commission suggested at least two Kashmiri migrant members (one a woman) with powers like those of nominated members in the Puducherry Assembly, and nomination of representatives of PoJK displaced persons.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Statutory, not constitutional The Commission is formed under Delimitation Acts, not under a constitutional article, although Arts. 81, 82, 170, 330 and 332 guide the process. Rule: Act of Parliament creates it; Articles only frame the work.
Trap 2 · Year of Commission versus year of Act The commissions were set up in 1952, 1963, 1973 and 2002, but the Acts are 1952, 1962, 1972 and 2002. Rule: second and third commissions come a year after their Acts (1962 Act, 1963 commission; 1972 Act, 1973 commission).
Trap 3 · Seats frozen, but boundaries redrawn The 84th and 87th Amendments kept the number of seats on the 1971 Census, yet boundaries and SC/ST reservation were redone on the 2001 Census. Rule: number of seats = 1971 base; boundaries and reservation = 2001 data.
Trap 4 · Which states were left out in 2008 The Fourth Commission's orders did not apply to Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Nagaland and Jharkhand; so 499 of 543 Lok Sabha seats were redrawn for 2009. Rule: five states skipped; 2020 Commission was to cover the four North-Eastern states and J&K, but the four were removed in March 2021.
