1 · Meaning and rationale#

Delimitation is the act of redrawing boundaries of parliamentary and legislative assembly constituencies so that citizens have fair and equal representation. It keeps constituencies in line with population changes recorded in each Census. The task lies with an independent, high-powered statutory body, the Delimitation Commission, constituted under successive Delimitation Acts of Parliament. Once the President publishes its orders they have the force of law and cannot be challenged in any court.

Constitutional ideal

One person, one vote, one value: each elected representative speaks for an approximately equal number of citizens.

Rationale for delimitation

1. Ensures electoral equality: parity in the value of each citizen's vote by equalising constituency populations. 2. Reflects demographic shifts: adjusts for migration, urbanisation and population growth. 3. Guarantees fair representation: prevents over- or under-representation of regions. 4. Strengthens democracy: periodic revision builds public trust. 5. Prevents gerrymandering: independent and non-partisan, so boundary drawing stays neutral.

Five reasons
EDFSP

Equal vote, Demographic shifts, Fair representation, Strengthens democracy, Prevents gerrymandering: "Every District Fights Some Politics".

2 · Constitutional framework#

Delimitation is guided by Articles 81, 82, 170, 330 and 332.

Articles
ArticleSubject
Article 81Fixes representation of States in the Lok Sabha based on population
Article 82Readjustment of constituencies after each Census through a Parliamentary Act
Article 170Delimitation for State Legislative Assemblies
Article 330Reservation of seats for SCs and STs (Lok Sabha)
Article 332Reservation of seats for SCs and STs (State Assemblies)
Articles
LRAS

81 = Lok Sabha seats, 82 = Readjustment, 170 = Assemblies, 330 and 332 = SC/ST reservation: "Let Real Articles Speak".

84th Amendment Act, 2001

  • Froze the total number of Lok Sabha and Assembly seats (based on the 1971 Census) till the first Census after 2026.
  • The 84th and 87th Amendments froze seat numbers on the 1971 Census but allowed reallocation and redrawing on 2001 data.
Article shortcut

81 = Lok Sabha seats. 82 = readjustment by Act. 170 = Assemblies. 330 and 332 = SC/ST reservation (LS and Assemblies).

Handout alert

The 3rd Commission table says seat allocation was fixed till the 2001 Census "(later extended till 2026)", while the 84th Amendment text says seats are frozen till the first Census after 2026. Treat both as the same freeze extended by the 84th Amendment.

5 · Functions, principles and procedure#

Functions

1. Redraw boundaries: demarcate parliamentary and assembly constituencies on the latest Census for balanced representation. 2. Reservation for SCs and STs: decide reserved constituencies under Articles 330 and 332 in proportion to their population in each State. 3. Population parity: nearly equal population across constituencies within a State ("one person, one vote, one value"). 4. Administrative integrity: avoid arbitrary division of districts, tehsils or panchayats and keep natural administrative boundaries. 5. Transparency and consultation: draft proposals published in the Gazette and local newspapers, inviting objections and suggestions before finalising. 6. Coordination with the Election Commission for implementing final orders.

Guiding principles
PrincipleExplanation
Equal representationNearly equal population per constituency, so uniform vote value
Geographical contiguityCompact and continuous, no disconnected territories
Administrative convenienceDistrict and block boundaries kept intact as far as practicable
Physical and natural featuresTerrain, rivers, transport links and population density considered
Public participationCitizens, local bodies and parties may object and suggest during hearings
Reservation paritySC and ST seats proportionate to demographic presence per Census

EGAPPR

Equal representation, Geographical contiguity, Administrative convenience, Physical features, Public participation, Reservation parity: "Even Good Advice Pushes People's Rights".

6 · Implementation and the Fourth Commission (2002)#

Legal effect

  • Once notified by the President the orders have force of law and cannot be modified by Parliament, State Legislatures or any court.
  • Under the Delimitation Act the decisions are final and non-justiciable, ensuring independence from political influence.
  • Orders are laid before Parliament and State Legislatures for information only, not approval. They take effect from the date in the Presidential Notification.

Fourth Commission dates
RNF

Report 2007, Notified 2008, First poll 2009: "Run Now, Finish".

Major outcomes of the 2008 implementation

  • Equalised representation: balanced populations, reducing rural-urban variation.
  • Urban representation enhanced: rapidly growing regions like Delhi, Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu gained fairer representation.
  • Administrative coherence: boundaries aligned better with district and tehsil borders.
  • Reservation adjustments: SC and ST seats re-identified on 2001 Census data.
  • Electoral legitimacy: uniform vote value upholding political equality.

Monitoring and coordination

  • Election Commission: implements new constituency maps during electoral roll revision and elections.
  • State governments: logistical readiness, updating administrative divisions, field support.
  • Central oversight: Ministry of Law and Justice oversees the process and notifies clarifications or corrections.

Limitations and deferral

1. The 2002 Commission's recommendations were not applied to certain North-Eastern States owing to internal disturbances and demographic sensitivities. 2. The 2020 Commission was set up to address the pending States and Jammu and Kashmir (after the Reorganisation Act, 2019).

Handout alert

Excluded States are five (Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Nagaland, Jharkhand) in the Fourth Commission section, but the 2020 Commission section lists only four North-Eastern States (Jharkhand is not named). Use the five-State list for the 2008 exclusion and the four-State list for the 2020 Commission's original brief.

Recommendations on representation

1. Kashmiri migrants: nomination of at least two members (one woman) from displaced Kashmiri Pandits to the Legislative Assembly, with parity with nominated members of the Puducherry Assembly in rights and privileges. 2. Displaced persons from PoJK: special nomination of representatives to the J&K Assembly for political participation.

Handout alert

The table speaks of "2 nominated women (if women are under-represented)", while the representation text recommends at least two nominated Kashmiri migrant members (one woman) and special nomination of PoJK displaced persons. The handout does not reconcile these. Use the table for the "2 nominated women" figure and the text for the migrant and PoJK nomination proposals.

Implications

  • Democratic reconfiguration: electoral readiness for the first Assembly election in the UT since reorganisation in 2019.
  • Greater representation for Jammu: addresses regional imbalance between Jammu and Kashmir.
  • Inclusion of ST communities: first-ever legislative representation for Gujjar-Bakarwal and Pahari tribal groups.
  • Administrative preparedness: lets the Election Commission begin voter list revision and poll preparations on the new boundaries.
