In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) is a constitutional body under Article 338-A, inserted by the 89th Amendment Act, 2003 into Part XVI (Special Provisions Relating to Certain Classes).
  • Its history: combined SC-ST Commission (1990), Ministry of Tribal Affairs (1999), 89th Amendment (2003), separate NCST formed (2004).
  • Chairperson has the rank of a Union Cabinet Minister, the Vice-Chairperson of a Minister of State, and Members of a Secretary to the Government of India. Term: three years.
  • It takes part in planning for ST development, evaluates and monitors safeguards, inquires into complaints, recommends corrective steps and sends an annual report to the President.
  • Under Art. 338A(8) it has civil-court powers for investigation and inquiry, but it is not a court: the Supreme Court (1996) held it cannot grant injunctions.
  • The handout also lists ST safeguards (Arts. 15(4), 46, 350A, 23, 24, 244, 275, 330, 332, 334, 371), protective laws and the Commission's main recommendations (funds, land, R&R, Forest Rights Act, certificates).

Key terms

1 · What the NCST is and its legal foundation#

  • The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) is a constitutional institution set up to safeguard the interests of Scheduled Tribes. It is not an administrative body created by executive order: it is established directly by the Constitution, which gives it a permanent and independent place in India's institutions for social justice.
  • Legal foundation: Article 338A of the Constitution, which provides for a dedicated national body for the safeguards, welfare and development of STs.
  • Location: Part XVI, titled "Special Provisions Relating to Certain Classes".
  • Inserted by: the 89th Constitutional Amendment Act of 2003.
  • Effect: because it is anchored in the Constitution, Union and State governments must engage with it as part of the formal policy and oversight system for tribal welfare.
  • Key point: Constitutional status means a permanent, independent body, not one created by executive order.
  • Memory aid (Nature of the body): Constitutional, Permanent, Independent: "Commission Protects Indigenous peoples". Pair it with the numbers: Art. 338A, Part XVI, 89th Amendment, 2003.

3 · Role and mandate#

  • The handout's list of roles begins at item "8" (items 1-7 are not in the text), so only the two roles below are given.

Participation in the planning process

  • The NCST actively participates in the planning process for the socio-economic development of STs.
  • It collaborates with both Union and State governments so that policies and programmes fit the needs of tribal communities.

Evaluation and monitoring

  • It evaluates the effectiveness of safeguards given to STs by the Constitution, existing laws and government directives.
  • It monitors the progress of ST development at the national and state levels.

Four specific functions

  • Investigate and advise: On matters of ST welfare
  • Inquire: Into specific complaints about ST rights and safeguards
  • Recommend: Corrective measures to address violations
  • Report: Present annual reports to the President of India

  • Memory aid (Four functions): Investigate and advise, Inquire into complaints, Recommend corrective measures, Report to the President: "I I Really Report".

5 · Evolution of a separate commission#

From combined commission to NCST

  • 1990: Combined Commission (NCSCST) established under Article 338
  • 1999: Ministry of Tribal Affairs created
  • 2003:
    • 89th Constitutional Amendment Act bifurcates the NCSCST into two constitutional bodies: NCSC and NCST
    • Inserts Art. 338A defining the structure, powers and functions of NCST
  • 2004: Separate National Commission for Scheduled Tribes formed

9 · Report of the Commission#

Where the report goes

  • Commission:
    • Submits an annual report to the President
    • May also present special reports whenever it considers necessary
  • President: Causes all reports to be laid before Parliament with a memorandum of action taken on its recommendations
  • If not accepted: The memorandum must state the reasons for non-acceptance

State matters

  • President: Forwards the report on a State matter to the Governor of that State
  • Governor: Places it before the State Legislature with a memorandum on action taken, including reasons for non-acceptance
  • Memory aid (Report chain):
    • Commission, President, Parliament: "Commission Passes to President, who places before Parliament"
    • For States the middle link is the Governor

10 · Powers of the NCST and the Supreme Court's limit#

  • The powers derive from Clause (8) of Article 338A. While investigating a matter under Clause (5)(a) or inquiring into a complaint under Clause (5)(b), the NCST has all the powers of a civil court trying a suit. These are procedural powers meant to make investigations and inquiries thorough.

Powers of a civil court

PowerMeaning
Summoning and enforcing attendanceSummon any person from any part of India and examine on oath
Discovery and production of documentsDemand records or documents needed for an inquiry
Evidence on affidavitsAccept sworn affidavits as evidence
Requisitioning public recordsRequest any public record or copy from any court or office
Issuing commissionsFor examining witnesses and documents
Other mattersAny additional power the President prescribes through rules
  • Nature of the body: The NCST works as a quasi-judicial body in investigations and inquiries. It is not a court of law, so it cannot grant injunctions or interim orders.

The Supreme Court ruling

How the limit was settled

  • Dispute: The erstwhile NCSCST directed Indian Overseas Bank to halt a promotion process pending inquiry
  • 1993: The Delhi High Court set the order aside
  • 1996: The Supreme Court upheld that decision in All India Indian Overseas Bank SC & ST Employees' Welfare Association vs. Indian Overseas Bank and Others
  • 1998: DoPT communicated the judgment's implications to all Ministries and Departments to ensure uniform understanding
  • The procedural powers are only for investigation and inquiry
  • They do not confer the status or powers of a civil court
  • No power to grant injunctions, temporary or permanent.
  • Powers are limited to summoning, collecting evidence, examining witnesses and requisitioning documents.
  • Memory aid (Six powers): Summon, Documents, Affidavits, Records, Issue commissions, Other (by President's rules): "Six Different Actions Reveal Issues Openly".
  • Shortcut: Court shortcut. Civil-court powers to find facts, not to order anything: no injunctions. Years: Delhi HC 1993, SC 1996, DoPT 1998.

Handout alert: The handout refers to the powers as Article 338A(8) for the NCST but speaks of "Article 338(8)" when describing the Supreme Court case on the old combined NCSCST. For the NCST answer Art. 338A(8); Art. 338 was the old combined commission's provision.

From Lakshmikanth · Indian Polity, 8th ed. Ch 49, National Commission for STs
  • Tenure under the President's Rules (2004): three years, and not more than two terms. Members are appointed by warrant under the President's hand and seal, and the Commission has a chairperson, a vice-chairperson and three other Members.
  • Why a separate body: STs are geographically and culturally different from SCs and their problems differ. The Ministry of Tribal Affairs (1999) was meant to coordinate all ST work, as the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment could not do so administratively.
  • The 65th Amendment Act (1990) came into force on 12 March 1992
  • The 89th Amendment Act (2003) came into force on 19 February 2004
  • In 2005 the President specified eight other functions:
    • Ownership of minor forest produce
    • Rights over mineral and water resources
    • Livelihood and development strategies
    • Relief and rehabilitation of those displaced by projects
    • Preventing land alienation and rehabilitating those already affected
  • The 2005 list also includes:
    • Cooperation of tribal communities in protecting forests and social afforestation
    • Full implementation of the PESA Act, 1996
    • And reducing and ending shifting cultivation that disempowers tribals and degrades land
  • The ST Commission has the same six statutory functions and six civil-court powers as the SC Commission, and the Centre and States must consult it on major policy matters affecting STs.
  • Rules behind the Commission: Conditions of Service and Tenure Rules, 2004, and the Specification of Other Functions Rules, 2005.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Ranks of the three offices The Chairperson is at Union Cabinet Minister rank, the Vice-Chairperson at Minister of State rank, and Members at Secretary to the Government of India rank. Rule: Cabinet, then MoS, then Secretary: rank falls as the post goes down.
Trap 2 · Powers of a civil court, but not a court Art. 338A(8) gives civil-court powers only to investigate and inquire. In the Indian Overseas Bank case (1996) the Supreme Court held the Commission cannot grant temporary or permanent injunctions. Rule: it can summon, collect evidence, examine and requisition; it cannot stop a promotion or give interim orders.
Trap 3 · Fifth versus Sixth Schedule Art. 244(1) applies the Fifth Schedule to Scheduled Areas and STs in all States except Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram and Tripura, which fall under the Sixth Schedule under Art. 244(2). Rule: four north-eastern States (Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Tripura) = Sixth; the rest = Fifth.
Trap 4 · Dates of the split The combined Commission came in 1990, the Ministry of Tribal Affairs in 1999, the 89th Amendment in 2003, and the separate NCST only in 2004. Rule: Act year 2003, working body 2004.
