2002PYQ 1(Prelims 2002)

Which one of the following Acts of British India strengthened the Viceroy's authority over his executive council by substituting 'portfolio' or departmental system for corporate functioning?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — The ICA 1861 gave legal recognition to the Portfolio System introduced by Canning56-62 in 1859 — the practice of allocating specific departments... to individual VEC members, whose decisions on their portfolios would be final, subject to the Viceroy's veto. This formalised cabinet-style administration at the centre.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — The Government of India Act 1858's subject was the transfer of rule from the Company to the Crown — it reorganised control in London, not the working of the executive council in India; the portfolio system entered the law only with ICA 1861.

(C) ✗ — ICA 1892's contribution lay on the LEGISLATIVE side — gradually growing the council's outer ring — not in executive working: the portfolio system had already been legally recognised three decades earlier, by ICA 1861. +.

(D) ✗ — ICA 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms) again expanded the legislative outer ring with marginal new powers — by then the portfolio system had been in legal force for nearly half a century (ICA 1861, formalising Canning56-62's 1859 practice). +. (Note: The Indian Councils Act, 1861 during the Viceroyalty of Lord Canning strengthened the viceroy authority over his executive council by substituting 'portfolio' or departmental system for corporate functioning.)

FYQ 1

**Stem**: The phrase "despotism controlled from home" — used to describe the operating principle of the Government of India under British rule — is most closely associated with which of the following?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — The Secretary of State for India, Charles Wood, described the Government of India as a "despotism controlled from home." This one line captured the fundamental feature of British Indian governance, and remained the operating principle right until 1947. Why others wrong:

(a) ✗ — Lord Canning (Canning56-62) was Viceroy when ICA 1861 was passed and is associated with the Portfolio System (1859), not with this phrase. (c) ✗ — Lord Dalhousie's name does not appear in in this connection. (d) ✗ — James Wilson is mentioned in as the first Finance Member (1859), not as the author of this phrase.

Memory hook: Wood spoke; the "Home" was London; the "despot" was the Secretary of State himself.

FYQ 2

**Stem**: With reference to the restructure carried out by the Indian Councils Act, 1861, consider the following statements: 1. The Act created two separate, independent institutions — a Viceroy's Executive Council and an Imperial Legislative Council. 2. Every Viceroy's Executive Council (VEC) member was automatically part of the Imperial Legislative Council (ILC). 3. The Commander-in-Chief was an extraordinary member of the VEC and therefore also sat in the ILC. 4. The expanded-council model created by the Act continued until the Government of India Act, 1919. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — - Statement 2 is correct — "Every VEC member was automatically part of the ILC." -

Statement 3 is correct — the Commander-in-Chief (C-I-C), already an extraordinary member HISTORY (Indian Councils Act, 1861 (ICA 1861)) of the VEC, by virtue of his military office, thus also sat in the ILC. - Statement 4 is correct — the record states the arrangement continued until 1919 (the Government of India Act 1919 / Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms); the 1909 Morley-Minto Reforms only added marginal new powers to the outer ring, not true separation. Why others wrong: - Statement 1 is incorrect — the record is explicit: the ICA 1861 did not create two separate, independent institutions. Memory hook: One council, two hats — executive hat (VEC) and legislative hat (ILC). The hats changed; the council did not.

FYQ 3

Memory hook: Letter "E" in VEC and GEC = Executive; letter "L" in ILC and PLC = Legislative.
