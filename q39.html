FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words The revolutionaries of 1924-31 were not simply continuators of the 1907-15 wave but represented a distinct ideological generation. Examine.

Demand of the question: "Examine" + "distinct ideological generation" demands a process-oriented contrast. The body must show what was new in the 1920s wave that the 1907-15 wave lacked.

Suggested intro (~25 words): The 1907-15 wave (Aurobindo, Anushilan, Yugantar) was rooted in religious-spiritual nationalism. The 1924-31 wave (HRA → HSRA) replaced that anchor with socialist analysis of class & State. Body guidance — 4 themes (see 'Why Did Revolutionary Activities Revive after 1922?' and 'Hindustan Socialist Republican Army / Association (HSRA 1928)' and 'Ideology of Bhagat Singh and HSRA1928'):

1. Generational shift. The 1924-31 wave was younger, educated post-1917 (post-Russian-Revolution era), & disillusioned by Gandhi's withdrawal of NCM after Chauri Chaura. The 1907-15 wave had pre-Russian-Revolution intellectual tools only.

2. Ideological substitution. The 1907-15 wave used religious-cultural nationalism (Aurobindo's Bhawani Mandir, Anushilan's worship-and-violence model). The 1924-31 wave replaced religion with class analysis — Bhagat Singh's Why I Am An Atheist is the formal statement.

3. HRA → HSRA — the structural rename. Adding 'Socialist' was not cosmetic. 'Hindustan Socialist Republican Army / Association (HSRA 1928)' anchors the September 1928 Feroz Shah Kotla meeting where Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev & Azad explicitly tied independence to anti-class-exploitation goals.

4. The CLA Bomb as theatre, not assassination. '1929 Bomb in CLA by Bhagat Singh & B.K. Dutt' anchors the deliberate choice — empty assembly zone, court arrest, use the trial as platform. The 1907-15 wave assassinated to remove individuals; the 1928-29 wave used arrest to broadcast ideology.

Closing verdict: The post-1922 wave shared the older wave's commitment to armed struggle but rebuilt its ideological foundation. Religion-anchored individual heroism became class-anchored systematic struggle. Bhagat Singh's hanging in 1931 made this synthesis a permanent feature of Indian political memory.

Revise: 'Revival' + 'HSRA Founding' + 'CLA Bomb' + 'Ideology' + 'Jail'.

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words Examine the significance of the Chittagong Armory Raid (1930-33) for the revolutionary tradition in India.

Demand of the question: "Examine the significance" demands more than a narrative — show what Chittagong added to the revolutionary repertoire that earlier episodes had not.

Suggested intro (~25 words): The Chittagong Armory Raid was the most ambitious revolutionary action in Indian colonial history — an attempt not just to assassinate but to capture territory & declare a republic. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Chittagong Armory Raid 1930–33'):

1. The scale of ambition. Unlike Kakori (a robbery) or the CLA bomb (a courtroom-theatre device), Chittagong attempted to seize the British armory, cut telegraph & rail lines, & declare a temporary Indian Republic. This was a republican insurrection model, not a sabotage operation.

2. Women in the revolutionary front. Pritilata Waddedar led the Pahartali European Club raid in 1932 & died by cyanide rather than be captured. Kalpana Datta was also part of the inner group. The Chittagong wave normalised women in armed revolutionary leadership.

3. The leadership profile. Surya Sen was a schoolteacher; his group included students & teenagers. The action proved that organised revolutionary insurrection could be planned by ordinary local cadres without national-level political networks. Sen was hanged in January

1934.

Closing verdict: Chittagong extended the revolutionary repertoire from sabotage to insurrection, from male-only to mixed-gender, & from elite networks to local cadres. The model remained unique in Indian history — no other revolutionary attempt approached its territorial ambition.

Revise: 'Chittagong Armory Raid'.
