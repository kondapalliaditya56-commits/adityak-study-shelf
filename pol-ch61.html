1 · The ombudsman idea and the Indian journey#

Lokpal is an ombudsman, a watchdog of administration.

Where ombudsmen began
PlaceDetail
SwedenFirst in the world, 1809
UKParliamentary commissioner, set up 1967
USAIn some states (Hawaii, Oregon, Nebraska) but not at the federal level

First ARC proposal

A two-tier set-up: Lokpal and Lokayuktas.

  • Lokpal: officials of Secretary rank and above, at both Union and State levels.
  • Union Lokayukta: officials below Secretary rank at the Union level.
  • State Lokayuktas: similar cases within their states.

The Indian timeline

1968
First Lokpal bill introduced
1971
Maharashtra is first state to appoint a Lokayukta
2013
Present Lokpal Bill passed
2019
Lokpal constituted: Justice PC Ghosh as Chairperson of the nine-member body

2 · Experience at the state level#

The handout calls the state experience not satisfactory:

  • Flooded with complaints: the apex body's performance depends on how well lower-level departments handle petty complaints.
  • Poor effectiveness: powers not adequate to investigate and prosecute properly.
  • Inter-state differences in qualifications, tenure, functions, powers, suo-moto powers; no separate investigation agency.
  • Survival at the state's will: created by state governments and sometimes abolished: Odisha 1992 by ordinance, Haryana 1999.
  • Paper tigers: mostly recommendatory.
  • Corruption in Lokayuktas: allegations of extortion.
  • Setup to intimidate political opponents: powers misused against opponents.
State failures
FPISPCS

Flooded, Poor effectiveness, Inter-state differences, Survival at state's will, Paper tigers, Corruption within, Set-up to intimidate: "Five Pink Iguanas Sing Poor Choir Songs".

3 · Lokpal Act, 2013: structure#

Composition

A nine-member body including the Chairperson.

Who can sit
SeatQualification
ChairpersonIs or has been CJI or a judge of the SC, or an eminent person with 25 years of experience in vigilance, anti-corruption, law, finance, administration and management
8 membersHalf judicial, half non-judicial
Judicial membersA judge of the SC or a Chief Justice of a High Court
Non-judicial membersEminent person with 25 years of experience in the same fields
Affirmative action50% for SC, ST, OBC, minorities and women

Other conditions (members must not be)

  • An MP, MLA or MLC.
  • A member of any local body.
  • Under 45 years of age.
  • Removed or dismissed from government service.
  • Holder of any other office of profit, doing business or engaged in a profession.
  • Affiliated with any political party.
Disqualifiers
MLAROP

MP/MLA/MLC, Local body member, Age below 45, Removed from service, Office of profit or business, Political party: "My Lord, All Rules Obey Principles".

Appointment

Made under the name and seal of the President on the recommendation of a selection committee:

  • Prime Minister (chairperson of the committee).
  • Speaker of Lok Sabha.
  • Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha.
  • CJI or a SC judge nominated by him.
  • An eminent jurist selected by the first four.
Handout alert

The handout notes that the Act does not say who replaces the Leader of Opposition if no LoP is recognised in Lok Sabha. Treat that as a gap in the law, not as a stated rule. The selection committee may appoint a 7-member search committee to shortlist candidates (also with 50% affirmative action for SC, ST, OBC, minorities and women). The selection committee can go beyond the names the search committee recommends.

Selection committee
PSLCE

PM, Speaker, LoP, CJI or nominee, Eminent jurist: "People Search Leaders, Choose Experts".

Tenure, salary, post-retirement

Terms
PointRule
Tenure5 years or maximum age 70
ReappointmentNot allowed, except a member elevated to Chairperson, if the total tenure as member and chairperson does not exceed the statutory limit of 5 years
SalaryChairperson equals the CJI; other members equal a SC judge. Service terms cannot be altered to disadvantage while in office
After retirementDisabilities similar to the CVC, plus ineligible to contest any election in India for 5 years

Organisation

  • Inquiry wing headed by the Director of Inquiry.
  • Prosecution wing headed by the Director of Prosecution.
  • A Secretary of Lokpal.
  • Appointed from a panel of names recommended by the Government of India.

4 · Should the Prime Minister be covered?#

2nd ARC opposed it

  • Would cripple administration while investigation goes on
  • foreign powers may exploit it to malign the PM
  • the PM is already covered under the PCA 1988

Arguments against that view

  • The CBI works under DoPT and so under the PM's direct control, which hampers investigation
  • the PM should lead by example (some states include the Chief Minister under their Lokayukta)
  • under Article 361 only the President and Governors are exempt, with no legal or constitutional exemption for the PM

What the Act does: partial coverage

  • Lokpal shall not investigate any PM decision relating to public order, internal and external security, atomic energy or space.
  • The full bench of the Lokpal with a two-thirds majority must approve starting an inquiry or investigation against the PM.
PM cover

Partly covered: four excluded subjects (order, security, atom, space) plus a two-thirds full-bench vote. Remember the letters O S A S.

5 · Jurisdiction, procedure and powers#

Scope

Lokpal can inquire into allegations against the Prime Minister, Union Ministers, MPs, Group A to D officials, and functionaries of boards, corporations, societies and trusts funded by the government (receiving more than Rs 10 lakh annually). Any individual can complain about offences under the PCA 1988.

Procedure

Complaint
Any individual, for PCA 1988 offences
Preliminary inquiry
Ordered through the Inquiry Wing, CVC or agencies like the CBI; Lokpal first seeks the public servant's explanation to establish a prima facie case; complaints against Group C and D officials go to the CVC; must finish in 60 days
After the report
Lokpal reviews it and hears the public servant
Outcomes
Order a full investigation, initiate departmental proceedings, close the case, or proceed against the complainant if allegations are false
Investigation and prosecution
Agencies report to Lokpal and the court; a Lokpal Bench (minimum 3 members) decides on sanction to prosecute, departmental action or closure

Powers

  • Sanction shift: Lokpal replaces the appointing or dismissing authority in granting sanction under Section 197 of CrPC and Section 19 of PCA.
  • Powers of search, seizure, asset attachment and of a civil court (Civil Procedure Code) for inquiries.
  • Superintendence over the CBI for cases referred by the Lokpal.

Other provisions (the handout's notes)

  • Annual declaration of assets and liabilities of public servants.
  • Annual Report presented to the President, who tables it in Parliament; it must carry a memorandum explaining why Lokpal's recommendations were rejected.
  • Limitation period of 7 years for filing a complaint.
Four outcomes
IDCP

Investigate fully, Departmental proceedings, Close, Proceed against false complainant: "I Declare Case Proved".

Handout alert

The scope line says Lokpal covers Group A to D officials, while the procedure says complaints against Groups C and D are referred to the CVC. Read it as: Lokpal has jurisdiction on paper, but C and D complaints are sent to the CVC.

6 · Removal of Lokpal members#

Two routes
GroundProcess
MisbehaviourReference to the Supreme Court, only if signed by at least 100 Members of Parliament
Other groundsThe President can dismiss on the advice of the Council of Ministers for insolvency, private employment, infirmity of body or mind, or acquiring prejudicial financial interest
Removal numbers

SC reference + 100 MPs for misbehaviour. Other grounds: President, on advice of the Council of Ministers.

7 · Issues, functioning since 2019 and the 2nd ARC#

Issues

  • Jurisdiction too wide.
  • Convoluted appointment process.
  • PM only partially covered.
  • 7-year limitation period.
  • Wide scope can lead to intimidation of NGOs.
  • Delays in appointments.

Functioning since 2019

  • Allegations of emasculating the body through legislation.
  • Government delayed rules prescribing the form for filing complaints and rules on disclosure of assets.
  • By 2020 the Prosecution and Investigation Wings were not set up.
  • Rules to be made by Lokpal for preliminary inquiry and investigation also delayed.
  • Status: by September 2019 it disposed of 1000 of 1065 complaints. Complaints kept falling: 1427 in 2019-20, 110 in 2020-21, and just 12 till August in 2021-22.
Handout alert

The figures appear to use different bases: "1000 of 1065" (by September 2019) and "1427 cases in 2019-20" are not reconciled in the handout. Quote them as given and stress the trend: a sharp fall (1427, 110, 12).

2nd ARC recommendations

  • Give Lokpal constitutional status (from statutory), for both the Rashtriya Lokayukta (Union) and State Lokayukta.
  • Rashtriya Lokayukta: Union Ministers, MPs and State Chief Ministers. State Lokayukta: State Ministers and state legislators. CVC: civil servants.
  • A Local Bodies Ombudsman for both political and administrative officials.

Lokpal is not a panacea

  • Corruption is a hydra-headed monster that needs a multi-pronged strategy.
  • The size of the bureaucracy is huge, so one body cannot handle all complaints.
  • Efficiency in handling petty complaints must be looked into, since Lokpal also receives them.
  • Inadequacies in the judicial process.
  • No ombudsman, citizen empowerment or willingness to complain as in developed nations, preventing a multi-pronged strategy.
Not a panacea
HSPJN

Hydra-headed, Size of bureaucracy, Petty complaints, Judicial inadequacy, No citizen empowerment: "Hydras Smile, Probing Just Numbers".

8 · Whistleblower Protection Act, 2014#

Officially the Public Interest Disclosure and Protection of Individuals making Disclosure (PIDPI) Act, 2014.

Key rules
PointRule
WhoAnyone can be a whistleblower about any public body or agency
Disclosure toPM for Union Ministers, Chief Minister for State Ministers, Presiding Officer for legislators, CVC for civil servants
Anonymous complaintsNot allowed
ProtectionCVC can issue directions for whistleblower protection
Frivolous complaintsPenalised with 2 years' jail
2015 amendment billLapsed later; proposed that the Official Secrets Act, 1923 override the Whistleblowers' Act

Challenges

  • Lack of anonymity: identity must be disclosed, exposing the whistleblower; the murder of Satyendra Dubey (who reported corruption in the NHAI) shows the danger.
  • Weak organisational implementation: unclear guidelines and awareness make employees reluctant.
  • Inadequate legal safeguards against victimisation, career setbacks, harassment and physical harm.
  • Rising complaints show latent potential: in 2018 NSE-listed companies received over 3,500 complaints.

Measures needed (2nd ARC and Law Commission)

  • Stronger law protecting genuine whistleblowers in public and private sectors.
  • Adopt the Law Commission's framework: confidentiality and anonymity, protection against victimisation, safeguards against harassment and bodily harm.
  • Extend cover to corporate whistleblowers exposing fraud or serious harm to public interest.
  • SEBI's Tipping Mechanism is a progressive step: rewards up to Rs 1 crore for actionable information against insider trading, with cooperation and confidentiality protection.
Disclosure to whom
PCPC

PM (Union Ministers), CM (State Ministers), Presiding officer (legislators), CVC (civil servants): "Pay Careful Public Complaints".

9 · Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 and the 2018 Amendment#

Who is a public servant

The Act covers all public servants:

  • Anyone in the pay or service of the government.
  • Public Sector Undertaking employees.
  • Local body functionaries.
  • Judges.
  • Office bearers of the government-funded cooperative sector.
  • Members of the Public Service Commission.
  • Employees of Central and State Universities.
  • Employees of Deemed Universities by an Apex Court decision of 2020.
  • Anyone holding an office that requires a public duty, including MPs and MLAs.

Prevention of Corruption (Amendment) Act, 2018

What changed
ChangeDetail
Giving a bribeCategorically an offence. In coercive corruption, if the person complains within 7 days, he is not guilty
Section 17AExtended to retired civil servants: sanction of the appropriate authority is required to act against them
Section 13(1)(d) dilutedOriginally a public servant could be prosecuted for a decision benefiting a commercial organisation and violating public interest; this is diluted
Jail termIncreased from 3 to 7 years
TrialCompleted in 2 years, up to a maximum of 4 years
Commercial organisationsProvisions for prosecution of commercial activities
PropertyProvisions for attachment or confiscation
2018 changes
BSDJTCP

Bribe-giving offence, Sec 17A, Diluted 13(1)(d), Jail 3 to 7, Trial 2 to 4 years, Commercial organisations, Property attachment: "Big Stick, Dull Just Too Complicated, Please".

Handout alert

In the chapter on the Problem of Corruption, Section 17A is described as needing permission for preliminary inquiry for all public servants except those caught red-handed. Here the point is that 17A is extended to retired civil servants. Both are in the handout: 17A covers serving servants and, by this amendment, retired ones too.
