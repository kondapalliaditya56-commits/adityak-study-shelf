In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The Union Executive sits in Part V, Chapter I (Arts. 52-78): the President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers headed by the PM, and the Attorney-General.
  • The President is the nominal head. He is elected indirectly by an Electoral College of elected MPs and elected MLAs (STV, secret ballot)
  • Real power lies with the Council of Ministers (Art. 74)
  • The President has executive, legislative, financial, judicial, diplomatic and military powers, including three vetoes, ordinances (Art. 123) and pardon (Art. 72), all used on ministers' advice.
  • The Vice-President is elected by MPs only, chairs the Rajya Sabha and acts as President for at most six months.
  • The PM is the real executive. The Council is capped at 15% of the Lok Sabha (91st Am.) and is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. Cabinet committees, the Cabinet Secretariat and the PMO support them.

Key terms

Electoral College
Elected MPs plus elected MLAs (including Delhi and Puducherry); no nominated members, no MLCs
Value of vote
MLA vote depends on State population (1971 Census) and number of elected MLAs; every MP's vote is equal
Absolute, suspensive, pocket veto
Withhold assent; return a non-Money Bill; take no action at all (no time limit)
Ordinance (Art. 123)
Law made when Parliament is not in session; lapses six weeks after reassembly unless approved
Pardon (Art. 72)
Clemency for Union-law offences, court martial and death sentences; five forms
Collective responsibility
Whole Council answers together to the Lok Sabha (Art. 75(3)); swims or sinks together
Cabinet Secretariat
Principal staff agency of the Cabinet and its committees, working under the PM
PMO
PM's own office: PMS 1947, renamed PMO 1977; the residual legatee

1 · Structure of the Union Executive and the President's status#

  • The Union Executive implements laws and runs day-to-day administration
  • The Legislature makes laws and the Judiciary interprets them. It sits in Part V, Chapter I (Articles 52 to 78)

The four parts

  • The President
  • The Vice-President
  • The Council of Ministers, headed by the Prime Minister
  • The Attorney-General of India
  • The President holds the highest constitutional position and is head of the State
  • All executive actions of the Union are taken in his name
  • Memory aid (Four parts): President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers (with PM), Attorney-General: "Polity Veterans Can Answer".

  • The real executive power lies with the Council of Ministers headed by the PM (Art. 74). This is the Parliamentary form: the President acts on aid and advice

2 · Election of the President (Articles 54, 55, 71)#

  • The President is not elected directly. An Electoral College (Art. 54) elects him, so he represents both the Union and the States.

Electoral College of the President

  • Included:
    • Elected members of both Houses of Parliament
    • Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of States, including Delhi and Puducherry (added by the 70th Amendment Act, 1992)
  • Excluded:
    • Nominated members of Parliament and of State legislatures (no democratic mandate)
    • Members of Legislative Councils (only directly elected representatives vote)
    • Members of a dissolved Assembly (they cease to be elected representatives and lose the vote)
  • The 104th Amendment ended nomination of the Anglo-Indian community to the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. The handout gives the year 2020 for this.

Method of voting and counting

How the election is run

  • Voting:
    • Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (STV), secret ballot, under the Election Commission of India
    • Voters mark preferences 1, 2, 3
  • First count: If no candidate has a majority of valid votes, the lowest candidate is eliminated and the votes are transferred by next preferences
  • Declaration: When a candidate gets more than 50% of valid votes the Returning Officer declares the result
  • Returning Officer: For the President's election: the Secretary General of Lok Sabha and of Rajya Sabha, by rotation (for Lok Sabha and Assembly elections it is generally the District Collector or Magistrate)

Disputes (Article 71)

  • The Supreme Court decides all questions or disputes about the election
  • Its decision is final
  • If the election is declared void, all acts of the President before the judgment remain valid.
  • A petition can be filed only by the losing candidate or by 20 or more electors together.
  • 39th Amendment 1975 changed the forum to an authority created by Parliament
  • 44th Amendment 1978 restored the Supreme Court
  • The election cannot be challenged merely because some Assemblies are dissolved or seats are vacant (PYQ 2023: election cannot be postponed on that ground).
  • Memory aid (Value of vote):

    • Every MP equal
    • Parity of Union and States: "Many Electors Participate"
  • Shortcut: Prelims shortcut. Nominated members and MLCs never vote for the President (but nominated MPs do vote for the Vice-President). 1971 Census for MLA vote value. Supreme Court for disputes (Art. 71).

  • Memory aid (Four tests): Citizen of India, Age 35 completed, Lok Sabha-qualified, No office of profit (exempt: President, Vice-President, Governor, Minister): "Clever Aspirants Learn Notes".

Conditions of office (Article 59)

  • Cannot be a member of Parliament or of any State Legislature
  • If elected he is deemed to have vacated the seat from the date he enters office
  • Cannot hold any other office of profit.
  • Rent-free official residences
  • Emoluments, allowances and privileges set by Parliament by law (until then, as in the Second Schedule)
  • Emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during the term.
  • Immunities:
    • Personal immunity for official acts
    • no criminal proceedings during the term, even for personal acts
    • Cannot be arrested or imprisoned
    • civil proceedings for personal acts only after two months' notice

Oath or affirmation (Article 60)

  • Taken before the Chief Justice of India (or, in his absence, the senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court).
  • Anyone acting as President or discharging the President's functions takes the same oath.
  • Memory aid (Oath):
    • Faithfully execute the office
    • Preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law
    • Devote himself to the service and well-being of the people: "Fine Plans Delivered". Loyalty is to the Constitution, not a party or government

4 · Term, vacancy and impeachment (Articles 56, 61, 62)#

  • Five years from the day he takes office
  • May resign by writing to the Vice-President
  • Or be removed by impeachment
  • Continues until his successor takes charge
  • no limit on re-election (the USA limits to two terms)

Who acts when the post is vacant

  • Vice-President: Acts as President until a new one takes office
  • If VP post is also vacant: Chief Justice of India, or in his absence the senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court
  • Within six months:

7 · Veto power of the President#

  • A Bill becomes law only after assent. The President may assent, withhold assent, or return a non-Money Bill. India has three vetoes
  • The qualified veto of the USA does not exist in India

The three vetoes

  • Absolute veto: President withholds assent: the Bill dies. Used for Private Members' Bills or when the Cabinet that introduced the Bill has lost power. Cases: PEPSU Appropriation Bill 1954 (first use) and MPs' Salary and Pension (Amendment) Bill 1991 (R. Venkataraman returned it unsigned)
  • Suspensive veto: President returns a non-Money Bill. If Parliament passes it again (with or without changes) he must assent. Case: Kalam 2006, Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Amendment Bill (Office of Profit Bill)
  • Pocket veto: No action at all: the Constitution fixes no time limit, so the Bill can be kept pending indefinitely. Case: Zail Singh 1986, Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill (interception of private mail on security grounds)
  • No qualified veto:
    • The legislature cannot override the executive by a special majority
    • A withheld Bill is dead unless introduced afresh

8 · Ordinance-making power (Article 123)#

  • The President can promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session
  • They have the same force as an Act of Parliament but are temporary. Unlike Emergency powers, this works in normal times. Ambedkar justified it as a tool for a situation that arises suddenly when Parliament is not sitting. It is rare among democratic constitutions (not in the USA or UK)
  • Memory aid (Four limits): Not in session, Necessity (immediate action), Competence (Union and Concurrent subjects), Laid before Parliament: "Never Neglect Constitutional Limits".

9 · Pardoning power (Article 72)#

  • An act of grace, not a judgment: it corrects judicial errors or relieves harshness. It is independent of the judiciary but is not an appeal and is exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers (Art. 74).
  • Memory aid (Which cases): Union law offence, Court martial sentence, Death sentence: "Under Court, Death".
  • Art. 72(2): does not affect military officers' power to suspend or remit sentences.
  • Art. 72(3): does not take away the Governor's power to suspend, remit or commute a death sentence under Art. 161.
  • Aims:
    • (1) correct judicial errors or excessive punishment
    • (2) extend mercy on humanitarian, social or public-welfare grounds

10 · Constitutional position, discretion and Article 143#

  • India has a parliamentary, not presidential, system: the President is head of the State only, unlike the US President who is head of State and government.
  • Ambedkar: "The President ... represents the nation but does not rule the nation ... a ceremonial device or a seal by which the nation's decisions are made known." In the Constituent Assembly K.T. Shah warned against a "gramophone of the Prime Minister".

Evolution of Article 74

How the advice became binding

  • Original Art. 74:
    • Did not say the President must act on advice
    • Implied by convention
  • 42nd Amendment (1976): Advice of the Council of Ministers made binding
  • 44th Amendment (1978):
    • President may once ask the Council to reconsider its advice
    • After reconsideration he is bound by the reaffirmed advice
  • 1997 case:
    • K.R. Narayanan returned the Cabinet's advice to impose President's Rule in Uttar Pradesh
    • The Cabinet dropped it
  • Shortcut: Prelims shortcut. 42nd = binding. 44th = may send back once (PYQ 2002). After the second advice, he must act.

12 · The Prime Minister#

  • The PM is the real (de facto) executive
  • The President is nominal (de jure). Ivor Jennings: "The Prime Minister is the keystone of the Constitution. All roads in the Constitution lead to the Prime Minister." Ambedkar: the PM, not the President, is comparable to the US President

Two views of the PM

  • Primus inter pares (first among equals):
    • Not a boss or captain
    • Coordinator, with decisions taken by the Cabinet as a whole. Example in the handout: Manmohan Singh
  • Luna inter stellas minores (a moon among lesser stars):
    • The boss, captain of the ship of State
    • Others are juniors. "Prime Ministerial government." Examples: Nehru, Indira Gandhi, Rajiv Gandhi, Modi

Appointment (Article 75)

  • Art. 75(1):
    • Appointed by the President
    • No detailed procedure, so convention applies. Ordinarily the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha
  • In a hung House the President may invite the leader of the largest party or coalition to seek a vote of confidence: Neelam Sanjiva Reddy appointed Charan Singh in 1979 after the Morarji Desai government fell.
  • Sudden death with no clear successor:
    • After Indira Gandhi's assassination in 1984, Zail Singh appointed Rajiv Gandhi
    • The Congress Parliamentary Party endorsed it

Judicial clarifications

  • Delhi High Court (1980): A person need not prove majority before appointment: appoint first, then require proof within a reasonable time. Applied to Charan Singh (1979), V.P. Singh (1989), Chandra Shekhar (1990), A.B. Vajpayee (1996)
  • S.P. Anand v. H.D. Deve Gowda (1997): A non-member can be PM but must become a member of either House within six months or cease to hold office
  • India versus Britain:
    • In Britain the PM must be in the House of Commons
    • In India he may belong to either House: Indira Gandhi (1966), Deve Gowda (1996) and Manmohan Singh (2004) were Rajya Sabha members

  • No fixed term

13 · Council of Ministers: appointment, responsibility, composition#

  • The Council of Ministers is the real executive (de facto). Art. 74 gives it the aid-and-advice role
  • The advice cannot be questioned in any court

Appointment and conditions (Art. 75)

  • PM appointed by the President
  • other ministers by the President on the PM's advice
  • Ministers hold office during the President's pleasure, in practice as long as the Lok Sabha trusts them (Art. 75(3)).
  • A non-member minister must become a member of Parliament within six months or cease to be a minister (Deve Gowda 1996 entered the Rajya Sabha within six months).
  • A minister of one House may speak in the other but vote only in his own.
  • Oath of office and secrecy by the President
  • Salaries set by Parliament

Recall sheet#

Cover the right column and answer. Same questions as the flashcards.

Where is the Union Executive in the Constitution?
Part V, Chapter I (Arts. 52-78): President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers with the PM, Attorney-General.
Why does Art. 52 say "shall"?
The office of President can never be abolished and shall never remain vacant.
Who is in the Presidential Electoral College?
Elected MPs and elected MLAs of States (including Delhi and Puducherry, 70th Am. 1992). Nominated members and MLCs are excluded.
What happens to an MLA's vote when an Assembly is dissolved?
The members are no longer elected representatives and lose the right to vote.
How is the value of an MLA's vote fixed?
By State population (1971 Census, 84th Am. 2001) in proportion to its number of elected MLAs; every elected MP's vote is equal.
2022 MLA vote values in the handout?
Uttar Pradesh 208, Maharashtra 175, Sikkim 7. MP vote: 700 (the handout also says 708 elsewhere).
Method of Presidential election?
Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote, secret ballot, under the Election Commission.
Who is the Returning Officer for the President's election?
Secretary General of Lok Sabha and of Rajya Sabha, by rotation.
Who is the only President elected after second preference votes?
V.V. Giri (1969). Only unopposed President: Neelam Sanjiva Reddy (1977).
Security deposit and nomination for President?
Rs. 15,000 (forfeited below 1/6th of votes needed to win); 50 proposers and 50 seconders.
Who decides disputes about the President's election?
The Supreme Court (Art. 71), final. Petition by the losing candidate or 20 or more electors; acts before a void judgment stay valid.
Qualifications of President (Art. 58)?
Citizen; 35 years; qualified for the Lok Sabha; no office of profit.
President's immunity (Art. 59)?
No criminal proceedings during the term, no arrest; civil proceedings for personal acts only after two months' notice.
Who administers the President's oath?
The Chief Justice of India, or in his absence the senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court.
Vacancy: who acts and how long to elect?
Vice-President, then CJI or senior-most SC Judge; fresh election within six months; full five-year term.
Ground and record for impeachment of President?
Violation of the Constitution (Art. 61), quasi-judicial; no President has been impeached.
How many members does the President nominate to the Rajya Sabha?
12 (literature, science, art, social service).
Which Bills need the President's prior recommendation?
Art. 3 (State reorganisation), Art. 110 (Money Bills), Art. 274 (taxation in which States are interested), Art. 304 (trade restrictions).
Name the three vetoes with a case each.
Absolute (PEPSU 1954; MPs' salary 1991); suspensive (Kalam 2006, Office of Profit Bill); pocket (Zail Singh 1986, Indian Post Office Bill). No qualified veto.
Four conditions of an ordinance (Art. 123)?
Parliament not in session; immediate necessity; same competence as Parliament (cannot amend Constitution); laid before Parliament and ceases six weeks after reassembly.
Maximum life of an ordinance?
About six months and six weeks.
Which cases govern ordinances?
R.C. Cooper (1970), A.K. Roy (1982), D.C. Wadhwa (1987: re-promulgation a fraud on the Constitution).
Three classes of cases under Art. 72?
Union law offences, court martial sentences, death sentences.
Define the five clemency powers.
Pardon (erases conviction), commutation (lighter kind), remission (shorter duration), respite (lesser punishment for special circumstances), reprieve (temporary stay).
Three pardon cases and their rule?
Maru Ram 1980 (Cabinet advice, review for mala fides); Kehar Singh 1988 (no oral hearing, can re-examine evidence); Shatrughan Chauhan 2014 (delay and mental illness can justify commutation).
What did the 42nd and 44th Amendments do to Art. 74?
42nd (1976): advice binding. 44th (1978): President may once send it back for reconsideration, then he must act on it.
Which President sent back the UP President's Rule advice and when?
K.R. Narayanan, 1997.
Art. 143(1) versus 143(2)?
(1) discretionary reference, the Court may decline; (2) mandatory for pre-constitutional treaties and agreements; Art. 145(3): five judges.
Voters and nomination in the Vice-President's election?
Only MPs (nominated included), one vote each; 20 proposers and 20 seconders; qualified for the Rajya Sabha.
Where is the Vice-President's removal moved?
Only in the Rajya Sabha, passed by an effective majority of its then members and agreed by the Lok Sabha by a simple majority.
Which six Vice-Presidents became Presidents?
Radhakrishnan, Zakir Husain, V.V. Giri, R. Venkataraman, Shankar Dayal Sharma, K.R. Narayanan.
Maximum period the VP acts as President?
Six months.
Two views of the PM's position?
Primus inter pares (first among equals) versus luna inter stellas minores (a moon among lesser stars).
Can a non-member be PM?
Yes, but must become a member of either House within six months (S.P. Anand v. Deve Gowda 1997).
Who appointed Charan Singh and Rajiv Gandhi?
Neelam Sanjiva Reddy (Charan Singh 1979) and Zail Singh (Rajiv Gandhi 1984).
What does the PM's resignation do?
Dissolves the whole Council of Ministers; another minister's resignation or death only creates a vacancy.
Three kinds of ministerial responsibility?
Collective (Art. 75(3), to Lok Sabha), individual (at President's pleasure on PM's advice), no legal responsibility (no countersignature).
What does the 91st Amendment do for the Union Council?
Ministers including the PM not more than 15% of the Lok Sabha; defectors cannot be ministers.
Where does "Cabinet" appear in the Constitution?
Only Art. 352(3) (44th Am. 1978).
Council of Ministers versus Cabinet size?
Council: about 40 to 60. Cabinet: about 15 to 20.
Who heads which committees apart from the PM?
Parliamentary Affairs: Minister of Defence. Accommodation: Minister of Home Affairs.
What is a Group of Ministers?
An ad hoc, issue-specific body, dissolved after the task; the Second ARC wanted selective, time-bound GoMs.
What is the Cabinet Secretary's rank and tenure?
11th in the Table of Precedence; minimum two years since 2010, extendable by two years.
PMS to PMO?
PMS created 1947; renamed PMO in 1977 by the Janata Party government; the PMO is the residual legatee.
Mains 2014: Instances of the President's delay in commuting death sentences
Intro: Art. 72 lets the President grant pardon, reprieve, respite, remission, suspension or commutation for Union-law offences, court martial and death sentences, on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers Body: The Constitution fixes no time limit for the President's action on Bills and none is stated for mercy petitions in the notes; Shatrughan Chauhan (2014) held that inordinate delay and mental illness can justify commutation and that convicts and families must be informed before execution; Maru Ram (1980) and Kehar Singh (1988) allow judicial review only for arbitrary, irrational, discriminatory or mala fide use; the power corrects judicial errors and extends mercy. Close: The notes support judicial safeguards against delay rather than a fixed outer limit on the President.
Mains 2014: The size of the cabinet should be as big as governmental work justifies
Intro: The Council of Ministers is capped at 15% of the Lok Sabha by the 91st Amendment, while the Cabinet is a small inner body Body: Council of about 40 to 60 versus Cabinet of about 15 to 20; Cabinet Committees (eight) and Groups of Ministers split the workload; Second ARC found GoMs delayed work and urged time-bound ones; Kitchen Cabinet can dilute the formal Cabinet; the notes link efficiency to specialisation and coordination, not to size alone. Close: Efficiency depends on structure and coordination, not on headcount alone.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Who votes for whom Nominated MPs and MLCs do not vote for the President, but nominated MPs do vote for the Vice-President. Rule: President = elected members only (MPs plus MLAs); Vice-President = all MPs, nominated too, and no MLAs.
Trap 2 · Which Bills can be returned The President can return only an ordinary Bill. A Money Bill cannot be returned, and a Constitutional Amendment Bill can be neither returned nor withheld. Rule: only ordinary Bills can be returned; Amendment Bill = assent is mandatory (24th Am.).
Trap 3 · 42nd versus 44th Amendment The 42nd (1976) made the Council's advice binding; the 44th (1978) let the President send it back once. Rule: 42nd = binding, 44th = one reconsideration, then he must act.
Trap 4 · Council is not Cabinet The 15% cap is for the Council of Ministers, and the word Cabinet appears only in Art. 352(3). Rule: Council = Arts. 74-75 (about 40 to 60); Cabinet = Art. 352(3) only (about 15 to 20).
