1 · Structure of the Union Executive and the President's status#

The Union Executive implements laws and runs day-to-day administration; the Legislature makes laws and the Judiciary interprets them. It sits in Part V, Chapter I (Articles 52 to 78).

The four parts

  • The President
  • The Vice-President
  • The Council of Ministers, headed by the Prime Minister
  • The Attorney-General of India

The President holds the highest constitutional position and is head of the State; all executive actions of the Union are taken in his name.

Four parts
PVCA

President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers (with PM), Attorney-General: "Polity Veterans Can Answer".

Key Articles on the position of the President
ArticleWhat it says
52"There shall be a President of India" (the word "shall" makes it mandatory: the office can never be abolished and shall never remain vacant)
53Executive power of the Union is vested in the President, exercised directly or through subordinate officers; also Art. 53(2): Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces
74Council of Ministers with the PM at the head to aid and advise the President, who shall act in accordance with that advice
75(3)Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha

The President is the nominal (constitutional) executive, like the British monarch; the real executive power lies with the Council of Ministers headed by the PM (Art. 74). This is the Parliamentary form: the President acts on aid and advice.

2 · Election of the President (Articles 54, 55, 71)#

The President is not elected directly. An Electoral College (Art. 54) elects him, so he represents both the Union and the States.

Who is in the Electoral College

Included

  • Elected members of both Houses of Parliament
  • elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of States, including Delhi and Puducherry (added by the 70th Amendment Act, 1992)

Excluded

  • Nominated members of Parliament and of State legislatures (no democratic mandate)
  • members of Legislative Councils (only directly elected representatives vote)
  • members of a dissolved Assembly (they cease to be elected representatives and lose the vote)

The 104th Amendment ended nomination of the Anglo-Indian community to the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. The handout gives the year 2020 for this.

Article 55: uniformity and parity

The Constitution wants uniformity among States and parity between the States as a whole and the Union. So every vote has a value:

Value of votes
VoterValue of vote
Elected MLAPopulation of the State (1971 Census, frozen by the 84th Amendment 2001) in proportion to the number of its elected MLAs: bigger population gives more weight, balanced by the number of MLAs. Disputes (Article 71)

  • The Supreme Court decides all questions or disputes about the election; its decision is final.
  • If the election is declared void, all acts of the President before the judgment remain valid.
  • A petition can be filed only by the losing candidate or by 20 or more electors together.
  • 39th Amendment 1975 changed the forum to an authority created by Parliament; 44th Amendment 1978 restored the Supreme Court.
  • The election cannot be challenged merely because some Assemblies are dissolved or seats are vacant (PYQ 2023: election cannot be postponed on that ground).

3 · Qualifications, conditions and oath (Articles 58, 59, 60)#

Qualifications (Article 58)

Four tests
CALN

Citizen of India, Age 35 completed, Lok Sabha-qualified, No office of profit (exempt: President, Vice-President, Governor, Minister): "Clever Aspirants Learn Notes".

Conditions of office (Article 59)

  • Cannot be a member of Parliament or of any State Legislature; if elected he is deemed to have vacated the seat from the date he enters office.
  • Cannot hold any other office of profit.
  • Rent-free official residences; emoluments, allowances and privileges set by Parliament by law (until then, as in the Second Schedule).
  • Emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during the term.
  • Immunities: personal immunity for official acts; no criminal proceedings during the term, even for personal acts; cannot be arrested or imprisoned; civil proceedings for personal acts only after two months' notice.

Oath or affirmation (Article 60)

  • Taken before the Chief Justice of India (or, in his absence, the senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court).
  • Anyone acting as President or discharging the President's functions takes the same oath.
Oath
FPD

Faithfully execute the office; Preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law; Devote himself to the service and well-being of the people: "Fine Plans Delivered". Loyalty is to the Constitution, not a party or government.

4 · Term, vacancy and impeachment (Articles 56, 61, 62)#

Term

  • Five years from the day he takes office; may resign by writing to the Vice-President; or be removed by impeachment.
  • Continues until his successor takes charge; no limit on re-election (the USA limits to two terms).

Vacancy

Three kinds of vacancy
KindWhenHow it is filled
AnticipatedTerm about to expireElection completed before the term ends, so there is no gap

Who acts when the post is vacant

Vice-President
Acts as President until a new one takes office
If VP post is also vacant
Chief Justice of India, or in his absence the senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court
Within six months
Fresh election; full five-year term from the date he takes charge

Anyone acting as President enjoys all powers and immunities of the President and his emoluments, allowances and privileges as fixed by Parliament.

The 1969 case

Dr. Zakir Husain died in May 1969; VP V.V. Giri acted as President, then resigned to contest the election; Chief Justice M. Hidayatullah officiated from 20 July to 24 August 1969.

Acting chain
VCS

Vice-President, Chief Justice of India, Senior-most SC Judge: "Very Careful Succession".

Impeachment (Article 61)

  • The only ground is "violation of the Constitution". It is a quasi-judicial process, deliberately rigorous to protect the dignity of the office.
  • No President has ever been impeached in independent India. The word "impeachment" is used in the Constitution in Art. 61.
  • It shows the President is accountable to Parliament; the difficult procedure prevents frivolous attempts.
  • Handout PYQ (1996): the State Legislative Assemblies are part of the Electoral College but not part of the forum for impeachment; Legislative Councils are in neither.

5 · Powers of the President: executive and legislative#

Executive power of the Union is vested in the President, exercised directly or through subordinates. In practice all powers are exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers.

Executive powers

The President appoints

  • The Prime Minister, and other ministers on the PM's advice
  • Governors
  • Attorney-General
  • CAG
  • Chief Election Commissioner and Election Commissioners
  • Chairpersons and Members of the Finance Commission, UPSC and other constitutional bodies
  • Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts
  • Ambassadors and High Commissioners

The President can remove

  • Ministers (on the PM's advice)
  • Governors
  • Attorney-General
  • members of constitutional commissions, under prescribed conditions
  • Head of administration: all executive actions are in his name.
  • Union Territories: administered through Administrators or Lieutenant Governors (Art. 239); special responsibility for the Fifth and Sixth Schedules (Scheduled and Tribal Areas).
  • Rule-making (Art. 77): rules for allocation of business among Ministers and for transaction of Government business (PYQ 2014: the President makes these rules; actions are in the President's name, not the PM's).
  • Seeking information (Art. 78): can call for information from the PM and require a Minister to submit proposals for the Council's consideration.
  • Administration: ensures faithful execution of laws; may appoint inquiry commissions.

Legislative powers

The President is a part of Parliament though not a member of either House; no law exists without him.

6 · Financial, judicial, diplomatic and military powers#

Financial powers

The President is the "guardian of the nation's finances": Parliament cannot spend or raise money without his approval.

Financial powers
PowerDetail
Annual Financial Statement (Art. 112)Laid before both Houses (estimated receipts and expenditure). PYQ 2024: the Finance Minister lays it on behalf of the President, not the PM
Money and Financial BillsIntroduced only in the Lok Sabha and only with the President's prior recommendation
Demands for Grants and Supplementary GrantsMade to the Lok Sabha only on the President's recommendation
Contingency Fund of India (Art. 267)At his disposal for urgent or unforeseen expenses
ReportsLays CAG, Finance Commission and Union Accounts reports before Parliament (PYQs 2010, 2012: the Finance Commission's recommendations and the CAG and NCSC reports are laid by the President; the Public Accounts Committee report is not in that list)

Judicial powers

  • Appoints the Chief Justice and Judges of the Supreme Court (Art. 124) and of the High Courts (Art. 217), in consultation with the judiciary through the collegium.
  • Removes judges only after Parliament's special impeachment procedure for proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
  • Pardon (Art. 72): see the Pardon section.
  • Advisory opinion (Art. 143): see the Constitutional Position section.

Diplomatic and military powers

Diplomatic

  • Represents India abroad
  • appoints ambassadors and high commissioners and receives foreign diplomats
  • treaties are made in his name but become binding only after Parliament's approval

Military

  • Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces (Art. 53(2))
  • appoints the Chiefs of Army, Navy and Air Force
  • can declare war or conclude peace only with Parliament's approval
  • used on the Council's advice

7 · Veto power of the President#

A Bill becomes law only after assent. The President may assent, withhold assent, or return a non-Money Bill. India has three vetoes; the qualified veto of the USA does not exist in India.

The three vetoes

Absolute veto
President withholds assent: the Bill dies. Used for Private Members' Bills or when the Cabinet that introduced the Bill has lost power. Cases: PEPSU Appropriation Bill 1954 (first use) and MPs' Salary and Pension (Amendment) Bill 1991 (R. Venkataraman returned it unsigned)
Suspensive veto
President returns a non-Money Bill. If Parliament passes it again (with or without changes) he must assent. Case: Kalam 2006, Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Amendment Bill (Office of Profit Bill)
Pocket veto
No action at all: the Constitution fixes no time limit, so the Bill can be kept pending indefinitely. Case: Zail Singh 1986, Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill (interception of private mail on security grounds)
No qualified veto
The legislature cannot override the executive by a special majority; a withheld Bill is dead unless introduced afresh
Veto types
ASP

Absolute (withhold), Suspensive (return), Pocket (sit on it): "A Sure Plan".

Veto cases
PMKZ

PEPSU 1954, MPs' salary 1991 (absolute); Kalam 2006 (suspensive); Zail Singh 1986 (pocket): "People Must Know Zail".

State Bills (Art. 200 and 201)

The Governor has four options for a State Bill: assent, withhold, return (non-Money Bill; assent if passed again) or reserve for the President. A Governor must reserve a Bill that endangers the position of the High Court (Art. 200, second proviso).

Money Bill: cannot be returned. Constitutional Amendment Bill: cannot be returned or withheld. Only ordinary Bills can be returned. PYQ 2023: no time limits for the President's assent to Bills.

8 · Ordinance-making power (Article 123)#

The President can promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session; they have the same force as an Act of Parliament but are temporary. Unlike Emergency powers, this works in normal times. Ambedkar justified it as a tool for a situation that arises suddenly when Parliament is not sitting. It is rare among democratic constitutions (not in the USA or UK).

Four limits
NNCL

Not in session, Necessity (immediate action), Competence (Union and Concurrent subjects), Laid before Parliament: "Never Neglect Constitutional Limits".

The four conditions
ConditionDetail
(a) Parliament not in sessionOnly when both Houses are not in session. An ordinance issued when either House sits would be void
(b) Immediate necessityPresident must be satisfied that immediate action is needed (on the Council's advice) and it is judicially reviewable: R.C. Cooper (1970) held satisfaction can be questioned for mala fides or abuse. The 38th Amendment 1975 made it final; the 44th Amendment 1978 deleted that clause
(c) Legislative competenceCoextensive with Parliament's powers except in duration: only Union and Concurrent subjects; may be retrospective; cannot violate Fundamental Rights or the Basic Structure; can amend or repeal laws but cannot amend the Constitution (Art. 368)
(d) Laying and durationLaid before both Houses on reassembly; ceases six weeks after reassembly unless approved; ends earlier if both Houses pass disapproving resolutions; if Houses meet on different dates the six weeks run from the later date
  • Maximum life about six months and six weeks. Acts done under an ordinance stay valid even if it lapses.
  • The President may withdraw an ordinance at any time, on the Council's advice.
  • Lok Sabha rules: a Bill replacing an ordinance must be accompanied by a statement of the circumstances that made it necessary.
  • PYQ 2003: the 2002 ordinance on electoral reforms, sent back by the Cabinet unchanged, was assented to under Article 123.

9 · Pardoning power (Article 72)#

An act of grace, not a judgment: it corrects judicial errors or relieves harshness. It is independent of the judiciary but is not an appeal and is exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers (Art. 74).

Which cases
UCD

Union law offence, Court martial sentence, Death sentence: "Under Court, Death".

  • Art. 72(2): does not affect military officers' power to suspend or remit sentences.
  • Art. 72(3): does not take away the Governor's power to suspend, remit or commute a death sentence under Art. 161.
  • Aims: (1) correct judicial errors or excessive punishment; (2) extend mercy on humanitarian, social or public-welfare grounds.

10 · Constitutional position, discretion and Article 143#

India has a parliamentary, not presidential, system: the President is head of the State only, unlike the US President who is head of State and government.

Ambedkar

"The President ... represents the nation but does not rule the nation ... a ceremonial device or a seal by which the nation's decisions are made known." In the Constituent Assembly K.T. Shah warned against a "gramophone of the Prime Minister".

K.R. Narayanan returned the Cabinet's advice to impose President's Rule in Uttar Pradesh; the Cabinet dropped it
Prelims shortcut

42nd = binding. 44th = may send back once (PYQ 2002). After the second advice, he must act.

Moral authority

  • Facing unconstitutional advice: Art. 78 (ask for further information) or Art. 74 (send it back).
  • The President is a "Great Grandfather in a large joint family": consulted, advises and warns, but the younger generation decides.
  • R. Venkataraman: the President's discretion is an "emergency light" that glows only when regular current fails.

Art. 143(1) discretionary

  • President may refer a question of law or fact of public importance
  • the Court may decline
  • opinion is advisory, not binding, but carries weight

Art. 143(2) mandatory

  • Questions on pre-constitutional treaties or agreements: the President must refer
  • the Court must give its opinion

Art. 145(3)

  • Any reference is heard by five judges
  • the Court returns it with the majority opinion
  • Used at least 15 times since 1950. Examples: Kerala Education Bill 1957, Ayodhya Reference 1993.
  • Recent reference: the President invoked Art. 143(1) on the powers of Governors and the President under Arts. 200 and 201, after an SC ruling set timelines for them and introduced "deemed consent".

11 · The Vice-President of India#

Art. 63: there shall be a Vice-President. He is the second-highest constitutional office (below the President in precedence), modelled on the American VP, and is ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan was the first VP (1952-1962).

12 · The Prime Minister#

The PM is the real (de facto) executive; the President is nominal (de jure). Ivor Jennings: "The Prime Minister is the keystone of the Constitution. All roads in the Constitution lead to the Prime Minister." Ambedkar: the PM, not the President, is comparable to the US President.

Primus inter pares (first among equals)

  • Not a boss or captain
  • coordinator, with decisions taken by the Cabinet as a whole. Example in the handout: Manmohan Singh

Luna inter stellas minores (a moon among lesser stars)

  • The boss, captain of the ship of State
  • others are juniors. "Prime Ministerial government." Examples: Nehru, Indira Gandhi, Rajiv Gandhi, Modi

Appointment (Article 75)

  • Art. 75(1): appointed by the President; no detailed procedure, so convention applies. Ordinarily the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha.
  • In a hung House the President may invite the leader of the largest party or coalition to seek a vote of confidence: Neelam Sanjiva Reddy appointed Charan Singh in 1979 after the Morarji Desai government fell.
  • Sudden death with no clear successor: after Indira Gandhi's assassination in 1984, Zail Singh appointed Rajiv Gandhi; the Congress Parliamentary Party endorsed it.

Judicial clarifications

Delhi High Court (1980)
A person need not prove majority before appointment: appoint first, then require proof within a reasonable time. Applied to Charan Singh (1979), V.P. Singh (1989), Chandra Shekhar (1990), A.B. Vajpayee (1996)
S.P. Anand v. H.D. Deve Gowda (1997)
A non-member can be PM but must become a member of either House within six months or cease to hold office
India versus Britain
In Britain the PM must be in the House of Commons; in India he may belong to either House: Indira Gandhi (1966), Deve Gowda (1996) and Manmohan Singh (2004) were Rajya Sabha members
Oaths of the PM (administered by the President)
OathContent
Oath of OfficeTrue faith and allegiance to the Constitution; uphold sovereignty and integrity of India; faithfully discharge duties; do right to all manner of people without fear or favour, affection or ill will
Oath of SecrecyNot to disclose any information that comes to his knowledge as a minister, except as required for official duties

Term, salary

  • No fixed term; holds office "during the pleasure of the President", but the President cannot dismiss a PM who has the Lok Sabha's confidence. On losing confidence he must resign (V.P. Singh 1990 and Deve Gowda 1997 resigned after losing confidence votes).
  • Salary and allowances set by Parliament: MP's pay plus sumptuary allowance, free residence, travel, medical and security. In 2018 the PM's sumptuary allowance was Rs. 3,000 per month.

  • Chairman of NITI Aayog, National Integration Council, Inter-State Council, National Water Resources Council.

13 · Council of Ministers: appointment, responsibility, composition#

The Council of Ministers is the real executive (de facto). Art. 74 gives it the aid-and-advice role; the advice cannot be questioned in any court.

Appointment and conditions (Art. 75)

  • PM appointed by the President; other ministers by the President on the PM's advice.
  • Ministers hold office during the President's pleasure, in practice as long as the Lok Sabha trusts them (Art. 75(3)).
  • A non-member minister must become a member of Parliament within six months or cease to be a minister (Deve Gowda 1996 entered the Rajya Sabha within six months).
  • A minister of one House may speak in the other but vote only in his own.
  • Oath of office and secrecy by the President; salaries set by Parliament.

14 · Cabinet committees and Groups of Ministers#

Cabinet committees

They ease the Cabinet's workload by detailed examination before decisions.

  • Extra-constitutional: they derive from the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961, framed under Art. 77(3).
  • Formed by the PM, who decides number, names and membership; typically six to eight standing committees plus ad hoc ones. Each has 3 to 8 members; Ministers of State may be included when their subjects are discussed.
  • The PM chairs the crucial ones; others are chaired by the Home or Defence Minister when delegated.
  • The Cabinet Secretariat provides staff, agenda papers and records decisions. Aim: specialisation, coordination, speed.

15 · Cabinet Secretariat, Cabinet Secretary, PMO and Central Secretariat#

Cabinet Secretariat

A principal staff agency that supports the Cabinet, its committees and the PM; it works under the PM's direction (the PM is the Chairperson of the Cabinet).

Core functions

  • Secretarial assistance to Cabinet and committees (agenda, minutes, circulation, objections) so that collective responsibility works
  • implements the rules of business
  • chief coordinating agency
  • keeps the President and ministers informed by a monthly summary
  • can promote new policy initiatives

PYQ 2014 and 2009

  • Preparation of agenda for Cabinet meetings and secretariat assistance to Cabinet Committees are its functions
  • allocation of financial resources to ministries is not. The Cabinet Secretariat works under the PM, not the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs

Cabinet Secretary

  • Heads the Secretariat; the senior-most position an IAS officer can reach; head of the civil services; 11th rank in the Table of Precedence (PYQ 2000: CJI, Cabinet Ministers, CEC, Cabinet Secretary in that order).
  • Principal adviser to the PM; "conscience keeper" of the civil service; a buffer between the political executive and the civil service.
  • Presides over the conference of Chief Secretaries; chairs the Civil Services Board that advises the Cabinet Committee on Appointments (PYQ 2015: the PM is not ex-officio chairman of it).
  • Since 2010: minimum tenure of 2 years, may go beyond retirement age and is extendable by 2 years.
  • N.R. Pillai was the first; Rajiv Gauba is the present Cabinet Secretary.

Rajiv Gandhi
PMO called the "government of government of India"; Cabinet Secretariat became its implementation arm
  • Members: a Minister of State, the Principal Secretary (presently P.K. Mishra), the National Security Advisor and others.
  • Functions: examines references and files for the PM; helps the PM as "CEO of the country"; handles departments in his direct charge, such as atomic energy and space (PYQ 2009: the Department of Atomic Energy is under the PMO); work as NITI Aayog chairperson; public relations; any issue not under any ministry (hence "residual legatee"); manages funds like PM Relief Fund, PM National Defence Fund, PM CARES Fund.
  • Its role reflects the PM's personality and strength; generally weak in coalition governments. As it strengthens, the Principal Secretary becomes more powerful than the Cabinet Secretary.
  • Need: continuity through the Principal Secretary's stable tenure; coordination among ministries; liaison with the offices of other countries' heads. It must work through the system, not bypass it.

Central Secretariat

The Government works on a "split system": ministries divide into policy making (generalist bureaucrats) and implementation (specialists). Examples: Health Department (policy) and Directorate of Health (implementation); Home department and Directorate General of Police.

  • The headquarters of all central ministries together form the Central Secretariat, responsible for administering Union List subjects.
  • Functions: policy advice (facts, figures, Supreme Court judgments); legislative work (draft Bills, briefing ministers for Questions); ministry budget; general supervision without day-to-day implementation; clearing house of decisions; institutional memory; continuity; channel between ministries, Centre and States, and bodies like the CAG and UPSC.

Recall sheet#

Read once, hide it, then rebuild the chapter from memory.

  1. Union Executive: President, VP, Council of Ministers (PM), Attorney-General; Part V Ch. I Arts. 52-78.
  2. President: Art. 52 (shall be), 53, 54-55 election, 56 term, 58 qualifications, 59 conditions, 60 oath, 61 impeachment, 62 vacancy, 71 disputes (SC).
  3. Electoral College: elected MPs + elected MLAs (with Delhi and Puducherry, 70th Am. 1992); no nominated; no MLCs; dissolved Assemblies drop out.
  4. Vote value: MLA = State population (1971 Census, 84th Am. 2001) vs elected MLAs; each MP equal; 2022: UP 208, Maharashtra 175, Sikkim 7, MP 700 (708 in one place).
  5. STV and secret ballot; Returning Officer: Secretary General of Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha by rotation; deposit Rs. 15,000 forfeited below 1/6th; 50 proposers and 50 seconders.
  6. V.V. Giri 1969 second-preference win; N. Sanjiva Reddy 1977 unopposed; no proxy vote, no NOTA.
  7. Qualifications: citizen, 35, Lok Sabha-qualified, no office of profit. Oath before CJI.
  8. Vacancy: VP acts (max 6 months), then CJI, then senior-most SC Judge; election within six months; full five-year term.
  9. Vetoes: absolute (PEPSU 1954; MPs salary 1991), suspensive (Kalam 2006), pocket (Zail Singh 1986); no qualified veto; Money Bill and Amendment Bill cannot be returned.
  10. Ordinance Art. 123: not in session; necessity; competence (no Constitution amendment); six weeks after reassembly; max 6 months + 6 weeks; R.C. Cooper, A.K. Roy, D.C. Wadhwa.
  11. Pardon Art. 72: Union law, court martial, death; pardon, commutation, remission, respite, reprieve; Maru Ram, Kehar Singh, Shatrughan Chauhan.
  12. Art. 74: 42nd binding; 44th once reconsideration; Narayanan 1997; Art. 143(1) discretionary, 143(2) mandatory, 145(3) five judges.
  13. VP: Art. 63, 64, 65, 66, 67, 68; MPs only (nominated vote); 20 + 20; five years; removal starts only in Rajya Sabha; six VPs became Presidents.
  14. PM: Art. 75; appointed by President; six-month rule (Anand v. Deve Gowda 1997); either House; oaths of office and secrecy; Art. 78 channel.
  15. Council: 91st Am. 15% cap; collective, individual, no legal responsibility; Cabinet only in Art. 352(3); Cabinet 15-20, Council 40-60.
  16. Cabinet committees: eight; Rules 1961, Art. 77(3); Parliamentary Affairs: Defence Minister; Accommodation: Home Minister; PM heads the other six.
  17. GoMs: ad hoc; 2nd ARC; Cabinet Secretary 11th in precedence, 2-year minimum tenure; PMS 1947 to PMO 1977; PMO "residual legatee".
