4.1 · Rethinking India's population policy#

Prelims + MainsSociety & Social Justice
The EAC-PM working paper "The Ghost of Population Past" argues that sterilisation incentives and family-size restrictions continue despite falling fertility; it calls for review while keeping contraception voluntary.
2.9 croreData2.3 croreDataRs 1,000Incentives favour sterilisation80%Incentives favour sterilisation70%Women bear the burden
● ConceptsTFR; replacement-level fertility about 2.1; population momentum (growth continues as large young cohorts enter reproductive age); family planning.
● DataTFR 1.9 in 2024 (SRS); annual births fell from about 2.9 crore (2001) to 2.3 crore (2023, UN). Bihar TFR 2.9, Uttar Pradesh 2.6; Andhra Pradesh 1.4, Tamil Nadu 1.3.
★ Why reviewstates differ (high-fertility need contraception, girls' education, maternal care; low-fertility need elder care and workforce planning); Mission Parivar Vikas must adapt locally.
● Incentives favour sterilisation2025-26 NHM gives ASHA Rs 1,000 for motivating a permanent method after two children; sterilisation took about 80% of combined sterilisation, IUD and injectable allocations.
● Two-child normsMaharashtra links small-family norm to government jobs and local-body elections; Andhra Pradesh removed its local-body restriction in 2024. These are state rules, not a national law.
● Women bear the burdenfemale sterilisation used by about 70% of currently married women (15-49) in Andhra Pradesh (NFHS-6).
➜ Reformsstate-specific planning (Karunakaran Committee 1991); informed consent and freedom from coercion; review sterilisation incentives (NHRC 2003); reconsider family-size conditions; male participation.
Prelims fodder
● Mission Parivar VikasUnion Health Ministry, access to contraception and family planning, first in high-fertility districts.
● IUCD (e.g. Copper-T) is reversible. Antarainjectable contraceptive, each dose protects three months.
● RMNCAH+Nintegrated continuum-of-care strategy under NHM.
Mains fodder
● China lessonone-child policy 1979-80; universal two-child 2016; three-child 2021. Review early, remove barriers to parenthood, prepare for ageing; India stays voluntary.

4.2 · Maternity rights: Rakhi Bisht v. Union of India (2026)#

Prelims + MainsSociety & Social Justice
Delhi High Court held that availing maternity leave cannot diminish a woman's professional status or career prospects; cited Articles 14, 15, 21, 42 and the Code on Social Security, 2020.
21.4%Challenges63.1%Challenges
● Principlesreturn to same or substantially equivalent role (pay, status, responsibilities, authority, promotion); protection covers duties, reporting, appraisal; explain reasons and alternatives before rejoining if original role is unavailable; no retaliation; Court noted a gap in post-maternity reintegration and told the Centre to frame safeguards within six months.
●Code on Social Security 2020 subsumed the Maternity Benefit Act 1961. Applies to factories, mines, plantations and shops/establishments with 10+ employees; eligibility 80 days of work in preceding 12 months.
● Leave26 weeks for fewer than two surviving children (up to 8 weeks before delivery); 12 weeks for two or more (up to 6 weeks before); adoptive and commissioning mothers 12 weeks; crèche for 50+ employees with four visits daily.
⚠ ChallengesTime Use Survey 2024 - 41% of women (15-59) did unpaid caregiving vs 21.4% of men; women 140 min/day vs men 74; employer-cost bias; weak reintegration; informal-sector exclusion (PLFS 2025: 63.1% of female non-agricultural workers in proprietary/partnership enterprises); weak crèches and enforcement.
➜ Way forwardshared parental leave (ILO C156); socialise costs (ILO C183); phased return, flexible work, lactation support; PMMVY; strengthen enforcement.
● Connecting newsTamil Nadu announced 365 days of maternity leave for state employees for a third child (up from 12 weeks). Central Civil Services (Leave) Rules 1972 give male employees 15 days paternity leave; no statutory private-sector paternity leave. Hamsaanandini Nanduri (2026) urged recognising paternity leave as social security.
Prelims fodder
● CEDAW 1979 Article 11non-discrimination in employment. ILO C183 (2000) Maternity Protection; ILO C156 (1981) Workers with Family Responsibilities; India has not ratified either.
● CasesMCD v. Female Workers (Muster Roll) 2000; Deepika Singh v. CAT 2022; Hamsaanandini Nanduri v. Union of India 2026 (removed three-month adoption limit).
Mains fodder
🔑 KeywordsMotherhood penaltySubstantive equalityWorkplace reintegration

4.3.1 · District-level labour market indicators (PLFS)#

PrelimsSociety & Social Justice
NSO released district-level PLFS labour indicators for the first time. PLFS design revised in January 2025: districts replaced NSS Regions as sampling basis; survey year moved from July-June to calendar year.
57.8%Findings40%Findings
● DefinitionsLFPR, WPR, Unemployment Rate, NEET.
● Findingsabout 57.8% of districts had female LFPR of 40% or more; 77.8% had unemployment between 0.5% and 5.5%; over 75% had NEET below 30%; among the 100 most populous districts Surat had highest LFPR and WPR, Darbhanga lowest.
● Usetargeted policy, District Skill Committee planning, local baseline to monitor schemes.

4.3.2 · Reparatory justice: CERD General Recommendation No. 40#

PrelimsSociety & Social Justice
UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination adopted General Recommendation 40 on reparatory justice for historical racial injustice.
● Reparation formsrestitution, compensation, rehabilitation, satisfaction (acknowledgement, apology, truth-seeking, memorials), guarantees of non-repetition.
➜ Recommendationsremove present inequalities linked to slavery and colonialism; preserve records and open archives; participation of people of African descent; institutions that benefited from slavery should acknowledge their role.
Prelims fodder
● CERD18 independent experts, monitors ICERD. General Recommendations are authoritative but not binding. ICERD adopted 1965, in force 1969, 182 parties including India.
🔑 KeywordsReparatory justice

4.4 · Society quick updates#

PrelimsSociety & Social Justice
Short items from September 2026.
●Global Gender Gap Index 2026 (WEF, 20th edition): India 131st of 145 economies, 64.5% gap closed (best in education, then health); Iceland first at 93%; global 69.2% closed, about 120 years to parity. Four dimensions: economic participation, education, health and survival, political empowerment.
●UN Gender Snapshot 2026 (UN Women and UN DESA): 32% of gender-equality institutions reported weakened power; women hold 27.4% of parliamentary seats; no SDG 5 indicator on track for 2030; women are 5.8% of CEOs and about 25.1% of listed-company board seats.
●EPFO wage ceiling raised from Rs 15,000 to Rs 25,000 per month; over 51 lakh more employees covered. EPFO: 1952 Act, Labour Ministry; schemes EPF 1952, EPS 1995, EDLI 1976; governed by tripartite Central Board of Trustees.
●PM Vishwakarma completed three years with 30 lakh beneficiaries; MSME ministry (with Skill and Finance), Central Sector Scheme, 18 trades, collateral-free loans up to Rs 3 lakh in two tranches at 5%.
●DWBDNC (Development and Welfare Board for De-notified, Nomadic and Semi-Nomadic Communities): society under Societies Registration Act 1860, set up by 2019 notification; held Samavesh Utsav. Criminal Tribes Act 1871 repealed 1952; Renke Commission 2008; NCDNT 2014.
● PM-AJAYlaunched 2021-22 by merging PMAGY, SCA to SCSP and BJRCY; components Adarsh Gram, Grants-in-Aid, Hostel; Social Justice ministry.
● NIMHANS (Bengaluru)nodal institution for BRICS Centres of Excellence in mental health; Institute of National Importance under NIMHANS Act 2012; deemed university 1994.
