Mains hooks

  • No Mains PYQ is shown on the slides; the book practice questions are the guide
  • Why did the Marathas lose despite numerical strength? Four reasons: factionalism, no unified command, EIC finances, naval and logistics advantage; a divided Maratha house was the EIC's most reliable ally

Factionalism pulled the EIC in at Surat, the Subsidiary Alliance disarmed the Peshwa, the loophole of 1816 ended in war, and by 1818 the Peshwaship was gone; the Marathas lost more from their own structure than from the EIC's strength.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: A divided Maratha house kept inviting the EIC in (Surat 1775, Bassein 1802), the Subsidiary Alliance then took away the Peshwa's own army, and the EIC's Paramountcy turned the loophole of 1816 into the war that ended the Peshwaship in 1818.

  1. The whole story on one board. The teacher puts the three wars on one line. The 1st war (1775-82): reason factionalism, it began at Surat 1775, the EIC 'won' and it ended at Salbai 1782 with a military alliance. The 2nd (1803-05): reason factionalism, it began with Bassein 1802, an SA signed for the Svarajya, and the Marathas lost. The 3rd (1817-19): reason a breach of the SA by the EIC, and the Marathas lost, with the Swarajya annexed.
    → So why did the EIC care so much about the Deccan?
  2. Why the Deccan mattered. The Deccan and the Konkan coast were commercially critical to the EIC: a large coastline, a major cotton trade from Gujarat to China, and Maratha control over parts of the Malabar coast. The Maratha Confederacy and Mysore were the last major powers able to check the EIC. Three Anglo-Maratha Wars and four Anglo-Mysore Wars decided the outcome.
    → The first of the three wars began inside the Maratha house itself.
  3. A claimant and a treaty at Surat. In 1773 Raghunath Rao 73-74 became Peshwa after the murder of his nephew Narayan Rao 72-73. In 1774, in the Baarbhai Conspiracy, Nana Fadnis and eleven other ministers removed him. He went to the EIC's Governor of Bombay, William Hornby, and in 1775 signed the Treaty of Surat: EIC help in return for Salsette, Bassein and some revenue from Bharuch and Surat.
    → But Calcutta did not like the bill.
  4. Warren Hastings cancels, then Purandhar collapses. Warren Hastings 73-85, the Governor General in Calcutta, overturned Surat to avoid expensive war expenditure and negotiated the Treaty of Purandhar (1776) with Nana Fadnis. Madhav Rao II 74-95 was recognised, Raghunath Rao 73-74 pensioned, Salsette and Bharuch went to the EIC, and no French presence was allowed. But Bombay kept sheltering Raghunath, Nana Fadnis offered the French a port, and the war resumed.
    → Now the Marathas gave the EIC a bloody nose.
  5. Wadgaon and the peace of Salbai. At Wadgaon (1779), near Pune, Nana Fadnis, Mahadji Scindhia and Tukoji Rao Holkar defeated the EIC and Raghunath Rao 73-74. The war then stalled, with both sides in difficulty, the Marathas also being tied to the 2nd Anglo-Mysore War (1780-84). In 1782 Mahadji Scindhia signed the Treaty of Salbai at Gwalior: a military alliance against Hyder Ali 61-82's retention of territories, Salsette and Bharuch to the EIC, no French, territory returned, Madhav Rao II 74-95 recognised and Raghunath pensioned.
    → Peace came, but the glue holding the Marathas together began to dissolve.
  6. Twenty-one years of decline. Between Salbai (1782) and Bassein (1802) the Marathas declined. Mahadji Scindhia died in 1794, Madhav Rao II 74-95 committed suicide in 1795, Bajirao II 96-18 became Peshwa in 1796, and Nana Fadnis, the one man who held the Confederacy together for two decades, died in 1800. Wellesley 98-05, the empire-builder, was waiting for exactly this opportunity.
    → Then a Holkar execution lit the fuse.
  7. Bassein: the Peshwa signs an SA. In 1801 Vithoji Rao Holkar was captured and executed on the orders of Bajirao II 96-18. To avenge his stepbrother, Yashwantrao Holkar defeated the Peshwa and Daulat Rao Scindhia in October 1802. Bajirao fled and signed the Treaty of Bassein (December 1802), an SA in which the army of the Svarajya was to be disbanded once the EIC restored its puppet Peshwa. The Gaikwad had already signed the Treaty of Cambay in June 1802.
    → Ironically, the SA united the old rivals.
  8. Assaye and a lost war. Scindhia, Holkar and Bhonsle united against the EIC. Arthur Wellesley fought them at Assaye (1803), which he called the toughest fight of his career, harder than anything against Napoleon. The Marathas lost: the Peshwa was a puppet; Scindhia lost all north of the Yamuna (Delhi, Agra) and all of Gujarat and signed an SA, with a British Resident in Delhi putting Shah Alam II 60-06 effectively under EIC indirect control; Bhonsle lost Orissa and signed only a mini alliance. Wellesley 98-05 was recalled for the war's cost, and Cornwallis 86-93 arrived in July 1805 and died in October.
    → After Wellesley the EIC pulled back, and trouble gathered at its edges.
  9. Pindaris, Paramountcy and a loophole. After Wellesley 98-05 the EIC followed non-intervention, giving Bhonsle and Holkar time to rebuild. Pindaris, disbanded soldiers of Scindhia and Holkar, raided the Northern Sarkars and Madras. Francis Hastings 13-23 crushed them and launched the Policy of Paramountcy (1813-58): the EIC was to be supreme and could even annex. Residents treated Bajirao II 96-18 as a puppet. In Nagpur Appa Sahib signed an SA in 1816 but, using the loophole that retainers were allowed, secretly recruited Arab and Rohilla mercenaries and had Parsoji murdered.
    → The loophole turned into a battlefield.
  10. The third war: Sitabuldi, Mahidpur, Koregaon. In late 1817 Bajirao II 96-18, Appa Sahib and Yashwantrao Holkar rose together. Appa Sahib, as Senapati, used his Arab mercenaries at Sitabuldi (November 1817) but was defeated. Holkar fell at Mahidpur (December 1817). At Koregaon (1 January 1818) a small EIC force of Mahar soldiers held off a much larger Peshwa army. The EIC used its largest force ever, about 1.2 lakh, and Bajirao surrendered in June 1818.
    → Now came the settlement.
  11. The settlement of 1818. The Peshwaship was abolished and Bajirao II 96-18 pensioned to Bithur near Kanpur, where his adopted son Nana Sahib would later lead the Kanpur revolt of 1857. Svarajya was annexed into the Bombay Presidency. Appa Sahib was deposed, and Bhonsle lost huge territory. Holkar signed an SA at Mandsaur (1818). Satara and Kolhapur were spared as the Chhatrapati's symbolic line; Satara was annexed in 1848 under Dalhousie 48-56's Doctrine of Lapse.
    → Why did such a great power fall, and where did the EIC look next?
  12. Why they lost, and the next wars. The Marathas lost for four reasons: factionalism, no unified command (Scindhia, Holkar, Bhonsle, Gaikwad), the EIC's finances, and its naval and logistics advantage. The EIC's army, with a vested interest in big threats, then overhyped border dangers: Nepal 1814-16 (Sugauli, a protectorate), Burma 1824-26 (Yandabo), Coorg 1834, Afghanistan 1838-42 (20,000 dead), Sindh 1843 (Napier's Peccavi), the Sikh wars and Burma II 1852-53.

2 · First Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82)#

Story link

Because a Peshwa was thrown out by his own ministers, he sold land to the EIC for help. Ask: why is Purandhar an example of factionalism within the EIC?

2.1 What triggered the war?

  • The cause was factionalism within the Maratha Confederacy itself, a pattern that repeats across all three wars.
  • In 1773 Raghunath Rao 73-74 became Peshwa after the murder of his nephew Narayan Rao 72-73.
  • In the Baarbhai Conspiracy (1774), Nana Fadnis and eleven other ministers of the Peshwa's council removed Raghunath Rao 73-74 from power.
  • Raghunath Rao 73-74 then approached the EIC's Governor of Bombay, William Hornby, and signed the Treaty of Surat (1775).
  • Under it the EIC agreed to help him regain the Peshwaship in return for Salsette and Bassein (Vasai, near Mumbai) along with some revenue from Bharuch and Surat.
Teacher's board
  • Everything is underlined: factionalism (the root cause), the names and years of the Peshwas, the Baarbhai Conspiracy 1774, the Governor of Bombay (William Hornby), and the deal itself: "EIC to help him" in return for "Salsette and Bassein" and revenue.
  • The deal is the thing to learn: help for land and money.
Handout note
  • The pattern, in the handout's words: this is exactly what happened in the Carnatic and Hyderabad during the Carnatic Wars: a succession dispute, a losing claimant, and the Europeans offering military help in exchange for territory and influence.
  • Factionalism of Indian States was the Europeans' most reliable ally. See Chapter 9.

  • 1st Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82) begins

3 · The inter-war period (1782-1803)#

Story link

Because Salbai bought peace, the Marathas had 21 years to repair themselves. Ask: why did they decline instead?

  • The 21-year gap between the Treaty of Salbai (1782) and the Treaty of Bassein (1802) was not a period of Maratha strength: it was a period of Maratha decline.

Story link

Story link

6.1 The Battle of Sitabuldi (November 1817)

  • In late 1817 Bajirao II 96-18, Appa Sahib (regent of the Bhonsle of Nagpur) and Yashwantrao Holkar united in revolt, determined to end the EIC's political domination.
  • (The handout's bracket: this is a violation of SA treaties.) This led to the 3rd Anglo-Maratha War (1817-19).
  • Bajirao II 96-18 had signed a Subsidiary Alliance under which the EIC army had replaced the Svarajya's own army, so technically he had no State army.

Recall sheet#

Read the question, answer in your head, then tap to check.

Why did the Deccan matter to the EIC?Large coastline; Gujarat-China cotton trade; Maratha control over parts of the Malabar coast. The Marathas and Mysore were the last major powers able to check the EIC.
What was the pattern behind all three Maratha wars?Factionalism: a succession or power dispute, a losing claimant, and the EIC offering help for territory or influence. A divided Maratha house was the EIC's most reliable ally.
What was the Baarbhai Conspiracy (1774)?Nana Fadnis and eleven other ministers of the Peshwa's council removed Raghunath Rao 73-74, who had become Peshwa in 1773 after the murder of Narayan Rao 72-73.
What did the Treaty of Surat (1775) give the EIC?For helping Raghunath Rao 73-74 regain the Peshwaship: Salsette and Bassein (Vasai) plus some revenue from Bharuch and Surat. Signed with the Governor of Bombay, William Hornby.
Why did Warren Hastings 73-85 overturn Surat?To avoid expensive war expenditure. He negotiated Purandhar (1776) with Nana Fadnis.
What were the clauses of Purandhar (1776)?Madhav Rao II 74-95 recognised and Raghunath Rao 73-74 pensioned; Salsette and Bharuch to the EIC; no French presence in Maratha domain.
Why did the Purandhar peace collapse?Bombay kept sheltering Raghunath Rao 73-74; Nana Fadnis offered the French a port; talks collapsed. The teacher: an example of factionalism within the EIC.
Who won at Wadgaon (1779)?Nana Fadnis, Mahadji Scindhia and Tukoji Rao Holkar defeated the EIC and Raghunath Rao 73-74; the war then stalled.
What were the terms of the Treaty of Salbai (1782)?Military alliance (Marathas fight for the EIC if Hyder Ali 61-82 did not restore territory); Salsette and Bharuch to the EIC; no French settlements; territory returned, Madhav Rao II 74-95 recognised, Raghunath Rao 73-74 pensioned. Signed at Gwalior by Mahadji Scindhia.
What was the Triple Confederacy (teacher's box)?An alliance of Marathas, Mysore and Hyderabad (1780-82), behind the 1st Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82) and the 2nd Anglo-Mysore War (1780-84).
Why were pensions a deliberate EIC practice?A pensioned rival could be used in future succession disputes.
What happened in the inter-war period (1782-1803)?Maratha decline: Mahadji Scindhia d. 1794; Madhav Rao II 74-95 suicide 1795; Bajirao II 96-18 Peshwa 1796; Nana Fadnis d. 1800, after which factionalism accelerated.
How did the 2nd Anglo-Maratha War begin?Vithoji Rao Holkar was executed on the Peshwa's orders (1801); Yashwantrao Holkar defeated the Peshwa and Daulat Rao Scindhia (Oct 1802); Bajirao II 96-18 fled and signed the Treaty of Bassein (Dec 1802), an SA; Scindhia, Holkar and Bhonsle then united.
Who signed an SA before the Peshwa?The Gaikwad, in June 1802 (Treaty of Cambay), for EIC help in a succession dispute.
Why is Assaye (1803) famous?Arthur Wellesley with his Royal regiment and the EIC army fought the Marathas; he called it the toughest fight of his career, harder than anything against Napoleon.
What did Scindhia lose in the 2nd war?All territory north of the Yamuna including Delhi and Agra, and all of Gujarat; he signed an SA; a British Resident in Delhi put the Emperor Shah Alam II 60-06 under indirect EIC control.
What happened to Bhonsle of Nagpur after the 2nd war?He lost Orissa and refused the full SA, signing only a mini alliance (territory ceded, an EIC army at his cost). The full SA came in 1816 (Treaty of Nagpur).
Why was Wellesley 98-05 recalled?The high war expenditure of his tenure; Cornwallis 86-93 was sent for non-interference (arrived July 1805, died October 1805).
What was the Vellore Mutiny (1806)?An uprising in Vellore Fort (north Tamil Nadu) under George Barlow (Acting GG 1805-07), against dress codes that banned tilak and turban and imposed a leather (cow or pig skin) cockade; the "Prelude to 1857".
Who were the Pindaris and who crushed them?Disbanded soldiers of Scindhia and Holkar raiding the Northern Sarkars and Madras Presidency; crushed by Francis Hastings 13-23.
What is the Policy of Paramountcy?1813-58, under Francis Hastings 13-23: the EIC is the paramount or supreme power and will do anything, even annex, to protect its interests; Residents treat rulers like Bajirao II 96-18 as puppets.
How did Appa Sahib beat the Treaty of Nagpur (1816)?The army had to be disbanded but retainers and police were allowed; he secretly recruited Arab and Rohilla mercenaries (and had Parsoji murdered to rule). His SA did not prevent war: its terms caused it (Prelims).
Name the battles of the 3rd Anglo-Maratha War.Sitabuldi (Nov 1817, Appa Sahib); Mahidpur (Dec 1817, Holkar); Koregaon (1 Jan 1818, Peshwa; Mahar soldiers, Bhima-Koregaon pillar); Peshwa surrendered June 1818. The EIC used its largest force ever, about 1.2 lakh.
What were the results of the 3rd war?Peshwaship abolished, Bajirao II 96-18 pensioned to Bithur; Svarajya into the Bombay Presidency; Appa Sahib deposed; Holkar's SA at Mandsaur 1818; Satara and Kolhapur not annexed.
Why were Satara and Kolhapur spared in 1818?The Chhatrapati was a powerless figurehead since the 1750s; keeping him cost nothing and avoided the optics of destroying a revered institution. Satara was annexed in 1848 under Dalhousie 48-56's Doctrine of Lapse.
List the four reasons why the Marathas lost.Factionalism; absence of unified command (Scindhia, Holkar, Bhonsle, Gaikwad); EIC's superior finances; naval and logistics advantage (Konkan blockade, sea and roads).
Mains practice: Why did the Marathas lose despite numerical strength? (150 words)Intro: they fell from their own structure more than the EIC's strength. Body: factionalism (Surat vs Purandhar, Bassein), no unified command, EIC finances, naval and logistics edge, the SA loophole. Closing: a divided Maratha house was the EIC's most reliable ally.
Mains practice: Subsidiary Alliance against the Policy of Paramountcy (150 words)Intro: SA bought control with promised help; Paramountcy openly made the EIC supreme. Body: SA = EIC army replaces State army (Bassein 1802); PoP 1813-58 = supreme, even annexation, Residents treat rulers as puppets; the loophole of 1816 shows the gap; trajectory SA, PoP, annexation (Svarajya 1818, Satara 1848). Closing: each step left less sovereignty.
Two kinds of EIC wars (teacher's tree)?For trade, or for T (territory); territory wars are driven by empire-builder Governor Generals or by the vested interest of the EIC army, which overhypes the threat to borders.
What is a protectorate?Symbolic sovereignty not taken; external functions go to Britain; domestic functions not taken. Like a Subsidiary Alliance. Nepal after Sugauli (1816).
Anglo-Nepal War?1814-16, Francis Hastings 13-23; Treaty of Sugauli 1816: Nepal a British Protectorate; lost Kumaon, Garhwal and the Terai.
1st Anglo-Burmese War?1824-26, Amherst 23-28; Treaty of Yandabo 1826 annexed Assam, Nagaland, Arakan and Tenasserim; Cachar annexed 1830; Ahom Assam, an EIC ally, was stabbed in the back.
Why was Coorg annexed (1834)?Under William Bentinck 28-35, on grounds of maladministration by Vira Raja; coffee plantations followed.
1st Anglo-Afghan War (1838-42)?Great Game (Britain vs Russia, over Central Asia); Auckland 36-42's Forward Policy to install Shah Shuja against Dost Mohammed; 20,000 dead, Rs 1.5 crore, one European survivor of the Kabul retreat; Auckland 36-42 recalled, Ellenborough 42-44 ended it.
Annexation of Sindh (1843)?Ellenborough 42-44; taken from the Char Yaar (four Baloch chiefs of the Talpur dynasty) without justification; commander Sir Charles Napier; "Peccavi" = "I have Sindh".
Which wars ended in Punjab 1849 and Pegu?1st and 2nd Anglo-Sikh Wars (1845-46, 1848-49; Hardinge 44-48 and Dalhousie 48-56) led to Punjab's annexation in 1849; 2nd Anglo-Burmese War (1852-53; Dalhousie 48-56) annexed Pegu.
Prelims 1999 trick?Match wars by their start year: 1775 Maratha I (3), 1780 Mysore II (4), 1824 Burmese I (1), 1838 Afghan I (2): option (c).
Prelims 2004 trick?Only B (1790-92 Mysore III) and C (1824-26 Burmese I) are right: 1767-69 is Mysore I; 1845-46 is Sikh I. Answer (d).
