Remember

Practice Mains 1 (book question, 150 words, 10 marks): "Why did the Marathas lose the three Anglo-Maratha Wars despite their numerical strength?" Skeleton from the notes: Intro: the Confederacy fell less from the EIC's military superiority than from its own structure. Body: (1) factionalism at every stage: Surat against Purandhar 1775-76, the Baarbhai Conspiracy, Bassein 1802 after a Holkar defeat; (2) no unified command: Scindhia, Holkar, Bhonsle and Gaikwad of Baroda never coordinated; (3) the EIC's superior finances; (4) the EIC's naval and logistics advantage: the Konkan coast could be blockaded and troops moved faster by sea and better roads; add the SA loophole (Bajirao II 96-18 fought with men he recruited himself). Closing: a divided Maratha house was the EIC's most reliable ally.

Remember

Skeleton from the notes: Intro: the Subsidiary Alliance bought control with promised help; Paramountcy openly made the EIC supreme. Body: (1) SA: the EIC army replaced the State's own army (Bassein 1802), the Peshwa became a puppet; (2) Paramountcy: the EIC to be paramount or supreme and to do anything, even annexation, to protect its interests; Residents now treated Bajirao II 96-18 and Appa Sahib as puppets (the teacher: PoP 1813-58); (3) the loophole shows the gap: the SA let rulers keep retainers (Nagpur 1816), the next step was war and annexation; (4) trajectory: SA, then Paramountcy, then outright annexation (Svarajya 1818, Satara 1848). Closing: each step left the Indian State with less sovereignty.

1 · Why did the Deccan matter so much to the EIC?#

  • The Deccan and the Konkan coast were commercially critical to the EIC: a large coastline, a significant cotton trade from Gujarat with China, and Maratha control over parts of the Malabar coast.
  • The Maratha Confederacy along with Mysore were the last major Indian powers able to check EIC expansion across the subcontinent.
  • Three Anglo-Maratha Wars and four Anglo-Mysore Wars (the previous chapter, Chapter 12) decided the outcome.

2 · First Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82)#

2.1 What triggered the war?

  • The cause was factionalism within the Maratha Confederacy itself, a pattern that repeats across all three wars.
  • In 1773 Raghunath Rao 73-74 became Peshwa after the murder of his nephew Narayan Rao 72-73.
  • In the Baarbhai Conspiracy (1774), Nana Fadnis and eleven other ministers of the Peshwa's council removed Raghunath Rao 73-74 from power.
  • Raghunath Rao 73-74 then approached the EIC's Governor of Bombay, William Hornby, and signed the Treaty of Surat (1775).
  • Under it the EIC agreed to help him regain the Peshwaship in return for Salsette and Bassein (Vasai, near Mumbai) along with some revenue from Bharuch and Surat.
Teacher's board

Everything is underlined: factionalism (the root cause), the names and years of the Peshwas, the Baarbhai Conspiracy 1774, the Governor of Bombay (William Hornby), and the deal itself: "EIC to help him" in return for "Salsette and Bassein" and revenue. The deal is the thing to learn: help for land and money.

Note

The pattern, in the handout's words: this is exactly what happened in the Carnatic and Hyderabad during the Carnatic Wars: a succession dispute, a losing claimant, and the Europeans offering military help in exchange for territory and influence. Factionalism of Indian States was the Europeans' most reliable ally. See Chapter 9.

2.3 Events and the Treaty of Salbai (1782)

  • In 1779, at the Battle of Wadgaon (near Pune), Nana Fadnis, Mahadji Scindhia and Tukoji Rao Holkar defeated the EIC and Raghunath Rao 73-74.
  • But the war continued inconclusively. By 1782 the EIC was in a difficult position, but so were the Marathas, who were also engaged in the 2nd Anglo-Mysore War (1780-84).
Memory hook

Wadgaon 79: N-M-T won.

Decode: Battle of Wadgaon (1779), near Pune: Nana Fadnis, Mahadji Scindhia and Tukoji Rao Holkar beat the EIC and Raghunath Rao 73-74. The war then stalled until 1782 while the Marathas were also busy with the 2nd Anglo-Mysore War (1780-84).

3 · The inter-war period (1782-1803)#

The 21-year gap between the Treaty of Salbai (1782) and the Treaty of Bassein (1802) was not a period of Maratha strength: it was a period of Maratha decline.

YearWhat happened
1794Mahadji Scindhia, who had dominated Maratha politics, dies
1795Madhav Rao II 74-95 commits suicide
1796Bajirao II 96-18 becomes Peshwa (the handout: Peshwa from 1796, formally tenured 1796-1818)
1800Nana Fadnis dies: the one man who had consistently held the Confederacy together for two decades; factionalism accelerates sharply

The Maratha capital, Shaniwar Wada at Pune, and the whole Confederacy were engulfed in factionalism. The empire-builder Wellesley 98-05 was waiting for exactly this opportunity.

Memory hook

94 dies, 95 suicides, 96 crowned, 1800 the glue goes.

Decode: 1794 Mahadji Scindhia dies; 1795 Madhav Rao II 74-95 commits suicide; 1796 Bajirao II 96-18 becomes Peshwa; 1800 Nana Fadnis dies, the Confederacy's glue. The gap from Salbai (1782) to Bassein (1802) is 21 years.

Why it sticks: four consecutive-ish years, four verbs: dies, suicides, crowned, gone; and 82 plus 21 is 1803, one year after Bassein.

4 · Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803-05): Wellesley 98-05's war#

4.1 The trigger: Bassein (December 1802)

  • In 1801 Vithoji Rao Holkar had been plundering the Peshwa's territories. He was captured and brutally executed on the orders of Peshwa Bajirao II 96-18.
  • To avenge the death of his stepbrother, Yashwantrao Holkar attacked and defeated both the Peshwa and Daulat Rao Scindhia (who had supported the Peshwa) in October 1802.
  • Bajirao II 96-18 fled and signed the Treaty of Bassein (December 1802): he agreed to a Subsidiary Alliance with the EIC in exchange for EIC help to regain the Peshwaship. The great army of Svarajya was to be disbanded once the EIC put its now-puppet Bajirao II 96-18 back as Peshwa.
  • The Treaty of Bassein also triggered the 2nd Anglo-Maratha War (1803-05).
  • Ironically, it made Scindhia, Holkar and Bhonsle, who had been rivals, unite against the EIC.
  • The Gaikwad had already signed an SA in June 1802 (Treaty of Cambay) to get EIC help in a succession dispute.
Teacher's board

He underlined Yashwantrao Holkar attacked and defeated, Peshwa, Scindhia, Treaty of Bassein (Dec 1802), Bajirao II 96-18 fled, Subsidiary Alliance, 2nd Anglo-Maratha War (1803-05) and Gaikwad. The sequence of the SAs matters for the exam (slide 72, section 4.3): Gaikwad (June 1802) came before the Peshwa (December 1802).

V-Y-B: Vithoji's Vengeance Brings Bassein.

Why it sticks: Vengeance chain: a stepbrother's blood leads to a Peshwa's flight.

Memory hook

Cambay before Bassein: June, then December.

Why it sticks: June comes before December, and Cambay (a small place) signs before Bassein.

4.2 The battlefield: Assaye (1803)

  • The EIC deployed significant military force.
  • Arthur Wellesley, commanding his Royal regiment alongside the EIC army, fought key engagements, including the Battle of Assaye (1803) against the Marathas.
  • He later described it as the toughest fight of his entire military career, harder, in his view, than anything he faced against Napoleon in Europe.

Arthur Wellesley is not the Governor General: the Governor General is Wellesley 98-05. (See Chapter 11 on the three Wellesley 98-05 brothers.)

4.3 Consequences

The war was lost by the Marathas. The consequences were far-reaching:

  1. Bajirao II 96-18 regained the Peshwaship but was now a puppet of the EIC.

6 · Third Anglo-Maratha War and the Pindari War (1817-19)#

6.1 The Battle of Sitabuldi (November 1817)

  • In late 1817 Bajirao II 96-18, Appa Sahib (regent of the Bhonsle of Nagpur) and Yashwantrao Holkar united in revolt, determined to end the EIC's political domination. (The handout's bracket: this is a violation of SA treaties.) This led to the 3rd Anglo-Maratha War (1817-19).

8 · Why did the Marathas lose?#

Four structural reasons explain the Maratha defeat:

  1. Factionalism.
  2. Absence of unified command. Scindhia, Holkar, Bhonsle and the Gaikwad of Baroda never coordinated effectively.
  3. The EIC's superior finances.
  4. Naval and logistics advantage. The Konkan coast could be blockaded by EIC ships; the EIC could move troops faster across India via sea and better road infrastructure than the Marathas.
Remember

Takeaway (slide 81): the Confederacy fell less from EIC military superiority than from its own structure: factionalism and no unified command, against the EIC's deeper finances and naval-logistics reach. The pattern repeats across all three wars: a divided Maratha house was the EIC's most reliable ally.

Recall sheet#

Read once, hide it, then rebuild the chapter from memory.

  1. Why the Deccan mattered: large coastline, Gujarat-China cotton trade, Maratha control over parts of the Malabar coast; Marathas and Mysore were the last major powers; three Maratha wars and four Mysore wars decided the outcome.
  2. 1st war trigger: factionalism; 1773 Raghunath Rao 73-74 Peshwa after Narayan Rao 72-73's murder; Baarbhai Conspiracy 1774 (Nana Fadnis and eleven ministers); Treaty of Surat 1775 with Hornby: Salsette and Bassein plus revenue from Bharuch and Surat.
  3. Purandhar 1776: Warren Hastings 73-85 with Nana Fadnis; Madhav Rao II 74-95 recognised, Raghunath Rao 73-74 pensioned, Salsette and Bharuch to the EIC, no French; Bombay kept sheltering Raghunath, Nana offered the French a port.
  4. Wadgaon 1779 (Nana Fadnis, Mahadji Scindhia, Tukoji Rao Holkar beat the EIC); Salbai 1782 signed at Gwalior by Mahadji Scindhia: military alliance against Hyder Ali 61-82's retention, Salsette and Bharuch, no French, territory returned, Madhav Rao II 74-95 recognised, Raghunath pensioned.
  5. Triple Confederacy 1780-82: Marathas, Mysore, Hyderabad; 1st Maratha War 1775-82 and 2nd Mysore War 1780-84; the pension was a deliberate EIC practice.
  6. Inter-war: 21 years of decline; Mahadji d. 1794; Madhav Rao II 74-95 suicide 1795; Bajirao II 96-18 Peshwa 1796; Nana Fadnis d. 1800.
  7. 2nd war trigger: Vithoji Rao Holkar executed 1801; Yashwantrao Holkar defeats Peshwa and Daulat Rao Scindhia Oct 1802; Bassein Dec 1802 (SA, Svarajya's army to be disbanded); Cambay June 1802 (Gaikwad); Scindhia, Holkar, Bhonsle unite.
  8. Assaye 1803: Arthur Wellesley's Royal regiment with the EIC army; his "toughest fight", harder than Napoleon.
  9. 2nd war results: Peshwa puppet; Scindhia loses all north of the Yamuna (Delhi, Agra) and Gujarat, signs SA, Resident in Delhi (Indirect Rule, Shah Alam II 60-06); Bhonsle loses Orissa and signs a mini alliance; Jats, Rajputs, Bundelas sign SA; Wellesley 98-05 recalled.
  10. SA order: Gaikwad 1802, Peshwa 1802, Scindhia 1803/04, Bhonsle 1816, Holkar 1818. Cornwallis 86-93 sent for non-interference: arrived July 1805, died October 1805.
  11. Vellore Mutiny 1806 under George Barlow: dress code against tilak and turban, leather cockade (cow or pig skin); "Prelude to 1857".
  12. Pindaris: disbanded soldiers of Scindhia and Holkar raiding the Northern Sarkars and Madras; Francis Hastings 13-23; Paramountcy 1813-58: EIC supreme, even annexation; Residents treat Bajirao II 96-18 as a puppet.
