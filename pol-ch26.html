1 · Roots and idea of Union Territories#

Union Territories began as British arrangements for backward and strategically important regions, and became a flexible tool of Indian federalism.

How the UT idea evolved

1874
Scheduled Districts Act creates "scheduled districts" for special governance
Later
They become Chief Commissioner's Provinces: Delhi, Coorg, Ajmer-Merwara, Andaman and Nicobar Islands
1935
Government of India Act, 1935: provinces centrally controlled through Chief Commissioners, the early model of direct Union administration
1950
Constitution classifies territories into Part A, B, C and D States; Parts C and D are governed directly by the Centre
1956
States Reorganisation Act, 1956 and the 7th Amendment (1956) merge the categories and give the modern concept of Union Territories

Several UTs later became full States: Himachal Pradesh, Manipur, Tripura, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa, showing a dynamic and flexible federal design.

Origin shortcut

74 (1874) scheduled, 35 (1935) commissioners, 50 (1950) Parts, 56 (1956) UTs born.

4 · How UTs are administered#

Articles 239 to 241 (Part VIII) govern UTs. All fall under the direct control of the Centre, but structures differ.

1. Administration by the President

  • Every UT is administered by the President, acting through an Administrator he appoints.
  • The Administrator is an agent of the President, not a constitutional head like a State Governor.
  • The President decides the designation: Lieutenant Governor, Chief Commissioner, or simply Administrator.
  • The President may appoint the Governor of a neighbouring State as Administrator of an adjoining UT; he then acts independently of the State's Council of Ministers.

2. Legislative and executive framework

  • Three UTs have a Legislative Assembly and a Council of Ministers headed by a Chief Minister: Delhi (1992), Puducherry (1963), Jammu and Kashmir (2019).
  • The other five are governed directly by the Centre through Administrators.
  • Elected institutions do not dilute the supreme authority of the President and Parliament.

3. Legislative powers of Parliament

4. President's power to make regulations

  • For UTs without legislatures, the President can issue regulations for peace, progress and good governance. The handout names Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep, Ladakh, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu.
  • These regulations have the same force as Acts of Parliament and may amend or repeal Central laws.
  • In Puducherry the power applies only when the Assembly is suspended or dissolved.
Handout alert

Five UTs have no legislature (including Chandigarh), but the regulation-making list above names only four and omits Chandigarh. Use the handout's list as given for answers, and remember the point is "UTs without legislatures".

5 · Delhi (NCT): Arts. 239AA and 239AB#

The 69th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991 re-designated Delhi as the National Capital Territory and its administrator as the Lieutenant Governor, inserting Articles 239AA and 239AB. Delhi combines features of a UT and a State.

Delhi at a glance

LawsState and Concurrent Lists except public order, police and land; Parliament's law prevails
Council of MinistersHeaded by the CM; up to 10% of the Assembly (i.e. seven)

Issue orders and make temporary arrangements to run Delhi under Articles 239 and 239AA

Ordinance power

LG
May promulgate ordinances during the recess of the Assembly, with the force of an Act of the Assembly

The Assembly must approve it within six weeks of reassembly

GNCTD (Amendment) Act, 2021

  • Delhi Assembly rules must not be inconsistent with those of the Lok Sabha.
  • The Assembly cannot make a rule allowing it to inquire into administrative decisions; all earlier rules on this are void.
  • The Delhi Government must obtain the LG's opinion before acting on decisions.
  • In these laws and documents, "Government" means the Lieutenant Governor.
  • The handout calls it an attempt to overrule the Supreme Court, which settled the position of the Centre and the Delhi Government in 2018.
Delhi's key numbers
STT

Seventy members, Ten per cent ministers (seven), Two articles (239AA, 239AB): "Seven Ten Two".

6 · Puducherry: Art. 239A#

Article 239A (inserted by the 14th Amendment Act, 1962) lets Parliament create legislatures and councils of ministers for certain UTs, including Puducherry. Parliament then passed the Government of Union Territories Act, 1963.

Puducherry's special provisions
FeatureDetail
AssemblyUnicameral; up to 33 members: 30 elected and up to 3 nominated by the Centre
Nominated membersUnique power of the Centre, upheld by the Supreme Court (2021): equal voting rights as elected members
LawsState and Concurrent Lists, subject to Parliament's overriding power; the President may give directions so laws fit national policy
ExecutiveThe LG is administrative head; the CM-led Council aids and advises on matters within the Assembly's competence
Discretion and differencesLG acts independently where discretion is required or where differences arise; such matters go to the President for final decision
President's regulationsOnly when the Assembly is suspended or dissolved; same force as Acts of Parliament, can amend or repeal laws
RegionsFour separated regions: Puducherry, Karaikal, Mahe, Yanam (Tamil, Malayalam and Telugu-speaking)

7 · Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh#

The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, in force from 31 October 2019, split the erstwhile State into two UTs to improve governance, security and regional development.

Jammu and Kashmir (with Legislature)

  • Legislative Assembly on the Delhi and Puducherry model
  • LG is administrative head and acts on aid and advice of a CM-led Council
  • laws on State and Concurrent Lists except public order and police
  • Parliament can legislate on any subject

Ladakh (without Legislature)

  • Administered by the President through the Lieutenant Governor
  • no elected legislature
  • LG exercises executive powers with Central officers
  • Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils (LAHDCs) for Leh and Kargil manage local development and cultural affairs
  • Both share one High Court of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh.
  • Parliament can make laws for both; the President may issue regulations for Ladakh for peace, progress and good governance.
J&K shortcut

J&K = Assembly, minus police and public order. Ladakh = no Assembly, hill councils (Leh, Kargil).

8 · Advisory Committees for UTs#

The Ministry of Home Affairs (the nodal ministry for UTs) set up advisory committees under the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, especially for UTs without legislatures.

Two advisory committees
CommitteeChairMembers and role
Home Minister's Advisory Committee (HMAC)Union Home MinisterHigh-level body for A&N Islands, Ladakh, Lakshadweep, Chandigarh and DNH and DD; discusses social, economic and infrastructure issues; reviews central schemes; guides administrators
Administrator's Advisory Committee (AAC)Administrator or Lt. Governor of that UTMPs, elected local representatives (District Panchayats or Municipal Councils) and UT officials; gives local feedback, coordination and accountability

Together they blend local representation with central oversight.

HMAC against AAC
HA

Home Minister heads the High-level one; Administrator heads the Advisory one at Administrator level: "Home is High, Administrator is At home".

Quick test

If the question says "Council of Ministers headed by a Chief Minister", the UT is Delhi, Puducherry or J&K. Everything else is run by an Administrator or LG under the President.
