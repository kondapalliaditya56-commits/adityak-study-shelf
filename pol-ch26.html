In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Union Territories (UTs) are areas run directly by the Centre under Arts. 239 to 241 (Part VIII). Their roots lie in the Scheduled Districts Act, 1874 and the Chief Commissioner's Provinces
  • After 1950 Part C and D territories were merged into UTs by the States Reorganisation Act and 7th Amendment (1956)
  • There are eight UTs today: Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Delhi, Lakshadweep, Puducherry, Chandigarh, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu (2020).
  • Each UT is administered by the President through an Administrator, who is the President's agent, not a constitutional head like a Governor. The title is Lieutenant Governor (LG) in five UTs and Administrator in three.
  • Only three UTs have an Assembly and a Chief Minister: Delhi (1992), Puducherry (1963), Jammu and Kashmir (2019). Even there, Parliament can make laws on any subject and its law prevails.
  • For UTs without a legislature the President makes regulations that carry the force of an Act of Parliament
  • Puducherry only when its Assembly is suspended or dissolved
  • Delhi is special: 69th Amendment (1991), Arts. 239AA and 239AB, 70 seats, ministers up to 10% (seven). It is also the only UT with its own High Court (1966).

1 · Roots and idea of Union Territories#

  • Union Territories began as British arrangements for backward and strategically important regions, and became a flexible tool of Indian federalism.

How the UT idea evolved

  • 1874: Scheduled Districts Act creates "scheduled districts" for special governance
  • Later: They become Chief Commissioner's Provinces: Delhi, Coorg, Ajmer-Merwara, Andaman and Nicobar Islands
  • 1935: Government of India Act, 1935: provinces centrally controlled through Chief Commissioners, the early model of direct Union administration
  • 1950:
    • Constitution classifies territories into Part A, B, C and D States
    • Parts C and D are governed directly by the Centre
  • 1956: States Reorganisation Act, 1956 and the 7th Amendment (1956) merge the categories and give the modern concept of Union Territories

How the UT idea evolved
  • Several UTs later became full States: Himachal Pradesh, Manipur, Tripura, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa, showing a dynamic and flexible federal design.
  • Shortcut: Origin shortcut. 74 (1874) scheduled, 35 (1935) commissioners, 50 (1950) Parts, 56 (1956) UTs born.

2 · Why UTs were created#

  • Statehood was impractical for each of these, for a different reason.

Political and administrative convenienceDelhi, ChandigarhCentral control for efficient national governance; neutral administration as national capital and joint capital
Cultural and historical distinctivenessPuducherry, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and DiuPreserve the French and Portuguese colonial heritage; special administrative treatment
Strategic and security importanceAndaman and Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep, LadakhKey maritime and border positions; defence and surveillance justify direct Central administration
Tribal and backward regionsFormer UTs Manipur, Tripura, MizoramFocused governance and development before statehood

4 · How UTs are administered#

  • Articles 239 to 241 (Part VIII) govern UTs. All fall under the direct control of the Centre, but structures differ.

1. Administration by the President

  • Every UT is administered by the President, acting through an Administrator he appoints.
  • The Administrator is an agent of the President, not a constitutional head like a State Governor.
  • The President decides the designation: Lieutenant Governor, Chief Commissioner, or simply Administrator.
  • The President may appoint the Governor of a neighbouring State as Administrator of an adjoining UT
  • He then acts independently of the State's Council of Ministers

2. Legislative and executive framework

  • Three UTs have a Legislative Assembly and a Council of Ministers headed by a Chief Minister: Delhi (1992), Puducherry (1963), Jammu and Kashmir (2019).
  • The other five are governed directly by the Centre through Administrators.
  • Elected institutions do not dilute the supreme authority of the President and Parliament.

3. Legislative powers of Parliament

  • Memory aid (Delhi's three barred subjects): Public order, Police, Land: "Polite People Leave".

4. President's power to make regulations

  • For UTs without legislatures, the President can issue regulations for peace, progress and good governance. The handout names Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep, Ladakh, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu.
  • These regulations have the same force as Acts of Parliament and may amend or repeal Central laws.
  • In Puducherry the power applies only when the Assembly is suspended or dissolved.

5 · Delhi (NCT): Arts. 239AA and 239AB#

  • The 69th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991 re-designated Delhi as the National Capital Territory and its administrator as the Lieutenant Governor, inserting Articles 239AA and 239AB. Delhi combines features of a UT and a State.

Delhi at a glance

FeatureDetail
Assembly70 directly elected members; elections by the Election Commission of India
LawsState and Concurrent Lists except public order, police and land; Parliament's law prevails
Council of MinistersHeaded by the CM; up to 10% of the Assembly (i.e. seven)
AppointmentCM appointed by the President; other ministers on the CM's advice
Tenure and dutyHold office during the President's pleasure; collectively responsible to the Assembly
RoleAids and advises the LG, except where the LG must act in his discretion

When the LG and ministers disagree

  • Disagreement: Between the LG and the Council of Ministers
  • Referred to: The President
  • Decision: Binding: Central oversight of the capital

President's Rule in Delhi (Art. 239AB)

  • Trigger:
    • Constitutional machinery fails
    • Normal governance breaks down (on the LG's report or otherwise)
  • President may: Suspend Article 239AA or laws made under it
  • Then: Issue orders and make temporary arrangements to run Delhi under Articles 239 and 239AA
  • Compare: Works like Article 356 for States
President's Rule in Delhi (Art. 239AB)

Ordinance power

  • LG: May promulgate ordinances during the recess of the Assembly, with the force of an Act of the Assembly
  • Condition: No ordinance can be issued or withdrawn without the President's prior approval
  • After: The Assembly must approve it within six weeks of reassembly

GNCTD (Amendment) Act, 2021

  • Delhi Assembly rules must not be inconsistent with those of the Lok Sabha.
  • The Assembly cannot make a rule allowing it to inquire into administrative decisions
  • All earlier rules on this are void
  • The Delhi Government must obtain the LG's opinion before acting on decisions.
  • In these laws and documents, "Government" means the Lieutenant Governor.
  • The handout calls it an attempt to overrule the Supreme Court, which settled the position of the Centre and the Delhi Government in 2018.
  • Memory aid (Delhi's key numbers): Seventy members, Ten per cent ministers (seven), Two articles (239AA, 239AB): "Seven Ten Two".

Also in the handout

  • All previous rules in this regard are void.

6 · Puducherry: Art. 239A#

  • Article 239A (inserted by the 14th Amendment Act, 1962) lets Parliament create legislatures and councils of ministers for certain UTs, including Puducherry. Parliament then passed the Government of Union Territories Act, 1963.

7 · Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh#

  • The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, in force from 31 October 2019, split the erstwhile State into two UTs to improve governance, security and regional development.

J&K against Ladakh

  • Jammu and Kashmir (with Legislature):
    • Legislative Assembly on the Delhi and Puducherry model
    • LG is administrative head and acts on aid and advice of a CM-led Council
    • Laws on State and Concurrent Lists except public order and police
    • Parliament can legislate on any subject
  • Ladakh (without Legislature):
    • Administered by the President through the Lieutenant Governor
    • No elected legislature
    • LG exercises executive powers with Central officers
    • Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils (LAHDCs) for Leh and Kargil manage local development and cultural affairs
  • Both share one High Court of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh.
  • Parliament can make laws for both
  • The President may issue regulations for Ladakh for peace, progress and good governance
  • Shortcut: J&K shortcut. J&K = Assembly, minus police and public order. Ladakh = no Assembly, hill councils (Leh, Kargil).

Traps#

Trap 1 · Puducherry: 1962 or 1963 The handout gives 1962 as the year Puducherry became a UT (14th Amendment, Art. 239A) but 1963 as the year it got its Assembly (Government of Union Territories Act, 1963). Rule: 1962 = UT status; 1963 = Legislative Assembly.
Trap 2 · Delhi: 1956, 1991 or 1992 Delhi became a UT in 1956. The 69th Amendment is of 1991, and the table says it was re-designated as NCT in 1992. Rule: amendment 1991, NCT and Assembly from 1992.
Trap 3 · What each Assembly cannot touch Delhi cannot legislate on public order, police and land. J&K cannot touch public order and police. Puducherry has no such exclusion. In all three, Parliament's law prevails. Rule: Delhi = 3 excluded subjects (police, public order, land); J&K = 2; Puducherry = none.
Trap 4 · Administrator versus Governor An Administrator is only the President's agent. If the President names a neighbouring State's Governor as Administrator, that Governor acts independently of the State's Council of Ministers. Rule: UT head = President's agent; a Governor acting as Administrator is not bound by State ministers.

Book edition: simple words and memory words#

In simple words

  • Union Territories (UTs) are areas run directly by the Centre under Arts. 239 to 241 (Part VIII). Their roots lie in the Scheduled Districts Act, 1874 and the Chief Commissioner's Provinces; after 1950 Part C and D territories were merged into UTs by the States Reorganisation Act and 7th Amendment (1956).
  • There are eight UTs today: Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Delhi, Lakshadweep, Puducherry, Chandigarh, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu (2020).
  • Each UT is administered by the President through an Administrator, who is the President's agent, not a constitutional head like a Governor. The title is Lieutenant Governor (LG) in five UTs and Administrator in three.
  • Only three UTs have an Assembly and a Chief Minister: Delhi (1992), Puducherry (1963), Jammu and Kashmir (2019). Even there, Parliament can make laws on any subject and its law prevails.
  • For UTs without a legislature the President makes regulations that carry the force of an Act of Parliament; Puducherry only when its Assembly is suspended or dissolved.
  • Delhi is special: 69th Amendment (1991), Arts. 239AA and 239AB, 70 seats, ministers up to 10% (seven). It is also the only UT with its own High Court (1966).

Key terms

Union Territory
An area under the direct control of the Central Government; governed under Arts. 239 to 241
Administrator / LG
The President's agent who runs a UT; the President decides the title (LG, Chief Commissioner or Administrator)
Scheduled Districts Act, 1874
British law creating special 'scheduled districts' that later became Chief Commissioner's Provinces
Regulation (President)
Law for peace, progress and good governance of a UT without a legislature; equals an Act of Parliament and can amend or repeal Central laws
NCT of Delhi
National Capital Territory, status given by the 69th Amendment (1991) with Arts. 239AA and 239AB
Art. 239AB
President may suspend Art. 239AA and take over Delhi if normal governance breaks down, like Art. 356 for States
Art. 239A
Inserted by the 14th Amendment (1962); lets Parliament create legislatures and councils of ministers for certain UTs such as Puducherry
HMAC / AAC
Home Minister's Advisory Committee and Administrator's Advisory Committee for UTs without legislatures, set up by MHA (the nodal ministry)
