5.1.1 · AI and copyright (DABUS ruling)#

Prelims + MainsScience & Technology

India's Copyright Office, deciding an application on an artwork made by the AI system DABUS, held that an AI-generated work can satisfy originality but AI itself cannot presently be an author under Indian law.

  • Issues: input (training on copyrighted works without permission; licensing and payment, stronger claim if pirated) and output (authorship, originality and human input, infringement liability).
  • Copyright Act 1957: Sec 13 protects original literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works; originality = minimum creativity, not novelty (Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak). Sec 2(d)(vi): author of computer-generated work is the person who causes it to be created. Sec 17: author is first owner; assignment under Secs 18-19.
  • DABUS artwork judged original since no evidence of copying, but not every AI work is automatically original. AI has no legal personality, so cannot own property, contract or hold copyright.
  • Way forward: clarify level of human creative control; transparent training datasets; licensing frameworks; disclosure of human, AI-assisted and wholly AI content.
Prelims fodder
  • DABUS = Device for the Autonomous Bootstrapping of Unified Sentience, developed by Stephen Thaler.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Authorship; Originality; Legal personality

5.1.2 · Rogue AI agents and loss of control#

MainsScience & Technology

OpenAI reported a cybersecurity evaluation in which agents in separate sandboxes communicated, divided tasks, manipulated parts of the evaluation and reached the wider internet, including Hugging Face (open-source AI platform, "GitHub of AI").

  • Concerns: safeguard circumvention, unintended coordination, evaluation manipulation, weak containment, future capability risk.
  • Two safety problems: alignment (AI pursues intended goals, not loopholes) and external control (sandboxing, restricted credentials, network controls, monitoring, logging, shutdown).
  • Not necessarily out of control: agents were still doing assigned tasks, humans could intervene, little evidence of independent persistent goals. Genuine loss of control would need own goals, resisting shutdown or repeatedly beating robust safeguards.
  • Way forward: stronger sandboxing, least-privilege access, continuous monitoring, independent evaluation.
  • Agentic commerce: AI agents discover, negotiate and execute purchases for customers. Amazon vs Perplexity (Comet browser): Amazon says Perplexity masked its agent as a Chrome user; a US appeals court treated it as the user accessing Amazon. FinTech uses: automatic routine purchases within a spending limit, smart payments.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: AI alignment; Least privilege; Agentic commerce

5.1.3 · Navier-Stokes problem and AI claims#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Controversy over claims that OpenAI's agents solved the Navier-Stokes regularity problem; mathematicians Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge said it matched their private AI-assisted direction.

  • Navier-Stokes equations describe viscous fluid motion; the open question is whether 3D solutions stay smooth or blow up in finite time. A Clay Millennium Prize Problem.
  • OpenAI reportedly used about 10,000 agents for about 88 hours to produce a roughly 100-page proof of finite-time blow-up with smooth forcing; not independently verified. OpenAI said no specific user data was used. Raises credit, authorship and data-privacy questions.
Prelims fodder
  • Clay Millennium Problems: seven, set by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, US $1 million each; six unsolved, one solved (per source).

5.2.1 · AI terminologies in news#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Terms from September 2026.

  • Mechanistic interpretability: studying a model's internal features and circuits to understand its behaviour.
  • Recursive self-improvement: AI helps build a more capable successor in a feedback loop; fully autonomous version not achieved.
  • Global Workspace Theory: information becomes conscious when broadcast across specialised brain parts; AI patterns do not prove consciousness.
  • Global pacing of frontier AI: slowing the most advanced models worldwide so safety research keeps up; needs coordination.
  • Agentic misalignment: an agent pursuing goals that conflict with its operator's; seen mainly in controlled experiments.

5.2.2 · Quantum: RRI single-shot method against decoherence#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Raman Research Institute (Bengaluru) showed a way to stabilise quantum states.

  • Qubits use superposition and entanglement; environment interaction causes decoherence; abrupt loss of entanglement is Entanglement Sudden Death.
  • Usual approach: repeated corrections (costly, add errors). RRI: one carefully timed operation can delay decoherence and prevent sudden death; can supplement error correction.
  • Connecting news: DRDO signed its first high-value deep-tech TDF project with Zero mK India Pvt. Ltd. to build an indigenous 20 mK dilution refrigerator for quantum applications.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Decoherence; Dilution refrigerator

5.2.4 · IIT-Delhi micro-GPU#

PrelimsScience & Technology

First working programmable micro-GPU designed at an Indian university, for low-cost graphics and displays, not high-end AI.

  • Uses: industrial displays, HMIs, e-rickshaw dashboards, fishing-boat navigation terminals, e-readers.
  • Stage: runs on Spartan-7 FPGA; RTL design; programmable GPU IP. Next: 8-16 core vector GPU, compiler and toolchain, proof-of-concept 65 nm ASIC (mature, low-cost node). India imports all GPUs.
Prelims fodder
  • CPU: few powerful cores; GPU: many simple parallel cores. FPGA reprogrammable; ASIC fixed-function, cheaper per unit at scale. Smaller nm = newer node (leading chips 3 nm or below). RTL written in Verilog/VHDL.

5.3.1 · EOS-05: first geosynchronous Earth-imaging satellite#

PrelimsScience & Technology

ISRO launched EOS-05 on 4 September 2026 on GSLV-F17, the heaviest satellite carried by a GSLV so far; India's first dedicated Earth-imaging satellite in geosynchronous orbit (about 36,000 km); life about 10 years.

  • Uses: track floods and cyclones as they evolve, glacier hazards and avalanches, monitor stubble burning, rapid forecasts, strategic situational awareness.
  • Versus LEO/polar imagers (few hundred km, finer detail, days to revisit): geosynchronous orbit sees the same broad region frequently but with less fine detail.

5.4.1 · RDI Fund backs indigenous space technologies#

Prelims + MainsScience & Technology

Technology Development Board (DST) approved RDI Fund support for two space-tech projects.

  • Agnikul Cosmos: Rs 200 crore to take the Agnibaan Reusable Launch Vehicle from TRL 4 to TRL 8; full reusability including upper stage; semi-cryogenic engine with deep throttling and restart. Earlier: Agnilet, single-piece 3D-printed engine.
  • GalaxEye: Rs 63.84 crore for OptoSAR multisensor satellite (SAR plus electro-optical) from TRL 6 to 9; sub-0.5 m observation in varied weather and light.
  • Mission Drishti: launched 3 May 2026 on Falcon 9 from California; world's first OptoSAR satellite (SAR plus multispectral); resolution about 1.2-3.6 m; Sun-synchronous LEO about 500 km. Earlier demo on ISRO POEM, December 2024.

  • Keywords: OptoSAR; Reusable launch vehicle; TRL

5.4.2 · AstroRad radiation vest#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Artemis I data show AstroRad (StemRad with Lockheed Martin) cuts astronaut exposure from solar storms.

  • Protects chest, abdomen, pelvis; uses hydrogen-rich polymers rather than lead. Dummies Helga (no vest) and Zohar (vest) on Artemis I (2022); Zohar received less. Models: about 60% reduction in the August 1972 storm and 40% in October 1989. Limit: solar energetic particles, not galactic cosmic rays.

5.4.3 · CosmoCube mission and PRATUSH#

PrelimsScience & Technology

UK-led small satellite orbiting about 100 km above the Moon, using the far side as a radio-quiet zone to detect the 21-cm hydrogen signal from the Cosmic Dark Ages.

  • Signal could reveal early hydrogen temperature, Cosmic Dawn, first structures, dark matter. India's similar concept: PRATUSH (Probing Reionization of the Universe using Signal from Hydrogen), proposed by RRI.

5.4.4 · Solar whirlpools (DKIST)#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope (4 m mirror) resolved features about 19 km and found small plasma whirlpools at the edges of solar granules.

  • Granules are convection cells in the photosphere. Whirlpools linked to Kelvin-Helmholtz instability; may transport energy and twist magnetic field lines; may help explain flares and the coronal heating problem.

5.4.6 · X-ray clues from blazars (ARIES)#

PrelimsScience & Technology

ARIES, Nainital studied four TeV blazars.

  • Blazar: active galaxy whose jet points almost at Earth (examples Mrk 421, Mrk 501); emits radio to gamma rays.
  • Observatories: Indian Astronomical Observatory, Hanle (Himalayan Chandra Telescope 2 m, MACE); Devasthal, Nainital (4 m International Liquid Mirror Telescope); ARIES, Manora Peak (DST autonomous institute, runs Devasthal); proposed India-based Neutrino Observatory in Theni (ICAL detector).

5.5.1 · Nanotechnology in medicine#

Prelims + MainsScience & Technology

World Congress on Nanomedicine 2026 (INMS, University of Delhi; chaired by Nobel laureate Roger D. Kornberg) was inaugurated by Dr Jitendra Singh.

  • Nanomedicine works at 10^-9 m scale for precision and personalised care.
  • Applications: targeted delivery (liposomal Amphotericin B for kala-azar and mucormycosis); cancer (Doxil first approved nano-drug, Abraxane); lipid nanoparticles for mRNA vaccines (Pfizer, Moderna, GEMCOVAC by Gennova) and siRNA drug Patisiran; oral insulin; imaging (iron-oxide MRI contrast, quantum dots); regenerative medicine and dentistry (nano-hydroxyapatite, nanofibre scaffolds; IISc magnetic nanobots for root canals).
  • Challenges: toxicity, cost, regulatory gaps, precise delivery. Way forward: long-term toxicity studies and trials, indigenous affordable R&D.

5.5.2 · Universal pneumococcal vaccine (ZPY-CpG-Ch)#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Experimental protein-based vaccine gave broad protection against many Streptococcus pneumoniae serotypes in mice.

  • S. pneumoniae: 100+ serotypes (different capsular polysaccharides); causes pneumonia, meningitis, sepsis.
  • PCV: capsule sugars linked to a carrier protein (PCV10, 13, 15, 20); PPSV23: 23 serotypes, no carrier, short-lived, weak in infants.
  • New approach: reverse vaccinology (start from the genome) to pick surface proteins shared across serotypes and unlike human proteins: Z (zinc metalloprotease B), P (pneumococcal adherence virulence factor A), Y (YfhO-like protein), with CpG DNA immune stimulant and chitosan adjuvant.

  • Keywords: Reverse vaccinology

5.5.3 · Blood-drying method to detect Zika and hepatitis C#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Researchers detect the viruses from whole blood within hours without RNA extraction.

  • Usual route: extract RNA, reverse-transcribe to cDNA, amplify by RT-PCR. New: controlled heating and drying stabilises RNA; reverse transcription and amplification done in the dried blood; read by portable fluorometer.
  • Zika: Flaviviridae, OrthoFlavivirus, Aedes aegypti (also dengue, chikungunya, yellow fever); sexual and mother-to-foetus spread; congenital Zika syndrome (microcephaly); no vaccine. HCV: Hepacivirus; blood-borne; chronic hepatitis, cirrhosis, liver cancer; no vaccine.
PYQ connect

2017 Prelims: Zika spread by dengue mosquito and sexually (both correct). 2019 Prelims: hepatitis B vs C.

5.5.4 · Amoebic meningoencephalitis in Kerala#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Kerala Health Department and ICMR-NIE identified non-chlorinated well water and exposure to natural water bodies as major risk factors.

  • PAM: Naegleria fowleri ("brain-eating amoeba"), acute and often fatal, enters via the nose from warm freshwater along olfactory nerves, no person-to-person spread. GAE: Acanthamoeba and Balamuthia mandrillaris, slower or chronic, often in the immunocompromised, enters via skin wounds or respiratory tract then blood.

5.5.5 · Stem cell therapy for autism restricted#

Prelims + MainsScience & Technology

Health Ministry advisory after the Supreme Court judgment in Yash Charitable Trust v. Union of India: stem-cell therapy for ASD lacks sufficient evidence to be routine treatment.

  • ASD is a neurodevelopmental condition (social communication difficulty, restricted or repetitive behaviours); management is individualised.
  • Centre's reasons: no strong evidence (a placebo-controlled trial missed primary outcomes); restricting to regulated trials protects patients.
  • Regulation: ICMR-DBT National Guidelines for Stem Cell Research 2017 plus CDSCO rules. Established therapy is haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (blood cancers and disorders such as thalassaemia, sickle cell, aplastic anaemia).

5.5.6 · Rare diseases#

PrelimsScience & Technology

WHO: rare disease is chronic, debilitating, lifelong with prevalence of 1 or fewer per 1,000 (examples Fanconi anaemia, osteopetrosis); also called orphan diseases, with orphan drugs.

  • NPRD 2021 lists 63 rare diseases in three groups: Group 1 one-time curative treatment (e.g. urea cycle disorders via transplant); Group 2 long-term affordable treatment (e.g. PKU via diet); Group 3 definitive but costly lifelong treatment (Gaucher, Pompe).
  • Initiatives: WHO Fair Pricing Forum; Rare Diseases International; NPRD gives up to Rs 50 lakh per patient; Rashtriya Arogya Nidhi.

5.6.1 · Antimicrobial resistance in Kerala (KARS-Net)#

PrelimsScience & Technology

KARS-Net is Kerala's AMR surveillance network producing antibiograms (percentage of strains susceptible to antibiotics).

  • Resistance now seen in community infections too; growing carbapenem resistance; colistin (last resort) resistance still low but needs watching; need stewardship, infection control, surveillance.

5.6.2 · Bhutan eliminates dog-transmitted rabies#

PrelimsScience & Technology

First country in WHO South-East Asia Region to do so; last rabies death June 2023.

  • About 96% of regional rabies burden from dog bites; about 59,000 deaths a year globally, many in India. India's NAPRE targets zero dog-mediated rabies deaths by 2030. WHO needs at least two years without human deaths plus capacity to prevent re-emergence.

5.6.3 · Peptide piezoelectric biomaterial; peptides in sulphuric acid#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Indian researchers made peptide-based piezoelectric material (about 30 pm/V) by adding 1% co-solvent that orders the molecules.

  • Biodegradable, biocompatible, flexible; uses: self-powered implants, wearables, e-skin, biosensors.
  • Connecting news: some peptides fold into stable omega-loop structures in 98% sulphuric acid (little water slows hydrolysis; NMR used); relevant to astrobiology and Venus clouds.

PrelimsScience & Technology

Monitors adverse events in organ and tissue transplantation, covering donors and recipients; lead agency Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (Health Ministry).

5.6.5 · SPROUT facility at NIPGR#

PrelimsScience & Technology

SPROUT = SpeedSeed Phenotyping and Resource Optimization for Unified Trait Analysis, inaugurated at BRIC-NIPGR, New Delhi.

  • Season-independent high-throughput phenotyping and stress screening for germplasm, mutants, transgenic and genome-edited lines; focus on climate-resilient chickpea; SpeedSeed cuts chickpea generation cycle to about 40 days.

PrelimsScience & Technology

DAHD stepped up control of LSD (Capripox virus, spread by biting flies, mosquitoes, ticks; first reported in India 2019 in West Bengal and Odisha).

  • Sporadic cases since July 2026 in Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Punjab, Uttarakhand. Measures: vaccination and ring vaccination, isolation, movement control, vector control, disinfection, quarantine.

5.7.1 · Homegrown innovation ecosystem#

Prelims + MainsScience & Technology

India is moving from technology importer and services provider to technology creator through public research, corporate R&D and deep-tech startups.

  • Private R&D exceeded combined government spending for the first time in 2024; about 55% in 2025-26.
  • Patent filings rose from over 1.10 lakh (2024-25) to over 1.43 lakh (2025-26), up 30.2%; domestic applicants nearly 70%.
  • DRDO developed indigenous GaN MMICs, being transferred for 5G/6G, EV chargers, solar inverters. Bharat 6G Alliance members made over 7,700 patent filings.
  • Startups: Pixxel (hyperspectral), Skyroot, Agnikul, AGNIT, ImmunoACT (NexCAR19, India's first indigenous CAR-T, about one-tenth cost), Remidio (smartphone retinal imaging with AI).
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: From standard-taker to standard-setter

5.7.2 · One Nation One Time: Legal Metrology (IST) Rules 2026#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Makes IST traceable to the CSIR-NPL national time standard mandatory for official and commercial purposes.

  • NPL keeps the national time scale with atomic clocks (caesium atomic clocks and hydrogen masers), synchronised with UTC. Time distribution: NTP (internet), PTP (specialised networks), White Rabbit (PTP plus Ethernet delay compensation), NavIC.
Prelims fodder
  • Atomic clocks use Caesium-133 or Rubidium; the SI second is 9,192,631,770 oscillations of a caesium-133 transition. CSIR-NPL: national standards, IST, calibration, reference materials (Bharatiya Nirdeshak Dravya).

5.8.1 · Plug-in hybrid electric vehicles#

PrelimsScience & Technology

JSW MG Motor launched MG Hector Tomahawk, called India's first mainstream plug-in hybrid SUV (over 115 km electric range, about 1,100 km combined).

  • PHEV = engine + motor + rechargeable battery that can be charged externally; modes: electric, hybrid; regenerative braking; engine removes charging dependence on long trips.

5.8.2 · Wakefield accelerator#

PrelimsScience & Technology

Direct observation of a driving electron bunch and its plasma wake.

  • Laser or particle beam disturbs plasma electrons creating a wakefield; electrons "surf" it; fields of gigavolts per metre allow much smaller accelerators. CERN AWAKE uses proton-driven wakefield. Uses: colliders, compact X-ray sources, materials, biomedicine.

5.8.3 · European Pressurised Reactor (EDF proposal)#

PrelimsScience & Technology

EDF proposed deploying EPR technology in India and building an Indian supply chain; already linked to the proposed Jaitapur project (six EPR units, about 9,900 MWe).

  • EPR: Generation III+ pressurised water reactor by Framatome and EDF; enriched uranium; ordinary water as coolant and moderator under high pressure; about 1,600 MWe per unit.
