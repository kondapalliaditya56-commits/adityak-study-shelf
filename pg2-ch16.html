1 · Speaker OF THE LOK Sabha · Powers · Impartiality · Reforms#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Executive and Legislature handout pp. 20-22. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • The Speaker presides, certifies Money Bills, decides anti-defection cases and guards privileges, yet is usually a party member. Safeguards (tenure, pay on the Consolidated Fund, casting vote only) exist; reform ideas are party-neutral convention and an independent anti-defection tribunal.

Powers AND Functions

Presiding

  • presides over Lok Sabha and joint sittings (Art 108)
  • Order
  • Points of order
  • Interpretation of rules
  • adjourn/suspend for disorder or lack of quorum (one-tenth)

Procedure

  • admissibility of questions, motions, resolutions
  • Regulates debate
  • Expunges unparliamentary words
  • May allow a secret sitting

Money Bill (Art 110)

  • Speaker decides
  • 110(3) says final
  • Rojer Mathew v. South Indian Bank (2019): certification is not immune from judicial review

Anti-defection

  • decides disqualification under the Tenth Schedule
  • Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992): decisions subject to judicial review

Committees, privileges

  • Guardian of House rights
  • Appoints chairs of several committees
  • chairs Business Advisory, Rules, General Purposes committees
  • Ex officio President of the Indian Parliamentary Group
  • Chairs Conference of Presiding Officers

Vote (Art 100)

  • No vote in the first instance
  • Casting vote on a tie

Independence AND ITS Limits

Safeguards

  • removal needs effective majority and 14 days' notice
  • salary charged on Consolidated Fund
  • Art 122: proceedings not questioned for procedural irregularity (constitutional violations are reviewable)
  • casting vote only
  • Tenth Schedule: may give up party membership on election without disqualification
  • ranks 7th in the Table of Precedence with the CJI
  • conduct discussed only by substantive motion

Challenges

  • usually retains party membership
  • delayed or partisan anti-defection rulings
  • Money Bill disputes (Rajya Sabha's rights)
  • discretion over motions, debates, discipline
  • pressure from a strong majority

ONCE A Speaker, Always A Speaker

Idea

  • Neutral even after tenure
  • In the UK the Speaker resigns from party and is generally returned unopposed
  • Vithalbhai Patel (1925) quit the Swaraj Party after becoming President of the Central Legislative Assembly
  • V. S. Page Committee (1967): sever party ties, seek consensus on an impartial Speaker, parties not to contest his seat, no ministerial office

For

  • impartiality
  • protects parliamentary democracy
  • better anti-defection rulings
  • credibility
  • Example: 2025, SC told the Telangana Speaker to decide pending disqualification petitions in a set time

Challenges

  • career disincentive
  • weak conventions in India
  • less political accountability
  • resigning from the party alone is incomplete

Reforms

  • bipartisan selection
  • independent anti-defection tribunal
  • strengthen neutrality conventions
  • transparent procedures
  • training
CountryPractice
UKSpeaker resigns from party; major parties do not contest the seat
Canadaelected by secret ballot of MPs
Australiausually keeps party affiliation but expected to preside impartially
Way forward / key point

G. V. Mavalankar: once elected Speaker he is "above parties, above politics ... belongs to all the members or to none. He holds the scales of justice evenly."

2 · Parliamentary Privileges · Articles 105, 194 · RAJA RAM PAL · SITA Soren#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Executive and Legislature handout pp. 22-24. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Privileges protect the independence of the House and its members. They are uncodified. Raja Ram Pal (2007) allowed expulsion but kept review; Sita Soren (2024, 7 judges) overruled Narasimha Rao: no immunity for bribery tied to speech or vote.

Sources AND Classification

Basis

  • Art 105 (Parliament), Art 194 (State Legislatures)
  • extend to those who may speak in proceedings (Union ministers, Attorney General) but not the President
  • sources: laws (none comprehensive), rules, conventions, judgments

Collective

  • publish and regulate proceedings (Art 361A protects true reports)
  • secret sittings
  • make rules
  • punish contempt (reprimand, admonition, suspension, imprisonment, expulsion)
  • be told of arrest/conviction of members
  • committees can summon
  • Art 122 bar on courts
  • permission before arrest or legal process in precincts

Individual

  • freedom of speech in House (Art 105)
  • no civil arrest during session and 40 days before and after (not criminal or preventive detention)
  • exempt from being a witness during sessions
  • exempt from jury service
Value addition

Privileges are functional, not personal; also called the "indirect rights of citizens".

Breach VS Contempt; Procedure

Breach of privilegeContempt of the House
violation of a specific right of the House, committee or memberany act obstructing or lowering the House's functioning, authority or dignity
narrower; e.g. obstructing an MPbroader; e.g. disobeying a lawful order
Note

Every breach of privilege may be a contempt, but every contempt need not be a breach of a specific privilege.

  1. 1
    member raises a privilege question with Speaker/Chairman's consent
  2. 2
    Presiding Officer checks for a prima facie case
  3. 3
    referred to the Committee of Privileges
  4. 4
    House accepts, modifies or rejects its recommendation

Judgments

Raja Ram Pal v. Speaker (2007)

  • Cash-for-Query (2005) expulsions upheld
  • privileges are not immune from review: constitutional violations and substantive illegality reviewable, not mere procedural irregularity

Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024)

  • seven judges: no immunity for bribery linked to speeches or votes
  • overruled P. V. Narasimha Rao (1998)
  • privileges protect legislative independence, not corruption

Codify

  • clarity
  • limits on punishment
  • fewer clashes with judiciary
  • protects FRs

Do not codify

  • loss of flexibility
  • majority may shape it
  • more litigation
  • less autonomy

Way forward

  • selective codification of major privileges and punishments
  • due process: hearing, reasons, proportionality
  • judicial balance (procedure vs constitutional violation)
  • transparent Committees of Privileges

3 · Bicameralism · Rajya Sabha VS LOK Sabha · Powers · Significance · Limits#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Executive and Legislature handout pp. 24-28. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Parliament = President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha (Art 79). Rajya Sabha is a permanent, indirectly elected federal chamber with equal powers in most things, inferior on money matters and accountability, and special powers under Arts 249, 312 and 67.

WHY TWO Houses

Art 79

  • Parliament = President + Rajya Sabha (Council of States) + Lok Sabha (House of the People)
  • President is not a member but summons, prorogues, dissolves the Lok Sabha, addresses Parliament, gives assent

For

  • federal voice for States
  • deliberation, less haste
  • experts via nominations
  • continuity (never dissolves)
  • check on unilateral constitutional change

Against

  • delay
  • duplication
  • indirect election
  • party-line voting
  • accommodating defeated politicians
Value addition

Deadlock tools: Joint Sitting (Art 108) and the Money Bill procedure.

Powers OF Rajya Sabha

Parity

  • ordinary bills (subject to joint sitting)
  • Art 368 amendments, passed separately, no joint sitting
  • Election/impeachment of President
  • Election and removal of VP
  • removal of SC/HC judges, CAG, CEC per rules
  • approves ordinances (Art 123) and Emergency proclamations (Arts 352, 356, 360)
  • discusses CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission reports
  • Ministers incl. PM may belong to either House

Inferior

  • Money Bills (Arts 109-110):
    • Introduced only in Lok Sabha
    • RS recommends within 14 days
    • Deemed passed if not returned
  • cannot vote on Demands for Grants
  • no no-confidence motion in RS
  • joint sitting:
    • Lok Sabha usually prevails
    • E.g. Prevention of Terrorism Bill 2002

Special

  • Art 249:
    • Authorise Parliament to legislate on a State List subject in national interest by two-thirds present and voting
    • Resolution valid one year, renewable yearly
    • Law ceases 6 months after
  • Art 312: create new All-India Services (two-thirds)
  • Art 67: removal of VP originates only in RS (majority of all members, agreed by LS)
  • approve Emergency proclamations if Lok Sabha is dissolved
Memory aid · 249-312-67

RS special powers: 249 State List · 312 All-India Services · 67 VP removal. (own mnemonic)

Significance AND Limits

Significance

  • House of Elders
  • revisory chamber
  • guardian of Constitution
  • federal chamber
  • 12 nominated experts (literature, science, art, social service)
  • "House of Corrections" vs Lok Sabha "House of Passions"

Limitations

  • unequal State representation (unlike US Senate)
  • 2003: domicile requirement removed
  • UTs and nominated members
  • Weak on Money Bills
  • Aadhaar controversy
  • Only three joint sittings
  • Could not stop the 42nd Amendment
  • accommodation of defeated politicians
  • disruption: Hamid Ansari (2013) "federation of anarchists"

Why unequal representation

  • population differences
  • India not formed by agreement of sovereign States
  • equal seats would give small States disproportionate influence
Way forward / key point

RS is neither as weak as the House of Lords nor as strong as the US Senate.

4 · Women's Reservation · NARI Shakti Vandan · 131st Amendment BILL 2026 · Delimitation#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Executive and Legislature handout pp. 28-30 (current affairs, verify). Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • The 106th Amendment (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023) reserves one-third of Lok Sabha and Assembly seats for women after Census and delimitation. The 131st Amendment Bill 2026 (more seats, population-based delimitation) was defeated; the debate is the North-South balance.

Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023

  • 33% in Lok Sabha, State Assemblies, Delhi Assembly
  • Inserted Arts 330A, 332A, 334A
  • Amended 239AA
  • one-third of SC/ST seats go to SC/ST women
  • takes effect after the required Census and delimitation
  • Seats rotate
  • 15-year period, Parliament can extend
  • not for Rajya Sabha or Legislative Councils

For

  • political equality
  • panchayat evidence on water, sanitation
  • leadership pipeline
  • inclusive debate

Concerns

  • proxy representation
  • rotation weakens constituency ties
  • tokenism
  • delay tied to Census and delimitation

Alternatives

  • party ticket quota of one-third (risk: weak seats)
  • dual-member constituencies (big electoral change)
  • Education, finance, safety, training
  • Party democracy, less criminalisation

131st Amendment BILL, 2026

Note

Current-affairs item. Per the handout: three Bills introduced in Lok Sabha on 16 April 2026: Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, Delimitation Bill, UT Laws (Amendment) Bill. The Constitution Bill was defeated in Lok Sabha in April 2026, so the other two became infructuous. Verify.

Proposals

  • Lok Sabha maximum 550 to 850 (up to 815 States, 35 UTs)
  • population-based reallocation
  • Parliament to fix the Census by law (2011 Census usable)
  • drop the wait for the first Census after the 106th Amendment so women's quota starts earlier

Delimitation Issues

Issues

  • North-South imbalance:
    • South (TN, Kerala, Karnataka, AP) slowed population growth
    • North (UP, Bihar, MP) grew faster
  • less constitutional certainty if timing and Census left to ordinary law
  • gerrymandering (allegations in J&K and Assam)
  • judicial review: Delimitation Commission orders have force of law, mostly shielded
  • weakens Rajya Sabha in joint sittings if only Lok Sabha grows
  • delays could delay women's reservation

Way forward / key point

Way forward: national consensus, balance population with regional equity, independent Commission, judicial safeguards, phased implementation.

5 · Functioning OF Parliament · Reforms · Mplads · Supreme NOT Sovereign#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Executive and Legislature handout pp. 30-34. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Parliament is weakened by fewer sittings, disruptions, thin debate, delegated legislation and criminalisation (46% MPs with declared cases in 2024). Reforms include fixed sittings and stronger committees. MPLADS (Rs 5 crore a year) was upheld in Bhim Singh (2010) but many panels want it scrapped. Parliament is supreme but not sovereign.

Declining Effectiveness

Issues

  • Fewer sitting days
  • Government controls timing
  • disruptions and adjournments (15th Lok Sabha 2009-14 low productivity)
  • thin debate: little committee scrutiny, anti-defection and party discipline, technical complexity
  • delegated legislation with inadequate scrutiny
  • cabinet dominance ("creation dominates the creator")
  • guillotine: Demands for Grants passed without discussion
  • frequent ordinances

Representation and accountability

  • criminalisation:
    • 16th LS (2014) about 34%
    • 17th (2019) 233 (43%)
    • 18th (2024) 251 (46%), 170 (31%) serious cases. Declared in affidavits, not findings of guilt
  • ageing: only about 11% of 18th LS aged 40 or below
  • population per MP up since the 1971 Census
  • ethics: Cash-for-Query, Cash-for-Vote, MPLADS irregularities
  • weak research support

Value addition

Pay should not be the main attraction nor too low; consider an independent body like the UK's IPSA.

Reforms

Reforms

  • Fixed calendar
  • ** proposals: 120 days Lok Sabha, 100 days** Rajya Sabha
  • stronger committees with experts and research
  • narrow whips to confidence votes and essential business
  • Opposition days (UK model)
  • Disclose criminal antecedents and funding
  • Enforce ethics
  • women's reservation and delimitation by consensus
  • Speaker neutrality
  • Less executive control of business
  • research services and training

Mplads

Features

  • Launched 1993 (P. V. Narasimha Rao government)
  • Nodal MoSPI
  • Rs 5 crore per MP a year, non-lapsable
  • durable assets: drinking water, sanitation, education, health, roads
  • MPs recommend
  • District Authority sanctions and implements
  • suspended 2020-21 (and initially 2021-22), restored in 2021-22
  • LS MP:
    • Own constituency
    • RS MP: any district in the State
    • Nominated MP: any district(s) in one State
  • No religious buildings, routine maintenance, assets named after persons
  • Audit by CAs/Local Fund Auditors on Accountant General's recommendation

Criticism

  • separation of powers
  • federalism (State/local subjects)
  • corruption, delays
  • non-lapsable funds encourage election-time spending
  • unspent:
    • 14th LS Rs 176 cr
    • 15th Rs 551 cr
    • 16th Rs 1,734 cr
  • Weak maintenance
  • Unequal district spread
  • weakens Panchayats and Municipalities
Body / casePosition
NCRWC 2002discontinue
Second ARCabolish (separation of powers)
Election Commissionopposes: unfair advantage to sitting MPs
Parliamentary Committeecontinue with reforms, change name
Bhim Singh v. Union (2010)5 judges upheld: MPs only recommend; local authorities sanction, so no breach of separation of powers

Supreme BUT NOT Sovereign

Why supreme

  • apex law-maker (Union, Concurrent)
  • residuary powers, Art 248
  • amends Constitution, Art 368
  • controls executive (Art 75, no-confidence)
  • judicial structure (Art 124, tribunals)
  • financial control: Consolidated Fund
  • impeachment/removal powers

Why not sovereign

  • Constitution is supreme
  • judicial review
  • Basic Structure (Kesavananda 1973)
  • federal structure
  • Fundamental Rights
Way forward / key point

Unlike the British Parliament, India's Parliament has constitutionally limited powers.
