1 · Why states get special provisions#

India is a "Union of States with diversity". Special provisions answer specific regional needs, often prompted by state reorganisation or the grant of statehood to Union Territories.

2 · Features of Part XXI and asymmetric federalism#

Features of the special provisions

  • Governing provisions: Articles 371 to 371J in Part XXI give tailored governance for 12 states: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Nagaland, Assam, Manipur, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Sikkim, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Goa and Karnataka.
  • Added by amendment: originally absent, they came through amendments during state reorganisations or Union Territory elevations to statehood.
  • Temporary nature: Part XXI falls under "Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions", suggesting operation until issues like secessionist sentiment or unrest subside, yet no explicit expiry dates exist. Parliament can amend as conditions evolve, balancing impermanence with enduring regional needs.
  • Key objectives: meet aspirations of backward regions, protect tribal cultural and economic interests, manage law-and-order challenges and safeguard local populations.
  • Asymmetric federalism: varying degrees of autonomy, not applied uniformly across states.
  • Union Territory exceptions: Article 239A enables a local legislature for Puducherry. Article 239AA empowers Delhi's NCT to legislate on State and Concurrent List subjects, excluding police, public order and land.
Asymmetric federalism

A structure where political, administrative and fiscal powers are distributed unevenly among constituent units; relationships between the federation and its units are not uniform.

Vertical asymmetry

  • Power balance between the Central Government and the States

Horizontal asymmetry

  • Variation in powers and status among the different States themselves

Evidence of asymmetry in India

Where it shows
TypeEvidence
Vertical: a strong UnionIndestructible Union of destructible States (Article 1); residuary powers with the Union (Article 248); Emergency provisions give overriding authority over State administrations; power to initiate Constitutional amendments lies exclusively with the Union; Governor's office and President's Rule as supervisory roles
Horizontal: regional and cultural protectionsArticle 371 special provisions for states or regions with backwardness, internal security hurdles, difficult geography or tribal identity; Sixth Schedule with high autonomy for tribal areas in four northeastern States
Representative and fiscal deviationsSeats in Parliament allocated to States non-uniformly, based on population (unlike federations with equal representation); Special Category Status (SCS) gives enhanced financial assistance to 11 States disadvantaged by geography or development
Vertical five
IREAS

Indestructible (Art. 1), Residuary (Art. 248), Emergency, Amendment initiation, Supervisory (Governor and President's Rule): "I Rule Every Assembly Strongly" is the Union's grip.

Vertical versus horizontal

Vertical = Centre over States (Art. 1, 248, Emergency, Governor). Horizontal = State versus State (Art. 371, Sixth Schedule, SCS, seats by population).

Handout alert

Sikkim's Assembly size: the Article table says "not less than 30 members", while the Recent Events section says Article 371F allows a 32-member assembly "below the usual 60-member constitutional minimum". Use 30 as the constitutional floor in 371F and read 32 as the Assembly's actual strength as the handout states. Also, the Significance section says Article 371 lets "the Governor" of Maharashtra and Gujarat set up boards; the Article table says the President authorises the Governor to have special responsibility for them. 5 · Significance#

The provisions balance national unity and regional diversity.

Four strands of significance
StrandPoint
1. Strengthening national integrationA "political safety valve" in the formative years. Neutralising secessionism: constitutional guarantees protecting tribal customs and land (Nagaland, Mizoram) brought insurgent groups to the table. Peaceful integration: for Sikkim, 371-F was the cornerstone for the move from "Associate State" to a full member of the Union with its identity protected
2. Protecting cultural and tribal identityProtects "the way of life" of indigenous communities. Customary law: 371-A (Nagaland) and 371-G (Mizoram) bar Parliament from legislating on religious or social practices, customary laws and administration of justice without the state assembly's consent. Land rights: prevents alienation of tribal land to outsiders
3. Addressing regional disparities"Equitable development" for neglected pockets. Development boards under Article 371 for Vidarbha, Marathwada, Saurashtra and Kutch ensure a fixed portion of the state budget goes to these regions. Education and jobs: 371-D and 371-J allow local-level reservations to prevent "brain drain" and make locals benefit from state growth
4. Administrative and security managementGovernor's "Special Responsibility" in border or sensitive states. Law and order: Arunachal Pradesh (371-H), strategic location and internal security. Hill area management: Manipur (371-C), a special committee of hill-area members so tribal interests are not overshadowed by the dominant valley population
Four strands
NCRA

National integration, Cultural and tribal identity, Regional disparities, Administrative and security: "No Community Right Abandoned".

6 · Recent events and controversies (2021-25)#

Article by article
Article and stateEventTakeaway
371A Nagaland, land rights versus Central laws (2021-24)SARFAESI Act conflict: NEDFi v. L. Doulo Builders. The court stressed that under 371A no Act of Parliament on ownership and transfer of land applies without a state assembly resolutionReinforces that local "Indigenous" status is a prerequisite for land ownership; national banking laws cannot override tribal land protections
371C Manipur, ethnic violence (2023-25)The 2023 violence began after a High Court order suggesting ST status for Meiteis, feared by Hill tribes (Kuki/Naga) as opening Hill land to Meiteis. Nov 2025 discourse: Kuki-Zomi groups demand a "Separate Budget" or "Separate Administration" invoking 371CThe Hill Areas Committee (HAC) is a consultative body, not legislative like the Sixth Schedule; centre of the valley-hill power debate
371D Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, cadre allocation (2024)After the 2014 bifurcation thousands of employees were stuck in allocation disputes; 371D manages the "Zonal System" for local recruitment. 2024 SC ruling gave finality to allocation of state-level employeesRespects "Local Cadre" principles so one state's residents do not dominate the other's job market
371J Karnataka, 37% quota and expansion (2022-25)Applies to Kalyana-Karnataka (formerly Hyderabad-Karnataka). 2024: judiciary and state upheld the 37% reservation for locals in state-wide education and employment. 2025: Development Board's scope widened to specialised industrial clusters371J used for targeted economic growth, not only social reservation
371F Sikkim, ethnic parity (2024)2024 Assembly polls: the "Sangha" seat (reserved for monks) and parity between Bhutia-Lepcha (BL) and other communities reinforced371F allows a 32-member assembly and ethnic reservations hard to justify under standard "secular" representation (as the handout says)
Judicial and national context (2023-25)
ItemPoint
Governor's discretion (371H and 371A), 2023SC clarified Governors with "Special Responsibility" for law and order (Arunachal and Nagaland) do not have "parallel government" status; they must consult the Council of Ministers except where specifically empowered to use "individual judgment"
2025 Budget "NE Push"Used the Article 371 framework to channel funds through Regional Development Councils, bypassing state-wide red tape for border security and development
Mizoram (371G) and UCC, 2023-24Mizoram Assembly resolutions: a Uniform Civil Code cannot apply without consent, citing Mizo customary law under 371G
Article 370 contrastAfter the 2019 abrogation of Article 370, fears arose in the Northeast that 371 was next; the Centre clarified 371 is a permanent feature of "Unity in Diversity" and will not be touched
2023 J&K verdictThe SC noted special provisions to some states are essential features of federal structure and differ fundamentally from the "internal sovereignty" once claimed under Article 370
Handout's conclusion

Article 371 is the "New Frontier" of Federalism, strengthened to manage ethnic diversity and regional inequality. Special provisions are not an "erosion" of the Constitution but an enhancement of it; they uphold "Unity in Diversity" so that regions do not feel "forced" into a monolithic mould.
