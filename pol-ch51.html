In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Articles 371 to 371-J (Part XXI, 'Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions') give special treatment to 12 states: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Nagaland, Assam, Manipur, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Sikkim, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Goa and Karnataka.
  • They were not in the original Constitution. They came by amendments when states were reorganised or a Union Territory became a state.
  • The aims: help backward regions, protect tribal culture and land, manage law and order, and safeguard local people. This is asymmetric federalism: units get different powers.
  • The usual tools are a Governor's special responsibility, development boards, committees of the Assembly, local reservation in jobs and education, and a minimum Assembly size.
  • Nagaland (371-A) and Mizoram (371-G): Acts of Parliament on religious or social practices, customary law, customary justice and land do not apply unless the State Assembly decides.
  • For Union Territories, Art. 239A gives Puducherry a legislature and Art. 239AA lets Delhi NCT legislate on State and Concurrent subjects, except police, public order and land.

Key terms

Asymmetric federalism
Powers and status of units are uneven: vertical (Centre versus states) and horizontal (state versus state)
Special responsibility
A duty the President can give the Governor to act on his own judgment on a named matter, such as law and order in Nagaland (371-A) and Arunachal (371-H)
Development board
A separate board for backward regions (Vidarbha, Marathwada, Saurashtra, Kutch; Hyderabad-Karnataka) with an annual report to the Assembly
Local cadre
Civil posts organised for parts of a state with local preference (Art. 371-D, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana)
Administrative Tribunal (371-D)
Hears service disputes outside the High Court; only the Supreme Court has jurisdiction over its matters
Hill Areas Committee
Assembly committee of members from the Hill Areas of Manipur (371-C); consultative, not legislative like the Sixth Schedule
Sixth Schedule
Gives high autonomy and special administration to tribal areas in four north-eastern states
Special Category Status
A way to give extra financial help to 11 states at a disadvantage

1 · Why states get special provisions#

  • India is a "Union of States with diversity". Special provisions answer specific regional needs, often prompted by state reorganisation or the grant of statehood to Union Territories.

2 · Features of Part XXI and asymmetric federalism#

Features of the special provisions

  • Governing provisions: Articles 371 to 371J in Part XXI give tailored governance for 12 states: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Nagaland, Assam, Manipur, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Sikkim, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Goa and Karnataka.
  • Added by amendment: originally absent, they came through amendments during state reorganisations or Union Territory elevations to statehood.
  • Temporary nature: Part XXI falls under "Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions", suggesting operation until issues like secessionist sentiment or unrest subside, yet no explicit expiry dates exist. Parliament can amend as conditions evolve, balancing impermanence with enduring regional needs.
  • Key objectives: meet aspirations of backward regions, protect tribal cultural and economic interests, manage law-and-order challenges and safeguard local populations.
  • Asymmetric federalism: varying degrees of autonomy, not applied uniformly across states.
  • Union Territory exceptions: Article 239A enables a local legislature for Puducherry. Article 239AA empowers Delhi's NCT to legislate on State and Concurrent List subjects, excluding police, public order and land.
  • Asymmetric federalism:
    • A structure where political, administrative and fiscal powers are distributed unevenly among constituent units
    • Relationships between the federation and its units are not uniform

Two directions of asymmetry

  • Vertical asymmetry: Power balance between the Central Government and the States
  • Horizontal asymmetry: Variation in powers and status among the different States themselves

Evidence of asymmetry in India

Where it shows

TypeEvidence
Vertical: a strong UnionIndestructible Union of destructible States (Article 1); residuary powers with the Union (Article 248); Emergency provisions give overriding authority over State administrations; power to initiate Constitutional amendments lies exclusively with the Union; Governor's office and President's Rule as supervisory roles
Horizontal: regional and cultural protectionsArticle 371 special provisions for states or regions with backwardness, internal security hurdles, difficult geography or tribal identity; Sixth Schedule with high autonomy for tribal areas in four northeastern States
Representative and fiscal deviationsSeats in Parliament allocated to States non-uniformly, based on population (unlike federations with equal representation); Special Category Status (SCS) gives enhanced financial assistance to 11 States disadvantaged by geography or development
  • Memory aid (Vertical five): Indestructible (Art. 1), Residuary (Art. 248), Emergency, Amendment initiation, Supervisory (Governor and President's Rule): "I Rule Every Assembly Strongly" is the Union's grip.
  • Shortcut: Vertical versus horizontal. Vertical = Centre over States (Art. 1, 248, Emergency, Governor). Horizontal = State versus State (Art. 371, Sixth Schedule, SCS, seats by population).

4 · Patterns in the table#

Customary law and land shield

  • 371-A Nagaland:
    • Acts of Parliament on Naga religious and social practices
    • Naga customary law
    • Justice under customary law
    • Land ownership and transfer
    • None applies unless the Assembly decides
  • 371-G Mizoram:
    • Same pattern for Mizo practices
    • Mizo customary law
    • Justice under it
    • Land ownership and transfer
    • Assembly decides

Governor's special responsibility

  • Law and order: Nagaland (371-A) and Arunachal Pradesh (371-H): individual judgment after consulting the Council of Ministers
  • Development or welfare:
    • Maharashtra and Gujarat (boards)
    • Sikkim (peace and equitable advancement)
    • Karnataka (Hyderabad-Karnataka board and local reservations)
    • Manipur (hill committee)

Jobs, cadres and tribunals

  • 371-D Andhra Pradesh and Telangana:
    • Equitable public employment and education
    • Local cadres
    • Local areas for admission
    • Administrative Tribunal under the Supreme Court only
    • 371-E Central University in AP
  • 371-J Karnataka:
    • Reservation in education, vocational training and state posts for the region
    • Local-cadre mechanism for human resource enhancement and employment

Hills and tribal committees

  • 371-B Assam: Committee of the Assembly with members from Tribal Areas
  • 371-C Manipur:
    • Committee of members from Hill Areas
    • Governor's annual report to the President
    • Centre can give directions to the State
  • Memory aid (Governor's law-and-order pair): Nagaland and Arunachal: "No Alarm": the Governor uses individual judgment for law and order after consulting the Council of Ministers.

5 · Significance#

  • The provisions balance national unity and regional diversity.

Four strands of significance

StrandPoint
1. Strengthening national integrationA "political safety valve" in the formative years. Neutralising secessionism: constitutional guarantees protecting tribal customs and land (Nagaland, Mizoram) brought insurgent groups to the table. Peaceful integration: for Sikkim, 371-F was the cornerstone for the move from "Associate State" to a full member of the Union with its identity protected
2. Protecting cultural and tribal identityProtects "the way of life" of indigenous communities. Customary law: 371-A (Nagaland) and 371-G (Mizoram) bar Parliament from legislating on religious or social practices, customary laws and administration of justice without the state assembly's consent. Land rights: prevents alienation of tribal land to outsiders
3. Addressing regional disparities"Equitable development" for neglected pockets. Development boards under Article 371 for Vidarbha, Marathwada, Saurashtra and Kutch ensure a fixed portion of the state budget goes to these regions. Education and jobs: 371-D and 371-J allow local-level reservations to prevent "brain drain" and make locals benefit from state growth
4. Administrative and security managementGovernor's "Special Responsibility" in border or sensitive states. Law and order: Arunachal Pradesh (371-H), strategic location and internal security. Hill area management: Manipur (371-C), a special committee of hill-area members so tribal interests are not overshadowed by the dominant valley population
  • Memory aid (Four strands): National integration, Cultural and tribal identity, Regional disparities, Administrative and security: "No Community Right Abandoned".

From Lakshmikanth · Indian Polity, 8th ed. Ch 78, Special Provisions for Some States
  • Part XXI is titled 'Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions'. Art. 371 was amended by the 7th Amendment (1956) and the Bombay Reorganisation Act (1960)
  • Andhra Pradesh was taken out of it by the 32nd Amendment (1973) and given Arts. 371-D and 371-E
  • Amendments that added the articles: 371-A the 13th (1962), 371-B the 22nd (1969), 371-C the 27th (1971), 371-D and 371-E the 32nd (1973), 371-F the 36th (1975), 371-G the 53rd (1986), 371-H the 55th (1986), 371-J the 98th (2012).
  • Nagaland, Governor's role: his decision on law and order is final, and the validity of anything he does cannot be questioned on the ground that he ought or ought not to have used individual judgment. Central money for a specific purpose must be put in the demand for grant for that purpose.
  • Tuensang district (Nagaland):
    • A 35-member regional council
    • Its Deputy Commissioner is ex-officio Chairman and the Vice-Chairman is elected by its members. For ten years from the formation of Nagaland (or longer, as the Governor specifies) the Governor runs the district, there is a Minister for Tuensang affairs, and its Assembly members are elected by the regional council, not directly
  • Assam (371-B): the committee has members elected from the Tribal Areas, which are North Cachar Hills, Karbi Anglong and Bodoland Territorial Areas. Manipur (371-C): 'Hill Areas' means areas the President declares by order.
  • Andhra Pradesh (371-D): the President may also abolish the tribunal when its continued existence is not necessary. The tribunal was set up by the Andhra Pradesh Administrative Tribunal Order, 1975.
  • Sikkim (371-F, from the 36th Amendment 1975):
    • Parliament may fix seats for sections of the population and delimit constituencies for them
    • The Governor acts in his discretion for peace and fair social and economic advancement, subject to the President's directions
    • The President can extend any state law to Sikkim with changes
  • Karnataka (371-J):
    • The Hyderabad-Karnataka region means six districts: Gulbarga, Bidar, Raichur, Koppal, Yadgir and Bellary. In 2010 the Assembly and the Council passed separate resolutions asking for special provisions
    • The aim is equitable funds, local cadres and reserved education and training seats

Goa; 56th Amendment Act, 1986; Assembly not less than 30 members.
371-J?
Karnataka; 98th Amendment Act, 2012; Hyderabad-Karnataka board, reservation in education and posts, local cadres.
Which two Articles allow local reservations in education and jobs?
371-D (AP and Telangana) and 371-J (Karnataka).
Which states had insurgent groups brought to talks via custom and land guarantees?
Nagaland and Mizoram (371-A and 371-G).
What was the NEDFi v. L. Doulo Builders issue?
Whether the SARFAESI Act applies in Nagaland; the court stressed that under 371A no Act of Parliament on land ownership and transfer applies without a state assembly resolution.
What triggered the 2023 Manipur violence, per the handout?
A High Court order suggesting ST status for Meiteis, feared by Hill tribes as opening Hill land protected by 371C.
Is the Hill Areas Committee legislative?
No: consultative, unlike the Sixth Schedule.
What did the SC clarify about Governors' special responsibility in 2023?
They do not have "parallel government" status and must consult the Council of Ministers except where individual judgment is empowered.
What did Mizoram say about the UCC?
The Assembly passed resolutions that the UCC cannot apply without its consent, citing Mizo customary law under 371G.
What does Karnataka's 371J quota cover?
37% reservation for Kalyana-Karnataka locals in state-wide education and employment (upheld 2024).

Traps#

Trap 1 · 371-D versus 371-E Art. 371-D covers local cadres, education and the tribunal for Andhra Pradesh and (since 2014) Telangana. Art. 371-E only lets Parliament set up a Central University in Andhra Pradesh. Rule: 371-D = jobs, admissions, tribunal; 371-E = Central University.
Trap 2 · Sikkim: 30 or 32 members Art. 371-F says the Sikkim Assembly has not less than 30 members. The recent-events part of the handout speaks of a 32-member Assembly. Rule: 30 is the constitutional minimum; 32 is the present strength.
Trap 3 · Minimum Assembly strength Sikkim (371-F), Arunachal (371-H) and Goa (371-I) need not less than 30 members; Mizoram (371-G) needs not less than 40. Rule: only Mizoram has 40; the other three have 30.
Trap 4 · Hill Areas Committee is not a Sixth Schedule council The Manipur Hill Areas Committee (371-C) is only consultative. The Sixth Schedule gives real autonomy to tribal areas in four north-eastern states. Rule: 371-C committee advises; Sixth Schedule governs.
