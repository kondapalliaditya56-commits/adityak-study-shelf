1 · Rationale behind Fundamental Duties#

They remind citizens that while the State guarantees rights, it also expects them to uphold values of national unity and social harmony.

  • The original Constitution had only rights and no duties; the framers believed civic responsibility would evolve naturally.
  • By the mid-1970s it was felt necessary to define duties to promote discipline, responsibility and commitment.
  • Inspiration: the Constitution of the erstwhile USSR, which treated rights and duties as inseparable. Unlike the USA, Canada, France or Australia, which stress individual rights, India aligns with nations balancing collective responsibility with personal freedom.
  • Main objective: an ethical and moral code of conduct, so constitutional ideals become civic behaviour.

Aims

  • Promote national integration (respect for Constitution, flag, national symbols)
  • Strengthen democracy (sovereignty, public property)
  • Foster social harmony (scientific temper, environment, inquiry)
  • Balance rights with responsibilities so freedoms are not misused

Philosophical objective

  • A moral compass guiding citizens towards justice, liberty, equality and fraternity
Four aims
IDHB

Integration, Democracy, Harmony, Balance: "Indians Do Honour Balance."

2 · Evolution and the Swaran Singh Committee#

From committee to Constitution

Emergency 1975-1977
Congress government sets up the Sardar Swaran Singh Committee (1976)
Committee advice
A separate chapter on duties; a new Part IVA; eight duties
42nd Amendment Act, 1976
Adds Part IVA (Article 51A) with ten duties, the first formal codification
86th Amendment Act, 2002
One more duty: education for children aged 6 to 14

What the committee recommended

  • Inclusion of Fundamental Duties: a new section to enshrine duties beside rights.
  • Moral and civic responsibility: enjoyment of rights is inseparable from performing duties.
  • Constitutional chapter: a new Part IVA with a code of duties for all citizens.

Recommendations not accepted

Three rejected suggestions
SuggestionWhy it was left out
Penal provisionsParliament to prescribe punishment for non-performance; not accepted as it could undermine Fundamental Rights
Judicial barNo court to question any law enforcing duties; seen as inconsistent with constitutional principles
Duty to pay taxesOmitted from the final list
Three rejected
PJT

Penalties, Judicial bar, Taxes: "Poor Judges Tremble."

Do you know

India was the first Commonwealth nation to include Fundamental Duties in its Constitution.

Handout alert

The duty count changes with the source: the committee suggested eight, the 1976 Amendment introduced ten (Art. 51A (a) to (j)), and with the 2002 addition (k) the list today has eleven. For "how many duties today" answer 11; for "original" answer 10.

3 · Article 51A: the eleven duties#

"It shall be the duty of every citizen of India":

Duties (a) to (k)
ClauseDutyWhat it means in the handout

(b)Cherish the ideals of the freedom struggleFollow ideals such as sacrifice, unity, self-reliance
(c)Protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of IndiaAct against threats to unity or independence
(d)Defend the country and render national service when called uponDefence in war and civil emergencies
(e)Promote harmony and renounce discriminationBrotherhood beyond caste, religion, language; denounce practices degrading women's dignity
(f)Preserve the rich cultural heritageArt, literature, monuments, practices of the composite culture
(g)Protect and improve the environmentForests, rivers, wildlife; compassion for living beings
(h)Develop scientific temper and humanismRational thinking, inquiry, reform; reject superstition
(i)Safeguard public property and renounce violencePublic assets as national trust; no destruction or violent protest
(j)Strive for excellence in all spheresContinuous improvement by individuals and institutions

First five (a to e)
CISDH

Constitution, Ideals of freedom struggle, Sovereignty, Defend, Harmony: "Cool Indians Stand Dear Harmony."

Last six (f to k)
HESPEE

Heritage, Environment, Scientific temper, Public property, Excellence, Education: "Hungry Elephants Seldom Pass Empty Eateries."

Not duties (PYQ traps)

Voting is not a duty (2011). Protecting weaker sections is not a duty (2012); it is Art. 46, a DPSP. Taxes, family planning are not in the list. Duties that are in: scientific temper, public property, composite culture, excellence, sovereignty-unity-integrity, abide by Constitution.

4 · Features of the Fundamental Duties#

Five features
FeatureDetail
Moral plus civicMoral: cherish freedom struggle ideals. Civic: respect Constitution, Flag, Anthem
Rooted in Indian traditionDrawn from civilisational heritage: compassion, harmony, respect for elders, environment, community living
Citizens onlyUnlike several FRs that cover all persons (citizens and foreigners), duties bind only citizens
Non-justiciableLike DPSP, not enforceable in court; no penalties for violation; the handout adds that Parliament may enact laws to give them effect
ComplementaryBalance rights with collective responsibility; with FRs and DPSP form a triad of moral framework

Fundamental Rights

  • Part III
  • justiciable
  • enforceable in court
  • extend to persons (several of them)

DPSP and Duties

  • Part IV and Part IVA
  • non-justiciable
  • DPSP bind the State morally, duties bind citizens morally

5 · The notion of duties under the Constitution#

Six ideas about why duties matter.

  • Gandhian view: if individuals perform their duties diligently, protection of rights follows; duties underpin justice and harmony.
  • Preventive and cautionary role: a moral warning against activities that undermine unity, integrity and harmony.
  • Reminder of civic responsibility: rights go hand in hand with obligations; citizenship means privileges and responsibilities.
  • Source of inspiration and discipline: citizens are active participants in nation-building, not spectators.
  • Democratic and moral balance: Indira Gandhi: the aim was "not to suppress rights but to balance them by making people equally aware of their duties". Law Minister H.R. Gokhale: a "sobering reminder" against anti-national and unconstitutional behaviour seen in the 1970s.
  • Guidance for legislature and judiciary: a moral and interpretative framework. In AIIMS Students' Union v. AIIMS (2001) the Supreme Court observed that duties are relevant in interpreting Fundamental Rights and ensuring responsible conduct.
Six notions
GPRSDG

Gandhi view, Preventive role, Reminder, Source of discipline, Democratic balance, Guidance for courts: "Good People Remember Serving Duty Gladly."

6 · Criticism of the Fundamental Duties#

Symbolically important, but weak in concept and practice.

Five criticisms
CriticismThe argument
Incomplete listOmits voting, honest tax payment, family planning. The committee itself proposed the tax duty, but it was not accepted
Vague and abstract"noble ideals", "composite culture", "scientific temper" are too broad; hard for common citizens to apply; limits educational and moral influence
Non-justiciableNo legal sanction, so merely a code of moral conduct; the committee's penalty proposal was ignored
UnnecessaryThey are inherent moral responsibilities; inclusion was superfluous and politically motivated, since the amendment passed during the Emergency
PlacementIn Part IVA after the DPSP, symbolically diminishing them; should have followed Part III immediately for equal footing with rights
Five criticisms
IVNUP

Incomplete, Vague, Non-justiciable, Unnecessary, Placement: "I Visited New Units Planned."

7 · Non-justiciability of the Fundamental Duties#

  • The Constitution has no provision for direct enforcement; no citizen can be punished by a court for violating a duty. In this they resemble the DPSP.
  • They were deliberately placed in Part IVA rather than at the end of the justiciable Part III, keeping them non-justiciable.
  • Contrast abroad: the erstwhile USSR, Yugoslavia and Albania made duties legally binding; in India they remain advisory.
  • V.K.R.V. Rao: duties will be ineffective without legal sanctions; people lack the courage to perform duties voluntarily; rights without enforceable duties lack a firm ethical foundation.

After the criticism

1998
The Supreme Court issues a notice to the Government on its plan to spread awareness of Fundamental Duties
1999
Government forms the Justice J.S. Verma Committee on Fundamental Duties of Citizens to examine operational aspects and suggest ways to teach and encourage obedience
Handout alert

The Features section says Parliament has power to enact laws to give duties effect, while the non-justiciability section says the Constitution gives no provision for direct enforcement. The 2017 PYQ key says no legislative process has been provided to enforce the duties and they are not correlative to legal duties (answer: neither). Use the PYQ key in exams.

8 · Verma Committee on Fundamental Duties (1999)#

Recommendations

  • Duties raise the standards of citizens in public life; everyone should obey and promote them.
  • Holders of public office avoid selfishness and nepotism, putting public interest first.
  • Integrity as the guiding principle in public office.
  • Accountability to the public for decisions and actions.
  • Transparency and openness in all decisions and actions.
  • Honesty at all times.
  • Leadership: public office holders promote these principles by example.
Verma principles
SIATHL

Selfishness and nepotism avoided, Integrity, Accountability, Transparency, Honesty, Leadership by example: "Some Intelligent Adults Teach Honest Leaders."
