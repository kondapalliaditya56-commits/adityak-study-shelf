1 · Who the CAG is#

The Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) is an independent constitutional authority under Article 148. He heads the Indian Audit and Accounts Department (IA&AD) and is the guardian of the public purse: public funds must be raised and spent in accordance with law.

  • Audits the financial transactions of both the Union and State governments.
  • Primary duty: to uphold the Constitution and parliamentary laws in financial administration.
  • Audit reports ensure accountability, transparency and fiscal discipline in the executive.
  • One of the key pillars of the democratic framework, alongside the Supreme Court, the Election Commission and the UPSC.
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called the CAG "the most important officer under the Constitution".
Pillars
SEUC

Supreme Court, Election Commission, UPSC, CAG: "See Every Unit's Cash".

Ambedkar line

CAG = Art. 148 = guardian of the public purse = "most important officer under the Constitution".

2 · History of the IA&AD and its vision#

Four stages of the department

Pre-Independence
Mid-19th century: separate accounts for the three Presidencies (Bengal, Madras, Bombay). 1860: office of Auditor-General of India created. GoI Acts (1919, then 1935) gave audit statutory recognition and independent status
Independence and Constitution
Auditor-General redesignated Comptroller and Auditor General under Art. 148; IA&AD became the machinery under the CAG for Union and State accounts and other bodies
Post-Independence
Practices, audit standards and field structure expanded; IAAS formalised as the service cadre; a unitary structure within the federal setup lets the CAG audit Union and States through one framework
Modern era
Emphasis on performance audit (value for money), compliance audits, accounting reforms and internal controls; the CAG's "A Thematic History" volumes cover 1990-2007
Vision, mission and values
ItemContent
VisionIndependent and credible assurance on public resources; be a global leader in public sector auditing
MissionMandated by the Constitution, promote accountability, transparency and good governance through high-quality auditing and accounting; independent, timely assurance to the Legislature, the Public and the Executive that public funds are collected and used effectively and efficiently
Institutional valuesProfessional standards; objective and balanced approach; independence; transparency
People valuesEthical behaviour; integrity; professional competence; fairness; social awareness
History steps
PIPM

Pre-Independence, Independence (Art. 148), Post-Independence (IAAS), Modern (performance audit): "Public Income Protected Methodically".

Date shortcut

1860 Auditor-General; 1919 and 1935 Acts give statutory status; Art. 148 renames the post CAG; thematic history 1990-2007.

Appointment, tenure and removal
PointProvision

Oath or affirmationBefore the President: faithfulness to the Constitution, uphold sovereignty and integrity, discharge duties faithfully, impartially and to the best of ability, knowledge and judgment, without fear or favour

AmendmentChanging term or age needs a change in the Act, not the Constitution

ATS

Act 1971, Term 6 years, Sixty-five years of age: "Act Tells Six and sixty-five", not the Constitution.

Tenure shortcut

Warrant by President, 1971 Act: 6 years / 65, resign to the President, removal = SC judge.

Key point

The term and age are statutory (1971 Act), so Parliament can change them by law without a constitutional amendment.

The handout says "6 year term and 65 years of age for retirement" without saying "whichever is earlier". Use the handout wording: 6-year term and age 65.

4 · Independence of the office#

Seven safeguards insulate the CAG from the Executive.

Seven safeguards
SafeguardWhat it means
Security of tenureDoes not hold office at the pleasure of the Executive; can be removed only by the prescribed procedure, not summarily dismissed by the President
Post-tenure ineligibilityNot eligible for any later office under the Centre or a State after vacating the CAG post, avoiding conflict of interest
Salary and conditions fixed by ParliamentPay equivalent to the highest judicial office; stops the Executive manipulating service terms
No detrimental alterationSalary, leave, pension and retirement age cannot be changed to his disadvantage after appointment
Consultative administrative arrangementsService conditions of IA&AD staff and the CAG's administrative powers are prescribed by the President after consulting the CAG
Charged expenditureAdministrative expenses of the CAG's office (salaries, allowances, pensions of staff) are charged on the Consolidated Fund and not subject to annual parliamentary vote
No ministerial shieldingMinisters do not represent the CAG in Parliament and cannot be held responsible for the CAG's actions
Seven safeguards
SPPPCCN

Security of tenure, Post-tenure bar, Parliament fixes pay, Protection from detrimental change, Consultation on staff conditions, Charged expenditure, No minister: "Safe Public Purse Protects People, Counting Cash Nightly".

Independence shortcut

Tenure, no later job, Parliament sets pay, no pay cuts, consult CAG on staff, charged on Consolidated Fund, no minister speaks for him.

Parliament

  • fixes the CAG's own salary and service conditions

President after consulting CAG

  • prescribes IA&AD staff conditions and the CAG's administrative powers

5 · Powers and responsibilities#

Under Article 149 Parliament defines the CAG's duties and powers on the accounts of the Union, States and other authorities. Parliament did this by the statute of 1971. A later amendment separated accounts from audit at the Centre.

Core audit mandate

  • Consolidated Funds: audit all expenditure charged on the Consolidated Fund of India, each State's Consolidated Fund and the Consolidated Funds of Union Territories with legislatures.
  • Contingency Fund and Public Account: audit expenditure from both at Union and State levels.
  • Department accounts: trading, manufacturing, profit and loss accounts, balance sheets and other subsidiary accounts kept by any Central or State department.
  • Revenue administration: audit receipts and related expenditure to check that systems, rules and procedures effectively control assessment, collection and allocation of revenue.
  • Institutions and corporations: bodies substantially financed from Centre or State revenues, Government companies and statutory corporations where law requires or the Executive requests.
  • Financial transactions: debt, sinking funds, deposits, advances, suspense accounts and remittances; with presidential approval other accounts such as receipts and stock accounts.
  • Special and requested audits: audit of any authority when requested by the President (Union) or the Governor (State).

Advisory and certification roles

Accounts and audit separation

A statutory amendment separated accounts compilation from audit at the Central level: the CAG kept the independent audit role, departmental accounts went to a separate accounting function. The CAG still compiles and maintains State accounts under the IA&AD.

6 · Role of the CAG: accountability and types of audit#

The CAG's basic duty is to safeguard the Constitution and parliamentary laws in financial administration. He is the instrument through which Parliament controls the executive: his reports are the cornerstone of Parliament's oversight of the Council of Ministers. He acts as an agent of Parliament, auditing expenditure on its behalf and remaining accountable solely to it.

Discretion

The CAG has greater discretion in the scope and method of audit of expenditure, but audit of receipts, stores and stock must follow procedures and rules approved by the executive government. He checks that funds were legally available for the purpose spent and that spending was strictly in accordance with the governing authority.

Four types of audit
TypeWhat it checks
Compliance audit (also legality, statutory or transactions audit)Whether money was spent according to financial law, was legally available and was spent on the activity authorised by Parliament
Financial auditWhether proper books of accounts are kept and recorded transactions faithfully represent what actually took place
Performance auditIncludes efficiency audit: eg the number of schools set up under a given amount and their standards and facilities
Propriety auditQuestions the wisdom and faithfulness of spending ('propriety' from 'proper'); eg 2G spectrum allocation on first come first served basis, audited by then CAG Vinod Rai: revenue loss of Rs. 1.76 lakh crore
  • The CAG does performance audit or reporting, not performance evaluation: reasons for inefficiency are outside his domain.
  • Propriety audit is needed because it is taxpayers' money, not officials' personal money, so officials spending someone else's money cannot be expected to show the caution they would with their own.
  • The handout says major financial frauds and scams come to light only through propriety audits.
Four audits
CFPP

Compliance, Financial, Performance, Propriety: "Clean Finances Prevent Plunder".

Performance audit

  • efficiency of spending (schools built, standards, facilities)
  • reporting not evaluation

Propriety audit

  • wisdom and faithfulness of spending (2G spectrum loss of Rs. 1.76 lakh crore)
Audit shortcut

Compliance = legality, Financial = books, Performance = efficiency, Propriety = wisdom.

7 · Limitations and the Comptroller versus Auditor role#

Limitations
LimitationDetail
Secret service expenditureThe CAG cannot demand detailed accounts or particulars; audit relies on a certificate of expenditure by the competent administrative authority
Comptroller versus AuditorIn practice he works primarily as an Auditor-General
No prior controlUnlike the British CAG who controls issue of public money, the Indian CAG does not control withdrawals from the Consolidated Fund; departments draw funds by cheque independently
Post-facto roleRole begins after expenditure; audit scrutiny follows completed transactions
Key point

Constitutionally he is Comptroller and Auditor but in practice an Auditor only: his scrutiny is post-facto.

Limits
SANP

Secret service, Auditor role, No prior control, Post-facto: "Silence All Night Please".

8 · Public corporations, government companies and the Audit Board#

His role in public corporations depends on the statute governing each entity.

Three categories of corporations
CategoryAudit
Direct audit by the CAGSome statutory corporations are audited entirely and directly by the CAG
Private auditors with CAG oversightProfessional auditors appointed by the Central Government in consultation with the CAG, who may later do a supplementary audit
Exclusive private auditA few corporations get only independent professional audits with no CAG involvement, though their reports go directly to Parliament
  • Government companies: audited by private auditors appointed by the Government on the CAG's advice; the CAG can conduct supplementary or test audits when necessary.

Audit Board (1968)

Audit Board

Established in the CAG's office on the recommendation of the Administrative Reforms Commission
Purpose
Assists audit of large, specialised enterprises: engineering, iron and steel, chemicals
Composition
Chairman and two Members, all appointed by the CAG
Effect
Adds technical depth and professional credibility to audit assessments
Corporations
DPE

Direct, Private with CAG oversight, Exclusive private: "Doing Proper Exams".

Board shortcut

Audit Board: 1968, ARC recommendation, three people (Chairman plus two Members), all appointed by the CAG.

Significance

Through his audit functions the CAG ensures legality, regularity and prudence in public spending and gives Parliament objective audit evidence.

9 · Appleby's critique of the audit establishment#

Paul H. Appleby criticised the audit establishment in India and questioned the utility and shape of the CAG's office. Six points are listed.

Six criticisms
CriticismArgument
Colonial inheritanceThe CAG's role is largely a legacy of colonial arrangements, not designed for an independent democracy
Inhibiting administrative actionAudit produced a "paralysing unwillingness to decide and to act": a repressive, negative influence
Parliamentary misconceptionParliament held an exaggerated view of auditing's contribution to legislative responsibility and had not defined the CAG's functions as the Constitution originally envisaged
Centrality questionedThe auditor's role is not as central to good governance as commonly assumed; auditors lack expertise to judge administration comprehensively
Narrow perspectiveAuditing is a narrow, procedural, "pedestrian" task: necessary for control but of restricted use for broader administration
Comparative knowledgeMiddle-level departmental officials (eg a deputy secretary) often understand departmental problems better than the CAG and his staff
Appleby's six
CIPCND

Colonial inheritance, Inhibiting action, Parliament's misconception, Centrality questioned, Narrow view, Deputy secretary knows more: "Critics Insist Parliament Counts No Difference".
