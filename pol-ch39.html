In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) is an independent constitutional authority under Article 148, head of the Indian Audit and Accounts Department and guardian of the public purse. Dr. Ambedkar called the CAG the most important officer under the Constitution.
  • The CAG is appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal and takes an oath before the President. The CAG Act, 1971 gives a 6-year term or age 65. Removal is like a Supreme Court judge.
  • Independence rests on security of tenure, no later government office, salary fixed by Parliament, no harmful change, staff rules after consulting the CAG, expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund and no ministerial shield.
  • Duties: audit Consolidated, Contingency and Public Account funds, departmental accounts, receipts, institutions and corporations. Advises on form of accounts (Art. 150), certifies net proceeds (Art. 279) and reports under Art. 151.
  • Four kinds of audit: compliance, financial, performance and propriety. The CAG is in practice an Auditor-General, not a Comptroller, because audit comes after spending.
  • Limits: no detailed particulars for secret service expenditure (a certificate is accepted). Appleby criticised the office as a colonial inheritance.

Key terms

Guardian of the public purse
Description of the CAG: makes sure public money is raised and spent as the law says
Compliance audit
Also called legality or transactions audit; checks money was spent under the law and Parliament's authority
Financial audit
Checks that books of accounts are proper and entries truly show what happened
Performance audit
Efficiency check, e.g. how many schools were built and their standards; it reports but does not evaluate reasons
Propriety audit
Questions the wisdom and faithfulness of spending; the 2G spectrum audit (loss Rs 1.76 lakh crore) is the handout example
Appropriation Accounts
Compare actual expenditure with Parliament's authorisation
Finance Accounts
Record the annual receipts and disbursements
Audit Board (1968)
Set up in the CAG's office on the Administrative Reforms Commission's advice; Chairman and two Members appointed by the CAG

1 · Who the CAG is#

  • The Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) is an independent constitutional authority under Article 148. He heads the Indian Audit and Accounts Department (IA&AD) and is the guardian of the public purse: public funds must be raised and spent in accordance with law.
  • Audits the financial transactions of both the Union and State governments.
  • Primary duty: to uphold the Constitution and parliamentary laws in financial administration.
  • Audit reports ensure accountability, transparency and fiscal discipline in the executive.
  • One of the key pillars of the democratic framework, alongside the Supreme Court, the Election Commission and the UPSC.
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called the CAG "the most important officer under the Constitution".
  • Memory aid (Pillars): Supreme Court, Election Commission, UPSC, CAG: "See Every Unit's Cash".
  • Shortcut: Ambedkar line. CAG = Art. 148 = guardian of the public purse = "most important officer under the Constitution".

2 · History of the IA&AD and its vision#

Four stages of the department

  • Pre-Independence: Mid-19th century: separate accounts for the three Presidencies (Bengal, Madras, Bombay). 1860: office of Auditor-General of India created. GoI Acts (1919, then 1935) gave audit statutory recognition and independent status
  • Independence and Constitution:
    • Auditor-General redesignated Comptroller and Auditor General under Art. 148
    • IA&AD became the machinery under the CAG for Union and State accounts and other bodies
  • Post-Independence:
    • Practices, audit standards and field structure expanded
    • IAAS formalised as the service cadre
    • A unitary structure within the federal setup lets the CAG audit Union and States through one framework
  • Modern era:
    • Emphasis on performance audit (value for money), compliance audits, accounting reforms and internal controls
    • The CAG's "A Thematic History" volumes cover 1990-2007

  • Seven safeguards insulate the CAG from the Executive.

5 · Powers and responsibilities#

  • Under Article 149 Parliament defines the CAG's duties and powers on the accounts of the Union, States and other authorities. Parliament did this by the statute of 1971. A later amendment separated accounts from audit at the Centre.

Core audit mandate

  • Consolidated Funds: audit all expenditure charged on the Consolidated Fund of India, each State's Consolidated Fund and the Consolidated Funds of Union Territories with legislatures.
  • Contingency Fund and Public Account: audit expenditure from both at Union and State levels.
  • Department accounts: trading, manufacturing, profit and loss accounts, balance sheets and other subsidiary accounts kept by any Central or State department.
  • Revenue administration: audit receipts and related expenditure to check that systems, rules and procedures effectively control assessment, collection and allocation of revenue.
  • Institutions and corporations: bodies substantially financed from Centre or State revenues, Government companies and statutory corporations where law requires or the Executive requests.
  • Financial transactions:
    • Debt, sinking funds, deposits, advances, suspense accounts and remittances
    • With presidential approval other accounts such as receipts and stock accounts
  • Special and requested audits: audit of any authority when requested by the President (Union) or the Governor (State).

Advisory and certification roles

6 · Role of the CAG: accountability and types of audit#

  • The CAG's basic duty is to safeguard the Constitution and parliamentary laws in financial administration. He is the instrument through which Parliament controls the executive: his reports are the cornerstone of Parliament's oversight of the Council of Ministers. He acts as an agent of Parliament, auditing expenditure on its behalf and remaining accountable solely to it.
  • Discretion: The CAG has greater discretion in the scope and method of audit of expenditure, but audit of receipts, stores and stock must follow procedures and rules approved by the executive government. He checks that funds were legally available for the purpose spent and that spending was strictly in accordance with the governing authority.

Four types of audit

TypeWhat it checks
Compliance audit (also legality, statutory or transactions audit)Whether money was spent according to financial law, was legally available and was spent on the activity authorised by Parliament
Financial auditWhether proper books of accounts are kept and recorded transactions faithfully represent what actually took place
Performance auditIncludes efficiency audit: eg the number of schools set up under a given amount and their standards and facilities
Propriety auditQuestions the wisdom and faithfulness of spending ('propriety' from 'proper'); eg 2G spectrum allocation on first come first served basis, audited by then CAG Vinod Rai: revenue loss of Rs. 1.76 lakh crore
  • The CAG does performance audit or reporting, not performance evaluation: reasons for inefficiency are outside his domain.
  • Propriety audit is needed because it is taxpayers' money, not officials' personal money, so officials spending someone else's money cannot be expected to show the caution they would with their own.
  • The handout says major financial frauds and scams come to light only through propriety audits.
  • Memory aid (Four audits): Compliance, Financial, Performance, Propriety: "Clean Finances Prevent Plunder".

Performance audit versus propriety audit

  • Performance audit:
    • Efficiency of spending (schools built, standards, facilities)
    • Reporting not evaluation
  • Propriety audit: wisdom and faithfulness of spending (2G spectrum loss of Rs. 1.76 lakh crore)
  • Shortcut: Audit shortcut. Compliance = legality, Financial = books, Performance = efficiency, Propriety = wisdom.

Also in the handout

  • The word 'propriety' is taken from the word 'proper'.

  • His role in public corporations depends on the statute governing each entity.

  • 1968: Established in the CAG's office on the recommendation of the Administrative Reforms Commission
  • Purpose: Assists audit of large, specialised enterprises: engineering, iron and steel, chemicals
  • Composition: Chairman and two Members, all appointed by the CAG
  • Effect: Adds technical depth and professional credibility to audit assessments

9 · Appleby's critique of the audit establishment#

  • Paul H. Appleby criticised the audit establishment in India and questioned the utility and shape of the CAG's office. Six points are listed.

Six criticisms

CriticismArgument
Colonial inheritanceThe CAG's role is largely a legacy of colonial arrangements, not designed for an independent democracy
Inhibiting administrative actionAudit produced a "paralysing unwillingness to decide and to act": a repressive, negative influence
Parliamentary misconceptionParliament held an exaggerated view of auditing's contribution to legislative responsibility and had not defined the CAG's functions as the Constitution originally envisaged
Centrality questionedThe auditor's role is not as central to good governance as commonly assumed; auditors lack expertise to judge administration comprehensively
Narrow perspectiveAuditing is a narrow, procedural, "pedestrian" task: necessary for control but of restricted use for broader administration
Comparative knowledgeMiddle-level departmental officials (eg a deputy secretary) often understand departmental problems better than the CAG and his staff
  • Memory aid (Appleby's six): Colonial inheritance, Inhibiting action, Parliament's misconception, Centrality questioned, Narrow view, Deputy secretary knows more: "Critics Insist Parliament Counts No Difference".

Traps#

Trap 1 · Term is in the Act, not the Constitution The 6-year term and age-65 limit come from the CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971, so changing them needs an amendment to the Act, not to the Constitution. Rule: term and age = 1971 Act; removal procedure = Constitution (like an SC judge).
Trap 2 · Comptroller or Auditor? The Constitution names both roles, but the CAG does not control issue of money from the Consolidated Fund. Departments draw funds by cheque, and the CAG audits after the spending. Rule: India's CAG = post-facto Auditor-General; the British CAG also controls issues.
Trap 3 · Performance audit is not evaluation The CAG reports on performance, for example the number and standard of schools, but the reasons for inefficiency are outside the CAG's domain. Rule: performance reporting is yes; performance evaluation is no.
Trap 4 · Contracts and corporations are not all alike Some corporations are audited directly by the CAG, some by private auditors with CAG oversight, and a few only by private auditors. Government companies get private auditors on the CAG's advice. Rule: the CAG's role depends on each corporation's own statute.

Book edition: simple words and memory words#

In simple words

  • The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) is an independent constitutional authority under Article 148, head of the Indian Audit and Accounts Department and guardian of the public purse. Dr. Ambedkar called the CAG the most important officer under the Constitution.
  • The CAG is appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal and takes an oath before the President. The CAG Act, 1971 gives a 6-year term or age 65. Removal is like a Supreme Court judge.
  • Independence rests on security of tenure, no later government office, salary fixed by Parliament, no harmful change, staff rules after consulting the CAG, expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund and no ministerial shield.
  • Duties: audit Consolidated, Contingency and Public Account funds, departmental accounts, receipts, institutions and corporations. Advises on form of accounts (Art. 150), certifies net proceeds (Art. 279) and reports under Art. 151.
  • Four kinds of audit: compliance, financial, performance and propriety. The CAG is in practice an Auditor-General, not a Comptroller, because audit comes after spending.
  • Limits: no detailed particulars for secret service expenditure (a certificate is accepted). Appleby criticised the office as a colonial inheritance.

Key terms

Guardian of the public purse
Description of the CAG: makes sure public money is raised and spent as the law says
Compliance audit
Also called legality or transactions audit; checks money was spent under the law and Parliament's authority
Financial audit
Checks that books of accounts are proper and entries truly show what happened
Performance audit
Efficiency check, e.g. how many schools were built and their standards; it reports but does not evaluate reasons
Propriety audit
Questions the wisdom and faithfulness of spending; the 2G spectrum audit (loss Rs 1.76 lakh crore) is the handout example
Appropriation Accounts
Compare actual expenditure with Parliament's authorisation
Finance Accounts
Record the annual receipts and disbursements
Audit Board (1968)
Set up in the CAG's office on the Administrative Reforms Commission's advice; Chairman and two Members appointed by the CAG
