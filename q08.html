1995PYQ 1Hughly piracy in the Bay of Bengal

Hughly was used as a base for piracy in the Bay of Bengal by

Answer: (A)

Why (a) is correct: The Portuguese had been misusing their trade privileges from their Bengal base — engaging in piracy & the slave trade. This is why Shahjahan27-58 ordered his Bengal governor Qasim Khan to take direct action in 1631–32, defeating the Portuguese and ejecting them from Hugli. You will find this in 'Royal Farman of 1618 and Thomas Roe (1615-19)'.

Why the other options are wrong:

  • (b) The French had no Bengal piracy base — their factories at Chandernagore (1690s) were trading posts, not pirate dens.
  • (c) The Danish base was at Tranquebar (Tamil Nadu) and Serampore (Bengal, from 1755) — both lawful trading posts.
  • (d) The British EIC's Bengal entry came after 1651 (Hugli factory under Shah Shuja's nishan) — well after the Portuguese piracy had been suppressed by Shahjahan27-58.
2003PYQ 2European-entry statements

With reference to the entry of European powers into India, which one of the following statements is not correct?

Answer: (A)

Why the other options are correct (and so not the answer):

  • (b) The English first South-India factory was at Masulipatnam (1611) — a real fact in 'What Made the Dutch VOC Historically Unique?' (cross-referenced).
  • (c) The English first Eastern-India factory was at Balasore and Hariharpur in Orissa in 1633 — a real fact.
  • (d) Dupleix42-54 did capture Madras in 1746 (1st Carnatic War, with La Bourdonnais35-46) — a real fact.

2006PYQ 41613 EIC factory permission

In the year 1613, where was the English East India Company given permission to set up a factory (trading post)?

Answer: (D)

Why (d) is correct: Jahangir05-27 issued a Royal Farman in 1613, the imperial decree that gave the EIC the legal right to set up a permanent factory at Surat, following the Battle of Swally (1612). You will find this in 'How EIC Broke Portuguese Dominance and Establish Itself in India?'.

Why the other options are wrong:

  • (a) Bangalore had no EIC factory in 1613 — it became part of British India only after the Anglo-Mysore Wars (1799 onwards).
  • (b) Madras was acquired from Hindu Nayakas in 1639, not 1613.
  • (c) Masulipatnam had a temporary EIC factory in 1611 — but the 1613 imperial farman was specifically for Surat.
2007PYQ 5First British fort

Which one of the following was the first fort constructed by the British in India?

Answer: (B)

Why (b) is correct: Fort St. George (Madras) was built between 1639 & 1644 — the first fort built by the EIC in India and the anchor of the first permanent settlement. You will find this in 'Madras Presidency (from 1652/1653)'.

Why the other options are wrong:

  • (a) Fort William at Calcutta was built between 1696–1700 — much later than Fort St. George.
  • (c) Fort Saint David at Cuddalore was acquired in 1690 (purchased from Maratha Chhatrapati Rajaram89-00) — also later, and not built but purchased.
  • (d) Fort St. Angelo (Kannur Fort) was built by the Portuguese in 1505, not the British.
2007PYQ 6Last Europeans to come

Who among the following Europeans, were the last to come to pre-independence India as traders?

Answer: (C)

Why (c) is correct: The French East India Company was founded in 1664 & set up its first Indian factory at Surat in 1668 — the latest among the four. You will find this in 'The French in India — and the Story of Pondicherry'.

Why the other options are wrong:

  • (a) Dutch first factory in India: Masulipatnam, 1605 ('What Made the Dutch VOC Historically Unique?'.
  • (b) English EIC first factory in India: Masulipatnam, 1611 ('How EIC Broke Portuguese Dominance and Establish Itself in India?'.
  • (d) Portuguese first arrived in India: Vasco Da Gama at Calicut, 1498 ('the Portuguese in India').

The full chronology is: Portuguese 1498 → Dutch 1605 → English 1611 → Danish 1620 → French 1664/1668.

2010PYQ 7Pondicherry occupation sequence

With reference to Pondicherry, consider: (1) The first European power to occupy Pondicherry were the Portuguese. (2) The second European power to occupy Pondicherry were the French. (3) The English never occupied Pondicherry.

Answer: (A)

Why (a) is correct: Statement 1 is correct — the Portuguese were the first to occupy Pondicherry (16th century). The full layered sequence is: Portuguese (16th c.) → Danes (briefly 1624) → French (1674) → Dutch (briefly 1693) → English (1761/1793/1814) → India (1954). You will find this in 'The French in India — and the Story of Pondicherry' (inside the ⚠ Alert block).

Why the other statements are wrong:

  • Statement 2 is wrong — the second occupiers were the Danes (briefly in 1624), not the French. The French came fourth, in 1674.
  • Statement 3 is wrong — the English captured Pondicherry multiple times (1761, 1793, 1814) — they restored it to the French each time via subsequent treaties.
2012PYQ 8English introductions to India

Which of the following were introduced into India by the English? (1) Land-revenue assessment by soil and crop. (2) Mobile cannons in warfare. (3) Tobacco and red chillies.

Answer: (D)

Why (d) is correct: None of the three were introduced by the English. You will find the cross-reference in 'Why Did the Portuguese Decline?' (Portuguese contributions).

Why each statement is wrong:

  • Statement 1 — Land-revenue assessment by soil and crop was already prevalent in medieval India (Sher Shah Suri, Akbar's Todar Mal system) long before the British.
  • Statement 2 — Mobile cannons in warfare were introduced by Babur26-30 at the First Battle of Panipat in 1526 — not by the English.
  • Statement 3 — Tobacco and red chillies were introduced by the Portuguese Sec. 4.8 (Why Did the Portuguese Decline?)
2015PYQ 91st quarter of 17C English factories

In the first quarter of seventeenth century, in which of the following was/were the factory/factories of the English East India Company located? (1) Broach (2) Chicacole (3) Trichinopoly

Answer: (A)

Why (a) is correct: Among the three, only Broach (Bharuch) had an EIC factory in the first quarter of the 17th century. EIC factories during 1616–19 (Roe-era expansion) were at Agra, Ahmedabad, & Broach (Bharuch) — all within the Mughal Empire. You will find this in 'Royal Farman of 1618 and Thomas Roe (1615-19)'.

Why the other options are wrong:

  • Chicacole (today's Srikakulam, Andhra Pradesh) had no EIC factory in this period — it was outside Mughal direct control and EIC trading there came much later.
  • Trichinopoly (Tamil Nadu) was deep in the Carnatic — the EIC's South India trade in the early 17th century was centred on Masulipatnam, not Trichinopoly.

2021PYQ 12St. Francis Xavier
