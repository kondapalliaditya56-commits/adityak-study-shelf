2014PYQ 1 Why were so many empire-shaking battles fought at Panipat?Mains 2014 · 10 marks · 150 words

"The third battle of Panipat was fought in 1761. Why were so many empire-shaking battles fought at Panipat?"

Demand of the question: Explain why this one place — Panipat — kept becoming the site of empire-deciding battles. You must cover two parts: (a) the geographic reason (why this terrain), and (b) the historical pattern (multiple battles, each empire-defining). The verb " why " calls for causal explanation, not narrative description. Keep the 1761 anchor in mind — your answer must connect to the Third Battle of Panipat as the prompt's reference point.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Many empire-shaking battles were fought at Panipat because Panipat was India's natural Delhi gateway from the northwest — and whoever won there won the throne, as the third battle of Panipat in 1761 again confirmed.

Guiding themes (pick 4–5 for a 150-word answer):

1. Geography — Delhi gateway (see Chapter 4, Marathas; see Chapter 5, Afghans).

2. 1st Panipat 1526 — Babur26-30 defeats Ibrahim Lodi → birth of the Mughal Empire (see Chapter 1, Mughal Background).

3. 2nd Panipat 1556 — Akbar56-05 vs Hemu → consolidation of Mughal rule (see Chapter 1, Mughal Background).

4. 3rd Panipat 1761 — Ahmad Shah Abdali vs Marathas → peak-and-break of Maratha imperial expansion (see Chapter 4, Marathas; see Chapter 5, Afghans).

5. Sikh-side resistance to Afghan invasions — 1606 + 1675 Guru martyrdoms + Ranjit Singh01-39 vs Zaman Shah 1799.

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): The empire-shaking battles fought at Panipat — 1526, 1556, 1761 — happened because controlling Panipat meant controlling the Delhi throne.

2017PYQ 2 Fragmented polity in mid-18th centuryMains 2017 · 10 marks · 150 words

"Clarify how mid-eighteenth-century India was beset with the spectre of a fragmented polity."

Demand of the question: Show how India was a fragmented polity by the mid-eighteenth century. The verb " clarify " means make the case clearly with evidence — give multiple regional examples. The phrase " spectre of a fragmented polity " tells you the question accepts the premise as a recognised condition; your job is to show how, not to debate whether it was fragmented.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Mid-eighteenth-century India was indeed beset by the spectre of a fragmented polity — Mughal authority had broken apart into many separate regional powers across the subcontinent.

Guiding themes (pick 4–5 for a 150-word answer):

1. Successor States — Bengal, Awadh, Hyderabad (see Chapter 3, Successor States).

2. Maratha Confederacy (see Chapter 4, Rise of Marathas).

3. Jats + Afghans (see Chapter 5, Jats and Afghans).

4. Sikh State — Banda Bahadur08-15 → Misl Confederacy → Ranjit Singh01-39.

5. Mysore — Haider Ali61-82 + Tipu82-99 (see Chapter 7 — 'How did Mysore go from being a minor kingdom to one of the most powerful states in 18th-century India?' + 'What made Tipu82-99 a different kind of ruler — and why did he still lose?').

6. Travancore — Marthanda Varma29-58 (see Chapter 7 — 'Travancore:' + 'Travancore vs Mysore & EIC').

7. Rajputs post-Aurangzeb58-07 (see Chapter 7 — 'Akbar56-05 and Rajputs' + 'Rajputs vs Aurangzeb58-07 and Marathas').

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): The fragmented polity that haunted mid-eighteenth-century India was already a settled reality — the EIC's later expansion only used this break-up; it did not cause it.

FYQ 1

With reference to Sikhism's foundational period, consider the following statements: 1. Guru Nanak Dev Ji (1469–1539) was a contemporary of Babur26-30. 2. Sikhism rose from around 1500 onwards. 3. Guru Nanak undertook four major spiritual journeys (Udasis) over 30 years, covering approximately 30,000 km across India, South Asia, Tibet, & Arabia. 4. Guru Nanak settled in Kartarpur (present-day Pakistan) & established the institution of Langar — the free communal kitchen designed to promote equality. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (C)

(c) 1, 2, 3 and 4. All four facts are taken straight from the record: Guru Nanak Dev Ji (1469–1539) was a contemporary of Babur26-30; Sikhism rose from 1500 onwards; the four Udasis spanned 30 years and ~30,000 km across India, South Asia, Tibet and Arabia; Kartarpur was the final settlement and the institution of Langar promoted equality.

FYQ 2

Which one of the following correctly describes the establishment of the Khalsa?

Answer: (B)

FYQ 5

Which one of the following correctly describes Bahadur Shah07-12's policy towards the Sikhs in 1707?

Answer: (B)

(b) is correct. The record carries the answer: Bahadur Shah07-12 followed a more conciliatory policy — granted a high Mansab to Guru Gobind Singh Ji76-08 and promised a Sikh State in and around Anandpur, but the promise was never fulfilled (partly to appease the Shivalik Hill Chiefs). (a), (c), (d) are factually wrong — Guru Gobind Singh Ji76-08 was assassinated in October 1708 at Nanded by Wazir Khan's plot, not by Bahadur Shah07-12.

FYQ 11

Answer: (A)

(a) 1-D, 2-C, 3-A, 4-B. The record carries the four-outcome breakdown of the Treaty of Amritsar 1809: Sovereignty = EIC recognises Ranjit Singh01-39 as sovereign ruler of Punjab (D); Alliance = military alliance between the two powers, later linked to the EIC's Ring Fence Policy (C); Border = the Sutlej River fixed as border (A); Expansion = Ranjit Singh01-39 free to expand on his side of the Sutlej without British interference (B).

FYQ 12

With reference to the 1838 Tripartite Treaty, consider the following statements: 1. The signatories were the Sikh State, the EIC, & Shah Shuja of Afghanistan. 2. The treaty was designed to establish British indirect rule in Afghanistan through Shah Shuja as a puppet ruler. 3. The strategic aim was to create a buffer of Afghanistan and Punjab against the feared Russian invasion of British India from the northwest. 4. Hari Singh Nalwa, who conquered Peshawar in 1834, signed the treaty on behalf of the Sikh State. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(b) 1, 2 and 3 only. Statements 1, 2, 3 are taken from the record: the 1838 Tripartite Treaty was between the Sikh State, the EIC, and Shah Shuja of Afghanistan; designed to establish British indirect rule through Shah Shuja as puppet; aimed at creating a buffer against feared Russian invasion. Statement 4 is the trap — Hari Singh Nalwa conquered Peshawar in 1834 (true) but did NOT sign the 1838 Tripartite Treaty. The Sikh State signatory was Ranjit Singh01-39 himself.

FYQ 1 How Did Sikhism Transform from a Spiritual Movement into a Martial Community? 150 words · 10 marks "Sikhism began as a peaceful spiritual movement under Guru Nanak Dev (1469-1539). By 1699, it had become a martial community under the Khalsa. Trace the transformation and explain its main triggers."

Demand of the question: Show how Sikhism moved from spiritual to martial + name the main triggers.

Suggested intro (~25 words): "Sikhism did not begin as a militant movement. Guru Nanak Dev preached one God + equality + service. The change to a martial community came through specific Mughal-state pressures."

Guiding themes:

1. Spiritual base — Guru Nanak Dev (1469-1539): one God, no rituals, no caste, honest living + service; Langar at Kartarpur. (see 'How did Sikhism begin, and what were its core beliefs?')

2. First trigger: 1606 execution of Guru Arjan Dev — by Jahangir05-27. Turning point: from peaceful spirituality to political-religious tension. (see 'When did conflict begin between the Sikhs and the Mughal State?')

3. Second trigger: 1675 execution of Guru Teg Bahadur — by Aurangzeb58-07. Sealed the Sikh-Mughal break. (see 'When did conflict begin between the Sikhs and the Mughal State?')

4. Khalsa formation 1699 — by 10th Guru Gobind Singh76-08 at Anandpur: military body to defend the Sikh Panth and fight for a Sikh State. (see 'What was the Khalsa, and why was it created?')

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): "From Nanak’s Kartarpur Langar to Gobind Singh’s Khalsa, Sikhism’s arc was bent by two Guru martyrdoms under Mughal rule."

Revise: 'How did Sikhism begin, and what were its core beliefs?' and 'When did conflict begin between the Sikhs and the Mughal State?' + 'What was the Khalsa, and why was it created?'.

FYQ 2 Ranjit Singh01-39’s Sikh State — Why Did It Hold While He Lived and Collapse After His Death? 150 words · 10 marks "Ranjit Singh01-39 built a powerful Sikh State that held together for almost forty years. Yet within a decade of his death, it was lost to the EIC. Examine the reasons."

Demand of the question: Show what held the Sikh State together under Ranjit Singh01-39 + show why those binding factors broke after his death.

Suggested intro (~25 words): "Ranjit Singh01-39 united the Misls into one strong Sikh State. The State held by his personal command. When he died in 1839, those personal threads snapped."

Guiding themes:

1. Misl unification + personal authority — Ranjit Singh01-39 brought the 12 Misls under one rule by 1801. Authority rested on his person, not on institutions. (see 'How did Ranjit Singh01-39 unify the Misls and build a powerful Sikh State?')

2. 1809 Treaty of Amritsar with the EIC — stopped Sikh State expansion south of the Sutlej. Bought time but locked the State into a fixed boundary. (see 'What were the terms of the 1809 Treaty of Amritsar with the EIC?')

3. Expansion + modernised army — post-1809 Ranjit Singh01-39 pushed west and north; built a European-trained army with French and Italian officers. (see 'How far did Ranjit Singh01-39 expand the Sikh State after 1809?' and 'What kind of State did Ranjit Singh01-39 build — and what made it hold together?')

4. Post-1839 collapse — weak successors, court factionalism, palace intrigues; the EIC won the First Anglo-Sikh War (1845-46) and the Second (1848-49); Punjab annexed

1849. (See Ch 14 — 'What were the terms of the 1809 Treaty of Amritsar with the EIC?' Sikh State annexation)

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): "Ranjit Singh01-39 built a State that ran on his person. With no institutions to outlast him, the State could not survive his death."

Revise: 'How did Ranjit Singh01-39 unify the Misls and build a powerful Sikh State?' and 'What were the terms of the 1809 Treaty of Amritsar with the EIC?' + 'How far did Ranjit Singh01-39 expand the Sikh State after 1809?' and 'What kind of State did Ranjit Singh01-39 build — and what made it hold together?' + Ch 14 'What were the terms of the 1809 Treaty of Amritsar with the EIC?'.
