2019PYQ 1(Prelims 2019)

With reference to the British colonial rule in India, consider the following statements. 1. Mahatma Gandhi was instrumental in the abolition of the system of 'Indentured Labour'. 2. In Lord Chelmsford's 'War Conference', Mahatma Gandhi did not support the resolution on recruiting Indians for World War. 3. Consequent upon the breaking of Salt Law by Indian people, the Indian National Congress was declared illegal by the colonial rulers. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Statements 1 and 3 are correct; statement 2 is wrong. Statement 1: Gandhi was instrumental in abolishing indentured labour — he led the campaign against the indenture system that culminated in its abolition in 1917; the record alert marks this as the examiner-tested anchor. Statement 2 fails: at the April 1918 Delhi War Conference Gandhi DID support recruitment of Indians for the war — so the claim that he did not support it is false.

Statement 3 is correct: after the Salt Law was broken in 1930, the colonial government declared the INC illegal.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — "1 and 2" keeps the false statement 2: Gandhi did NOT oppose recruitment at the 1918 War Conference — he supported it, hoping wartime loyalty would earn post-war reform.

(C) ✗ — "2 and 3" rests on the same false statement 2 and throws away the true statement 1 — the indenture-abolition campaign.

(D) ✗ — "1, 2 and 3" overcounts: statement 2 inverts Gandhi's actual War-Conference position. (Note: The first agitation that Mahatma Gandhi launched during his days in South Africa was against the system of 'Indentured Labour'. It was a system based on contract, in which the labours from India were sent to work in distant British colonies.)

FYQ 1

At Pietermaritzburg railway station in 1893, Gandhiji was ejected from a first-class compartment despite holding a valid ticket. What was the immediate consequence of this incident for him?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — Chapter states the humiliation at the cold station platform became his political catalyst — the moment he resolved to fight racial discrimination rather than quietly endure it.

Why others wrong: (a) He stayed on for twenty-two years, not returned. (c) Natal Indian Congress was founded in 1894, not the same week. (d) He continued legal work; no renunciation mentioned.

Memory hook: Pietermaritzburg platform — cold night, hot resolve.

FYQ 2

Consider the following statements regarding the Franchise Amendment Bill, 1894 (Natal): 1. It sought to disenfranchise Indians by stripping them of the right to vote. 2. Some Indians had earlier qualified to vote on a wealth/property criterion. 3. The Bill was confined to the Transvaal province. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — Stmt 1 correct: Bill sought to disenfranchise Indians — to strip them of the Right to Vote. Stmt 2 correct: Some Indians had earlier qualified for the vote based on a wealth criterion. Stmt 3 wrong: it was Natal, not Transvaal.

Why others wrong: Any option including Stmt 3 fails — Bill was Natal-specific.

Memory hook: Natal '94 — vote stripped on race, not on coin.

FYQ 3

The Immigration Law Amendment Bill, 1895 (Natal) gave Indian indentured labourers, on completion of their contract, which of the following choices?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(D) ✓ — Chapter: Indians had to leave South Africa, or re-indenture for two more years. If they chose neither, they had to pay a poll tax of £3 per year.

Why others wrong: (a) No citizenship offer in Chapter. (b) Inter-provincial movement is the 1907 Transvaal law, not this. (c) Registration Certificate belongs to the 1906 Black Act.

Memory hook: Three doors, all closing — leave, re-bond, or pay £3.

FYQ 4

Which of the following correctly describes Gandhiji's Phase 1 — the Constitutional Struggle (1896–1906) in South Africa?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — Chapter: He organised petitions, prayers, and public meetings. He founded the Natal Indian Congress. He also launched a newspaper, Indian Opinion.

Why others wrong: (a) Bonfires & mass disobedience belong to Phase 2 (post-1906). (b) No armed self-defence; approach was non-violent and constitutional. (d) No INC-coordinated boycott mentioned in this phase.

Memory hook: Phase 1 — three Ps: petitions, prayers, press.

FYQ 5

Non-violent — absolutely, even under provocation 3. Fearless — willing to face arrest and imprisonment 4. Vengeful toward the wrongdoer Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — Stmts 1, 2, 3 correctly listed in Chapter. Stmt 4 is the opposite of Gandhiji's code: a Satyagrahi had to be "Loving toward the wrongdoer — hate was to have no place."

Why others wrong: Any option including Stmt 4 contradicts the loving-toward-wrongdoer requirement.

Memory hook: Satyagrahi — truth, ahimsa, fearless, love (never hate).

FYQ 6

Phoenix Settlement, established by Gandhiji in 1904 near Durban, was inspired by which of the following works?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — Chapter: In 1904, inspired by John Ruskin's Unto This Last, he established the Phoenix Settlement near Durban.

Why others wrong: (a) Tolstoy's writings inspired Tolstoy Farm (1910), not Phoenix Settlement.

(c) Thoreau not mentioned in Chapter. (d) Tilak's Gita commentary not mentioned in Chapter in this connection.

FYQ 7

The Asiatic Law Amendment Act 1906 — bitterly called the 'Black Act' — was passed by which government and what did it require?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — Chapter: The Asiatic Law Amendment Act 1906…was passed by the Transvaal government. It required every Indian to carry a Registration Certificate (RC) at all times.

Why others wrong: (a) Poll tax belongs to the 1895 Natal Immigration Law, not the 1906 Black Act. (b) Property bar not in Chapter. (d) Disenfranchisement was the 1894 Franchise Bill (Natal).

Memory hook: Black Act '06 — Transvaal, carry your card.

FYQ 10

Gopal Krishna Gokhale's strategic advice to Gandhiji in 1912 was to focus the South African movement on which two specific demands?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(D) ✓ — Chapter: Gokhale advised Gandhiji to focus on abolition of the Poll Tax (under the Immigration Law) and abolition of the Registration Certificate requirement (under the Black Act).

Why others wrong: (a) Indentured-labour abolition is a separate Prelims 2019 anchor, not Gokhale's 1912 advice. (b) Inter-provincial movement was an issue but not one of Gokhale's two named focuses. (c) Franchise was the 1894 issue; not on Gokhale's 1912 short-list.

Memory hook: Gokhale 1912 — two arrows: poll tax + RC.

Chapter-derived Mains practice with the demand, guiding themes and a conclusion.

FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words

"Gandhiji did not bring Satyagraha to India — he brought it back from South Africa." Examine.

Demand of the question: The prompt rejects the popular framing that Gandhi was the architect of Satyagraha as an Indian innovation. "Examine" requires you to test the claim against the evidence — was the method actually built in South Africa, & what makes that location pedagogically necessary?

Suggested intro (~25 words): Satyagraha was forged not in India's freedom struggle but in 22 years of South African experiment (1893–1915) — the method, the personnel and the conviction all preceded Gandhiji's 1915 return. Body guidance — 4 themes:

1. The personal code came before the political programme (see 'Phase 2 — The Extra-Constitutional Struggle (1906–1915)'). Gandhiji's 5-point Satyagrahi standard — truthfulness, non-violence, fearlessness, willingness to suffer, love for the wrongdoer — was distilled from a decade of personal experiment in South Africa, not theorised from a desk.

2. The training infrastructure was built in SA, not India (see 'Phase 2 — The Extra-Constitutional Struggle (1906–1915)'). Phoenix Settlement (1904, Ruskin-inspired) & Tolstoy Farm (1910) were physical training grounds for Satyagrahis and their families. India had no equivalent until Sabarmati Ashram (1917), built on the SA template.

3. The repertoire was tested live on real laws (see 'Phase 2 — The Extra-Constitutional Struggle (1906–1915)'). Bonfire of Registration Certificates, Jail Bharo, deliberate boundary-crossing arrests, hawking without licence — every tactic later used in India was rehearsed against the Black Act 1906 & the Transvaal Immigration Restriction Act 1907.

4. The mass character came from the 1913 turning point (see 'The Poll Tax Campaign and the Final Countdown (1913)'). The Poll Tax Campaign drew in women, miners and plantation workers — Satyagraha was no longer a middle-class lawyers' protest but a multi-class movement. The Champaran Satyagraha (1917) inherited this pattern directly.

Closing verdict: Gandhiji's 22 South African years were Satyagraha's R&D phase — code, training, repertoire and mass-character all developed in the Transvaal and Natal before any of it touched Indian soil. India received the finished method, not the prototype.

Revise: 'Satyagrahi code' + 'Phoenix Settlement' + 'Tolstoy Farm' + 'Satyagraha Against Registration Certificates (1906–1914)' + 5.4 (Black Act + Immigration Satyagrahas) + '1913 mass turn'.

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words The year 1913 marked the decisive turn in Gandhiji's South African struggle. Identify the three developments that converged that year, & explain why they together pushed the movement to its peak.

Demand of the question: "Identify... and explain" = enumerate + give the mechanism for each. The prompt frames 1913 as a convergence point, not a single-cause turning point.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Until 1913 the struggle had been confined to the educated Indian middle class fighting specific laws. Three converging developments in that one year transformed it into a mass movement with imperial-level backing. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'The Poll Tax Campaign and the Final Countdown (1913)'):

1. The Christian-marriage judgement. The South African Supreme Court ruled in 1913 that only Christian marriages were legally valid — automatically de-recognising Hindu, Muslim and Parsi marriages of Indians in SA. The ruling hit every Indian family, including those who had not previously joined the struggle. Mechanism: turned a legal grievance into a personal-family insult, drawing women into the movement for the first time.

2. The Poll Tax Campaign acquires mass character. Gokhale's 1912 strategic advice had narrowed the demand to two emotionally resonant items — the £3 Poll Tax and the RC. By 1913, miners and plantation workers (the population actually paying the tax) joined the campaign in large numbers. Mechanism: the demand finally aligned with the grievance of the worst-affected class.

3. Viceroy Hardinge10-16's public condemnation. An unprecedented diplomatic intervention — a British Viceroy in India publicly condemned a British dominion government for treating Indians as inferior subjects. Mechanism: the SA government could no longer treat the issue as a local Indian-community matter; it was now an imperial embarrassment.

Closing verdict: Marriage-validity (family front), Poll Tax mass mobilisation (class front), Hardinge's pressure (imperial front) — three simultaneous fronts forced the Smuts-Gandhi compromise of 1914.

Revise: 'The Poll Tax Campaign and the Final Countdown' + 'Outcome'.
