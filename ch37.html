Exam lens#

Prelims hooks

  • Pilots: Champaran (Rajkumar Shukla, Tinkathia 3/20th, Rajendra Prasad, J.B. Kripalani, first CD, Act 1918); Ahmedabad (Sarabhai, plague bonus, 50 / 35 / 27.5, Textile Labour Association, founder Gandhi); Kheda (ryotwari remission, Patel, NC not CD)
  • Rowlatt Act (Feb 1919, ILC, Sedition Committee, DoI Act 1915, imprisonment without trial, Chelmsford 16-21), Rowlatt Satyagraha as Gandhi's first all-India venture, Jallianwala Bagh (13 April, 379 against 1,500, Hunter Committee, Tagore, Montague's 'preventive murder')
  • Khilafat and NCM: Khilafat Committee Mar 1919, Allahabad Jun 1920, Calcutta Sep 1920 (Lajpat Rai), Nagpur Dec 1920 (C. Vijayaraghavachariar), Hasrat Mohani and complete independence (2001, 2004), programme (boycott, Swadeshi, CW; titles surrendered)
  • NCM events and results: Prince of Wales Nov 1921, Chauri Chaura Feb 1922 (3 killed, 22 policemen, Malaviya's defence), Bardoli Resolution, what the NCM gave (mass Congress, fear removed) and what it did not (concessions)
  • Passive Phase: Swarajists against No-Changers, Gaya 1922, Swaraj Party 1923, Belgaum 1924 and the Gandhi-Das Pact, Responsivists, INC leaves the councils 1926; chronology Minto-Morley, Montford, Chauri Chaura, Dandi (2004)

Three pilots built the method, Rowlatt showed what a leaderless crowd does, and the NCM made the INC a mass body, until Chauri Chaura sent it into quiet years of Constructive Work.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: MG built a method in three small pilots, learnt from the Rowlatt failure that a mass movement needs control, and used the Khilafat anger to make the INC a mass organisation (NCM 1920-22). When Chauri Chaura broke that movement, the INC split over council entry but stayed whole, and Constructive Work kept it alive until the Simon Commission.

  1. The cake is cut (1915-18). MG arrives in January 1915, tours India on Gokhale's advice and stays out of the INM during WWI because he will not use the enemy's weakness. After two years he picks three local fights: Champaran (civil disobedience against indigo planters, Tinkathia abolished, Act 1918), Ahmedabad (fast and arbitration: 50, 35, 27.5) and Kheda (first non-cooperation, law on the ryots' side, Patel joins).
    → But a pilot is not a nation: the next call is for all Indians.
  2. The Rowlatt promise broken (1919). In February 1919 the ILC passes the Rowlatt Act over the opposition of every Indian member: it carries the Defence of India Act 1915 into peacetime, with WWI misery and the fear of communism behind it. MG, who backed the war hoping for self-government and who hates a breach of promise (Montague Statement 1917, MC Report 1918), calls a nationwide non-violent strike.
    → But a strike without a driver runs wild.
  3. The driverless truck (April 1919). Leaders are arrested, violence spreads in Punjab, O'Dwyer empowers and Dyer applies martial law. On 13 April at Jallianwala Bagh Dyer's troops fire for 10 minutes: 379 dead by the official count, 1,500 by the INC's. The Hunter Committee lets Dyer off; MG withdraws the movement with two lessons: control the INC, and train people in non-violence.
    → Anger over Punjab was about to meet anger over Turkey.
  4. Khilafat joins the cause (1919-20). The Treaty of Sèvres (1920) strips the Khalifa, and the Ali Brothers set up the Khilafat Committee in March 1919. At Allahabad in June 1920 the movement is launched with boycott and a no-tax call. MG sees a once-in-a-hundred-years chance for Hindu-Muslim unity and brings the INC along, even C.R. Das; Tilak, B.C. Pal, Besant, Jinnah and the ML stay out or oppose.
    → So the INC meets, first in Calcutta, then in Nagpur.
  5. Calcutta and Nagpur (September and December 1920). At Calcutta (Lajpat Rai presiding) the INC launches the NCM with three goals: Swaraj, Punjab wrongs, Khilafat wrongs; Swaraj is promised in one year, with a CDM after. At Nagpur (C. Vijayaraghavachariar presiding) MG takes over: the method changes from the pen to Satyagraha, and a CWC of 15, linguistic PCCs, a 4-anna fee, Hindi and Princely States' members make the INC a body of the masses.
    → Now the movement has to run.
  6. The movement runs (1920-22). The Tilak Swaraj Fund raises Rs 1 crore; the Reading talks fail because MG refuses to be used for Divide and Rule. Peasants (Awadh Kisan Sabha), workers, tribals and new regions join, British imports halve and only 5% vote in 1920. When the Prince of Wales visits in November 1921 the strikes turn violent, and MG confines the CDM to Bardoli.
    → But the next violence he could not contain.
  7. Chauri Chaura breaks it (February 1922). Police fire on satyagrahis, killing 3; the crowd burns 22 policemen alive. MG withdraws the NCM, suspends the Bardoli CDM and passes the Bardoli Resolution: Constructive Work must precede any movement. 170 are sentenced to death; Malaviya defends them and 19 are hanged. The Rowlatt Act is repealed in March 1922, but Khilafat ends in 1924 when Turkey abolishes the Khalifate.
    → With no mass movement to run, the INC asks what to do next.
  8. Mend it or boycott it (1922-24). All agree there is no CDM yet. The Swarajists (Das, Motilal Nehru) want to end or mend the councils; the No-Changers (Patel, Rajendra Prasad, Rajaji) want boycott and Constructive Work. The Gaya resolution fails, the Swarajists form the Congress Khilafat Swaraj Party and contest the 1923 elections. MG opposes but compromises, and at Belgaum 1924 the Gandhi-Das Pact makes them the INC's parliamentary wing.
    → But the party in the councils soon came apart.
  9. The Swarajists in the councils (1923-26). They demand a new constitution, civil liberty and help for Indian industry, and use the adjournment motion. But they have no street support, Das dies in 1925, the ML is lost over Bengal's Muslim tenants, and the 1924 riots split off the Responsivists. The party loses heavily in 1926 and the INC leaves the councils.
    → Meanwhile the quiet work went on.
  10. The quiet machine (1922-27). Constructive Work keeps cadres busy, unites castes and communities, deepens the INC's rural hold, builds the masses' confidence and sets up national schools and panchayat courts. The Passive Phase is 'anything but passive'; the Simon Commission of 1927 rekindles the INM.

In simple words#

  • Gandhi (MG) tested three methods in three local pilots: Champaran 1917 (civil disobedience), Ahmedabad 1918 (fast and arbitration) and Kheda 1918 (non-cooperation by withholding revenue).
  • The Rowlatt Act (Feb 1919) kept wartime powers in peacetime
  • MG's strike turned violent without leaders, and Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919) followed: he learnt to want a controlled mass movement
  • Khilafat anger and Punjab anger joined in the NCM (1920-22): Calcutta launched it, Nagpur gave the INC to MG with a new structure and the goal of Swaraj by peaceful means.
  • Chauri Chaura (Feb 1922) ended the mass movement
  • The Bardoli Resolution put Constructive Work first, and the Passive Phase split the INC into Swarajists and No-Changers
  • The split was healed by MG's trust (Gandhi-Das Pact, Belgaum 1924), while Constructive Work built cadres and rural roots until the Simon Commission (1927).

2 · Champaran Satyagraha, 1917: the first civil disobedience#

Story link

Because MG needed a first test, Rajkumar Shukla brought him to Bihar. Ask: why did the Tinkathia system get worse during WWI?

  • Rajkumar Shukla, a peasant leader from Bihar, met MG at the Lucknow Session of the INC in 1916 and asked him to visit Champaran.
  • The grievance was the Tinkathia System: ryots were forced to grow indigo on 3/20th of their land under contracts with British planters.
  • Tinkathia = three kathas (a unit of land) out of every twenty.
  • After the Indigo Revolt (1859-60) in Bengal, indigo plantation shifted almost entirely to Bihar by 1863.
  • German synthetic dyes became popular after 1895 and ate into the indigo market
  • During WWI those dyes were unavailable, so planters pushed the Tinkathia System harder than ever
Teacher's board
  • Underlined: Rajkumar Shukla, peasant leader, Tinkathia, three kathas, Indigo Revolt 1859-60, shifted to Bihar by 1863, German synthetic dyes, unavailable in WWI.
  • Side note: revise the post-1857 peasant revolts (Chapter 22).
Handout alert
  • Name check. The bank's practice answer says Rajendra Prasad "joined here".
  • PYQs 2005, 2008 and 2010 use him with J.B.
  • Kripalani as Gandhi's Champaran colleagues

2.1 Events and result

  • MG arrived
  • The authorities ordered him to leave the district. He refused.
  • Disobeying a direct official order and courting arrest = Civil Disobedience.
  • Unsure how to handle him, the government made MG head of an Inquiry Committee.
  • He used it to document the exploitation systematically.
  • He convinced the administration to abolish the Tinkathia System: the Champaran Agricultural Act, 1918.
  • Planter oppression did not fully stop.
Teacher's board
  • Dispute resolution = arbitration:
    • (1) inquiry
    • (2) reveal the TRUTH of both sides
    • (3) Madhyamarg (the middle path)
  • "Truth revelation" is the work of the Inquiry Committee.
  • Why oppression continued: the contracts were legal, and planters could oppress through non-indigo matters, e.g. rents. Hallmark: civil disobedience.
Key takeaway
  • Champaran = civil disobedience: MG disobeyed a direct official order, accepted the legal consequence, and the system followed arbitration: Inquiry Committee, then legislation resolving a decades-old grievance.
  • Significance (Prelims 2018): peasant unrest joined India's national movement.

3 · Ahmedabad Mill Strike, 1918: the first hunger strike#

Story link

Because the second test was a labour dispute, MG met two people he knew. Ask: how did the three wage figures reach a midpath?

  • Hallmark: a new technique of arbitration plus MG's first hunger strike.
  • MG was invited by Anasuya Sarabhai, a social worker.
  • Her brother Ambalal Sarabhai headed the Ahmedabad Mill Owners' Association.
  • MG knew both sides: an awkward middle position.
Teacher's board

Chain: plague → medical expenses up and earnings down (cannot work); + WWI inflation → mill owners ended the plague bonus; the common man wants economic security → WC strike with a 50% wage-rise demand → MG fast with 35% → arbitration.

  • During WWI plague hit Ahmedabad
  • Owners paid a Plague Bonus to compensate for working through the epidemic, then discontinued it while WWI inflation (and the Spanish Flu) crushed workers' purchasing power
  • Workers demanded a 50% wage rise
  • owners refused even to negotiate until the strike ended
  • MG demanded 35% and began a fast unto death, as moral pressure on the owners
  • It also made it hard for workers to abandon the strike
  • An Arbitration Board settled on 27.5%.

3.1 Impact

  • The strike laid the foundation of the working-class movement in Ahmedabad and led to the Textile Labour Association (TLA) in 1920 (organised WC movement).
  • 1920 was a landmark for the WC: the AITUC was formed in 1920 by Lala Lajpat Rai (the first all-India working-class organisation), and the CPI was set up in 1920 at Tashkent under Roy (M.N.), reconstituted in India in 1925.
  • Two hallmarks: the first hunger strike by MG and arbitration (an economic midpoint, not winner-takes-all).
  • The midpath returns at Bardoli 1928.

4 · Kheda Satyagraha, 1918: the first non-cooperation#

Story link

Because the third test was against the State itself, MG chose the law's side. Ask: why is withholding revenue NC and not CD?

  • Hallmark: first Non-Cooperation / No-Revenue Movement.
  • Under the Ryotwari system, Kheda (Gujarat) farmers were entitled by law to a remission of Land Revenue (LR) if the crop fell below one-quarter of normal output.
  • The 1918 harvest failed
  • plague added to the misery
  • The government refused the legal remission: it needed money for WWI.
  • MG asked peasants to withhold payment of LR.
  • The government eventually agreed not to confiscate the property of those who could not pay: only those who could pay would be required to.
  • Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel became MG's follower here
  • Kheda was his first experience of organised mass action.
Kheda: why Non-Cooperation, not Civil Disobedience
Teacher's board
  • In NC you do not break the law.
  • At Kheda the law was on the side of the ryots and the government was not following the law.
  • The word "withhold" means a temporary delay: "we are ready to pay LR, but our right (to remission) first." Therefore NC.
  • Otherwise a no-tax campaign is always CD.

5.1 The Rowlatt Act

  • In February 1919 the Imperial Legislative Council (ILC) passed the Rowlatt Act, the Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, based on a Sedition Committee chaired by Justice S.A.T. Rowlatt.
  • Two bills were passed together as one Act.
  • It was passed by the ILC, not the Central Legislative Assembly (CLA)
  • the CLA came only in December 1919 under the GOI Act 1919.

  • That Act was for WWI (1914-19) only: detention without trial, press suppression, trials without juries.
  • The British wanted to keep these powers permanently.
  • It passed despite the unanimous opposition of every Indian member of the ILC.

  • Communal riots of 1924 divided the councils and split the party: the Responsivists.
  • They wanted to cooperate with the government, and sought British support to protect Hindu interests
  • Lajpat Rai and Malaviya were associated with them and accused Motilal Nehru of being too pro-Muslim
  • Kelkar (N.C.) and Jayakar joined the Executive Council in the Central Provinces ("seat-hungry"), against "End or Mend."
  • By the 1926 elections communal unity in the party had gone
  • The Bengal Pact collapsed with Das
  • The Swaraj Party lost heavily in 1926, losing Muslim voters and leaders
  • Response: they reiterated commitment to a mass CDM, and the INC decided to withdraw from the councils in 1926 (all withdrew finally in 1930 after the Lahore Resolution of 1929 for Purna Swaraj).

13 · Constructive Work in the Passive Phase#

Story link

Because the INC could not run a movement, it built a machine. Ask: which of the five gifts mattered most for the next round?

  • (i) Work for party cadre in a politically inactive phase, keeping the INC organisation active among masses
  • Cadres were trained.
  • (ii) Unity within communities: upper-caste urban workers in rural CW learned the conditions of lower castes and villages, bridging the rural-urban divide.
  • (iii) Deepened the INC's hold in rural areas: CW became a recruitment tool in villages.
  • (iv) Built the masses' confidence in the INC: a trust no editorial or council speech could create.
  • (v) Parallel grassroots institutions: Swadeshi education through national schools and local panchayats as independent arbitration courts, bypassing British justice: CW nurtured nationalism.
Key takeaway
  • The Passive Phase was "anything but passive": no mass movement ran, but it built organisation, cadres, rural roots, mass trust and nationalist consciousness.
  • The announcement of the Simon Commission in 1927 rekindled the INM and ended the Passive Phase (Chapter 40).

CAKE (the teacher's mnemonic)?C = Champaran, A = Ahmedabad, K = Kheda, plus E (read as Era): the pilots before the Gandhian Era. "CUT" is written above it.
What did each pilot test?Champaran: passive resistance and civil disobedience. Ahmedabad: mill strike, hunger strike, arbitration. Kheda: non-cooperation through a no-revenue movement.
Who invited MG to Champaran and what was Tinkathia?Rajkumar Shukla (Lucknow 1916). Tinkathia: ryots forced to grow indigo on 3/20th of their land (three kathas in twenty).
Result of Champaran?Inquiry Committee, then the Champaran Agricultural Act 1918; Tinkathia abolished; oppression (rents) did not fully stop.
Why did Champaran oppression continue?The contracts were legal; planters could oppress through non-indigo matters such as rents (teacher).
Ahmedabad grievance and figures?Plague bonus ended amid WWI inflation; workers 50%, MG 35% with a fast unto death, Arbitration Board 27.5%.
Who were Anasuya and Ambalal Sarabhai?Anasuya, a social worker, invited MG; her brother Ambalal headed the Ahmedabad Mill Owners' Association.
What came of Ahmedabad?Textile Labour Association (1920, founder Gandhi); first hunger strike; arbitration (midpath), back at Bardoli 1928.
Kheda grievance?Ryotwari remission of land revenue if the crop fell below one-quarter; the government refused after the 1918 failure.
Why is Kheda NC and not CD?Law on the ryots' side, goal not Swaraj, temporary withholding; an always-on no-tax campaign is CD.
What did Kheda give Patel?His first experience of organised mass action (imp); the title Sardar came in 1928 from the Bardoli ryots and women.
Why did MG back the War Conference?He thought justice lay with Britain (victim of aggression by the Central Powers) and expected self-government in return.
What was the Rowlatt Act?Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, Feb 1919, ILC, Sedition Committee (Justice S.A.T. Rowlatt); carried DoI Act 1915 emergency powers into peacetime.
Why did the British pass it despite all Indian members opposing?WWI misery (Spanish Flu, soldiers' deaths, inflation) and fear of communism (Oct Revolution 1917, CPI 1920 at Tashkent).
Why did MG react so strongly?Breach of promise: Montague Statement 1917 and the MC Report 1918, while the Act kept dictatorial powers.
What is the pattern of a leaderless movement?Violence by masses, like a driverless truck at 200 km/hr; the answer is organisation; MG style = controlled mass movement.
O'Dwyer or Dyer?O'Dwyer (LG) empowered; Dyer (General) applied martial law and ordered the firing.
Jallianwala Bagh facts?13 April 1919, Amritsar, Baisakhi gathering, 10 minutes of firing; 379 (official) against 1,500 (INC); Hunter Committee (Oct 1919, report 1920) let Dyer off.
Lessons of the Rowlatt Satyagraha?Control over the INC and training in non-violent Satyagraha (through Constructive Work).
What does a rural movement need?A charismatic leader and/or an emotive issue and/or very good organisation (CEO).
Who called the Khilafat Committee and when?The Ali Brothers, March 1919; Allahabad Conference June 1920 launched the movement (NC plus CD).
Calcutta 1920: president and goals?Lajpat Rai; Swaraj, Punjab wrongs, Khilafat wrongs; NC for one year, then CDM.
Three streams of the NC programme?Boycott (titles, councils, courts, schools, British goods), Swadeshi (panchayats, national education, khadi), Constructive Work (liquor pickets, anti-untouchability, Hindu-Muslim unity).
What changed at Nagpur 1920?MG took over the INC; method from pen to Satyagraha; CWC 15, linguistic PCCs, 4 annas, Hindi, Princely States' members; CDM phase 2 = no-tax plus resignations.
Who was C. Vijayaraghavachariar?Nagpur president, "Lion of South India" / "Hero of Salem"; not C. Rajagopalachari, not M. Vijayaraghavachariar.
Why did Gandhi-Reading talks fail?Reading wanted MG to criticise the Ali Brothers; MG refused to be used for Divide and Rule.
What happened at Chauri Chaura and after?Feb 1922: 3 satyagrahis killed by police firing, 22 policemen burned; NCM withdrawn, Bardoli CDM suspended; Bardoli Resolution; 170 sentenced, Malaviya defended, 19 hanged.
Main NCM negatives?WEMC and capitalists stayed away, khadi dear, anti-untouchability only MG's, violence (Malabar, Prince, Chauri Chaura), communal riots, revolutionaries disappointed, Khilafat ended 1924.
Main NCM positives?Rowlatt repealed, NWFP, WC, peasants, tribals, lower castes, new regions, imports halved, 5% voted in 1920, Das's sacrifice, women, new leaders.
Swarajists against No-Changers?Swarajists (Das, Motilal): end or mend the councils (Gaya 1922). No-Changers (Patel, Prasad, Rajaji): boycott and Constructive Work.
How was a Surat-style split avoided?DATA: Divide and Rule prevented, All accepted MG, Trust and respect, Already done by 1924; Belgaum 1924 Gandhi-Das Pact.
Why did the Swaraj Party decline?No street support, Das died 1925, lost the ML (Muslim tenants), riots of 1924 (Responsivists); lost heavily in 1926; INC quit the councils 1926.
