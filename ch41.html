The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: Irwin 26-31 turned down the Delhi Manifesto, so INC adopted Purna Swaraj at Lahore and MG offered eleven demands as a last chance. When they were refused, he broke the salt law at Dandi and the whole country followed, from Vedaranniyam to Peshawar. Violence and exhaustion forced a truce in the Delhi Pact, but the 2nd RTC deadlocked on separate electorates, the Communal Award and the Poona Pact followed, and MG turned the 2nd phase into a campaign against untouchability. CDM ended in 1934 with little to show on the street, yet INC cashed in the political capital in the 1937 elections.

  1. Delhi Manifesto turned down. The Manifesto asked for an RTC acting as a Constituent Assembly with dominion status, an INC majority among Indian delegates and amnesty in the Bomb in CLA and Meerut cases. Irwin 26-31 refused all of it.
    → So INC had to answer at its next session.
  2. Lahore chooses Purna Swaraj. At Lahore (Dec 1929, Nehru presiding) INC made complete independence its goal, fixed 26 January 1930 as the pledge day, rejected the RTC, quit the legislatures and empowered the AICC to launch CDM.
    → But MG did not launch it at once.
  3. Why MG waited. Muslim leaders of INC wanted an INC-ML pact like Lucknow, capitalists feared the Great Depression, ML1906 spoke of Hindu raj, other parties envied INC's popularity and CPI1925 called INC bourgeois.
    → So MG offered an olive branch first.
  4. Eleven demands. In January 1930 MG listed four middle-class, two peasant and five general demands, from the 1s 4d rupee to abolition of the salt tax. If the British met them, INC would not need to fight.
    → But Irwin 26-31 rejected all eleven.
  5. Salt as the weapon. Salt was used by everyone, was neither a Hindu nor a bourgeois issue and carried little risk of immediate repression. MG began at Dandi from Sabarmati Ashram in March 1930 and the Salt Satyagraha spread across regions.
    → Now the movement was all-India.
  6. Regions join in. Rajaji marched to Vedaranniyam, Kelappan to Poyannur, Inchudi became the Second Dandi, Ankola the Bardoli of Karnataka, and Andhra built Sibirams. INC's organisation since 1920 made this possible.
    → But the Frontier had its own story.
  7. Peshawar and the Garhwalis. Ghaffar Khan's Red Shirts met machine guns at Qissa Khwani Bazaar on 23 April 1930. The Royal Garhwal Rifles refused to fire on the unarmed Muslim crowd.
    → So the movement kept growing, and so did the state's anger.
  8. Dharasana and arrest. MG warned the British about the Dharasana raid and was arrested in May 1930. Naidu, Manilal and Imam Saheb led 2,500 volunteers against 400 police; Webb Miller's report shocked the world.
    → But violence at Chittagong, Peshawar and Sholapur was rising.
  9. Truce and Karachi. The Delhi Pact of 5 March 1931 suspended CDM and brought INC to the 2nd RTC. Bhagat Singh was hanged on 23 March, and Karachi approved the Pact amid black flags, with rights and an economic programme.
    → But London produced no settlement.
  10. Deadlock and Award. The 2nd RTC failed on separate electorates and MG resumed CDM. INC was banned in January 1932 and the Communal Award of August 1932 extended separate electorates to the Depressed Classes.
    → So MG fasted, and the Poona Pact followed.
  11. Pact and Harijans. The Poona Pact (24 Sept 1932) replaced separate electorates with reserved seats raised from 71 to 147. MG's 2nd phase turned to anti-untouchability, with the Harijan Sevak Sangh, Fund and magazine. CDM ended in 1934; INC won big in 1937.

In simple words#

  • After Irwin 26-31 said no to the Delhi Manifesto, INC chose Purna Swaraj at Lahore and kept CDM in reserve.
  • MG first made 11 demands: if the British met them, no struggle was needed.
  • When they were refused, he began with salt because everyone uses it and nobody could oppose it.
  • Dandi was the spark; regional leaders (Rajaji, Kelappan, Inchudi, Ankola, Andhra) carried the fire.
  • Peshawar showed non-violence among Pashtuns; the Garhwalis showed unity.
  • Violence and tiredness led to the Delhi Pact, Karachi and the 2nd RTC, which failed on separate electorates.
  • The Communal Award led to MG's 6-day fast and the Poona Pact: no separate electorates, but 147 reserved seats.
  • The 2nd phase was about untouchability: it was weak, but INC won in 1937.

0 · The chapter on one board#

Story link

Because Irwin 26-31 turned down the Delhi Manifesto, the Congress had nowhere left to go except Purna Swaraj and a mass struggle. Ask: why did Gandhi still wait three months before marching?

Teacher's board
  • Chain on the board: Irwin 26-31 Statement (Diwali Declaration, 31/10/1929) → Delhi Manifesto (Nov 1929) → rejected by Irwin 26-31 → Lahore Session (Dec 1929).
  • Three forces holding CDM back: ML1906 said CDM would lead to Hindu Raj; capitalists feared an additional negative impact of CDM on profits; the Muslims of INC wanted an LP1916-like pact first.
  • Context line: Great Depression 1929 began in Oct 1929 with the NYSE crash (World History link).
  • CPI 1925 strategy, two stages: (A) Communists powerful, capitalists weak: the line was 'either you are with us or against us', socialists = villain. (B) Capitalists in trouble, communists gaining: search for an alliance with socialists. In stage A the CPI treated INC as the enemy, which is why it opposed CDM.
  • Diwali Declaration (31 Oct 1929): Irwin 26-31's statement offering dominion status gradually and an RTC; the Delhi Manifesto of 1 Nov 1929 was the Indian reply (see Chapter 40).
  • CDM (1930-34) in one line: Lahore goal (Purna Swaraj) + 11 demands + Salt Satyagraha (1st phase) → Delhi Pact → failure of 2nd RTC → 2nd phase (anti-untouchability) → end in 1934.

1 · The INC Lahore Session, December 1929#

Story link

Because Irwin 26-31 refused all three asks of the Delhi Manifesto, INC had to answer at its next session. Ask: what did Lahore change, and what did it leave to the AICC?

  • Viceroy Irwin 26-31 (1926-1931) rejected the Delhi Manifesto in November 1929.
  • The Manifesto asked for three things:
    • The Round Table Conference (RTC) to function as a Constituent Assembly drafting a constitution with dominion status.
    • A majority of Indian representatives in the RTC to come from INC.
    • Amnesty in the Bomb in CLA case (1929) and the Meerut Conspiracy Case (1929).
  • Irwin 26-31 refused all of it.
  • The response came at the INC Lahore Session, December 1929, with Jawaharlal Nehru as President. INC took five major decisions.

1.1 The five decisions

No.DecisionDetail
1.1Purna SwarajGoal of INC is now complete independence, not dominion status.
1.2Independence Day26 January 1930 to be celebrated by taking an independence pledge. Date chosen as the last Sunday of January 1930, a holiday, for maximum participation and publicity.
1.3RTC rejectedINC rejected the RTC (three sessions: 1930, 1931, 1932) and did not attend the 1st RTC of 1930.
1.4LegislaturesWithdraw from legislatures immediately: end of the Pro-Changer strategy of working the CLA, CoS and PLAs from within.
1.5AICC empoweredThe AICC (set up by the Nagpur Session of 1920) empowered to launch CDM at an appropriate time.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board on 26 January: it is called Purna Swaraj Divas. Republic Day 1950 reuses the date. Constitution Day is 26 November because the Constitution was adopted (finalised) on that day in 1949; it came into effect (became law) on 26 January 1950.
  • RTC and INC: 1930 = no INC, 1931 = INC participated, 1932 = no INC. Teacher writes the three years as NO INC / INC / NO INC.
  • Legislature withdrawal: the board reads it as NC by INC, i.e. a non-cooperation step taken already at Lahore.

2 · Why Was CDM Not Launched Immediately?#

Story link

Because Lahore had only authorised the AICC to launch CDM, and the year given at Calcutta 1928 was up, the pressure on MG was heavy. Ask: who wanted him to wait, and why?

  • The 1928 Calcutta Session resolved that if dominion status was not granted within one year, INC would launch CDM for Purna Swaraj. That year was now up, so pressure on MG to act was high.
  • MG, true to his style of inclusiveness, wanted broad consensus before moving. Several forces opposed an immediate CDM:
No.ForcePosition and reason
2.1INC Muslim leadersWanted an INC-ML1906 unity pact before CDM, like LP1916 which preceded NCM 1920-22. Without it they would face opposition from Muslim masses when mobilising them.
2.2Capitalist classUnsure of the economic impact of CDM because the Great Depression (GD1929) began in October 1929 with the US Stock Market Crash. The US economy was linked to the world through import-export and FDI, and by 1914 the US was the top economy, so a US depression meant a global one.
2.3ML1906Called CDM a ploy of INC to establish Hindu raj.
2.4Sikh parties, Hindu Mahasabha, Justice Party of MadrasThe Justice Party (lower castes) had not supported NCM 1920-22. All three opposed CDM because it would raise INC's popularity. Except the Justice Party they had joined the All Parties Conference and demanded Purna Swaraj or Dominion Status, but did not want INC to become the sole mass leader.
2.5CPI 1925Opposed CDM on directions of the Comintern (Third International), the body of all communist parties led by the Communist Party of the USSR. CPI1925 called Bose, Nehru and INC agents of the Bourgeoisie and INC a party of the capitalist class.
  • Result: instead of launching CDM directly, MG gave his 11-point demands in January 1930.
Teacher's board
  • Depression logic: GD means falling demand; factories cut output; layoffs or wage cuts follow; people spend less, so demand falls again (a loop).
  • Why CPI said INC = party of Bourgeoisie: socialism was strong in INC in the 1930s (Bose, Nehru); GD1929 brought negative press for capitalism; the CPI said Bose and Nehru were agents of the bourgeoisie and Purna Swaraj would mean Capitalist Raj.
  • USA and the world (board): links are Diaspora, Remittances (via MN Banks), Internet + MNCs, FII and FDI, Imports and Exports, dollar as forex, world trade routes. If USA sneezes, the world catches cold. By 1914 USA was no. 1 economy.
Teacher's board
  • 'w/o WH no MIH' (teacher's own line): each movement links to a world event. Swadeshi 1905-11: Russo-Japanese war 1904-05 (a European power beaten by an Asian one) and the Russian Revolution 1905, the first. NCM (1920-22): WWI 1914-19 with Spanish flu deaths, inflation, Treaty of Sevres 1920. CDM (1930-34): GD1929. QIM 1942-45: WW II 1939-45.

3 · MG's 11-Point Demands, January 1930#

Story link

Because MG wanted to give the British a chance to deliver Swaraj's substance without a fight, he turned Swaraj into eleven concrete benefits. Ask: what did each group get on the list?

  • The 11 demands defined what Swaraj would mean for the masses: the benefits ordinary people would get under self-government.
  • MG's argument: if the British delivered these on their own, INC was ready to sacrifice its own interest in political power. This is MG's style of arbitration: offer a chance to deliver the substance without a confrontation.
Teacher's board
  • Board logic: because of the lack of consensus on CDM in Dec 1929, the 11 demands went to Irwin 26-31, who rejected them, and CDM began in March 1930. Meeting the 11 demands would have meant a huge rupee loss to the government.
  • If India were independent, an INC government would have done these 11 things for the people. If the British did them, INC would be happy and there would be no CDM.
Number card · 11 demands
4 · 2 · 5
Four for business, two for farmers, five for everyone.
4Middle-class / Bourgeoisie issues (3.1-3.4)2Peasants' issues (3.5-3.6)5General issues (3.7-3.11)

3.1 Four Middle-Class / Bourgeoisie issues

  • 3.1 Exchange rate: lower the Rupee to 1 Rs = 1s 4d (1 shilling and 4 pence). This is depreciation of the Rupee: it boosts Indian exports and discourages import of British goods by making them costlier (in line with Make in India, i.e. domestic production).
  • 3.2 Cotton tariffs: protective tariffs for cotton, i.e. import duty on imported cotton cloth.
  • 3.3 Indian Tariff Board: full status and powers, so Indian businessmen have a say in import/export duties.
  • 3.4 Postal Reservation Bill: reserve 50% of seats in the postal department for Indians: jobs for the Western Educated Middle Class (WEMC).
Handout alert
  • Note on the rupee (Hilton-Young): in 1926 the Hilton-Young Commission fixed the rupee high at 1s 6d. Fewer rupees were needed to pay fixed debts in pounds, so remittances to Britain flowed easily and India looked financially stable to lenders. The high rupee made British goods cheaper in India and hurt Indian capitalists, workers and farmers by making Indian goods expensive abroad. Businesses pressed for 1s 4d since 1926 (see Chapter 47).
Teacher's board
  • Remittances: money moves from India to Britain. Financial stability: it makes India look creditworthy, like a good CIBIL score. High rupee: British imports into India become cheaper.
  • Strong rupee, less rupee needed for a foreign expense (example: if 1 dollar = 1 rupee, paying a dollar debt is easy).

3.2 Two Peasants' issues and five General issues

  • 3.5 Land Revenue: 50% decrease and zero LR during famine.
  • 3.6 Bardoli: release confiscated property of those in the Bardoli Satyagraha (1928).
  • 3.7 Expenditure: decrease military expenditure and salaries of civil servants.
  • 3.8 Arms Act 1878: amend it to give Indians the right to bear arms for self-defence.
  • 3.9 Prohibition: total prohibition on intoxicants.
  • 3.10 Political prisoners: release those not guilty of violence.
  • 3.11 Salt tax: abolish the salt tax (8.2% of Government revenue) and the Government salt monopoly.
Teacher's board
  • Beside 3.10 the board writes Delhi Manifesto, Nov 1929: the prisoner demand carries over from the Manifesto.
  • The salt demand was not new: the British Indians' Association (1851), Madras Native Association (1852) and Bombay Association (1852) sent similar demands to Parliament before the Charter Act of 1853. The Salt Act 1882 applied tax on salt and reaffirmed the Government monopoly.
  • Irwin 26-31 rejected all 11 demands. CDM was now inevitable.

4 · Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM), 1930–34: Reasons#

Story link

Because the 11 demands were rejected and the Depression was crushing farmers and workers, the push for struggle came from both politics and the pocket. Ask: why were ryots and workers both restless?

  • 4.1 Rejection of MG's 11-point demands by Irwin 26-31.
  • 4.2 Economic grievances from the Great Depression (GD1929):
    • i) Cash crops: prices crashed in international markets; cotton and jute, both export crops, were badly hit, devastating the ryots. (Recall: the end of the US Civil War 1861-65 also cut raw cotton prices and contributed to the Bombay Deccan Riots of 1875.)
    • ii) Food crops: prices of wheat and rice fell in domestic markets, but Land Revenue stayed the same. Moneylenders stopped lending and demanded old loans back. Peasants sold land to fund the next sowing season.
    • iii) Working Class (WC): low wages and lay-offs.
    • iv) Net effect: ryots in rural areas and WC in urban areas were restless.
Teacher's board

6 · CDM Plan of MG#

Story link

Because MG's method is rising tempo, the plan climbs from the salt law to revenue and forest laws. Ask: which issue was safe from every critic?

8 · NWFP: The Khudai Khidmatgars and the Qissa Khwani Bazaar Massacre#

Story link

Because CDM was an all-India fire, it reached the Frontier as well, and there it took its most striking form. Ask: how did a Pashtun army become non-violent?

  • NWFP was one of the most remarkable chapters of CDM.
  • Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan was the Frontier Gandhi, Badshah Khan, Fakhr-e-Afghan (Pride of Afghans): an educator and social reformer who opened schools across NWFP and published the Pashto political journal Pakhtun (1928).
  • In 1929 he founded the Khudai Khidmatgars (Servants of God), the Red Shirts or Surkh Posh. Uniforms were dyed with cheap brick-dust because white khadi got dirty too fast in rugged terrain.
  • Ideological fusion: the Pashtun code of honour (Pukhtunwali) joined to Gandhian non-violence; non-violence was the ultimate test of bravery for a true Muslim and true Pashtun.
  • The oath: forgive oppressors, live a pure life, serve humanity without reward.
  • Why Britain feared it: a disciplined non-violent army of tens of thousands of Pashtuns, a people historically feared for martial spirit, was a strategic nightmare.

8.1 The Qissa Khwani Bazaar massacre, 23 April 1930

  • On 23 April 1930 Britain arrested Ghaffar Khan in Peshawar. Massive unarmed crowds gathered at the Qissa Khwani Bazaar (Storytellers' Market).
  • The British drove armoured cars into the crowd, ran over several people, and fired machine guns. Hundreds of peaceful protesters were killed, yet the crowd kept coming forward. Eventually the Pashtuns erupted in violence.
  • Royal Garhwal Rifles: these predominantly Hindu soldiers were ordered to fire on the unarmed Muslim crowd. Led by Chandra Singh Garhwali they flatly refused, saying they would not shoot unarmed citizens, and were court-martialled with severe sentences for mutiny.
  • Their defiance became the ultimate symbol of Hindu-Muslim unity during CDM, and proof that non-violent resistance had reached even the British Indian Army.

9 · Implementation: 2nd Stage (May 1930 – March 1931)#

Story link

Because MG openly announced the raid on Dharasana, the state had to arrest him, and that arrest opened the 2nd stage. Ask: how did one journalist turn a beating into a world story?

  • Dharasana Satyagraha, May 1930 (Gujarat): MG gave the British prior warning of a planned peaceful raid on the Dharasana salt works (south of Dandi). A satyagrahi is fearless: crimes are hidden, but politics is public.
  • This led to MG's arrest in May 1930 and the 2nd Stage of CDM began.
  • Leadership passed to Abbas Tyabji, a 76-year-old retired judge, who marched with MG's wife Kasturbai; both were arrested before reaching Dharasana.
  • 21 May 1930: Sarojini Naidu, MG's son Manilal and Imam Saheb (a follower since the South African struggle) took charge. About 2,500 volunteers marched at the works where about 400 police waited with steel-tipped lathis. The satyagrahis were beaten and none raised an arm; over 300 were injured.
  • Webb Miller, American journalist of United Press, witnessed it all. His report, printed in over a thousand newspapers, turned international opinion against British rule. The British tried to censor it; MG later said Miller 'helped make' Indian independence.
  • Assam: protests against the Cunningham Circular (1930-31), which forced parents to give an assurance of good behaviour of their children, a way to keep youth out of CDM.

16 · Communal Award / MacDonald Award, August 1932#

Story link

Because Indians could not agree on joint electorates, the British decided for them. Ask: why did INC neither accept nor reject, and why did MG still fast?

  • Announced by British PM Ramsay MacDonald (17 August 1932) because Indians could not reach consensus on joint electorates.

  • MG's fear: separate electorates would permanently divide Hindus and make untouchability permanent. He was not against greater reservation; he was against the method of separate electorates. His issue was how representation would be elected, not how much.

Because the 2nd phase opened with INC banned and MG in jail, his energy turned from the street to society. Ask: what organisation did he build for the new campaign?

  • 18.1 January 1932: INC banned; MG imprisoned in Yeravda (after the decision to resume CDM).
  • 18.2 Fast and release: MG's 1932 fast ended with the Poona Pact in September 1932. On 8 May 1933 he began a 21-day fast for self-purification and to energise the anti-untouchability (Harijan) campaign; he was released at once on health grounds.
  • Primary focus of the 2nd phase: the anti-untouchability campaign.
  • 18.3 Penance: MG asked Hindus to do penance and appealed for complete eradication of untouchability.
  • 18.4 Internal reform: he worked on reform among Harijans in education, health and hygiene and asked Harijans to remove untouchability within their own communities too.
  • 18.5 Organisation:
    • i) Harijan Sevak Sangh (1932) / All India Anti-Untouchability League (1932).
    • ii) Harijan Sevak Fund (1932).
    • iii) Awareness: the Harijan Magazine (1933), a 20,000 km march across India, and two fasts.
Key takeaway
  • MG the organisation man: recall the Tilak Swaraj Fund of 1920 for NCM. Every MG campaign had an organisation, a dedicated fund and a media arm.

19 · Results of CDM's 2nd Phase#

Story link

Because the 1st phase had ended in a truce and the Harijan work spoke to society, not to politics, the response was thin. Ask: yet who gained in the end?

  • 19.1 Low response, with demoralisation after the suspension of the 1st phase.
  • 19.2 Caste response: neither Upper Castes nor Lower Castes were enthusiastic about the Harijan campaign. LC were becoming more radical and wanted political and economic rights; MG mainly addressed social dignity.
  • 19.3 Repression did the rest. CDM was formally ended in 1934.
  • 19.4 Yet INC benefitted: it did very well in the 1937 provincial elections under the GOI Act 1935. Political capital from years of mass mobilisation was cashed in at the ballot box.

19.1 Nellie Sengupta, third woman INC President (1933)

  • Born Edith Ellen Gray in Cambridge, England; married to J.M. Sengupta. At the 1933 Calcutta Session, after the arrest of the designated President Madan Mohan Malaviya, she defied a government ban to chair it.
  • She is the third woman President of INC (after Annie Besant, 1917 and Sarojini Naidu, 1925) and the second foreign-born woman to preside.

Who presided over the Lahore Session of December 1929?Jawaharlal Nehru.
Name the five decisions of Lahore (POLAR).Purna Swaraj goal, Oath (pledge on 26 Jan 1930), Legislatures withdrawal, AICC empowered to launch CDM, RTC rejected.
Why was 26 January 1930 chosen?Last Sunday of January 1930, a holiday, for maximum participation and publicity.
Why did MG not launch CDM at once?He wanted consensus; INC Muslims wanted a pact, capitalists feared the Depression, ML1906, Sikh parties, Mahasabha, Justice Party and CPI1925 opposed.
How many demands in MG's 11 points, and in what groups?4 middle-class, 2 peasant, 5 general.
What rupee rate did MG demand and what did Hilton-Young fix?1s 4d demanded; 1s 6d fixed in 1926.
What share of Government revenue did the salt tax form?8.2%.
Why was salt chosen? (SALT)Staple for all, Apolitical, Low repression risk, Tyranny over basic necessities.
What was the 2nd stage of MG's plan?No-revenue in Ryotwari and Mahalwari areas, Chowkidari tax non-payment in Zamindari areas, forest laws in Central Provinces.
Why not a no-rent campaign?It would pit Indian ryots against Indian landlords; MG wanted Indians versus the British.
From where did the Dandi March start?Sabarmati Ashram (set up 1917), to the Dandi coast.
Who led the Tanjore coast march, with how many volunteers and on which date?C. Rajagopalachari, 98 volunteers, 13 April 1930 (Tiruchirappalli to Vedaranniyam).
Who was the first woman in India to pay a penalty under the salt laws?Rukmini Lakshmipathi.
Which place was called the 'Second Dandi' and the 'Bardoli of Karnataka'?Inchudi (Odisha) and Ankola (Karnataka).
What were Sibirams?Military-style training camps in Krishna, Godavari and Guntur districts of Andhra.
Who founded the Khudai Khidmatgars and when?Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan, 1929; the Red Shirts (Surkh Posh).
What did Chandra Singh Garhwali's unit do at Peshawar?The Royal Garhwal Rifles refused to fire on the unarmed Muslim crowd.

Who was Webb Miller?United Press journalist whose eyewitness report turned world opinion against British rule.
List CROWD, the positives of the 1st phase.Capitalists, Rural and urban, Overcame violence, Women, Disobedience at all-India level.
