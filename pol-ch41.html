1 · NITI Aayog: introduction and background#

NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) was set up on 1 January 2015 by an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet, replacing the Planning Commission (1950-2014). It is the premier policy think-tank of the Government of India.

  • Aims to promote cooperative and competitive federalism, with states taking part in national development through evidence-based planning and performance-driven governance.
  • Governed by a Governing Council chaired by the Prime Minister.
  • Gives strategic policy inputs; it does not exercise executive or financial control.
  • Marks a shift from centralised planning to decentralised, evidence-based policy formulation.

Background and rationale

  • The Planning Commission (1950) led economic planning through Five-Year Plans.
  • It was criticised for top-down control, limited state participation and lack of adaptability in a globalised, market-oriented economy.
  • The 2014 Independence Day address of PM Narendra Modi outlined the need for an institution suited to cooperative federalism, dynamic policy-making and real-time monitoring.
  • On 1 January 2015 the Planning Commission was replaced: from rigid resource allocation to flexible, evidence-based governance and Centre-State coordination.

4 · Objectives and functions#

The vision: an inclusive, sustainable and globally competitive economy.

Seven objectives and functions

  • Policy vision and strategic planning: long-term blueprints such as Strategy@75 and Vision@2047, aligned with the SDGs and global trends.
  • Cooperative and competitive federalism: structured Centre-State dialogue through the Governing Council and Sub-Groups of CMs.
  • Evidence-based and decentralised planning: replaces rigid Five-Year Plans with flexible, data-centric approaches.
  • Monitoring and evaluation: frameworks such as the Aspirational Districts Programme, Global Innovation Index and Health Index, using real-time data and outcome-based metrics.
  • Knowledge and innovation hub: repository of best practices; technology-driven service delivery.
  • Public-private and global partnerships: mobilise expertise, finance and technology.
  • Capacity building and technical support: to states, ministries and departments for design, implementation and impact assessment.

6 · Cooperative federalism and NITI Aayog#

Cooperative federalism means Union-State collaboration in decision-making, planning and implementation. NITI is its institutional anchor: consensus-driven governance by structured dialogue, joint planning and performance-based coordination rather than top-down control.

  • Governing Council as federal forum: the PM, Chief Ministers and Lt Governors deliberate on priorities like Gati Shakti, Viksit Bharat 2047 and social sector reforms (the "Team India" platform).
  • Sub-Groups of Chief Ministers: on Skill Development, Swachh Bharat, Crop Diversification; states co-design central policies.
  • Shared developmental goals: SDG localisation; states align State Development Indicators (SDIs) with the national SDG India Index.
  • Joint monitoring: the Aspirational Districts Programme (2018) monitors 112 backward districts with data-based feedback loops.
  • Policy coordination on reforms: a bridge on GST-related issues, agricultural market reforms and social sector expenditure tracking.
  • Fiscal and administrative convergence: aligns Finance Commission recommendations with state fiscal performance via sectoral reviews and peer learning workshops.
  • Decentralised capacity building: through the Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office (DMEO), NITI holds workshops and state reviews.

7 · Competitive federalism and NITI Aayog#

Competitive federalism fosters healthy rivalry among States and UTs through rankings, performance indices and outcome-based evaluations, replacing entitlement-based planning with efficiency-driven governance metrics.

  • Performance ranking frameworks: Health Index, School Education Quality Index, Composite Water Management Index, assessed annually.
  • SDG India Index: introduced 2018; ranks states on 17 SDGs; Kerala and Tamil Nadu consistently lead.
  • Ease of Doing Business (EoDB) rankings: prepared jointly with DPIIT; incentivise faster approvals, digital clearances and private investment.
  • India Innovation Index: Karnataka and Maharashtra are frequent top performers.
  • Aspirational Blocks Programme (2023): extends the Aspirational Districts model to 500 underperforming blocks.
  • Data transparency and peer learning: public dashboards and reports create reputational pressure and inter-state learning.
  • Recognition and incentives: high performers are acknowledged at NITI forums and considered for greater autonomy in centrally sponsored schemes.

Cooperative federalism

  • Governing Council as federal forum
  • Sub-Groups of CMs
  • SDG localisation
  • joint monitoring (ADP)
  • GST and agriculture reform coordination
  • DMEO workshops

Competitive federalism

  • Rankings and indices (Health, SEQI, water, SDG, EoDB, innovation)
  • Aspirational Blocks
  • public dashboards
  • recognition and greater autonomy for top performers
Federalism shortcut

Council, groups, goals = cooperative; indices, rankings, rewards = competitive.

9 · National Development Council (NDC)#

Set up in August 1952 through an executive resolution of the Government of India as the apex body for policy coordination and economic planning between the Centre and States. It worked alongside the Planning Commission and was the final authority for approving Five-Year Plans and reviewing their implementation.

Composition

NDC members
MemberDetail
ChairpersonPrime Minister of India
MembersAll Union Cabinet Ministers
MembersChief Ministers of all States and Union Territories with legislatures
MembersAdministrators or Lt Governors of Union Territories without legislatures
MembersMembers of the Planning Commission (now replaced by NITI Aayog)
SecretaryThe Secretary of the Planning Commission served as Secretary to the NDC

Objectives

  • Strengthen Centre-State cooperation in framing and implementing national plans.
  • Ensure plan approval: review, approve and guide Five-Year and Annual Plans.
  • Promote balanced development across regions and sectors.
  • Review plan implementation and suggest mid-course corrections.
  • Encourage people's participation: national consensus and political support.

Functions

  • Approval of national plans: draft Five-Year and Annual Plans from the Planning Commission.
  • Prescribe guidelines: broad directives for economic and social planning.
  • Coordinate efforts among Union ministries, state governments and the Planning Commission.
  • Monitor implementation and recommend remedial measures.
  • Promote decentralisation: region-specific strategies.
  • Advise on resource allocation between the Centre and the States.
  • Foster cooperative federalism as a forum for collective decision-making.

10 · NDC: abolition, legacy and comparison with NITI#

In January 2016 the Government announced a plan to subsume the NDC into the Governing Council of NITI Aayog, but no official resolution has been passed.
