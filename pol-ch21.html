In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Water, trade and law cross state borders, so the Constitution gives tools to keep states in harmony: water tribunals, the Inter-State Council, full faith and credit, free trade and Zonal Councils.
  • Art. 262: Parliament can set up bodies for river disputes and bar all courts. The two 1956 Acts are the River Boards Act (advisory boards) and the Inter-State Water Disputes Act (ad hoc tribunal, final award). Nine tribunals were set up from 1969 to 2018.
  • Art. 263:
    • The President can create an Inter-State Council. The permanent one began in 1990 after the Sarkaria call
    • The Prime Minister chairs it and it only advises
  • Art. 261: public acts, records and judicial proceedings get full faith and credit. Civil judgments run anywhere in India, but criminal ones do not.
  • Arts. 301-307:
    • Trade is free across India (Art. 301) with limits in 302-305
    • The authority under Art. 307 has never been appointed
  • Zonal Councils came from the States Reorganisation Act, 1956: five zones, the Union Home Minister as common chairman. The North-Eastern Council has its own 1971 Act.

Key terms

Full Faith and Credit
Art. 261: every state must honour public acts, records and judicial proceedings of the Centre and of other states
Public acts and records
Acts are laws and executive actions; records are official books or registers kept by public servants
Ad hoc tribunal
Temporary body set up under the 1956 Act to decide one river dispute; its award is final and binding
River Board
Advisory body set up on the request of the states for an inter-state river (River Boards Act, 1956)
Inter-State Council
Art. 263 forum chaired by the Prime Minister; advisory, meets in camera and works by consensus
Intercourse (Art. 301)
Free movement of goods, services and people; covers inter-state and intra-state trade
Zonal Council
Statutory advisory body for each of five zones; the Union Home Minister is common chairman
North-Eastern Council
Separate body under the 1971 Act for Assam, Manipur, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Tripura and Sikkim

1 · Why inter-state relations matter#

  • The federal system depends on smooth relations among the states as well as between Centre and states. Conflicts arise over resources, jurisdiction and legislative matters, so the Constitution gives tools for cooperation, coordination and dispute resolution. The aim is national integration, economic development and social harmony.

2 · Inter-State water disputes (Art. 262)#

  • Water is vital and finite, so rivers shared by states cause tension. Article 262 lets Parliament make laws to adjudicate disputes about the use, control and distribution of inter-state rivers or river valleys. It also lets Parliament exclude the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court or any other court over these disputes, which gives a dedicated and binding mechanism.

The two Acts passed under Art. 262

River Boards Act versus Inter-State Water Disputes Act

  • River Boards Act, 1956:
    • Central government may set up river boards
    • For regulation, development and management of inter-state rivers and valleys
    • Primarily advisory
    • Constituted on the request of the concerned states
    • Promotes cooperative planning and equitable use
  • Inter-State Water Disputes Act, 1956:
    • Central government sets up an ad hoc tribunal to adjudicate disputes over waters of a river or valley
    • Decision is final and binding
    • Once referred, no court can interfere
    • Speedy and focused

How a water dispute is settled

  • Dispute: States quarrel over the waters of a river or river valley
  • Centre acts: Sets up an ad hoc tribunal under the Inter-State Water Disputes Act, 1956
  • Tribunal decides: Award is final and binding on the states involved
  • Courts barred: Art. 262: Supreme Court and other courts cannot interfere once the dispute is referred
How a water dispute is settled
  • Why tribunals, not courts: The Supreme Court could decide such cases as a matter of law. But they involve complex inter-state and public-interest questions beyond simple legal rights. Tribunals allow a technical, equitable and cooperative settlement.

Why Art. 262 matters

  • A safeguard for cooperative federalism
  • Adjudication is impartial and insulated from political influence and electoral considerations
  • Tribunals are neutral platforms where states present technical and legal claims, building trust.
  • It preserves the balance between Union and State interests and keeps national unity amid regional disagreements.
  • It promotes equitable and sustainable distribution and stops unilateral state action.
  • It reduces the burden on constitutional courts by sending complex technical cases to expert bodies.

3 · Inter-State Council (Art. 263)#

  • Article 263 lets the President create a consultative body when public interest would benefit from formal cooperation between the Union and States or among States. The President decides whether to create it, its remit, and its organisation and procedure. It is flexible and does not touch judicial functions or the distribution of legislative powers.

Three kinds of work under Art. 263

ClauseTask
(a)Examine and advise on disputes between States
(b)Study and discuss subjects of shared interest (States, or Centre and States)
(c)Make recommendations, especially to improve coordination of policy and action
  • Because it is advisory, it complements the courts. Where a legal dispute needs a binding decision, the Supreme Court's jurisdiction under Article 131 remains. The Council is best for political, technical and policy questions that need negotiation and consensus.

    • Permanent Secretariat established
    • Headed by a Secretary-level official

How it works

  • Mandate: investigate and discuss matters of shared concern, recommend better coordination, and take up matters referred by the Chair.
  • Meets periodically
  • Its rules envisage at least three meetings a year
  • Business is in camera
  • Decisions by consensus, not majority
  • Standing Committee (Home Minister in the chair, with some Union Ministers and Chief Ministers) processes items for the full Council and follows up decisions.
  • Secretariat does agenda preparation, research, coordination with States and Central Ministries, and monitoring of implementation.
  • Memory aid (Council set-up dates): Sarkaria 1983-88, Council 1990, Secretariat 1991, Standing Committee 1996, Zonal link 2011: "Some Councils Sit Silently, Zero fuss."

Sectoral and regional councils under Art. 263

  • Examples: Central Council of Health and Family Welfare, Central Council of Local Government, and regional councils for sales-tax coordination across Northern, Eastern, Western and Southern zones. They pool technical knowledge, harmonise standards and fix operational problems before they become constitutional disputes.

Strengths and constraints

Value versus limit

  • Strengths:
    • Predictable space for negotiation
    • Policy convergence on cross-cutting issues (disaster management, inter-state infrastructure, centrally sponsored schemes)
    • Less litigation and less central coercion
  • Constraints:
    • Advisory only
    • Recommendations carry moral and political force but are not legally enforceable
    • Depends on political will
    • Sarkaria and Punchhi urged stronger capacity, secretariat support and systematic follow-up
  • Exam trap: Sarkaria wanted the Council to handle mainly clauses (b) and (c) of Art. 263 (investigation, discussion, recommendation on common interest). It is advisory: do not say it can bind states.

4 · Full faith and credit (Art. 261)#

  • Each state's authority is territorial, so acts, records and judgments of one state must be recognised in another. Article 261 states the Full Faith and Credit principle. It applies to the Union and the states and covers legislative, executive and judicial outputs.
  • Public acts: all laws and executive actions issued by a government.
  • Public records: official books, registers or records kept by public servants in their official capacity.
  • Judicial proceedings: judgments and decisions of courts.

How Art. 261 works

  • Clause 1: Public acts, records and judicial proceedings of the Union and every state must be respected across India
  • Clause 2: Parliament can lay down the mode of proof and the effect of such acts, records and proceedings
  • Result: Land records, birth or marriage certificates are generally admissible across India, subject to parliamentary law on proof

Civil versus criminal

  • Civil judgments:
    • Final civil court judgments of any state can be executed anywhere in India without a fresh suit
    • Helps commerce, property deals and civil disputes
  • Criminal matters:
    • Not covered: criminal judgments or penal laws of one state do not automatically bind the courts of another
    • States keep jurisdictional autonomy over criminal law enforcement
  • Memory aid (Art. 261 pieces): Acts, Records, Judicial proceedings, Parliament proves: "A Right Judgment Persists."
  • Shortcut: Shortcut. Civil = travels, criminal = stays home. Clause 2 = Parliament decides proof and effect. The clause balances state autonomy with national coherence and reflects cooperative federalism.

Also in the handout

  • An important aspect of this principle is its application to judicial decisions.
  • This greatly facilitates inter-state commerce, property transactions, and civil dispute resolution, as parties can rely on the enforceability of judgments across state boundaries.
  • It reflects the broader spirit of cooperative federalism by enabling mutual recognition and enforceability of laws and decisions while allowing Parliament to regulate procedural and evidentiary standards for smooth inter-state operation.

5 · Inter-State trade and commerce (Arts. 301-307)#

  • A seamless domestic market is a federal necessity. Articles 301 to 307 regulate trade, commerce and intercourse within India, protecting free movement of goods, services and people. Article 301 guarantees that trade, commerce and intercourse throughout India shall be free. It covers inter-state and intra-state trade, so restrictions at borders or inside a state both count. The freedom is not absolute. Exceptions in the public interest:

Exceptions and who may restrict (handout's text)

ArticleHandout's statement
302Parliament may impose restrictions on trade between states or within a state in public interest; no discrimination or preference between states, except in scarcity of essential goods
303 and 304A state legislature may impose reasonable restrictions within the state or with other states in public interest; prior presidential approval is mandatory; no preference to its own producers and no discrimination between states
304 (taxation)States may tax goods imported from other states or UTs, but the tax must be uniform and comparable to tax on goods made in the state; discriminatory tax favouring local goods is prohibited
305Union or states may make laws creating government monopolies in sectors (trades, industries, services, exclusively or partly) without violating Art. 301
Handout alert

Handout alert: The prose lists 303 and 304 together as state restrictions, but the closing Key Articles table gives: 303 = limits on legislative powers (Union and state laws must not discriminate between states), 304 = state restrictions with President's prior approval, 305 = saving of existing laws and state monopolies. For the exam use the table's article numbers, with the prose giving the content.

Art. 307 and the missing authority

  • Parliament may appoint a regulatory authority to implement the provisions, monitor restrictions and ensure non-discrimination. No such authority has been appointed so far.

Also in the handout

  • This provision is not limited to inter-state transactions but also applies to trade and commerce within a state, ensuring that restrictions imposed at any stage—whether at the borders or internally—do not hinder the free flow of economic activity. The underlying objective of Article 301 is to dismantle internal barriers and foster a unified national market, thereby enhancing economic efficiency and equitable development.
  • Discriminatory taxation that favors local goods over imports from other states is constitutionally prohibited, ensuring fairness and preventing economic fragmentation.
  • This ensures the state’s ability to regulate or control critical sectors while balancing free trade principles.
  • This framework becomes particularly crucial in the context of India’s growing economic interdependence among states, the expansion of markets, and the rise of interstate commerce in the post-liberalisation era.

6 · Zonal Councils and the North-Eastern Council#

  • Zonal Councils are statutory, not constitutional, created by the States Reorganisation Act, 1956. The Act divided India into five zones: Northern, Central, Eastern, Western and Southern, using geography, river systems, communications, linguistic and cultural ties, economic development, law and order and internal security.

Objectives and functions

  • They are deliberative and advisory, not decision-making. Aim: cooperation, coordination and emotional integration among states, UTs and the Centre. Topics include economic and social planning, development projects, border disputes, linguistic minorities and inter-state transport.

North-Eastern Council (NEC)

  • Set up under the North-Eastern Council Act, 1971 for the special needs of the north-east. Members: Assam, Manipur, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Tripura and Sikkim (eight). Extra tasks: formulate a coordinated regional plan
  • periodically review security and public order measures of member states
  • promote socio-economic development in a harmonised way
  • Memory aid (NEC's eight): Assam, Manipur, Mizoram, Arunachal, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Tripura, Sikkim: "All My Mates Are Nice Mountain Travellers, Smile."

Recall sheet#

Cover the right column and answer. Same questions as the flashcards.

What does Art. 261 provide?
Full faith and credit: public acts, records and judicial proceedings of the Union and each state are recognised throughout India; Parliament may prescribe the mode of proof and effect.
Do civil and criminal judgments both travel across states under Art. 261?
Only civil: final civil judgments execute anywhere in India without a fresh suit; criminal judgments do not automatically bind other states.
What does Art. 262 allow Parliament to do?
Provide for adjudication of inter-state river disputes and exclude the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and other courts.
Name the two 1956 Acts under Art. 262.
River Boards Act (advisory boards) and Inter-State Water Disputes Act (ad hoc tribunal, final and binding).
Which tribunals were set up in 1969?
Krishna-I, Godavari and Narmada.
Which tribunals date from 2010?
Vansadhara (Odisha, Andhra Pradesh) and Mahadayi (Goa, Karnataka, Maharashtra).
Which is the latest tribunal in the handout's table?
Mahanadi Water Disputes Tribunal, 2018 (Odisha and Chhattisgarh).
Why are tribunals preferred to courts for water disputes?
Complex inter-state and public-interest questions beyond simple legal rights; technical, equitable and cooperative settlement.
Name challenges in inter-state water management.
Scarcity; slow adjudication (Cauvery); non-compliance (Arts. 256 and 253); complex river management; politicisation.
Who can create an Inter-State Council and under which Article?
The President, under Art. 263.
List the three works of an Art. 263 council.
(a) Advise on disputes between states; (b) study subjects of shared interest; (c) make recommendations for coordination.
Which Article keeps the Supreme Court available for binding inter-state legal disputes?
Article 131.
Who chairs the Inter-State Council and when was it set up?
The Prime Minister; 1990, after the Sarkaria Commission.
Who chairs the Standing Committee and since when?
The Union Home Minister; constituted 1996.
Since when has the Secretariat also served Zonal Councils?
2011 (the Secretariat itself dates from 1991).
How often should the Inter-State Council meet?
At least three times a year per its rules; in camera; decisions by consensus.
Name sectoral councils under Art. 263.
Central Council of Health and Family Welfare; Central Council of Local Government; regional sales-tax councils.
Does Art. 301 cover only inter-state trade?
No: inter-state and intra-state.
What does Art. 302 allow?
Parliament may restrict trade in public interest, without discriminating between states (except scarcity of essential goods).
What is Art. 306?
Repealed; it had empowered certain Part B states to restrict trade.
What is Art. 307?
Authority for trade and commerce; none appointed so far.
Are Zonal Councils constitutional?
No, statutory: States Reorganisation Act, 1956.
Who chairs the Zonal Councils and who are vice-chairmen?
Union Home Minister; Chief Ministers of member states by rotation, one year each.
Name the five zones.
Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, Southern.

North-Eastern Council Act, 1971; eight states (Assam, Manipur, Mizoram, Arunachal, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Tripura, Sikkim).

Northern New Delhi; Central Allahabad; Eastern Kolkata; Western Mumbai; Southern Chennai.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Tribunal binds, Council only advises Once a dispute goes to an Art. 262 tribunal, no court can interfere and the award is final. The Art. 263 Council handles political, technical and policy questions, its recommendations are not enforceable, and legal disputes between governments go to the Supreme Court (Art. 131). Rule: tribunal = binding; Inter-State Council = advice only.
Trap 2 · Civil only Full Faith and Credit lets a final civil judgment be executed anywhere in India without a fresh suit. Criminal judgments and penal laws of one state do not bind another. Rule: civil judgments travel; criminal ones do not.
Trap 3 · Free, but not absolute Art. 301 covers inter-state and intra-state trade, but Arts. 302-305 allow limits: Parliament may restrict without preferring a state (except in scarcity); a state bill needs the President's prior sanction; Art. 305 saves monopoly laws. Art. 307's authority has never been appointed. Rule: 301 free, 302-305 exceptions, 307 authority missing.
Trap 4 · Statutory, not constitutional Zonal Councils are statutory bodies from the 1956 Act, with five zones. The North-Eastern Council is separate (1971 Act) and is not one of the five. Chief Ministers are Vice-Chairmen by rotation for one year each. Rule: five Zonal Councils + one NEC; Home Minister is chairman of all five.

Book edition: simple words and memory words#

In simple words

  • Water, trade and law cross state borders, so the Constitution gives tools to keep states in harmony: water tribunals, the Inter-State Council, full faith and credit, free trade and Zonal Councils.
  • Art. 262: Parliament can set up bodies for river disputes and bar all courts. The two 1956 Acts are the River Boards Act (advisory boards) and the Inter-State Water Disputes Act (ad hoc tribunal, final award). Nine tribunals were set up from 1969 to 2018.
  • Art. 263: the President can create an Inter-State Council. The permanent one began in 1990 after the Sarkaria call; the Prime Minister chairs it and it only advises.
  • Art. 261: public acts, records and judicial proceedings get full faith and credit. Civil judgments run anywhere in India, but criminal ones do not.
  • Arts. 301-307: trade is free across India (Art. 301) with limits in 302-305; the authority under Art. 307 has never been appointed.
  • Zonal Councils came from the States Reorganisation Act, 1956: five zones, the Union Home Minister as common chairman. The North-Eastern Council has its own 1971 Act.

Key terms

Full Faith and Credit
Art. 261: every state must honour public acts, records and judicial proceedings of the Centre and of other states
Public acts and records
Acts are laws and executive actions; records are official books or registers kept by public servants
Ad hoc tribunal
Temporary body set up under the 1956 Act to decide one river dispute; its award is final and binding
River Board
Advisory body set up on the request of the states for an inter-state river (River Boards Act, 1956)
Inter-State Council
Art. 263 forum chaired by the Prime Minister; advisory, meets in camera and works by consensus
Intercourse (Art. 301)
Free movement of goods, services and people; covers inter-state and intra-state trade
Zonal Council
Statutory advisory body for each of five zones; the Union Home Minister is common chairman
North-Eastern Council
Separate body under the 1971 Act for Assam, Manipur, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Tripura and Sikkim
