Exam lens#

Prelims hooks

  • Six PYQs sit in this chapter: 1995 (Independence for India League), 1998 and 2013 (why Simon was boycotted), 2010 (Simon's key recommendation), 2011 (Nehru Report) and 2017 (Butler Committee).
  • Simon Commission: 7 British MPs, chair Sir John Simon, member Clement Attlee, no Indian. Boycott reason = no Indian member.
  • Nehru Report (Aug 1928): Dominion Status, 19 FRs, Universal Adult Franchise first time, no separate electorates, unitary British India, Union of India with Princely States.
  • Muslim League ladder: 4 Delhi Proposals (Dec 1927), 3 Calcutta Amendments (Dec 1928), 14 Points (Mar 1929).
  • Butler Committee (1927; report 1929): Paramountcy stays; States not to join a Union without consent. Diwali Declaration (31 Oct 1929) and Delhi Manifesto (Nov 1929) with its 3 conditions.

Mains hooks

  • Why the Nehru Report could not deliver Hindu-Muslim consensus: communal clauses, the Calcutta Amendments, the 14 Points.
  • Simon Commission and Birkenhead's challenge: how British insult produced the first Indian constitutional blueprint.
  • Nehru Report and the idea of the Basic Structure in the national movement (teacher's value-addition).
  • Princely States and Paramountcy: Butler Committee, AISPC, and how the 1947 integration challenge began in 1927.

Simon insulted, Nehru drafted, Jinnah demanded, Irwin offered nothing, and the Delhi Manifesto set the terms.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: In 1927 Britain sent an all-British Simon Commission to review the 1919 reforms, two years early. Indians were insulted, and Birkenhead dared them to write their own constitution. INC set up the All Party Conference and produced the Nehru Report (Aug 1928), but the Muslim League's Delhi Proposals, Calcutta Amendments and 14 Points showed that consensus had broken. Britain answered with the Butler Committee for the Princely States and Irwin's vague Diwali Declaration. INC replied with the Delhi Manifesto, which set the conditions for the Round Table Conference.

  1. The review arrives early. The Montague Statement of 1917 promised gradual Swaraj, and GOI Act 1919 was the first step with a review after 10 years. In 1927 the Conservative Government in Britain appointed the Simon Commission two years early. It did not want to leave a sensitive question to the next government after the coming elections.
    → But the Commission had seven members and not one of them was Indian.
  2. Not a single Indian. All seven were British MPs under Sir John Simon, and Clement Attlee was one of them. Swaraj includes a say for Indians in their own constitution. GOI Acts and Councils Acts were British legal instruments, so excluding Indians even from the review was an insult.
    → So INC rejected it, and Birkenhead dared Indians to do better.
  3. Birkenhead's dare. The Secretary of State for India, Birkenhead, challenged Indians in the British Parliament to draft a constitution themselves. He believed their internal differences would stop them from acting together. This taunt gave INC its task.
    → So the Madras session of 1927 rejected Simon and set up an All Party Conference.
  4. Madras 1927 and the streets. INC rejected the Commission and decided on an APC to draft the Principles of a Constitution. When Simon came again in October 1928, protests swept the country. In Lahore Lala Lajpat Rai was lathi-charged by SP James Scott and died in November 1928.
    → Meanwhile the Muslim League made its own offer before the APC could finish.
  5. Four Delhi Proposals. In December 1927 ML1906 sent Four Delhi Proposals to Motilal Nehru: three new Muslim Majority Provinces, one-third at the Centre, reservation in Punjab and Bengal, and joint electorates if all that was met. INC first accepted them, but the APC later rejected them.
    → So the Nehru Report had to settle the communal question itself.
  6. The Nehru Report. The APC under M.A. Ansari formed a Motilal Nehru committee in May 1928, and the Report came in August 1928. It asked for Dominion Status, a Union of India with the Princely States, a unitary British India, 19 Fundamental Rights and no separate electorates. It conceded some Muslim demands and refused others.
    → But ML1906 wanted more and raised its demands at Calcutta.
  7. Calcutta, December 1928. Jinnah proposed Three Amendments in the APC: one-third at the Centre, Punjab-Bengal reservation until UAF, and residuary powers to Provinces. All were rejected. INC then approved the Nehru Report. Bose's Purna Swaraj resolution failed, so INC set a one-year deadline for Dominion Status and passed a Constructive Work resolution.
    → So Jinnah turned the rejected demands into one master list.
  8. The 14 Points. In March 1929 Jinnah issued the 14 Points, a summary of every communal demand ever made by any Muslim organisation. It became the blueprint of all later ML1906 politics. Birkenhead won his challenge: Indians could not agree.
    → Meanwhile Britain was dealing with the Princely States separately.
  9. Butler Committee and AISPC. The Butler Committee, appointed in 1927, reported in 1929 that Paramountcy over the States was re-affirmed and that States should not be moved into a Union of India without their consent. The AISPC, set up in 1927, told it that the people, not the Princes, represent the States. This is the 1927 root of the Patel challenge of 1947.
    → Now Viceroy Irwin spoke on behalf of Britain.
  10. Diwali Declaration. On 31 October 1929 Irwin said Dominion Status is implicit in the Montague Statement and that the intent is to give it at a future date, with a Round Table Conference in London. It had no decision, no timeline and the wrong forum, since it proposed an RTC in London and no Constituent Assembly in India.
    → So INC answered with conditions.
  11. Delhi Manifesto. In November 1929 INC leaders set three conditions for joining the RTC. It must act as a Constituent Assembly for Dominion Status, a majority of Indian delegates must be from INC, and there must be amnesty in the Bomb in CLA case and the Meerut Conspiracy Case. The next chapter picks up from here.

In simple words#

  • Simon Commission 1927: seven British MPs, no Indian. INC boycotted it because Indians had no say in their own constitution.
  • Birkenhead's dare led to the All Party Conference and the Nehru Report (Aug 1928), the first Indian constitutional blueprint.
  • Dominion Status means the Crown keeps sovereignty, external affairs and the power to change the constitution, while Indians run domestic affairs with democracy.
  • ML1906's ladder: Delhi Proposals (Dec 1927), Calcutta Amendments (Dec 1928), 14 Points (Mar 1929). Every step was about a bigger share of political power.
  • Butler Committee protected Paramountcy and Princes' consent; the AISPC said the people, not the Princes, speak for the States.
  • Diwali Declaration offered an intent and an RTC in London. Delhi Manifesto made INC's participation conditional on a Constituent Assembly, an INC majority and amnesty.

1 · Indian Statutory Commission / Simon Commission (1927)#

Story link

Because GOI Act 1919 had a 10-year review and Britain wanted to control the timing, the Commission arrived early with no Indian. Ask: why did Indians see the exclusion as an insult?

1.1 Why it came and why early

  • The appointment of the Indian Statutory Commission in 1927 ended INC's passive phase that had lasted since the Bardoli Resolution of 1922.
  • GOI Act 1919 had a built-in provision to review constitutional reforms after 10 years. This was the link to the gradual Swaraj promise of the Montague Statement of 1917.
  • The Commission was appointed 2 years ahead of schedule. The Conservative Government in Britain did not want to leave this sensitive matter to the next government.
  • Added pressures: Pro-Changers within INC, rising revolutionary activity through the 1920s, and growing fear of communism spreading in India.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: in 1926 the Swarajists (Pro-Changers) lost the elections and INC said Leave (check your notebook for the exact point of the letter L). Simon and Butler both came in 1927.

1.2 Composition: not a single Indian

  • The seven-member Commission was headed by Sir John Simon and consisted of Members of the British Parliament.
  • One member was Clement Attlee, later Prime Minister of Britain (1945-51), who oversaw Indian independence.
  • Not a single Indian was among the 7. INC opposed the Commission precisely on this ground: the principle of self-government promised in the Montague Statement of 1917 was being violated, as Indians had no say in their own future constitution.
  • ICAs and GOI Acts were legal instruments serving British administrative purposes. They were not a Constitution of Indians, and exclusion even from this review was an insult.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: British Parliament decides our COI. That decision covers the state structure, how to populate this structure, and the powers within it. The structure splits into L, E, J (check your notebook for the expansion of the board's Sym Sov).
  • Question on the board: is this Swaraj within British Raj? Should we not have a say in the above? We would be fine if British plus Indians decide the COI.

1.3 Birkenhead's challenge

  • Birkenhead, Secretary of State for India, challenged Indians (not INC specifically) in the British Parliament to draft a constitution themselves.
  • He believed Indian leaders could not overcome their internal differences and act unitedly.
  • He was essentially taunting Indians. He was partially proved right, not because Indians lacked ability but because British-era communal divisions made consensus difficult.
Handout alert
  • Prelims 1998, 2013: boycott reason = no Indian member. Prelims 2010: the Commission's key recommendation = abolition of Dyarchy at provincial level, i.e. replacement of diarchy by responsible government in the provinces.

2 · What did INC do? The 1927 INC Madras Session#

Story link

Because Simon excluded Indians, INC chose to write the structure itself. Ask: what does drafting only the Principles of a Constitution mean?

  • At its Madras session in 1927, INC rejected the Simon Commission and decided to set up an All Party Conference (APC) to draft the Principles of a Constitution.
  • Think of this as drafting the basic structure of a Constitution; the full legal language would take years.
  • The Commission's first visit was in February 1928 (teacher's addition). Its second visit in October 1928 triggered massive anti-Simon protests across the country.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: a student can use MIH (the Motilal Nehru Report 1928) in Basic Structure. UPSC Polity and MIH together give the Idea of BS (Basic Structure) in the INM (check your notebook for the letter J on the board).

2.1 Lahore lathi charge

  • Lala Lajpat Rai was subjected to a brutal lathi charge by SP James Scott during the protests in Lahore; the injuries led to his death in November 1928.
  • This led to the assassination of John Saunders (a DSP, mistaken for Scott) by Bhagat, Sukhdev, Rajguru, Azad, investigated as the Lahore Conspiracy Case of 1928.
Handout alert
  • Handout alert: the handout says Saunders was killed by Bhagat Singh and calls him a British police officer; the slide gives the fuller list Bhagat, Sukhdev, Rajguru, Azad and the rank DSP. Use the slide's list.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board (timeline): Anti-Simon agitations 1928 and LLR martyr Nov 1928 lead to HSRA 1928; then the Lahore Conspiracy Case 1928; then the Bomb in CLA 1929, which was against the Public Safety Bill and the Trade Disputes Bill; then BSR martyr 23 March 1931.

Because INC was drafting a constitution, the Muslim League sent its own list. Ask: what did ML1906 want and what price did it name for joint electorates?

  • Even as INC was setting up the APC, ML1906 put forward the Four Delhi Proposals of December 1927, addressed to Motilal Nehru.
  • INC initially accepted them, but the APC later rejected them.

4 · Nehru Report (August 1928)#

Story link

Because Birkenhead dared Indians to draft a constitution, the APC under Ansari produced the Nehru Report. Ask: how much self-government, and how to treat the communal question?

4.0 Making of the Report

  • In 1928 the APC was formally set up in Delhi, headed by M.A. Ansari.
  • In May 1928 a committee under the APC was constituted under Motilal Nehru to draft the Principles of a Constitution.
  • Result: the Nehru Report of August 1928, the first Indian attempt at a constitutional blueprint.
  • Independence for India League was founded by Nehru and Bose, the radical wing of INC, in opposition to the Report's Dominion-status goal (Prelims 1995).

4.1 What did it demand? Dominion Status

  • The Report asked for Dominion Status, the maximum self-government possible without leaving the British Empire.
  • Sovereignty would remain with the British Crown. Britain would retain the power to change the constitution and would be overall responsible for Defence and Foreign Policy. In the domestic sphere Indians would have self-government.
  • Compare Princely States: Paramountcy since 1858 effectively meant Dominion Status minus democracy. The Indian Prince had self-governance in domestic affairs and the British Crown controlled everything else.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: Paramountcy (1858-1947) in 565 PS: the top is Sym Sov (Br), Ext (Br), and Dom (Prince) with no democracy.
  • Dominion Status for British India: Sym Sov (Br), Ext (Br), and Dom (Indians) with people and democracy. The only difference is democracy in the domestic sphere (check your notebook for the expansion of Sym Sov).

4.1 Union of India and the structure

  • For the first time the Report demanded a Union of India bringing together British India and the Princely States, with federal relations between the two.
  • Think of this as 565 versions of Article 370 without democracy: non-interference by the Centre, dominated by British India's politicians, in affairs of Princely States.
  • For British India itself it proposed a unitary structure: a strong Centre with residuary powers with the Centre against Provinces.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: Union GoI acts as agent of the Crown (D, ?, FA; check your notebook for the middle letter) over the whole of India. It is unitary in domestic affairs over the Provinces of British India and confederal in domestic affairs over the 565 PS (Art 370), where the Prince controls 100% of domestic affairs.
  • Test: if the Centre holds at least 80% of powers and subjects the structure is unitary; if the Centre holds 20% or less it is confederation.
  • 7th Schedule today: Union List has much more subjects, State List much fewer, Concurrent List some. So today's COI leans unitary.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: British India = 78% population, 63% territory; Princely States = 22% population, 37% territory, which is x 565 = 565 PS.
  • A State is an organisation that rules a territory and its people, with LEJ in domestic affairs. The board counts Total States = 566, i.e. 566 country-like units.
  • Result of the INM by 1947: people = 1 = nation (excluding Portuguese and French territories).
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: before 1928 the Crown sat over the Union GoBrI (over Provinces) and separately over the 565 PS. The Nehru Report proposed one Union of India under the Crown with Unitary Provinces of British India and a Confederal 565 PS.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: USA: (1) 13 colonies became independent, (2) they united to form a union. It is a voluntary union with a choice to join or not. An indestructible union of indestructible States; cannot change borders of provinces.
  • Union of India: step 1: the Union was created. It is easy for the Centre to change borders of provinces and the nature of government, e.g. create a UT.

6 · INC Calcutta Session (December 1928)#

Story link

Because INC was split between old and young leaders, it adopted the Nehru Report with a deadline. Ask: why was Constructive Work needed before any mass movement?

  • 6.1 INC formally approved the Nehru Report.
  • 6.2 S.C. Bose moved a resolution to adopt Purna Swaraj as INC's goal instead of Dominion Status, but it failed.
  • 6.3 Compromise between old guard and younger leaders: if Dominion Status was not granted within one year, INC's goal would become Purna Swaraj and it would launch a Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM) for it.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: MG style = arbitration. MG wanted time to prepare masses for non-violent satyagraha, which is why the deadline carried a year.

6.4 Constructive Work (CW) resolution

  • Gandhiji wanted to prepare the masses for non-violent satyagraha before any mass movement.
  • Focus areas: boycott of foreign cloth and promotion of Khadi and self-reliance; anti-liquor campaign; anti-untouchability campaign; awareness on women empowerment; training in non-violent satyagraha; organisational strengthening of INC.
  • Khadi and self-reliance were not for political Swaraj but for the income of the poor, so this is not Non-Cooperation.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: CW builds self-strength (atmashakti; check your notebook for the exact word) at two levels. Community gives unity (anti-untouchability and women's awareness). Individual gives economic, health and spiritual strength.
  • Plus: INC organisation, satyagraha political work and awareness.

7 · Jinnah's 14 Points Demand (March 1929)#

Story link

Because the Delhi Proposals and Calcutta Amendments both failed, Jinnah collected every demand into one list. Ask: what did the list leave out?

  • After the failure of the Four Delhi Proposals (Dec 1927) and the Three Calcutta Amendments (Dec 1928), Jinnah/ML1906 brought the 14 Points Demand in March 1929.
  • Birkenhead won the challenge that Indian leaders could not agree on a constitution.
  • It was a summary of every communal demand ever made by any Muslim organisation and the blueprint for all future political actions of ML1906.
  • It contained every divisive demand except the demand for Pakistan, which came later.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: no PYQ in Prelims or Mains on the 14 Points themselves (the exam relevance is its role in the Pakistan story).
  • Teacher's board: the 14 Points = 2 nation 1 state, i.e. Power to ML1906. The later Pakistan demand is written as a separate step (check your notebook for the exact shorthand).

8 · Butler Committee (1927), Report (1929)#

Story link

Because Simon covered only British India, Britain needed a separate body for the 565 States. Ask: what would happen to States in a Union of India?

  • The Butler Committee was appointed in 1927 to examine and clarify the relationship between the British Crown and the Princely States.
  • Its 1929 report had two findings: British Paramountcy over Princely States was re-affirmed; and Paramountcy should remain intact to preserve the States, which should not be transferred into a Union of India without their own consent.
  • This is why GOI Act 1935 used an Instrument of Accession (IOA) for States to join the proposed federation. The IOA remained operative as our COI until 26 January 1950.
  • Teacher's slide note: a three-member committee headed by Harcourt Butler, appointed on 16 December 1927.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: the challenge of Patel after 1947 was set in 1927, because the Committee told States they could not be moved into a Union without consent.

8.1 All India State People's Congress (AISPC)

  • AISPC, set up in 1927, was an INC-like organisation working within the 565 Princely States.
  • It told the Butler Committee that the AISPC, not the Princes, should be treated as the representative body of the States, a challenge to the feudal authority of the Princes from their own people.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board, 2 Indias: British India has INC 1885 and then PCCs 1886; the 565 PS have AISPC 1927 and then the SPC1, SPC2 (State People's Congresses). States people = people of the Princely States.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board on the 2017 PYQ: whenever the word Province appears in MIH, it means British India. The Princes' concern was the fear of being made part of a Union of India.

9 · Diwali Declaration / Irwin Statement (31 October 1929)#

Story link

Because INC had a one-year deadline running, Irwin spoke in October 1929. Ask: what did he actually promise?

Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: Irwin 26-31 brought Lahore session 1929, CDM 1930-34, Gandhi-Irwin Pact / Delhi Pact 1931, RTC (1930) in London and the special session at Karachi 1931.
  • The same board runs: 1926 elections lost by Swarajists; Simon and Butler 1927; anti-Simon agitation 1928; LLR martyr Nov 1928; HSRA 1928; Lahore C Case 1928; Bomb CLA 1929; BSR martyr 1931; IRA 1930-33 Surya Sen; Nehru sedition 1933 (2 yr jail); APC 1928 / MLN Report 1928; 4DP 1927, 3CAm 1928, 14ptD 1929; Sarda Act 1929.
  • Viceroy Irwin 26-31 issued a statement on 31 October 1929, the Diwali Declaration.
  • Text: *Dominion status is implicit in the Montague Statement of 1917 and it is the intent of the British to give Dominion Status at a future date, and for this purpose Britain will hold a Round Table Conference (RTC) in London.*
  • 9.2 No decision, only an intent. No timeline, only a vague future date. Wrong forum: an RTC in London, not a Constituent Assembly in India.
  • 9.3 In short, the British offered nothing concrete, so INC responded with the Delhi Manifesto in November 1929.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: Montagu 1917 gave self-government gradually (SG gradually); Irwin 26-31 (1929) gives Dominion gradually plus an RTC.
