What to prepare

Prelims hooks: the 12 features with their Article numbers; the six types of majority; 395 to 470 Articles, 22 to 25 Parts, 8 to 12 Schedules; voting age 21 to 18 (61st Amendment, 1988); which duty was added in 2002.

Mains hooks: whether the Constitution is borrowed or adapted; federal or unitary (quasi-federal); the Government of India Act, 1935 as source (2016 asks if it laid down a federal constitution); rigid and flexible; separation of powers as functional.

Closing idea: The Constitution borrowed widely but adapted carefully; its flexibility and its blend of ideals are strengths, and Ambedkar's warning is that political democracy needs social and economic democracy.

1 · The twelve features at a glance#

The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and enforced on 26 January 1950. The framers studied many countries' Constitutions and the working of the Government of India Act, 1935, then took what suited India.

All twelve in one sentence
LPBFDDFUJSSS

"Long Parliaments Blend Fine Debates, Duties; Federal Unions Judge Secular Single Separations." Order: Lengthiest, Parliamentary, Blend of rigid/flexible, Fundamental Rights, DPSP, Duties, Federation, Universal suffrage, Judiciary, Secular, Single citizenship, Separation of powers.

Four clusters

Size and form (1-3), Rights side (4-6), Federal side (7, 11), People and law (8-10, 12). Sort each feature into its cluster and the list stops feeling long.

2 · 1. The lengthiest written Constitution#

It is the most detailed written Constitution in the world (written, like the American; unlike the unwritten British).

Size then and now
At adoptionToday (handout)
Articles395470
Parts2225
Schedules812

Amendments deleted about 20 Articles and one Part (Part VII) and added nearly 95 new Articles, four Parts (IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA) and four Schedules (9, 10, 11, 12).

Four new Parts
4A9A9B14A

IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA. Chant it as "IV-A, IX-A, IX-B, XIV-A": the A, A, B, A pattern is all you need.

Why so long (four reasons)

  • Vast geography and diversity needing special provisions.
  • Influence of the lengthy Government of India Act, 1935.
  • One Constitution for both Union and States, unlike federations such as the USA.
  • A lawyer-dominated Constituent Assembly that preferred detailed drafting.

Subjects that other democracies leave to convention or ordinary law (electoral system, administrative machinery) are written into this Constitution.

Handout alert

The handout says 470 Articles here, but in the Critique section says "now over 450". Use 470 as the current figure given under Feature 1, and note the mismatch.

3 · 2. Parliamentary form of government#

India took the Westminster model, not the American presidential system, as better suited to its social diversity and democratic traditions. It runs on cooperation between legislature and executive, not strict separation.

Who holds power

President
Nominal head of State
Prime Minister and Council of Ministers
Real authority; collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha
No-confidence motion
Can remove the government

Differences from the British model

  • Parliament is not sovereign: bound by a written Constitution and judicial review
  • Head of State is an elected republican, not a hereditary monarch

Criticisms

  • Instability in coalition politics
  • executive dominance under majority rule
  • growth of Prime Ministerial Government

The framers chose it deliberately for its balance of stability with accountability.

4 · 3. Blend of rigidity and flexibility#

Neither wholly rigid nor wholly flexible.

Rigid

  • Federal structure, judiciary, fundamental rights
  • special majority of Parliament plus ratification by half the states (Art. 368)

Flexible

  • Formation of new states, change of names or boundaries
  • simple majority like ordinary laws

Types of majority (Additional information, redrawn)

Six majorities and where each is used
TypeMeaningUsed for
1 Simple (ordinary or functional)More than 50% of members present and voting; default rule (Art. 100)Ordinary, Money and Financial Bills; motions (confidence, no-confidence, censure, adjournment, thanks); President's Rule (Art. 356); Financial Emergency (Art. 360); Speaker/Deputy Speaker election (Art. 93); Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Art. 89); disapproval of National Emergency (Art. 352)
2 EffectiveMore than 50% of the effective strength = total membership minus vacant seats ("majority of all the then members")Removal of Vice-President (Art. 67, Rajya Sabha); Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Art. 90); Speaker and Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha (Art. 94)
3 AbsoluteMore than 50% of the total strength of the House, regardless of vacancies or absenteesNot stand-alone; a component of Special Majority I. A party needs an absolute majority (≥ 272 in Lok Sabha) to form government
4 Special, Type I(a) Majority of the total membership of each House and (b) two-thirds of members present and votingAmendment (Art. 368); removal of SC judges (Art. 124) and HC judges (Art. 217); CAG (Art. 148); CEC (Art. 324); State Election Commissioner (Art. 243K); approval of National Emergency (Art. 352)
5 Special, Type IITwo-thirds of the total membership of each House (no "present and voting")Impeachment of the President (Art. 61)
6 Special, Type IIITwo-thirds present and voting in the Rajya Sabha onlyArt. 312 (new All-India Services); Art. 249 (laws on State List subjects in the national interest)
Six majorities
SEASSS

Simple, Effective, Absolute, then three Specials. Hook: "Some Elephants Always Sing Sweet Songs."

Shortcut

Simple = "present and voting". Effective = "minus vacancies". Absolute = "of the whole House". Special I = "majority of all and two-thirds present". Special II = "two-thirds of all" (only the President's impeachment). Special III = "Rajya Sabha only".

Handout alert

Article 352 appears in two places: disapproval of a National Emergency (Simple) and approval of a National Emergency (Special I). Likewise the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha appears under Art. 89 (Simple, election) and Art. 90 (Effective, removal).

5 · 4. Fundamental Rights#

Part III, the cornerstone of democracy, guarantee civil and political liberties and check arbitrary State power. They are justiciable.

The six categories
RightArticlesCore idea
Equality14-18Equality before law, no discrimination, abolition of untouchability and titles
Freedom19-22Speech, association, movement, residence, profession; protection on conviction and personal liberty
Against exploitation23-24Bans trafficking, forced labour, child labour
Freedom of religion25-28Conscience; profess, practise, propagate
Cultural and educational29-30Language, culture, minorities' right to run educational institutions
Constitutional remedies32Move the Supreme Court; Ambedkar: "heart and soul"
Six rights
EFERCC

Equality, Freedom, Exploitation, Religion, Cultural-educational, Constitutional remedies: "Every Fine Engine Runs Cool Completely." Originally seven rights. The Right to Property (Art. 31) was removed by the 44th Amendment, 1978 and made a legal right under Art. 300-A (Part XII).

6 · 5. Directive Principles of State Policy#

Part IV: non-justiciable guidelines for a welfare State and socio-economic democracy. Ambedkar: a "novel feature".

Three categories
CategoryExamples in the handout
SocialistRight to work, education, livelihood
GandhianPanchayati Raj, village industries, prohibition of intoxicants
Liberal-intellectualUniform civil code, protection of environment, promotion of international peace

They are not enforceable but fundamental in governance, and influenced land reforms, MGNREGA and the Right to Education Act.

Three types
SGL

Socialist, Gandhian, Liberal-intellectual: "See Gandhi Lead".

7 · 6. Fundamental Duties#

Inspired by the Constitution of the USSR. They instil civic responsibility and balance rights with obligations.

How the duties came

1976
42nd Amendment places them in Part IVA, Art. 51A: 10 duties
2002
86th Amendment adds the 11th: parents or guardians to provide educational opportunities to children aged 6 to 14 (Art. 51A(k))

They are non-justiciable: a moral code guiding national unity, integrity and constitutional values.

8 · 7. Federation with a strong centralising tendency#

Art. 1 calls India a "Union of States". States cannot secede: the Union is indestructible.

Federal features

  • Dual polity
  • division of powers
  • written Constitution
  • independent judiciary
  • bicameralism at the Centre

Tilt to the Centre

  • Most important subjects with the Union
  • emergency takeover
  • one Constitution
  • one citizenship

K.C. Wheare called it "quasi-federal": structurally tilted to the Centre for unity and stability.

9 · 8. Universal adult suffrage#

Art. 326: every citizen aged 18+ votes in Parliament and State Legislature elections. Voting age was 21, reduced to 18 by the 61st Amendment (1988). Equal votes regardless of caste, religion, gender, education or economic status. Over 900 million eligible voters make India the world's largest democracy.

10 · 9. Independent judiciary#

An integrated judiciary: Supreme Court at the apex, High Courts, subordinate courts; one system enforces both Central and State laws. Judicial review under Arts. 13, 32 and 226 makes it the guardian of the Constitution and Fundamental Rights, and it decides Union-State disputes. Independence rests on security of tenure, fixed service conditions and a rigorous impeachment process.

11 · 10. Secular State#

No State religion; Arts. 25-28 guarantee the right to profess, practise and propagate any faith, or none. The 42nd Amendment (1976) inserted "Secular" in the Preamble. Unlike Western absolute separation, India follows Sarva Dharma Sambhava: equal respect for all religions, with State intervention for reform (banning untouchability, gender equality in places of worship) while staying neutral.

12 · 11. Single citizenship#

Unlike the US (dual citizenship), India has one citizenship. Every Indian enjoys the same political and civil rights (vote, movement, equality before law) irrespective of state or union territory. It prevents parochial loyalties and promotes national integration and fraternity.

13 · 12. Separation of powers#

Montesquieu's doctrine: legislature makes laws, executive implements, judiciary interprets. In India it is functional and flexible, not rigid.

Where the powers deliberately overlap
OverlapExample in the handout
Executive in legislatureMinisters are drawn from the legislature; accountable through Question Hour, no-confidence motions, committee scrutiny
Judiciary over othersJudicial review of legislative enactments and executive decisions
Legislature in judiciaryRole in impeachment of judges
Executive in judiciaryParticipation in judicial appointments

Result: a dynamic system of checks and balances, not watertight compartments.

14 · Critique: the eight charges and the replies#

Critics inside and outside the Assembly called it borrowed, bulky and over-legalistic. The framers defended each choice by India's diversity, history and governance needs.

Eight charges
BCUUEPLA

Borrowed, Carbon copy, Un-Indian, Un-Gandhian, Elephantine, Paradise of lawyers, Lacks consistency, Ambedkar's warnings: "Bad Copies Usually Upset Every Proud Lawyer, Ambedkar warned."

Charge, critic and defence
ChargeThe critics saidThe defence
1 A borrowed Constitution"Bag of borrowings"; Ivor Jennings: "patchwork", "lawyer's paradise"Ambedkar: none framed so late in history could avoid resemblance. Granville Austin: borrowing was adaptation, not imitation
2 Carbon copy of the 1935 ActN. Srinivasan: "close copy of the 1935 Act"; P.R. Deshmukh: "1935 Act with only adult franchise added"Ambedkar: India already ran under the 1935 Act, so retaining its administrative machinery was practical; democracy's soul, adult franchise and fundamental rights were new
3 Un-Indian or anti-IndianK. Hanumanthaiya: "music of an English band" not Veena or Sitar; Lokanath Misra: "slavish imitation of the West"Granville Austin: "absurd"; Indianness is not fixed; liberty, equality, fraternity are universal and India needed them for a secular democratic plural state
4 Un-GandhianGandhi wanted village-based decentralised self-reliance; Hanumanthaiya: "the kind of Constitution Mahatma Gandhi did not want"; T. Prakasam blamed Ambedkar's distance from Gandhian politicsArt. 40 (village panchayats) and later the 73rd and 74th Amendments; Ambedkar: villages were "dens of darkness", empowering them without social reform would empower local elites
5 Elephantine sizeJennings: "too long and complicated"; H.V. Kamath: the Assembly's emblem was an elephant, so an "elephantine constitution"Diversity needed detailed provisions on minorities, states and Centre-State relations; one Constitution for Union and States; detail reduced ambiguity and litigation
6 Paradise of the lawyersJennings: "lawyer's paradise"; H.K. Maheswari: Indians would become more litigiousA Constitution is a legal document, so precision was unavoidable; needed to protect rights and federalism
7 Lacks theoretical consistencyMixes liberal ideals (liberty, equality) with traditional concerns (cow protection, prohibition)Hanna Lerner: deferral, ambiguity and non-justiciability managed divisive issues (UCC, prohibition); inconsistency reflected plurality and let the Constitution evolve
8 Ambedkar's warningsHis own: political democracy cannot last without social and economic democracy; "era of contradictions" (political equality with socio-economic inequality); beware hero-worshipHe stressed constitutional morality and methods, not mass protests or blind faith in leaders
