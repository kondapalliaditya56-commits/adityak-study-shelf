The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: Territory turns a faith into a power: the Mughals kill two Gurus, so the Sikhs arm themselves (Khalsa) and demand a State at Anandpur. Banda Bahadur 08-15 builds one State and loses it; the Misl confederacy keeps the idea alive as a weak centre; Ranjit Singh 01-39 centralises it, then balances it, and signs treaties with the EIC.

  1. A peaceful faith. Guru Nanak Dev (1469-1539), a contemporary of Babur 26-30, teaches four things: one formless God, direct access without rituals or priests, no caste, honest living and selfless service. He makes four Udasis over 30 years (about 30,000 km), then settles at Kartarpur and starts the Langar, a free kitchen for equality.
    → But a faith meets a State the moment territory enters the picture.
  2. Territory, and the first blow (1606). The teacher says a State is an organisation with a territory motive, and until the Sikhs wanted territory there was no dispute. They begin demanding a Sikh State in and around Anandpur (west of Shimla, south of Chamba), land held by Shivalik hill chiefs who are Mughal allies. In 1606 Jahangir 05-27 executes the 5th Guru, Arjan Dev, and a peaceful movement starts turning martial.
    → A second Guru is killed, and the answer is an army.
  3. The Khalsa (1675-1699). In 1675 Aurangzeb 58-07 has the 9th Guru, Teg Bahadur, executed in Delhi. In 1699 the 10th Guru, Gobind Singh 76-08, founds the Khalsa at Anandpur: a Sikh military organisation, something like an NSG or STF, to defend the Panth and struggle for a Sikh State. Its identity is the five Ks.
    → But Anandpur never comes, and the Guru's family pays.
  4. The Sahibzadas and a broken promise (1704-1707). In December 1704 the two elder sons, Ajit Singh (18) and Jujhar Singh (14), die at Chamkaur. The same month the two younger, Zorawar Singh (9) and Fateh Singh (6), are buried alive in a wall on the orders of Wazir Khan, Faujdar of Sirhind. In 1707 Bahadur Shah 07-12, on a pacifist policy, gives the Guru a high Mansab and promises Anandpur, but never keeps it.
    → And the promise is followed by a plot.
  5. Nanded and the Book (1708). On 7 October 1708 Wazir Khan's assassins strike the Guru at Abchal Nagar Sahib in Nanded; he kills them but dies of his injuries at 42, and Wazir Khan is not punished. A few days before, he had declared the Guru Granth Sahib the final Guru, to stop Jat versus Khatri tension: Jats dominated the Khalsa and all ten Gurus were Khatri traders. The Book was installed at the Akal Takht.
    → Now someone must carry the struggle and take revenge.
  6. Banda Bahadur 08-15's State. From 1708 Banda Bahadur 08-15 carries on. On 10 May 1710 at Chappar Chiri he kills Wazir Khan and takes Sirhind. His State between the Ravi and the Jamuna has its own coins, officials and seal, which the teacher reads as full independence. Farrukhsiyar 13-19 sends Abdus Samad Khan as Governor of Lahore to crush it.
    → But a State needs its own people behind it.
  7. Why it fell (1715-1716). The Khatri traders, hurt by constant war, turned away. Jahandar Shah 12-13 brought Ijaradari to Punjab to co-opt them, and he and Farrukhsiyar 13-19 offered them Mansabs. Hindu Jat zamindars switched too: Churaman 95-21 went over when the Mughals recognised his Jat Kingdom. Banda Bahadur 08-15 was also not a Guru. He was defeated in 1715 and executed in 1716.
    → Yet the Sikh State does not die.
  8. The Misl confederacy. The Mughals are too weak to take Punjab back, so power spreads to Misl chiefs: kinship clans that unite in wars, especially against Afghan invasions, and otherwise act alone. Conquered land is shared by contribution. By 1770 there are 12 major Misls with the Dal Khalsa at the centre; at Manupur 1748 it prepares a joint defence and Abdali is defeated. This is a weak centre, a fragmented polity, like the Maratha Confederacy.
    → But power tends to gather in a strong personality.
  9. Ranjit Singh 01-39 centralises. Zaman Shah's invasions are a crisis that becomes an opportunity for Ranjit Singh 01-39 of the Sukerchakia Misl, who takes Lahore from the Bhangi Misl and is crowned Maharaja in 1801. The other Misl chiefs become tribute-paying vassals holding Jagirs, not Watan. The handout dates Lahore to 1801, the slides to 1799.
    → Now he must settle accounts with the EIC across the Sutlej.
  10. Treaties and conquests (1809-1838). In 1809 Charles Metcalfe, the EIC's Resident at Delhi, signs the Treaty of Amritsar: Ranjit Singh 01-39 is recognised as sovereign, there is a military alliance, the Sutlej is the border, and he is free to expand on his side. He then takes Multan (1818), Kashmir (1819) and Peshawar (1834, Hari Singh Nalwa). The 1838 Tripartite Treaty with Shah Shuja makes Afghanistan and Punjab a buffer against feared Russian invasion.
    → So what kind of State held all this together?
  11. A balanced, secular State. Ranjit Singh 01-39 built power by coercion, diplomacy and balance between Misl chiefs, the Khalsa army, Khatri traders and non-Sikh nobles such as the Dogras. Only 40% of land was Jagirdari; the rest paid the King directly, collected by Kardars (collector, magistrate, police chief, customs officer). Village autonomy was respected, Fakir Azizuddin was Foreign Minister, Dewan Dina Nath Finance Minister, and Sahajdhari Sikhs were included.

In simple words#

  • Guru Nanak (1469-1539) gave Sikhism four ideas: one formless God, no rituals or priests, no caste, honest living and service.
  • Sikhs wanted a State at Anandpur. The Mughals killed two Gurus (1606, 1675), so a peaceful movement turned martial: the Khalsa of 1699.
  • The Guru Granth Sahib became the final Guru to end the Jat versus Khatri rift. Banda Bahadur 08-15 built a first Sikh State (Ravi to Jamuna) but lost the Khatris and the Jats.
  • After him the Misl confederacy (12 major Misls by 1770, with the Dal Khalsa) was a weak centre: a fragmented polity.
  • Ranjit Singh 01-39 (Sukerchakia Misl) unified it, was crowned in 1801, signed the Treaty of Amritsar (1809), took Multan, Kashmir and Peshawar, and joined the 1838 Tripartite Treaty.
  • His State was balanced and secular: 40% Jagirdari, Kardars, village autonomy, a Muslim Foreign Minister and a Hindu Finance Minister.

1 · How did Sikhism begin, and what were its core beliefs?#

Story link

Chapter 1 of the story: a peaceful faith with four ideas. Ask: what did Nanak teach that a State could not tolerate?

  • Guru Nanak Dev (1469-1539) was a contemporary of Babur 26-30.
  • Sikhism rose from 1500 onwards, built on four core principles:
  • 1. One supreme and formless God.
  • 2. Direct access to God without rituals or priests.
  • 3. Rejection of the caste system.
  • 4. Emphasis on honest living and selfless service.

  • Guru Nanak undertook four major spiritual journeys (Udasis) over 30 years, covering about 30,000 km across India, South Asia, Tibet and Arabia.
  • He finally settled in Kartarpur (present-day Pakistan), where he established the Langar, the free communal kitchen designed to promote equality.

2 · When did conflict begin between the Sikhs and the Mughal State?#

Story link

Because the Sikhs began to demand a State at Anandpur, the Mughals struck twice. Ask: why does 1606 turn a movement martial?

  • Initially there was no conflict. But as the Sikh population grew and Sikhs began demanding a Sikh State in and around Anandpur (west of Shimla and south of Chamba), an area under the Shivalik Hill Chiefs who were Mughal allies, religious and political tensions built up.
  • First flashpoint, 1606: the 5th Guru, Arjan Dev, was executed by Jahangir 05-27.
  • A turning point: a peaceful spiritual movement began its transformation into a martial community.
  • Second flashpoint, 1675: the 9th Guru, Teg Bahadur, was executed in Delhi by Aurangzeb 58-07.

3 · The Khalsa (1699)#

Story link

Because two Gurus were killed, the tenth Guru built an army. Ask: why is it compared to NSG or STF?

  • In 1699, at Anandpur, the 10th Guru, Gobind Singh 76-08, established the Khalsa, a Sikh military organisation created to defend the Sikh Panth and struggle for a Sikh State.
  • The handout compares it to an NSG/STF: its creation marked the formal militarisation of the Sikh community's political ambitions.

4 · December 1704 to October 1708: the Sahibzadas and the Guru's death#

Story link

Because the Khalsa threatened the Mughal order, the Guru's family paid. Ask: who promised, and who broke the promise?

  • December 1704, Battle of Chamkaur: the Guru's two elder sons, Sahibzada Ajit Singh (aged 18) and Sahibzada Jujhar Singh (aged 14), died in battle.
  • The same month his two younger sons, Sahibzada Zorawar Singh (aged 9) and Sahibzada Fateh Singh (aged 6), were buried alive in a wall on the orders of Wazir Khan, the Mughal Faujdar of Sirhind.
  • (A Faujdar = a district military commander, comparable in local authority to a Superintendent of Police today.
  • Since 2022, 26 December is observed as Veer Bal Divas in memory of the younger Sahibzadas.)

5 · Why the Guru declared the Guru Granth Sahib the final Guru#

Story link

Because a living successor could inflame Jats against Khatris, the Guru chose a Book. Ask: why is a Book neutral?

  • A few days before his death, at Abchal Nagar Sahib, Guru Gobind Singh 76-08 declared that he would be the last living Guru and that all spiritual authority would henceforth be vested in the Guru Granth Sahib Ji, the holy book he had compiled.
  • The decision was deliberate: to prevent community tensions between the Jat community, which dominated the Khalsa, and the Khatri community of traders, to which all ten Gurus had belonged.
  • By vesting authority in the holy book rather than a human successor, the question of community dominance was permanently closed.
Why the Guru became a Book
  • The Guru Granth Sahib Ji was installed at the Akal Takht in the Golden Temple complex, Amritsar.
  • From the Akal Takht the Gurus issued Hukumnamas (formal orders) and Sikh warriors took blessings before going into battle against the Mughals.
  • The 19th-century sects of Nirankaris and Namdharis believe in a living Guru, which has historically led to serious conflicts within the community.

6 · Banda Bahadur 08-15: the first Sikh State, and why it collapsed#

Story link

Because the Guru was dead, Banda took revenge and built a State. Ask: what makes a State real (coins, officials, seal)?

  • From 1708 the rebellion was carried forward by Banda Bahadur 08-15.
  • He defeated and killed Wazir Khan at the Battle of Chappar Chiri on 10 May 1710, which also led to the conquest of Sirhind.
  • He established a Sikh State over territories between the River Ravi and the River Jamuna, with its own coins, its own officials and its own seal for administrative orders (the teacher's ink: full independence).
  • Farrukhsiyar 13-19 appointed Abdus Samad Khan as Governor of Lahore specifically to suppress this Sikh State.
  • By 1715 Banda Bahadur 08-15 was defeated
  • Captured and executed in Delhi in 1716 by Farrukhsiyar 13-19
Handout note

Flag on the territory: the handout places Banda's State between the Ravi and the Jamuna; the teacher's ink on the board says "a small state in and around Patiala." Both appear in the class material; the handout's wording is the answer-writing one.

Why his State fell (three reasons):

  • 1. Khatri traders turned against him. Constant warfare hurt trade, and the commercial class needs decisive victors and political stability to function.
  • 2. Divide and rule.Jahandar Shah 12-13 introduced Ijaradari (the revenue-farming system) in Punjab specifically to co-opt Khatri traders with business opportunities.
  • Both Jahandar Shah 12-13 and Farrukhsiyar 13-19 offered Mansabs to Khatri traders, who switched sides.
  • (Note: this further increased the number of Mansabdars, adding to the Jagirdari Crisis; see Chapter 2.)
  • 3. Hindu Jat zamindars switched sides.Churaman 95-21 Jat (1695-1721), for instance, went over to the Mughals after they recognised his Jat Kingdom, granting him conditional dynastic autonomy in exchange for loyalty.

7 · What kept the Sikh State alive: the Misl confederacy#

Story link

Because Banda fell but the Mughals were weak, power spread to Misl chiefs. Ask: is a confederacy strength or weakness?

  • The defeat of Banda Bahadur 08-15 did not end the Sikh State.
  • The Mughals were too weak to fully reassert control over Punjab, and power became decentralised among Misl Chiefs.
  • Misls are kinship-based clans. They united during wars, particularly against Afghan invasions, but otherwise operated independently.
  • Whenever a Misl conquered territory, the lands were distributed among its members in proportion to their military and monetary contribution.Even the lowest-ranking soldier received a share of land.
  • By 1770 there were 12 major Misls in Punjab, with the Dal Khalsa at the centre under a leader chosen by the Misl chiefs: a confederacy structure with a weak centre and strong constituent units.
  • Takeaway (handout): compare it to the Maratha Confederacy under the Peshwas, where a nominal centre held together largely autonomous regional chiefs.
  • Decentralisation was both a source of resilience and, ultimately, a vulnerability.

8 · Ranjit Singh 01-39 unifies the Misls#

Story link

Because power gathers in a strong personality, Ranjit Singh 01-39 unifies the Misls. Ask: which crisis became his opportunity?

  • Ranjit Singh 01-39 of the Sukerchakia Misl played a crucial role in defeating Zaman Shah's third Afghan invasion of 1799 (the slide reads: he rose during Zaman Shah's Afghan invasions of 1796-1799).
  • This gave him the platform to establish dominance over the other Misls, and he was coronated King (Maharaja) of the Sikh State in 1801.
  • The other Misl chiefs were reduced to tribute-paying vassals, retaining conditional control of their lands in return for loyalty and tribute to the King (the teacher's ink: lands held as Jagirs, not Watan).

9 · The Treaty of Amritsar (1809)#

Story link

Because he now borders the EIC, he settles the frontier. Ask: what did he gain, and which way did he then expand?

  • In 1809 Ranjit Singh 01-39 signed the Treaty of Amritsar with the EIC, represented by Charles Metcalfe, the EIC's Resident in the Delhi court.
  • (Recall that the Scindias had been removed from Delhi in 1803 and the EIC had set up indirect rule there through a British Resident.) Four outcomes:
  • 1. The EIC recognised Ranjit Singh 01-39 as the sovereign ruler of Punjab.
  • 2. It led to a military alliance (later link to the EIC's Policy of Ring Fence).
  • 3. The Sutlej River was fixed as the border between the Sikh State and the Company State.
  • 4. Ranjit Singh 01-39 was free to expand on his side of the Sutlej without British interference.

10 · How far did Ranjit Singh 01-39 expand after 1809?#

Story link

Because the east was secured, he turned west and north. Ask: which conquest completed what the Marathas left unfinished?

  • With his eastern border secured, Ranjit Singh 01-39 turned west and north:
  • 1. 1818:removed the Afghans from Multan.
  • 2. 1819:conquered Kashmir. (Recall that Ahmad Shah Abdali 47-72 captured Punjab and Kashmir; Punjab was taken back by the Marathas in 1758 but not Kashmir; Ranjit Singh 01-39 completed what the Marathas left unfinished.)
  • 3. 1834:conquered Peshawar (present-day Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, bordering Afghanistan along the Durand Line).
  • Peshawar was conquered by Hari Singh Nalwa, whose reputation was so fearsome that Afghan mothers used his name to frighten unruly children:"If you don't stop crying, Haria Ragle will turn up."

10.1 The 1838 Tripartite Treaty

  • In 1838 Ranjit Singh 01-39 signed the Tripartite Treaty between the Sikh State, the EIC and Shah Shuja of Afghanistan.
  • The design: to establish British indirect rule in Afghanistan through Shah Shuja as a puppet ruler.
  • The aim: to create a buffer of Afghanistan and Punjab against the feared Russian invasion of British India from the north-west.

Recall sheet#

Read the question, answer in your head, then tap to check.

State (teacher's definition)?An organisation with a territory (T) motive; the Sikh-Mughal dispute began when Sikhs wanted a territory.
Four principles of Sikhism?One formless God; direct access without rituals or priests; rejection of caste; honest living and selfless service.
What were the Udasis?Four major spiritual journeys over 30 years, about 30,000 km, across India, South Asia, Tibet and Arabia.
What is the Langar?Free communal kitchen at Kartarpur, to promote equality.
Why did Anandpur matter?The Sikh State demand; west of Shimla, south of Chamba, under Shivalik hill chiefs who were Mughal allies.
Two flashpoints?1606 Arjan Dev (Jahangir 05-27); 1675 Teg Bahadur (Aurangzeb 58-07).
What was the Khalsa?A Sikh military organisation, like NSG or STF, founded 1699 to defend the Panth and struggle for a Sikh State.
Five Ks?Kesh, Kangha, Kada, Kirpan, Kachera.
What happened at Chamkaur (Dec 1704)?The two elder Sahibzadas (Ajit Singh 18, Jujhar Singh 14) died.
Younger Sahibzadas?Zorawar Singh (9) and Fateh Singh (6), buried alive in a wall on Wazir Khan's orders; Veer Bal Divas on 26 December.
Who was Wazir Khan?The Mughal Faujdar of Sirhind; a Faujdar is a district military commander.
What did Bahadur Shah promise in 1707?A high Mansab and a Sikh State at Anandpur (never fulfilled).
What did the Guru do on the last day (teacher)?Declared the Guru Granth Sahib the final Guru: spiritual authority in the Holy Book.
Who are the Nirankaris and Namdharis?19th-century sects who believe in a living Guru.
What was the Akal Takht's role?Seat from which Gurus issued Hukumnamas; Sikh warriors took blessings before battle; Guru Granth Sahib installed there.
What did Banda Bahadur 08-15 establish?A Sikh State between the Ravi and Jamuna with its own coins, officials and seal.
Why did Banda Bahadur 08-15 lose?Khatris (trade hurt; Ijaradari and Mansabs), Jat zamindars (Churaman 95-21), and he was not a Guru.
What are Misls?Kinship-based clans that united in wars, otherwise independent; land in proportion to contribution.
What was the Dal Khalsa?The assembly of Misl chiefs, the confederacy's weak centre.
Mains PYQ on fragmented polity?By the mid-18th century India was a spectre of fragmented polity; Punjab FYQ: by 1715.
How did Ranjit Singh 01-39 rise?Zaman Shah's invasions, Sukerchakia Misl, Lahore from the Bhangi Misl, coronation 1801; Misl chiefs as vassals.
Treaty of Amritsar outcomes?Sovereignty recognised; military alliance; Sutlej border; free expansion north of the Sutlej.
Conquests after 1809?Multan 1818; Kashmir 1819; Peshawar 1834.
Why was Kashmir's capture notable?The Marathas retook Punjab in 1758 but not Kashmir; Ranjit Singh 01-39 completed it.
Tripartite Treaty 1838?Sikh State, EIC and Shah Shuja: a puppet Shah Shuja and a buffer against Russia.
How did the Kardar work?Chief administrative officer of a Taluqa: revenue collector, magistrate, police chief and customs officer.
Why is Ranjit Singh 01-39's State called secular?Fakir Azizuddin (Muslim) as Foreign Minister and Dewan Dina Nath (Hindu) as Finance Minister; Sahajdhari Sikhs included.
Who are Sahajdhari Sikhs?Non-Khalsa Sikhs who do not maintain the five Ks.
