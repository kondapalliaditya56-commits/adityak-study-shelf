1 · Attorney General of India: the office#

The office is established by Article 76. The Attorney General (AG) is the principal legal adviser to the Union Government and holds the highest rank among law officers in the country.

  • The post represents the highest legal authority who advises and appears for the Government of India in the courts.
  • Constitutional recognition underlines the essential role of a senior legal adviser in the Union's governance.
Core idea

Article 76 = office of the Attorney General: appointment, qualifications, duties and functions.

2 · Appointment, tenure and pay of the AG#

Who may be appointed

  • The President appoints the Attorney General.
  • The appointee must have the same qualifications as a Judge of the Supreme Court.
  • In short: an Indian citizen who, in the President's view, is one of three types:
Qualification routes for the AG
RouteRequirement
1Served as a Judge of a High Court for five years
2Advocate of a High Court for ten years
3Eminent jurist (in the President's view)

These criteria ensure a person of proven legal standing and competence.

Tenure and cessation

  • The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed term.
  • It does not lay down a procedure or specific grounds for removal.
  • The AG holds office during the pleasure of the President: the President may remove the incumbent at any time.
  • The AG may resign by tendering resignation to the President.
  • By convention, the AG offers resignation when the Council of Ministers resigns or is replaced, since the appointment is made on the advice of the government in office.

Remuneration

The Constitution does not fix the AG's pay. The AG receives such remuneration as the President determines.

Life of the AG's office

Appointed by the President
Qualified as an SC Judge (citizen; HC Judge 5 yrs, or HC advocate 10 yrs, or eminent jurist)
Serves
During the pleasure of the President; no fixed term
Pay
As the President determines
Leaves
Removed at any time, resigns to the President, or by convention goes with the Council of Ministers
Qualifications of the AG
CJAE

Citizen, Judge of a High Court five years, Advocate of a High Court ten years, Eminent jurist: "Courts Judge All Experts".

Pleasure shortcut

No term, no removal procedure, no fixed pay: tenure and pay both rest with the President (AG) or the Governor (Advocate-General). Both go when the government goes (by convention).

Handout alert

The qualification criteria (HC Judge 5 years, HC advocate 10 years, eminent jurist) are given as the Supreme Court Judge standard, so use these three for the AG. The Advocate-General instead needs High Court Judge qualification (10 years as shown below).

3 · AG as principal legal adviser#

Core advisory responsibilities

  • Legal advice to the Union: the chief duty is to advise the Government of India on legal questions referred to the office by the President. This is the constitutional foundation of the office.
  • Other legal tasks assigned by the President: duties of a legal character, not an exhaustive list.
  • Constitutional and statutory functions: those conferred by the Constitution or by any law.

Litigation and representation for the Union

  • Supreme Court: appears on behalf of the Government of India in all matters in the Supreme Court in which the Union is a party or has a stake.
  • Presidential references (Article 143): represents the Government in matters referred by the President to the Supreme Court for its advisory opinion and explains the Union's legal stance.
  • High Courts: may appear in any High Court in cases where the Union is concerned, when required by the Government of India.
Three duties of the AG
AOS

Advise the Union, Other legal tasks from the President, Statutory and constitutional functions: "All Official Stuff".

4 · Rights and parliamentary privileges#

  • Right of audience: entitled to appear and plead in all courts across India.
  • Parliament: may take part in proceedings of both Houses, joint sittings, and committees of Parliament of which he may be named a member (the later section says "invited"), but cannot vote.
  • Privileges and immunities of members of Parliament while performing parliamentary functions: this allows candid advice and free participation.
Article 88

Every Minister and the Attorney-General has the right to speak in, and otherwise take part in, the proceedings of either House, any joint sitting, and any committee of Parliament of which he may be named a member, but shall not by virtue of this article be entitled to vote.

Article 88

  • Right to speak and take part in both Houses, joint sittings and committees
  • no vote

Article 105

  • Same privileges and immunities as MPs while performing duties in Parliament
Handout alert

For committees the handout says both "of which he may be named a member" (the Article 88 text) and "to which he may be invited". For the exact Article wording use named a member.

5 · Limitations on the AG#

These limits prevent conflicts of interest and keep the office single-client when acting for the Union.

Five prohibitions
LimitRule
No adverse representationMust not advise or act against the Government of India in any matter
No dual briefsMust not advise or hold briefs in cases where he is required to advise or represent the Government
Criminal defenceMust not defend accused persons in criminal proceedings without prior permission of the Government of India
Commercial directorshipsNo directorship in companies or corporations without Government approval
Centralised referralMust not accept instructions directly from ministries, departments, statutory bodies or PSUs; references go through the Ministry of Law and Justice (Department of Legal Affairs)
The five limits
ADCCR

Adverse (never against the Government), Dual briefs, Criminal defence needs permission, Company directorships need approval, Routed through the Law Ministry: "All Documents Come Countersigned, Routed".

Status and practice

  • The AG is not a government servant and not a full-time salaried counsel.
  • Private practice is not barred, subject to the limits above; this lets eminent practitioners hold the post and gives government matters priority when required.
  • The AG acts on the reference of the President or the Government; rights and privileges apply only in that official capacity.
Balance

Rights give authoritative representation and candid participation in Parliament. Limits protect public confidence and the integrity of government legal advice.

6 · Solicitor General and key Articles for the AG#

To assist the AG there are the Solicitor General of India and Additional Solicitors General.

  • These are not constitutional offices; they support the AG in litigation and advisory work.
  • Only the Attorney General has constitutional recognition under Article 76.

7 · Advocate-General: appointment and terms#

Article 165 provides for the Advocate-General (AdvG) of each State: the highest law officer of the State and legal adviser to the state government. It is the state counterpart of the Attorney General.

Appointment

  • The Governor appoints the Advocate-General.
  • The person must be qualified to be appointed a Judge of a High Court: an Indian citizen, who has held judicial office for at least 10 years, or been an advocate of a High Court for at least 10 years.
  • The appointment is made on the advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister, though formally exercised by the Governor.

Term of office

  • The Constitution fixes no tenure.
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the Governor: may be removed at any time, or resign by submitting resignation to the Governor.
  • By convention, resigns when the government resigns or changes, because the appointment rests on its advice. This keeps the state's legal adviser aligned with the political executive in power.

Remuneration

  • The Constitution does not prescribe salary or allowances.
  • Pay is determined by the Governor, which gives the state flexibility according to administrative arrangements and financial capacity.

Handout alert

The chapter's text and comparison table both state 10 years for both routes (judicial office, High Court advocate). Use these for the Advocate-General. The AG (Supreme Court standard) uses 5 years as HC Judge or 10 years as HC advocate.

8 · Duties and functions of the Advocate-General#

He is the chief legal adviser and representative of the state government.

  • Advisory role: legal advice to the state government on matters referred by the Governor.
  • Assigned duties: other legal duties assigned by the Governor.
  • Constitutional and statutory functions: those conferred by the Constitution or any state law.
  • Court representation: entitled to appear in any court of law within the state, including High Courts and subordinate courts.
  • Legislative participation: right to speak and take part in the proceedings of both Houses of the state legislature (where there are two) and their committees; no right to vote; enjoys all privileges and immunities of a member of the state legislature.
Article 177

Every Minister and the Advocate-General has the right to speak in, and take part in the proceedings of, the Legislative Assembly (or both Houses where there is a Legislative Council), and any committee of the Legislature of which he may be named a member, but not to vote by virtue of this article.

Functions of the AdvG
AACCL

Advise (on Governor's reference), Assigned duties, Constitutional and statutory functions, Court representation, Legislative participation: "All Advocates Cross-examine Courts, Legislators".

9 · Position and significance#

  • The AdvG is the bridge between law and governance in the state.
  • By advising the government and representing it in courts, he ensures executive actions are legally sound.
  • His presence in the legislature lets him clarify legal points in debate, aiding law-making.
  • The office upholds the rule of law: governance within the constitutional framework.
Why it matters

Executive action is legally checked at the state level without a separate veto: advice, representation and clarification in the House.
