In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Article 76 creates the Attorney General (AG): the principal legal adviser to the Union Government and its highest law officer.
  • The President appoints the AG, who needs the Supreme Court Judge standard: a citizen who was an HC Judge for 5 years, or an HC advocate for 10 years, or an eminent jurist.
  • There is no fixed term, no removal procedure and no fixed pay: the AG serves during the pleasure of the President, and by convention goes when the Council of Ministers goes.
  • The AG may speak in both Houses, joint sittings and committees but cannot vote (Art. 88), has MPs' privileges (Art. 105) and a right of audience in all courts
  • Five limits prevent conflict of interest
  • The Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General only assist and are not constitutional posts. The state twin is the Advocate-General (Art. 165), appointed by the Governor on the High Court Judge standard (Arts. 177 and 194(2)).

1 · Attorney General of India: the office#

  • The office is established by Article 76. The Attorney General (AG) is the principal legal adviser to the Union Government and holds the highest rank among law officers in the country.
  • The post represents the highest legal authority who advises and appears for the Government of India in the courts.
  • Constitutional recognition underlines the essential role of a senior legal adviser in the Union's governance.
  • Core idea: Article 76 = office of the Attorney General: appointment, qualifications, duties and functions.

Union law officers at a glance

OfficerStatusRole
Attorney GeneralConstitutional (Art. 76)Principal legal adviser; highest law officer
Solicitor GeneralNot constitutionalAssists the Attorney General
Additional Solicitors GeneralNot constitutionalAssist the Attorney General

Also in the handout

  • This role makes the office the foremost legal voice of the Union before the nation’s highest court.

2 · Appointment, tenure and pay of the AG#

Who may be appointed

  • The President appoints the Attorney General.
  • The appointee must have the same qualifications as a Judge of the Supreme Court.
  • In short: an Indian citizen who, in the President's view, is one of three types:

Qualification routes for the AG

RouteRequirement
1Served as a Judge of a High Court for five years
2Advocate of a High Court for ten years
3Eminent jurist (in the President's view)
  • These criteria ensure a person of proven legal standing and competence.

Tenure and cessation

  • The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed term.
  • It does not lay down a procedure or specific grounds for removal.
  • The AG holds office during the pleasure of the President: the President may remove the incumbent at any time.
  • The AG may resign by tendering resignation to the President.
  • By convention, the AG offers resignation when the Council of Ministers resigns or is replaced, since the appointment is made on the advice of the government in office.

Remuneration

  • The Constitution does not fix the AG's pay. The AG receives such remuneration as the President determines.

Life of the AG's office

  • Appointed by the President: Qualified as an SC Judge (citizen; HC Judge 5 yrs, or HC advocate 10 yrs, or eminent jurist)
  • Serves:
    • During the pleasure of the President
    • No fixed term
  • Pay: As the President determines
  • Leaves: Removed at any time, resigns to the President, or by convention goes with the Council of Ministers
Life of the AG's office
  • Memory aid (Qualifications of the AG): Citizen, Judge of a High Court five years, Advocate of a High Court ten years, Eminent jurist: "Courts Judge All Experts".
  • Shortcut: Pleasure shortcut. No term, no removal procedure, no fixed pay: tenure and pay both rest with the President (AG) or the Governor (Advocate-General). Both go when the government goes (by convention).

3 · AG as principal legal adviser#

Core advisory responsibilities

  • Legal advice to the Union: the chief duty is to advise the Government of India on legal questions referred to the office by the President. This is the constitutional foundation of the office.
  • Other legal tasks assigned by the President: duties of a legal character, not an exhaustive list.
  • Constitutional and statutory functions: those conferred by the Constitution or by any law.

Litigation and representation for the Union

  • Supreme Court: appears on behalf of the Government of India in all matters in the Supreme Court in which the Union is a party or has a stake.
  • Presidential references (Article 143): represents the Government in matters referred by the President to the Supreme Court for its advisory opinion and explains the Union's legal stance.
  • High Courts: may appear in any High Court in cases where the Union is concerned, when required by the Government of India.
  • Memory aid (Three duties of the AG): Advise the Union, Other legal tasks from the President, Statutory and constitutional functions: "All Official Stuff".

Where the AG appears for the Union

  • Supreme Court: All matters where the Union is a party or has a stake
  • Art. 143 reference: Advances and explains the Union's stance in the advisory opinion
  • High Courts: Only when the Government of India requires it

Also in the handout

  • These are not limited by an exhaustive list and may include discrete legal assignments necessary for the Government’s functioning.
  • This means the office responds both to constitutional prescriptions and to statutory responsibilities where the law expressly contemplates the Attorney General’s involvement.

4 · Rights and parliamentary privileges#

  • Right of audience: entitled to appear and plead in all courts across India.
  • Parliament: may take part in proceedings of both Houses, joint sittings, and committees of Parliament of which he may be named a member (the later section says "invited"), but cannot vote.
  • Privileges and immunities of members of Parliament while performing parliamentary functions: this allows candid advice and free participation.
  • Article 88: Every Minister and the Attorney-General has the right to speak in, and otherwise take part in, the proceedings of either House, any joint sitting, and any committee of Parliament of which he may be named a member, but shall not by virtue of this article be entitled to vote.

Union side: Arts. 88 and 105

  • Article 88:
    • Right to speak and take part in both Houses, joint sittings and committees
    • No vote
  • Article 105: Same privileges and immunities as MPs while performing duties in Parliament
Handout alert

Handout alert: For committees the handout says both "of which he may be named a member" (the Article 88 text) and "to which he may be invited". For the exact Article wording use named a member.

5 · Limitations on the AG#

  • These limits prevent conflicts of interest and keep the office single-client when acting for the Union.

Five prohibitions

LimitRule
No adverse representationMust not advise or act against the Government of India in any matter
No dual briefsMust not advise or hold briefs in cases where he is required to advise or represent the Government
Criminal defenceMust not defend accused persons in criminal proceedings without prior permission of the Government of India
Commercial directorshipsNo directorship in companies or corporations without Government approval
Centralised referralMust not accept instructions directly from ministries, departments, statutory bodies or PSUs; references go through the Ministry of Law and Justice (Department of Legal Affairs)
  • Memory aid (The five limits): Adverse (never against the Government), Dual briefs, Criminal defence needs permission, Company directorships need approval, Routed through the Law Ministry: "All Documents Come Countersigned, Routed".

Status and practice

  • The AG is not a government servant and not a full-time salaried counsel.
  • Private practice is not barred, subject to the limits above
  • This lets eminent practitioners hold the post and gives government matters priority when required
  • The AG acts on the reference of the President or the Government
  • Rights and privileges apply only in that official capacity
  • Balance: Rights give authoritative representation and candid participation in Parliament. Limits protect public confidence and the integrity of government legal advice.

Also in the handout

  • Professional and Conflict-avoidance Constraints To prevent conflicts between public duty and private practise, and to ensure the Attorney General’s undivided loyalty to the Union when required, a set of prohibitions applies to the office-holder: - No Adverse Representation: The Attorney General must not advise or act against the Government of India in any matter.

6 · Solicitor General and key Articles for the AG#

  • To assist the AG there are the Solicitor General of India and Additional Solicitors General.
  • These are not constitutional offices
  • They support the AG in litigation and advisory work
  • Only the Attorney General has constitutional recognition under Article 76.

7 · Advocate-General: appointment and terms#

  • Article 165 provides for the Advocate-General (AdvG) of each State: the highest law officer of the State and legal adviser to the state government. It is the state counterpart of the Attorney General.

Appointment

  • The Governor appoints the Advocate-General.
  • The person must be qualified to be appointed a Judge of a High Court: an Indian citizen, who has held judicial office for at least 10 years, or been an advocate of a High Court for at least 10 years.
  • The appointment is made on the advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister, though formally exercised by the Governor.

Term of office

  • The Constitution fixes no tenure.
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the Governor: may be removed at any time, or resign by submitting resignation to the Governor.
  • By convention, resigns when the government resigns or changes, because the appointment rests on its advice. This keeps the state's legal adviser aligned with the political executive in power.

Remuneration

  • The Constitution does not prescribe salary or allowances.
  • Pay is determined by the Governor, which gives the state flexibility according to administrative arrangements and financial capacity.

Life of the Advocate-General's office

  • Appointed by the Governor: On the advice of the CM-led Council of Ministers
  • Qualified as an HC Judge:
    • Citizen
    • 10 years judicial office or 10 years as HC advocate
  • Serves: During the pleasure of the Governor
  • Leaves:
    • Removed or resigns to the Governor
    • By convention goes with the government

8 · Duties and functions of the Advocate-General#

  • He is the chief legal adviser and representative of the state government.
  • Advisory role: legal advice to the state government on matters referred by the Governor.
  • Assigned duties: other legal duties assigned by the Governor.
  • Constitutional and statutory functions: those conferred by the Constitution or any state law.
  • Court representation: entitled to appear in any court of law within the state, including High Courts and subordinate courts.
  • Legislative participation:
    • Right to speak and take part in the proceedings of both Houses of the state legislature (where there are two) and their committees
    • no right to vote
    • Enjoys all privileges and immunities of a member of the state legislature
  • Article 177: Every Minister and the Advocate-General has the right to speak in, and take part in the proceedings of, the Legislative Assembly (or both Houses where there is a Legislative Council), and any committee of the Legislature of which he may be named a member, but not to vote by virtue of this article.
  • Memory aid (Functions of the AdvG): Advise (on Governor's reference), Assigned duties, Constitutional and statutory functions, Court representation, Legislative participation: "All Advocates Cross-examine Courts, Legislators".

9 · Position and significance#

  • The AdvG is the bridge between law and governance in the state.
  • By advising the government and representing it in courts, he ensures executive actions are legally sound.
  • His presence in the legislature lets him clarify legal points in debate, aiding law-making.
  • The office upholds the rule of law: governance within the constitutional framework.
  • Why it matters: Executive action is legally checked at the state level without a separate veto: advice, representation and clarification in the House.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Solicitor General is not constitutional Only the Attorney General is recognised by Article 76. The Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General are created to help him. Rule: Art. 76 names only the AG; the other Union law officers are not in the Constitution.
Trap 2 · Supreme Court standard versus High Court standard The AG needs Supreme Court Judge qualifications (HC Judge 5 years, HC advocate 10 years, or eminent jurist). The Advocate-General needs High Court Judge qualifications: 10 years of judicial office or 10 years as HC advocate. Rule: the jurist route and the 5-year HC judge route belong to the AG only.
Trap 3 · Speak but never vote Arts. 88 and 177 let the law officer take part in the House and its committees but not vote. The handout words committees as 'named a member' in the Article text and 'invited' in one later line. Rule: for exact wording use 'named a member'; the answer on voting is always no.
Trap 4 · Not a government servant The AG is not a full-time salaried counsel and private practice is not barred, but he cannot advise or act against the Government of India. Rule: private practice allowed, subject to the five limits (adverse brief, dual brief, criminal defence, directorships, referral via Law Ministry).

Book edition: simple words and memory words#

In simple words

  • Article 76 creates the Attorney General (AG): the principal legal adviser to the Union Government and its highest law officer.
  • The President appoints the AG, who needs the Supreme Court Judge standard: a citizen who was an HC Judge for 5 years, or an HC advocate for 10 years, or an eminent jurist.
  • There is no fixed term, no removal procedure and no fixed pay: the AG serves during the pleasure of the President, and by convention goes when the Council of Ministers goes.
  • The AG may speak in both Houses, joint sittings and committees but cannot vote (Art. 88), has MPs' privileges (Art. 105) and a right of audience in all courts; five limits prevent conflict of interest.
  • The Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General only assist and are not constitutional posts. The state twin is the Advocate-General (Art. 165), appointed by the Governor on the High Court Judge standard (Arts. 177 and 194(2)).

Key terms

Pleasure of the President
The AG can be removed at any time or resign; no fixed term and no laid-down grounds
Right of audience
Right to appear and plead in all courts across India
Article 143 reference
President's request for the Supreme Court's advisory opinion; the AG represents the Government there
Article 88
Minister and AG may speak and take part in Houses, joint sittings and committees, but not vote
Article 105
Gives the AG the privileges and immunities of Members of Parliament

A non-constitutional law officer who assists the AG, along with Additional Solicitors General
Advocate-General
State's highest law officer under Art. 165, appointed by the Governor

Advocate-General may speak and take part in the State Legislature and its committees, but not vote
