0 · What the name means: Subsidiary, or Subordinate, Alliance#

1.1 Arthur Wellesley: a Royal Army officer, not an EIC officer

  • Arthur Wellesley was a military commander, but he was NOT an EIC officer. He was a commissioned officer in the British Royal Army, commanding the 33rd Regiment of Foot (that is, infantry).
  • He led his Royal regiment alongside EIC forces in the 4th Anglo-Mysore War (1798–99), including the Siege of Seringapatam (1799), and in the 2nd Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05), including the Battle of Assaye (1803).
  • He also fought Napoleon's army between 1808 and 1815.
Memory hook

Arthur's three marches.

"Ninety-nine, Tipu 82-99's town; Oh-three, Assaye's crown; Oh-eight, the Frenchman's frown."

Decode: 1799 = Siege of Seringapatam (4th Anglo-Mysore War 1798-99, Tipu 82-99 falls). 1803 = Battle of Assaye (2nd Anglo-Maratha War 1803-05). 1808 to 1815 = fights Napoleon's army. Regiment: 33rd Foot, a Royal (not EIC) unit.

Teacher's board

Written beside the Arthur row: "Br State helped EIC in India, e.g. 3rd CW 1756-63; therefore the EIC GOT HELP of the Br State in Empire BUILDING in India." The reasoning: Arthur is proof that the British State's own army fought the EIC's wars. This was not new in 1798: Royal help goes back to the 3rd Carnatic War (1756-63). So the empire was built by the Company and the British State together, not by a private company alone.

1.3 Arthur's own verdict on the Marathas

  • Arthur Wellesley on record stated that the Marathas were the most formidable enemy he ever faced, and that the Battle of Assaye (1803) against them was the toughest fight of his entire military career. The Marathas were harder to defeat militarily.
Remember

The handout's conclusion: had India not been a fragmented polity, that is, had there been one State ruling the whole of India, India would not have been colonised. This is a ready Mains line on why the EIC won.

  • Wellesley 98-05 re-initiated conquest by overhyping the threat of a Napoleon and Tipu 82-99 alliance.
  • Napoleon had attacked Egypt in 1798 with the goal of controlling the Middle East and hurting British trade, and he was in communication with Tipu 82-99 of Mysore.
  • A threat existed, but not a real one. Britain's navy was superior to France's, and a French fleet heading for India through the Arabian Sea would have been destroyed before arriving.

  • But the British State agreed anyway, because protecting India from European rivals was the primary goal of British foreign policy and it did not want to take any risks.

3.1 Wellesley 98-05's step: from a device to a policy

  • Wellesley 98-05 took the next step. He formalised the relationship in a written treaty, took over the Indian State's Foreign Policy explicitly, and disbanded its army.
  • The Subsidiary Alliance was NOT a Wellesley 98-05 invention. BUT the Policy of Subsidiary Alliance was initiated by him.
  • Why "policy": a policy means a pre-meditated, formalised plan of action with a pre-decided intent, which is reflected in a series of events executed in line with that policy.
  • Wellesley 98-05 formalised the SA into a policy, added the takeover of Foreign Policy and the disbanding of the Indian State's army, and implemented this policy State by State across India.
Remember

Invention versus policy. The idea is older: Dupleix 42-54 (Hyderabad, 1751) and Clive 57-67 (Awadh, ToA 1765). The policy is Wellesley 98-05's: a written treaty, Foreign Policy taken over, the army disbanded, applied State by State. If a statement says "Wellesley 98-05 invented the Subsidiary Alliance", it is wrong; if it says he "initiated the Policy of Subsidiary Alliance", it is right.

4 · The model treaty of the Subsidiary Alliance and its legal logic#

The standard terms of any Subsidiary Alliance fell into two groups, though the actual terms varied a little from State to State, depending on context.

4.0 The teacher's board: how the SA splits sovereignty

Teacher's board

The teacher starts from the word Sovereignty and separates Symbolic Sovereignty (who is called king) from the "power to do" (the three circled letters L, E, J; the board does not expand them here). The power to do is then cut in two. External Sovereign Functions (D, C, FA: Defence, Communications, Foreign Affairs) are "[Taken by EIC]". Domestic Sovereign Functions (Home, Finance, Transport, Agri, Health etc.) stay with the "[Indian king]".

Under Def he writes the heart of the SA: "EIC will maintain the Army BUT TAKES money = SUBSIDY: Def BD [Decided by EIC]." That is: the subsidy is simply the Indian State's defence budget (Def BD), but the size of that budget is decided by the EIC, not by the ruler who pays it.

Memory hook

EIC keeps the "DC-FA" cupboard; the king keeps the home shelf.

Decode: External functions taken by the EIC = D-C-FA = Defence, Communications, Foreign Affairs. Domestic functions with the Indian king = Home, Finance, Transport, Agri, Health (and symbolic sovereignty). Subsidy = Def BD, size decided by the EIC.

Why it sticks: "outside the house" (external) goes to the foreigner; "inside the house" (domestic) stays with the king.

Remember

The chapter comes full circle on money. High war expenditure produced the ban on conquest in Pitt's India Act 1784. Wellesley 98-05 got round the ban with the Napoleon threat and the Subsidiary Alliance. And high war expenditure is what finally got Wellesley 98-05 recalled.

Recall sheet#

Read once, hide it, then rebuild the chapter from memory.

  1. Subordinate (Subsidiary) Alliance: an alliance is between equals (Treaty of Amritsar 1809, Sikh and EIC); "subordinate" means one party's rights are less respected (1798, EIC above Hyderabad).
  2. Three brothers: Richard Wellesley 98-05 (GG of Bengal 1798-1805, empire builder, Fort William College Calcutta 1800); Henry (administration and diplomacy of EIC); Arthur (Royal Army, 33rd Regiment of Foot, NOT an EIC officer; Seringapatam 1799, Assaye 1803, Napoleon 1808-15).
  3. Two European armies from the 3rd Carnatic War 1756-63 till 1858: Company's Army (paid by EIC, mostly Indian sepoys, British senior officers) and King's Army (Royal regiments, cost reimbursed by EIC). The British State cannot escape responsibility.
  4. Arthur's verdict: Marathas the most formidable enemy, Assaye 1803 the toughest fight; a fragmented polity is why India was colonised.
  5. Pitt's India Act 1784 banned conquest: no offensive war without the Court of Directors or the Board of Control; defence allowed. Reason: high war expenditure. Board chain: GBF 1770, EIC bankrupt by 1772, loan plus Regulating Act 1773, more wars, PIA 1784.
  6. Wellesley 98-05 overhyped the Napoleon and Tipu 82-99 alliance (Egypt 1798). Threat was there but not real: British Navy superior. British State agreed anyway. Middle path = SA = control without annexation.
  7. Two roots: Dupleix 42-54 at Hyderabad 1751 (Salabat Jung 51-62, Bussy, Northern Sarkars); Clive 57-67's ToA 1765 at Awadh (mini SA: army not disbanded, Foreign Policy not taken). Wellesley 98-05: not the inventor, but the initiator of the policy.
  8. Board on sovereignty: external functions (Defence, Communications, Foreign Affairs) taken by the EIC; domestic functions and symbolic sovereignty with the Indian king. Subsidy = defence budget decided by the EIC.
  9. Model treaty: EIC gets 5 (Resident, military alliance, army disbanded, annual subsidy, Foreign Policy); ruler gets 2 (protection, non-interference in succession, Diwani, Nizamat minus military). Legal logic: Foreign Policy gives the right to deploy the army.
  10. Formula: PoSA = PoIR (informally) + PoRF + EIC military replacing the State's military + annual subsidy + loss of Foreign Policy. Subsidy is NOT a fixed income for the Company (Prelims 2018 and 2021).
  11. Seven links: unemployment; EIC's free army; Drain of Wealth; tax hikes and peasant revolts; maladministration charge (Awadh 1856); territory in lieu of cash (Awadh 1801, Berar 1853); loss of alliance-forming capacity. Bali analogy: military power shift.
  12. Five reasons rulers signed willingly: internal revolts, external threats, better than annexation, royal dignity, succession disputes and neighbours.
  13. Ten SAs 1798-1818: Hyderabad 1798, Mysore 1799, Awadh 1801, Gaikwad June 1802 (Cambay), Peshwa Bajirao II 96-18 Dec 1802 (Bassein), Scindia 1803, Jats-Rajputs-Bundelas 1803-05 (group), Travancore 1805; then Bhonsle 1816 (Nagpur) and Holkar 1818 (Mandsaur) under Francis Hastings 13-23.
  14. Direct annexations: Mysore territory 1799, half of Awadh 1801 (Ceded Districts, NWP 1836), Carnatic 1801, Orissa from Bhonsle and Gujarat plus north of the Yamuna including Delhi from Scindia (1803-05). Recalled for high war expenditure.
