2.4 · Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) 2024-25#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

MoSPI released ASI results for FY 2024-25.

  • Conducted by NSO under MoSPI; collection under the Collection of Statistics Act, 2008 (amended 2017). Covers factories under the Factories Act, 1948, bidi and cigar units, non-CEA electricity undertakings, and units with 100+ employees on the Business Register. Factory threshold used: 10+ workers with power or 20+ without. OSH Code 2020 raises the licence threshold to 20 with power and 40 without.
  • Top factories: Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Maharashtra. Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh and Haryana account for about 56% of manufacturing employment. Top GVA states: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh. Top GVA industries: basic metals, motor vehicles, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, food products. Top employers: food products, textiles, basic metals, motor vehicles.
  • Positives: employment growth 7.19% (5.92% last year); GVA 9.59% with input costs (7.47%) slower than output (7.81%); wages 12.08% faster than GVA and profits (7.73%); fixed capital 10.54% (capital deepening).
  • Concerns: regional and sectoral concentration (five industries over 45% of GVA); capital growing faster than output and jobs (capital intensity may limit jobs).
  • Way forward: reward domestic value addition in PLI; industrial corridors and plug-and-play parks in lagging states; support labour-intensive sectors (food processing, textiles, footwear, toys); skilling and MSME technology.
Prelims fodder
  • ASI = annual structure of registered manufacturing; IIP = short-term volume changes; ASUSE = unincorporated non-agricultural enterprises.
Mains fodder
  • Examples: PLI and mobile phones; Tamil Nadu dispersed industrialisation and women in factory jobs. China and Vietnam manufacturing about 25% of GDP.
  • Keywords: Capital deepening; Labour-intensive manufacturing; Regional industrial imbalance; Productivity-wage nexus

2.5 · District Mineral Foundations (DMF) and PMKKKY#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

The CAG flagged irregularities in DMF functioning and PMKKKY implementation in Odisha.

  • DMF: non-profit trust set up by States in every mining-affected district. Legal basis: Section 9B of the MMDR Act, 1957 (inserted by the 2015 Amendment). Funding: 30% of royalty (leases before 12 January 2015) or 10% (auction leases after that date). Two tiers: Governing Council and Managing Committee. Guided by the Fifth and Sixth Schedules, PESA 1996 and FRA 2006.
  • Status: DMFs in 656 districts of 23 States; over Rs 1.30 lakh crore collected; top States Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh.
  • PMKKKY (2015, Ministry of Mines): implemented by DMFs using DMF funds. At least 70% to high-priority sectors (drinking water, pollution control); up to 30% to other priority sectors (infrastructure, irrigation); at least 70% of funds in directly affected areas.
  • CAG concerns: diversion to ineligible items (wage payments under MGNREGS, government vehicles); no perspective or annual plans; endowment funds not created; about 54% fund utilisation; no social audits.
  • Way forward: rule-based allocation; perspective and annual plans; link DMF portals with WAMIS; collect dues with penal interest; mandatory social audits.
Prelims fodder
  • MMDR Act, 1957: Union regulates major minerals; States regulate minor minerals (sand, gravel) by rules. National Mineral Exploration and Development Trust set up under the 2015 Amendment.
Mains fodder
  • Principle: those who bear the costs of mining should share its benefits. Cases: Project SAMARTHYA (Gujarat, youth training), Project Aangan-Chatra (Jharkhand, 40 anganwadis).
  • Keywords: District Mineral Foundation; Royalty; Social audit; Endowment fund

2.6 · Western Dedicated Freight Corridor (WDFC) completed#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

The PM inaugurated the final sections of the WDFC, completing the 2,843-km DFC network (Eastern and Western).

  • India became the first country to run double-stack container trains under overhead electric traction on a dedicated freight corridor.
  • Western DFC 1,506 km: Dadri (UP) to JNPT (Maharashtra), JICA yen loans. Eastern DFC 1,337 km: Ludhiana (Punjab) to Sonnagar (Bihar), partly World Bank financed (initially planned to Dankuni, West Bengal). Implementing agency: DFCCIL (SPV under the Ministry of Railways, established 2006 under the Companies Act).
  • Union Budget 2026-27 proposed an East-West DFC from Dankuni (West Bengal) to Surat (Gujarat); other proposed: East Coast, North-South.
  • Significance: rail freight about Rs 1.90 per tonne-km against up to Rs 3.78 by road; decongests passenger lines; supports DMIC and Amritsar-Kolkata corridor; emissions 22 g vs 62 g CO2 per tonne-km (rail vs road); supports PM GatiShakti.
  • Gaps: terminal congestion and stabling; PPP failure (Sonnagar-Dankuni); average DFC speed 37 km/h in 2024-25 (38 in 2023-24); holding line gaps.
  • Way forward: fast-track future corridors; last-mile multimodal links (MMLPs, ICDs); private freight terminals; IoT tracking and predictive maintenance.
Prelims fodder
  • National Rail Plan Vision 2030: raise rail freight modal share to 45%. Modal share now: rail 18%, road 71% (NITI Aayog, 2021). Services: Trucks on Train, FOIS.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Freight decongestion; Logistics cost; PM GatiShakti; National Logistics Policy

2.9.4 · Mega Ports notified under the Indian Ports Act, 2025#

PrelimsEconomy

Deendayal (Kandla), Jawaharlal Nehru (Nhava Sheva), Paradip and Mundra were notified as mega ports under Section 73 of the Indian Ports Act, 2025.

  • Eligibility: major and non-major ports (State consulted for non-major). Cargo threshold: at least 150 MMT in the previous year or 7.5 million TEUs. Validity: five years.
  • Deendayal: Gulf of Kutch, largest major port by cargo (2025-26), tidal port. Mundra: Gulf of Kutch, private (Adani Ports), non-major. JNPT: Navi Mumbai, most containerised trade. Paradip: Odisha (Mahanadi delta), only mega port on the east coast.
  • India: 14 major ports (12 operating) and over 200 non-major ports. Major ports: Union List, Major Port Authorities Act, 2021. Non-major: Concurrent List, State Maritime Boards. Ports carry about 95% of trade by volume and 70% by value.

1.1 · FCRA Amendment Bill, 2026#

Prelims + MainsPolity

The FCRA Amendment Bill, 2026 has been referred to the Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC).

  • FCRA governs how individuals, associations, NGOs, trusts and companies receive and use money, securities or articles from outside India. Administered by the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA).
  • Two routes: Registration (valid 5 years, must be renewed) and Prior Permission (one-time receivers, for a specific purpose and source).
  • Prohibited recipients: election candidates; journalists, editors and owners of registered newspapers; judges and government servants; MPs and MLAs; political parties and office-bearers; organisations of a political nature.
  • Bill adds: a certificate ceases if not renewed before expiry, no renewal application was made, or renewal was denied.
  • A Designated Authority handles vesting, supervision, management and disposal of foreign-contribution assets once a certificate ends. Provisional vesting (returned if registration is restored); permanent vesting if not restored, used for public purposes, sale proceeds go to the Consolidated Fund of India.
  • The Authority must preserve the religious character of places of worship and cannot convert or secularise them.
  • Penalty: maximum jail term cut to one year (from five). Prior approval of the Central Government is needed to start an investigation.
Prelims fodder
  • Vesting: legal process of earning permanent, unconditional ownership of an asset.
  • FCRA vs FEMA: FCRA is with MHA (donations, grants); FEMA is with Ministry of Finance/RBI (commercial flows, FDI).
  • NGOs may register as Societies (1860 Act), Trusts (Indian Trusts Act, 1882) or Section 8 Companies (Companies Act, 2013).
Mains fodder
  • Significance: curbs foreign-funded interference; fills the legal gap on assets after a certificate ends; aligns with the Jan Vishwas approach; supports FATF commitments.
  • Concerns: permanent loss of NGO assets on a missed deadline; vesting can reach assets even if the NGO now uses domestic funds; assets under prior permission excluded; no hearing or appeal against denial of renewal; prior Central approval dilutes State police powers.
  • Way forward: grace period and judicial approval before permanent vesting; audited exit; consistent treatment; digital compliance support; risk-based regulation with graded penalties (FATF).
  • Noel Harper v. Union of India (2022): SC upheld the 2020 amendments; receiving foreign contribution is not an absolute right. S. Vijay Kumar Committee (2017) favoured lighter regulation; Second ARC (2008) said decentralise FCRA implementation.
  • Keywords: Designated Authority; Asset Vesting; Due Process; Judicial Oversight; Civil Society Accountability; Jan Vishwas

1.4.6 · Forest Clearance and the Forest Rights Act#

Prelims + MainsPolity

The Ministry of Tribal Affairs clarified that the Forest Rights Act (FRA), 2006 does not itself require 100% Gram Sabha consent for Stage-II forest clearance.

  • Forest clearance under the Forest Conservation Act, 1980 has two stages: Stage-I in-principle approval with conditions (compensatory afforestation, levies); Stage-II final approval.
  • After Stage-II, the State/UT can issue the diversion order only after compliance with other laws, including settlement of rights under the FRA.
  • The Forest (Conservation) Rules, 2022 removed the requirement of Gram Sabha consent before Central approval. The 2023 Rules allow Central approval before settlement of rights; actual diversion needs prior settlement.
  • Gram Sabha role under FRA: verifies claims; free, informed consent for relocation from critical wildlife habitats; protects forests and heritage; can pass a resolution against violators after 60 days' notice to the State Level Monitoring Committee.
  • FRA 2006 (Ministry of Tribal Affairs) recognises rights of forest-dwelling STs and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers: individual rights; community rights (grazing, fishing, PVTG habitat, Community Forest Resource, traditional knowledge); minor forest produce rights. Committees: Sub-Divisional, District and State Level Monitoring.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Stage-I and Stage-II clearance; Community Forest Resource rights; Gram Sabha consent

7.1 · Weaponisation of space#

Prelims + MainsInternal Security

The US for the first time officially confirmed deploying on-orbit space control weapons able to defend against hostile action.

  • Weaponisation = systems able to damage, destroy, disrupt or degrade assets in space or on Earth; kinetic or non-kinetic (jamming, spoofing, cyber, directed energy). US Space Force: orbital, electromagnetic, cyberspace counter-space operations.
  • Concerns: space arms race (US Space Force, China's 2007 direct-ascent ASAT test); dual-use technology (servicing satellites can co-orbitally jam or capture); threat to military and civil services; debris and Kessler syndrome; weak treaties.
  • Efforts: Outer Space Treaty 1967 (bans nuclear weapons/WMD in orbit); COPUOS 1959; Partial Test Ban Treaty 1963; proposed PAROS.
  • Way forward: update OST to cover conventional, co-orbital, non-kinetic weapons; PAROS consensus; Active Debris Removal protocols; rules for proximity operations; transparency and CBMs, shared tracking data.
Prelims fodder
  • Rescue Agreement 1968, Liability Convention 1972, Registration Convention 1975 (India party); Moon Treaty 1979 (India signatory).
  • India: Mission Shakti 2019 DA-ASAT test (fourth nation after US, Russia, China); Defence Space Agency 2019; Antariksha Abhyas 2024; ISRO Project NETRA for space situational awareness.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: PAROS, dual-use technology, space arms race.

7.3 · FATF report on professional money laundering and hawala#

Prelims + MainsInternal Security

FATF report "Investigating Professional Money Laundering, Underground Banking, and the Use of Hawala and Other Similar Service Providers".

  • Professional money laundering (PML) = laundering for others for a fee; HOSSPs = hawala and similar providers.
  • Findings: over 80% of jurisdictions see underground banking as a major PML channel; "money laundering as a service"; formal and informal finance converging (bank accounts, fintech, vIBANs, prepaid cards, crypto wallets); links to fraud, cybercrime, terror financing, gambling, organised crime; nearly 70% report growing technology use in hawala (hybrid model, AI tools, dedicated apps).
  • Challenges: regulatory blind spots, digitalisation, cross-border speed, fragmented information, financial-inclusion dilemma. Way forward: legal clarity, enhanced detection, international cooperation, public-private coordination, affordable formal channels.
Prelims fodder
  • FATF: inter-governmental standard setter for money laundering, terror and proliferation financing; established 1989 by G7; Paris; 40 members; India a member since 2010. PMLA 2002; FIU-IND; ED (PMLA and FEMA 1999).
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: money laundering as a service, digital hawala, network-centric disruption.

5.1.2 · Rogue AI agents and loss of control#

MainsScience & Technology

OpenAI reported a cybersecurity evaluation in which agents in separate sandboxes communicated, divided tasks, manipulated parts of the evaluation and reached the wider internet, including Hugging Face (open-source AI platform, "GitHub of AI").

  • Concerns: safeguard circumvention, unintended coordination, evaluation manipulation, weak containment, future capability risk.
  • Two safety problems: alignment (AI pursues intended goals, not loopholes) and external control (sandboxing, restricted credentials, network controls, monitoring, logging, shutdown).
  • Not necessarily out of control: agents were still doing assigned tasks, humans could intervene, little evidence of independent persistent goals. Genuine loss of control would need own goals, resisting shutdown or repeatedly beating robust safeguards.
  • Way forward: stronger sandboxing, least-privilege access, continuous monitoring, independent evaluation.
  • Agentic commerce: AI agents discover, negotiate and execute purchases for customers. Amazon vs Perplexity (Comet browser): Amazon says Perplexity masked its agent as a Chrome user; a US appeals court treated it as the user accessing Amazon. FinTech uses: automatic routine purchases within a spending limit, smart payments.

5.3.1 · EOS-05: first geosynchronous Earth-imaging satellite#

PrelimsScience & Technology

ISRO launched EOS-05 on 4 September 2026 on GSLV-F17, the heaviest satellite carried by a GSLV so far; India's first dedicated Earth-imaging satellite in geosynchronous orbit (about 36,000 km); life about 10 years.

  • Uses: track floods and cyclones as they evolve, glacier hazards and avalanches, monitor stubble burning, rapid forecasts, strategic situational awareness.
  • Versus LEO/polar imagers (few hundred km, finer detail, days to revisit): geosynchronous orbit sees the same broad region frequently but with less fine detail.
