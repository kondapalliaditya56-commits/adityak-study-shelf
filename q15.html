2000PYQ 2Prelims 2000

The last major extension of British Indian territory took place during the time of:

Answer: (A)

Why (a) is correct: Dufferin84-88 added Upper Burma in January 1886 after the 3rd Anglo-Burmese War 1885–86. This was the last major territorial extension of British India before independence. You will find this in 'Dufferin's Upper Burma (1886)'.

Why the other options are wrong :

  • (b) Dalhousie48-56 was the big annexation-era GG (Punjab 1849, Awadh 1856, Doctrine of Lapse) — but his last major extension was Awadh 1856. Dufferin came later (1886) and added Upper Burma.
  • (c) Lytton76-80 carried forward the Forward Policy (see 'Forward Policy in Afghanistan: Ellenborough → Lytton') but did NOT make territorial extensions.
  • (d) Curzon99-05 came later; his Partition of Bengal 1905 was administrative reorganisation, NOT territorial extension.

Nuance on Burma: Dufferin84-88 added Upper Burma to British India & Burma was ruled as a province of British India until 1937. Burma is not traditionally part of India — hence if we are talking about India then the answer is Dalhousie48-56, but if we are talking about the political entity (territory of the British State in India) then the answer is Dufferin84-88. Such questions are weird. Legally the answer should be Dufferin84-88 because the PYQ mentions British India. But the famous answer is Dalhousie48-56 — so the UPSC answer key may say Dalhousie48-56.

Memory hook: *Dufferin = 1886 = Upper Burma = last major extension.*

1998PYQ 6Wellesley98-05 + Press Regulation Act 1799

(C) ✓ — A is true but R is false. Wellesley98-05 DID impose the Press Regulation Act (also called Censorship Act) in 1799 — A is correct. Answer: (D)

Why (d) is correct : The Charter Act of 1833 did not provide that an Indian was to be appointed as a law member in the Governor-General Council. The other provisions are. It redesignated the Governor-General of Bengal as the Governor-General of India. Under this provision Lord William Bentinck became the first Governor-General of India. However, this provision was negated after opposition from the court of directors who continued to hold the privilege of appointing company officials.   • (b) Was provided — the Act re-designated the GG of Bengal as the GG of India (Bentinck became the first GG of India).
  • (c) Was provided — all law-making powers were centralised in the GG-in-Council under the Act.

1999PYQ 13Imperial Preference

The term "Imperial Preference" was applied to the:

Answer: (A)

Why (a) is correct: Imperial Preference was a commercial-tariff arrangement — constituent units of the British Empire (Britain, the Dominions and India) granted one another preferential lower tariff rates denied to non-Empire competitors. Formalised at the Ottawa Conference of 1932. The operational effect on India: British goods entered Indian ports at lower tariff than German, Japanese or American competitors. This is option (a) verbatim. You will find this in 'Imperial Preference'.

Why the other options are wrong :

  • (b) Racial discrimination — wrong. Racial discrimination was a separate phenomenon (covered in 'Racism + Authoritarianism (Post 1857)' — Racism + Authoritarianism Post 1857), not the same as Imperial Preference.
  • (c) Subordination of Indian interest in general — wrong. This is the general description of colonialism, not the specific definition of Imperial Preference. The specific definition is the tariff arrangement.
  • (d) Preference given to British political agents over Indian Princes — wrong. This is Political Department protocol (a separate administrative-ceremonial matter), NOT Imperial Preference.

Memory hook: *Imperial Preference = tariff = British imports get special treatment in India.*

2014PYQ 1 Foreign Ideologies Motivating India's Anti-Colonial StruggleMains 2014 · 10 marks · 150 words

"What were the major political, economic and social developments in the world which motivated the anti-colonial struggle in India?"

Demand of the question: The question asks for major political, economic and social developments in the world that motivated India's anti-colonial struggle — not domestic Indian developments. The directive verb is implicit ("What were..."), calling for an enumeration with explanation across the 3 categories named in the prompt — political + economic + social. The trap: students who focus on Indian developments alone miss the prompt's world-developments anchor.

Suggested intro (~25 words): India's anti-colonial struggle was motivated by major political, economic and social developments worldwide — Enlightenment-era revolutions, deindustrialisation under empire, Russian Revolution, and the post-WWI principle of self-determination.

Guiding themes (pick 4–5 for a 150-word answer):

1. Political — Pre-1900 Revolutionary Currents — American Revolution (1765–83) + French Revolution 1789 + European revolutions 1830 + 1848 + Irish Home Rule (1870–1914) and Irish armed struggle (1916–21) all challenged imperial superiority and inspired colonised peoples.

2. Economic — Industrial Revolution Deindustrialisation — destruction of Indian handloom + deindustrialisation fed Drain Theory of Dadabhai Naoroji and R.C. Dutt.

3. Social — Russian Revolution 1917 — gave the Indian left a peasant-worker mobilisation model and an anti-imperial vocabulary.

4. Social — Wilson's Self-Determination 1918 — post-WWI Fourteen Points + rising anti-racism made continued British rule indefensible in international forums.

5. Returning Indian leaders brought currents home — Gandhi from South Africa (1893–1915) + Nehru from Cambridge — Indian-diaspora struggles + Western liberal-democratic theory absorbed and applied in India.

Suggested conclusion (~25 words): The major political, economic and social world developments — from American/French Revolutions through Russian Revolution to Wilson's self-determination — together motivated India's anti-colonial struggle as part of a worldwide conversation about freedom.
