1996PYQ 1(Prelims 1996)

Consider the following statements about Jawaharlal Nehru:
1. He was the President of the Congress Party in 1947.
2. He presided over the Constituent Assembly.
3. He formed the first Congress Ministry in United Province before India's Independence.
Of these statements

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — All three fail. The INC President in 1947 was J.B. Kripalani (Azad held the post till 1946); the Constituent Assembly was presided over by Dr Rajendra Prasad; the first Congress Ministry in the United Provinces was formed by Govind Ballabh Pant. Nehru held none of the three roles, hence 'none correct'.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Each statement is contradicted: Kripalani (1947 President), Rajendra Prasad (Constituent Assembly), Pant (first UP ministry).

(B) ✗ — Keeps 1 and 3, but Kripalani was the 1947 President and Pant formed the first UP ministry.

(C) ✗ — Keeps 1 and 2, but Kripalani was the 1947 President and Rajendra Prasad presided over the Constituent Assembly.

1996PYQ 2(Prelims 1996)

Which one of the following first mooted the idea of a constituent assembly to frame a Constitution for India?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Among the options, the Swaraj Party (resolution of May 1934) was the first political party to demand a Constituent Assembly. M.N. Roy floated the idea first in 1934, but as an individual; since no individual is an option, the Swaraj Party is keyed. INC later rejected GOI 1935 entirely and demanded an Assembly elected by Universal Adult Franchise.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — INC pressed the demand, but the first party to make it was the Swaraj Party (1934); 1936 is too late.

(C) ✗ — No Constituent Assembly proposal of 1942 is credited to the Muslim League; its recorded stance on GOI 1935 was fear of a Hindu-majority Centre.

(D) ✗ — The All Parties Conference's document was the Nehru Report (August 1928), not a 1946 Constituent Assembly proposal.

2000PYQ 4(Prelims 2000)

Which one of the following is not a feature of the Government of India Act of 1935?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — GOI 1935 proposed dyarchy at the Centre but abolished it in the provinces. A Council of Ministers headed by a Premier (Chief Minister) was made responsible to the PLA. Dyarchy 'at the Centre as well as in the provinces' was never the 1935 scheme. (Dyarchy in the provinces belonged to GOI 1919.)

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — A bicameral Centre (Federal Assembly plus Council of States) is a recorded feature.

(C) ✗ — Provincial autonomy is the Act's signature working feature: all provincial subjects became transferred under popular ministers.

(D) ✗ — An All-India Federation of British India and the Princely States was provided for, though it never came into operation.

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Jawaharlal Nehru was President of the AISPC, the organisation of the Princely States' people (first session Dec 1927, Bombay). The Congress-ministries years (1937-39) are exactly when this States' people's movement surged, with new Praja Mandals set up in more States.

Wrong options:

(D) ✗ — Patel's recorded chair of this era is the Karachi INC Session of 1931.

2002PYQ 6(Prelims 2002)

The real intention of the British to include the princely states in the Federal Union proposed by the India Act of 1935 was to

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — One-third of Federal Assembly members were to be nominees of the Princes, not elected. This practically eliminated the chance of an INC majority in the FA, the more powerful lower house. The Princes were brought in precisely as a counter-weight to the nationalists.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Direct control was not the design: Paramountcy stayed as it was, and a State entered only by voluntarily signing the Instrument of Accession.

(B) ✗ — The Princes' role was to send nominees to the FA, a legislative counterweight to INC, not to administer the colony.

(C) ✗ — A complete take-over is ruled out by the Butler Committee (1927) promise not to change the status of the States without the Princes' consent; teacher's board: Art. 370-like, domestic affairs untouched.

Hook: 1/3 of the FA held for Princes' nominees = a built-in counter-weight to INC.

2004PYQ 7(Prelims 2004)

2005PYQ 10(Prelims 2005)

Consider the following statements. The Government of India Act, 1935 provided for:
1. the provincial autonomy.
2. the establishment of Federal court.
3. All India Federation at the centre.
Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — All three are provisions: provincial autonomy (all provincial subjects became transferred subjects), a Federal Court (actually set up in 1937) and a Federation of British India and the Princely States. The Federation failed to materialise only because the Princes never signed the required Instruments of Accession.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Drops 3, but the All India Federation WAS provided for; it failed only because the Princes never signed.

(B) ✗ — Drops 1, but provincial autonomy is the Act's signature achievement.

(C) ✗ — Drops 2, but the Federal Court is a recorded provision, set up in 1937, ancestor of the Supreme Court.

2018PYQ 12(Prelims 2018)

GOI Act 1935, residuary powers were given to:

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — The Viceroy retained residuary powers, veto powers, ordinance powers, the power to restore cuts in grants and the power to certify rejected bills. Residuary powers under GOI 1935 vested in the Governor-General (Viceroy). (Teacher's board: under the COI 1950, residuary power lies with Parliament.)

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The Federal Legislature got no residuary powers; 80% of the central Budget was not even votable by it.

(C) ✗ — Provinces got ownership of the Provincial-list subjects only.

(D) ✗ — Governors held veto, ordinance and take-over powers within provinces, but the residuary category belonged to the Governor-General.

2021PYQ 14(Prelims 2021)

Consider the following statements.
1. The Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms of 1919 recommended granting voting rights to all the women above the age of 21.
2. The Government of India Act of 1935 gave women reserved seats in legislature.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Statement 2 holds: GOI 1935 gave women reserved seats through 'internal carve-outs' inside each communal quota. Statement 1 fails: even under GOI 1935 only about 10% of the population qualified, and a woman's vote was largely derived from her husband's income or property.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Reverses the record: statement 1 is the false one.

(C) ✗ — Statement 1 fails: no all-women-above-21 franchise existed.

(D) ✗ — Statement 2 stands: reservation for women (and labour) was provided.

Hook: Reserved seats yes; universal women's franchise no - only 10% of the population voted.

2024PYQ 15(Prelims 2024)

With reference to the Government of India Act, 1935:
1. Provided for All India Federation.
2. Defence/Foreign Affairs under federal legislature.

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Statement 1 holds: India was to become a Union of British India and the Princely States. Statement 2 fails: Defence and External Affairs were among the four Reserved subjects under the absolute Legislative and Executive control of the Viceroy, not the federal legislature (teacher: you cannot seek accountability on Reserved subjects).

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — Exactly backwards: the Federation was provided for, while Defence and Foreign Affairs were Reserved to the Viceroy.

Hook: Federation on paper; Defence and External Affairs Reserved to the Viceroy.

FYQ 3

With reference to the Three Round Table Conferences (1930-32), which one of the following statements is correct?

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — At the 1st RTC (1930) Princes and non-INC parties took part and the Princes demanded a federation, a union combining British India and the Princely States; INC boycotted it. Only the 2nd RTC had INC, and it rejected Purna Swaraj and responsible government at the Centre. INC boycotted the 3rd, which was practically irrelevant.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — INC boycotted the 1st and 3rd RTCs; only the 2nd had INC.

(B) ✗ — Purna Swaraj and responsible government at the Centre were rejected at the 2nd RTC.

(C) ✗ — INC boycotted the 3rd RTC; participation was so low it was practically irrelevant.

FYQ 4

Under the federal scheme of the GOI Act 1935, the minimum threshold of Princely States that had to sign the Instrument of Accession for the Federation to come into effect was:

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — Section 6 of GOI 1935 required at least 50% of the Princes to sign the IoA. The threshold was never met, so the Federation never came into effect and GOI 1919 machinery kept running at the Centre.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The threshold was 50%, not one-third.

(B) ✗ — The threshold was 50%, not two-thirds.

(C) ✗ — The threshold was 50%, not three-fourths.

(D) ✓ — A State would transfer only Defence, Foreign Affairs and Communications; Finance was NOT one of them. Teacher: structurally identical to how J&K signed only an IoA in 1947 (the Art. 370 legacy); the rest, domestic affairs, stayed with the Prince.

FYQ 7

FYQ 9

Wrong options:

Wrong options:

With reference to the impact of the Congress Ministries of 1937-39, consider the following statements:
1. The Ministries energised people in the Princely States through new and expanded Praja Mandals (State People's Congresses).
2. The Narendra Mandal or Chamber of Princes was set up in 1921 as a popular response from below to the Princely States question.
3. INC gained crucial administrative and governance experience during this 28-month period.
Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Statement 2 is wrong: the Narendra Mandal (Chamber of Princes, 1921) was an advisory body of the Princely States to the British, the British response from ABOVE; the Congress ministries energised the popular response from BELOW. Statements 1 and 3 hold.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Retains false statement 2 and drops 3.

(B) ✗ — Retains false statement 2 and drops 1.

(D) ✗ — Includes false statement 2.

Memory hook: Praja Mandals from below (1937-39); Chamber of Princes from above (1921).

FYQ 1 The Federation proposed under GOI 1935 was the most ambitious constitutional construction of British rule in India - yet it never came into effect. Examine why. (15 marks, 250 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine why it never came into effect' requires tracing both the structural design flaw and the contingent failures.

Suggested intro (~25 words): GOI 1935 proposed a federal structure linking British India provinces with Princely States - an unprecedented constitutional architecture. By 1939, it had collapsed without ever operating.

1. The structural prerequisite: Section 6 required at least 50% of the Princes to sign the Instrument of Accession; the British made accession voluntary, anticipating gradual accession (Butler Committee 1927 promise: no change in the States' status without the Princes' consent). FLAG: the companion says '50% of population and 50% of seats'; the handout says 'at least 50% of Princes' - handout followed.

2. The Princely refusal: four fears - Paramountcy not abolished (two bosses: Union Government and British Crown); financial autonomy (GOI could compel Defence Budget contributions); smaller States' fear of low representation at the Centre; fear of democracy (INC movements inside the States). The IoA would leave an Art. 370-like status (only Defence, Foreign Affairs, Communications transferred), yet the threshold was never met. [Companion adds: fewer than 14 small States had acceded - not in the handout.]

3. The Congress critique: INC rejected the whole Act and demanded a Constituent Assembly on Universal Adult Franchise; 1/3 FA seats for Princes' nominees and indirect FA elections were designed to eliminate an INC majority at the Centre. [Companion adds Nehru's 'feudal autocrats' remark - not in the handout.]

4. WWII and the closure: the war began in September 1939 and the Federation work was shelved; the 1919 machinery kept ruling the Centre while only the provincial half ran from 1937. [Companion only.]

Closing: The Federation failed because the Princes held a veto over their own inclusion and used it; voluntary federation between feudal and democratic units cannot work, a lesson that shaped the 1947 integration approach (Standstill plus Instrument of Accession). Revise: Centre features, provincial autonomy in operation, result.

FYQ 2 The Congress Ministries (1937-39) under GOI 1935 were the first taste of self-government for Indian leaders. Examine what they achieved and why they resigned in October 1939. (10 marks, 150 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine what achieved and why resigned' demands both halves.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Congress Ministries governed 8 of 11 provinces from April 1937 to October 1939 (28 months) - the first sustained Indian-led government experience. They resigned over WWII.

1. Achievements: morale and organisation of cadre, peasants and workers boosted (peasant and working-class movements strengthened); crucial administrative and governance experience; Princely States' people inspired - new and expanded Praja Mandals (State People's Congresses). [Companion only: tenancy reforms, mass education, prohibition in Madras and Bombay, labour welfare, release of political prisoners; ministers Patel, Rajagopalachari, B.G. Kher.]

2. The constraints: Governors retained veto, ordinance and take-over powers (ancestor of Art. 356), 40% of the provincial Budget was non-votable, and the Federation never came into effect, so the Centre stayed under the Viceroy with GOI 1919 machinery.

3. The resignation (October 1939): [Companion only, handout covers this in Ch 43] Linlithgow declared India a co-belligerent in WWII (September 1939) without consulting Congress provinces; Congress demanded a clear post-war independence commitment, was refused, and all its Ministries resigned in October-November 1939.

Closing: The Ministries proved Indian capacity for self-government but also showed that genuine self-rule needed an end to the Viceroy's reserve powers; the resignation set the stage for Quit India 1942. Revise: Provincial autonomy and Congress Ministries.

PYQ 1 Mains PYQ (Guiding Theme)It would have been difficult for the Constituent Assembly to complete its historic task of drafting the Constitution for Independent India in just three years but for the experience gained with the Government of India Act, 1935. Discuss.

FLAG: the companion gives only the question for this Mains PYQ; the answer below is built from the handout (teacher's lists and comparisons) and the finished chapter notes.

Demand of the question: 'Discuss' - weigh how far the 1935 Act supplied ready institutional material and experience to the Assembly, and note where the Constitution broke from it.

Suggested intro: The Constituent Assembly did not write on a blank slate; the 1935 Act, the longest and most detailed constitutional document the British gave India, supplied the architecture, vocabulary and a trained political class.

1. Structure: Federal, Concurrent and Provincial lists (ancestor of the 7th Schedule); a federal Centre with a bicameral legislature; provincial autonomy with a Premier responsible to the PLA, the model of provincial parliamentary government.

2. Institutions: Federal Court, set up 1937, ancestor of the Supreme Court; the Governor's take-over power as ancestor of Art. 356; Reserved subjects and tribal areas as link to the 5th Schedule; Art. 370-like IoA logic of 1935 (Defence, Foreign Affairs, Communications) resurfaced in 1947.

3. Experience: Congress ministries governed 8 of 11 provinces for 28 months (1937-39), giving INC administrative and governance experience invaluable after 1947; SC reserved seats in elected seats under joint electorates anticipated later safeguards.

4. Where the Constitution broke away: INC had rejected the Act and demanded a Constituent Assembly elected by Universal Adult Franchise (only about 10% voted in 1935); no Viceroy's reserve powers, 80% non-votable Budget or communal electorates; residuary power moved from Governor-General to Parliament.

Closing: The Assembly borrowed the Act's machinery but replaced its spirit; borrowed experience, not authority, let it finish in about three years.
