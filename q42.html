1996PYQ 1(Prelims 1996)

Consider the following statements about Jawaharlal Nehru: 1. He was the President of the Congress Party in 1947. 2. He presided over the Constituent Assembly. 3. He formed the first Congress Ministry in United Province before India's Independence. Of these statements

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Claims all three statements; each one is contradicted by the record — Kripalani (1947 President), Rajendra Prasad (Constituent Assembly chair), Pant (first UP Congress Ministry).

(B) ✗ — Keeps statements 1 and 3, but the 1947 INC President was J.B. Kripalani and the first UP Congress Ministry was Govind Ballabh Pant's — neither role was Nehru's.

(C) ✗ — Keeps statements 1 and 2, but Kripalani was the 1947 President and Dr Rajendra Prasad presided over the Constituent Assembly. (Note: Maulana Abul Kalam Azad was the President till 1946 and in 1947 J.B Kripiani was President. Dr. Rajendra Prasad presided over Constituent Assembly. 2000PYQ 4(Prelims 2000)

Which one of the following in not a feature of the Government of India Act of 1935?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Statement (a) is the non-feature: GOI1935 proposed dyarchy at the Centre but abolished it in the provinces — Dyarchy ended. A Council of Ministers headed by a Premier (Chief Minister) was made responsible to the PLA. Dyarchy 'at the Centre as well as in the provinces' was never the 1935 scheme.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — A bicameral legislature IS a recorded feature — the Federal Assembly plus the Council of States — so it cannot be the 'not a feature' pick.

(C) ✗ — Provincial autonomy is the Act's signature working feature: all subjects in provinces became transferred subjects — under PLAs & popular ministers. A real feature cannot be the answer.

(D) ✗ — An All-India Federation of British India and the Princely States was provided for — it never came into operation, but it was very much a feature of the Act. (Note: Dyarchy was introduced at centre but not in the provinces. Dyarchy was introduced in the provinces through Govt. of India act, 1919 but it was abolished by Government of India Act, 1935.)

2002PYQ 6(Prelims 2002)

The real intention of the British to include the princely states in the Federal Union proposed by the India Act of 1935 was to

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — One-third of FA members were to be Nominees of the Princes — not elected. This practically eliminated the chances of an INC majority in the FA, which was the more powerful lower house. The Princes were brought in precisely to counter-balance the nationalist leaders.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Direct control was not the design: Paramountcy stayed as it was, and a Princely State entered the Federation only by voluntarily signing the Instrument of Accession — a 50% threshold that was never met.

(B) ✗ — The Princes' role in the scheme was to send nominees to the Federal Assembly — a legislative counterweight to INC, not active administrators of the colony.

(C) ✗ — A complete take-over is contradicted by the record: the Butler Committee had promised the British would not change the status of the Princely States without the consent of the Princes — accession was consensual, not confiscatory. (Note: The princely states remained loyal to the British authorities till independence so Brtish included the princely states in the federal union proposed by the India Act of 1935 to use the princes to counter the nationalists.)

Answer: (B)

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Undercounts: statement 2 is also true — the Governor retained veto power over bills and the power to legislate by ordinance.

(C) ✗ — Includes statement 3, which is false — communal representation was continued and even expanded, not abolished — and wrongly drops statement 1 (dyarchy did end in the provinces).

(D) ✗ — Fails on statement 3: separate electorates for all minorities continued as under GOI1919, with reservation extended to new groups. (Note: The communal representation persisted in the Government of India Act of 1935.

Statements 1 and 2 are correct.)

FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words The Federation proposed under GOI 1935 was the most ambitious constitutional construction of British rule in India — yet it never came into effect. Examine why.

Demand of the question: "Examine why it never came into effect" requires tracing both the structural design flaw & the contingent failures.

Suggested intro (~25 words): GOI 1935 proposed a federal structure linking British India provinces with Princely States — an unprecedented constitutional architecture. By 1939, it had collapsed without ever operating. Body guidance — 4 themes:

1. The structural prerequisite (see 'Features at the Centre — The Part That Never Happened'). Federation required Princely States representing at least 50% of total population & at least 50% of available seats in the Federal Legislature to accede. The British made this voluntary, anticipating gradual accession.

2. The Princely refusal (see 'Features at the Centre — The Part That Never Happened'). Most Princes saw the Federation framework as a threat to their internal sovereignty — the Federal Legislature could legislate on matters they had previously decided autonomously. By 1939, fewer than 14 small Princely States had acceded; the population threshold was nowhere near met.

3. The Congress critique (see 'Features at the Provinces — The Part That Did Work (from 1937)'). Congress rejected Federation on different grounds — it gave Princely States disproportionate representation in the Federal Legislature (more seats per population than democratically elected British India provinces). Nehru called the Princes "feudal autocrats" being given veto over democratic India.

4. WWII & the closure (see 'Features at the Centre — The Part That Never Happened'). September 1939 — WWII began. Britain quietly shelved Federation work. The provincial autonomy provisions continued operating, but the Centre stayed under the GOI 1919 framework (modified) for the rest of British rule. Federation was de facto cancelled in 1939, de jure never revived.

Closing verdict: GOI 1935's Federation failed not because it was poorly designed, but because the constituents the British wanted to include (Princes) had veto power over their own inclusion & refused to use it. The principle of voluntary accession ensured an inability to ever assemble a federation. The lesson — that voluntary federation between feudal & democratic units cannot work — shaped the 1947 integration approach (Patel + Menon's Standstill + Instrument of Accession framework).

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words The Congress Ministries (1937-39) under GOI 1935 were the first taste of self-government for Indian leaders. Examine what they achieved & why they resigned in October 1939.

Demand of the question: "Examine what achieved & why resigned" demands both halves.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Congress Ministries governed 8 of 11 provinces from April 1937 to October 1939 — the first sustained Indian-led government experience. They resigned over WWII. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Features at the Provinces — The Part That Did Work (from 1937)'):

1. Achievements. Legislative reforms: tenancy reforms favouring peasants, mass education expansion, prohibition policies in Madras & Bombay, labour welfare measures, release of political prisoners. Administrative experience: Congress cadres learned governance — Patel, Rajagopalachari, B.G. Kher emerged as ministerial leaders.

2. The Constraints. Governors retained reserve powers (could veto, dissolve, appoint ministers under exceptional circumstances). The Federation never came into effect at the Centre — so Congress provinces operated without a federal Indian government above them; the Centre stayed under the Governor-General.

3. The Resignation (October 1939). Viceroy Linlithgow declared India a co-belligerent in WWII (September 1939) WITHOUT consulting Congress provinces. Congress demanded a clear post-war commitment to independence — refused. All Congress Ministries resigned in protest October-November 1939. The British declared "President's Rule" via Governors.

Closing verdict: The Ministries proved Indian capacity for self-government — but also revealed that genuine self-rule required ending the Viceroy's reserve powers entirely. The 1939 resignation set the stage for Quit India 1942.

Revise: 'Provincial Autonomy + Congress Ministries'.
