1998PYQ 1(Prelims 1998)

What is the correct sequence of the following events?
1. The August Offer.
2. The I.N.A. trial.
3. The Quit India Movement.
4. The Royal Indian Naval Ratings Revolt.
Select the correct answer using the codes below:

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — August Offer 1940; Quit India Movement August 1942; INA trial November 1945; Naval Ratings Revolt February 1946. With the stem's numbering the order reads 1, 3, 2, 4.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Puts the INA trial (Nov 1945) before Quit India (1942): impossible.

(B) ✗ — Starts with Quit India (1942) before the August Offer (1940): the Offer came first.

(D) ✗ — Fails twice: Quit India cannot precede the 1940 Offer, and the Feb 1946 Naval Revolt cannot precede the Nov 1945 INA trial.

Memory hook: 1940 Offer, 1942 Quit India, 1945 INA trial, 1946 Naval Revolt.

1999PYQ 2(Prelims 1999)

Assertion [A]: Lord Linlithgow described the August Movement of 1942 as the most serious rebellion since the Sepoy Mutiny.
Reason [R]: There was massive upsurge of the peasantry in certain areas.
Codes:

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Both are true and R explains A. Rich and poor peasants both took part, and their anger was aimed at the British, with no violence against zamindars or moneylenders. Peasants were the soul of the QIM, which is why Linlithgow rated it the most serious rebellion since 1857.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — R is precisely why Linlithgow rated it the most serious rebellion since 1857, so R does explain A.

(C) ✗ — R is not false: peasants of eastern UP, Bihar, Midnapore and Satara drove the movement.

(D) ✗ — A is not false: Linlithgow's 'most serious rebellion since the Sepoy Mutiny' remark is recorded.

Memory hook: Peasants were the soul of the QIM.

Chapter-derived Mains practice with the demand, guiding themes and a conclusion.

FYQ 1 The Quit India Movement (1942-45) was the most radical departure of the INM from constitutional politics. Examine its causes, course & long-term significance. (15 marks, 250 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine' + 'causes/course/long-term' = comprehensive treatment.

Suggested intro (~25 words): QIM was not just another mass movement; it was the INM's first declaration that British rule was illegitimate by definition, not merely reformable.

1. Causes: cumulative failure of negotiation (Linlithgow 1939, August Offer 1940, Cripps 1942); war-time British weakness (fall of Singapore, loss of Burma, Japanese threat); inflation, food shortage, repression; time pressure as Japan advanced.

2. Course: Wardha CWC (July 1942); Bombay AICC resolution 8 Aug 1942; Gandhi's 'Do or Die'; mass arrests from 9 Aug; underground leadership (JP, Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta); parallel governments (Ballia, Midnapore, Satara); railway and telegraph sabotage. Largely leaderless at the top but locally improvised.

3. British response: legal tools (Revolutionary Movement Ordinance, Defence of India Rules), press gagged, aircraft-mounted machine guns, collective fines, villages burnt, 60,000 arrested by end 1942; mass phase crushed in about six weeks.

4. Long-term significance: INC's mass legitimacy; administrative ungovernability; pressure on post-war Britain towards the Cabinet Mission 1946; context for the INA agitations and Naval Mutiny.

Closing: QIM was crushed but changed the British calculation; by 1945 the question was no longer whether to transfer power but to whom and on what terms.

Conflict flags (handout wins): the companion's '~10,000 killed' and 'Operation Zero Hour' are not in the handout and are dropped; the handout figure is 60,000 arrested by end 1942, and 'crushed by mid-1943' is replaced by the handout's 'six weeks' for the mass phase. Talcher is in the companion only; omit or use sparingly.

FYQ 2 The Parallel Governments formed during the Quit India Movement (1942-44) are a unique feature of Indian nationalism. Examine their character & limits. (10 marks, 150 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine character & limits' demands both halves.

Suggested intro (~25 words): During QIM, local people's administrations arose at Ballia (UP), Midnapore (Bengal) and Satara (Maharashtra), each claiming legitimate authority in place of the British.

1. Character: Ballia (Aug 1942, Chittu Pandey) overthrew the Collector and freed INC leaders. Jatiya Sarkar, Midnapore (Dec 1942-Sep 1944): arbitration courts, school grants, rice for the poor, cyclone relief, armed wing Vidyut Vahini. Patri Sarkar, Satara (mid-1943-1945): people's courts, village libraries, prohibition, Gandhi Marriages.

2. Significance: showed the practical possibility of local self-government and trained nationalist cadres; authority could be substituted locally, not just protested.

3. Limits: confined to specific districts, not cities; Ballia dissolved when British forces returned; they were episodic rather than sustained alternatives.

Closing: laboratory experiments in self-rule, proving administrative feasibility but not able to withstand full repression; their legacy is cadre experience.

Conflict flags (handout wins): the companion says 'Tamluk / Tamralipta Sarkar' and 'Satara under Y.B. Chavan + Nana Patil' and 'crushed within weeks'; the handout names the Midnapore body the Jatiya Sarkar (Tamluk-Midnapore = Tamralipta is the same body), says Nana Patil set up the Patri Sarkar with Achyut Patwardhan's guidance (Chavan led only till his arrest at end 1942; the board says Achyut then took over the guiding role), and the Patri Sarkar ran 1943-45, not weeks. Companion's Talcher is not in the handout.

FYQ 3 FYQ (teacher's board)What was the utility of fasts in Gandhi's politics? (teacher's board question)

Demand of the question: explain what Gandhi's fasts achieved as a method; give a theme per use, with dates.

Suggested intro: Gandhi used fasts as an instrument of moral pressure, from Ahmedabad 1918 to Aga Khan Palace 1943.

1. Arbitration and moral pressure: forced arbitration (Ahmedabad 1918, Poona Pact 1932).

2. Awareness: the 1932-34 fasts against untouchability; international awareness in 1943; a method of protest.

3. Courage to the people (1943) and unity: the 1932-34 fasts united upper and lower castes; the 1943 fast also made even QIM opponents demand his release.

4. Opposing communal violence.

Closing: fasts converted personal suffering into political leverage and moral unity.

Source note: from the teacher's board (L41e p-136) and Sec 3.4 'Impact of the Fast' (mass upsurge, Viceroy's Council members resigned, international media attention, unity); not in the companion.

Past UPSC Mains questions with an answer skeleton.

PYQ 1 Mains (guiding theme)What were the events that led to the Quit India Movement? Point out its results.

Demand of the question: two halves: 'events that led to' (causes and trigger) and 'results' (what it achieved). Give both a fair share.

Suggested intro: With Cripps's failure in 1942, the last hope of a negotiated settlement went, and Gandhi's Bombay resolution of 8 August 1942 began the INM's most radical mass phase.

1. Events leading up: Cripps Mission failure (March 1942); war-time inflation and shortages; the British abandoning Burma and Malaya to Japan; Gandhi's reasoning that a high-spirited nationalist people would resist Japan better than a demoralised one.

2. Launch: CWC at Wardha (July 1942); special INC session at Bombay, resolution passed 8 August 1942 with the demand for immediate Purna Swaraj; 'Do or Die' at Gowalia Tank; entire top leadership arrested on 9 August.

3. Results: India proved ungovernable by force; loyalty of the administrative machinery broke; parallel governments (Ballia, Midnapore, Satara) showed Indians could self-govern; international attention on British repression; unity of Indians behind Gandhi's release.

Closing: The movement did not bring immediate independence, but it produced a psychological shift in India and in the British mind that made the end of colonial rule inevitable.

Source note: the companion gives only the question and a pointer to the Sections; this skeleton is from the teacher's board and the chapter (Sections 2, 6).
