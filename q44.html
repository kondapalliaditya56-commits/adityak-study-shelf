(B) ✗ — Code 3, 1, 2, 4 starts with Quit India (1942) before the August Offer of 1940 — the Offer came first.

(D) ✗ — Code 3, 1, 4, 2 fails twice: Quit India (1942) cannot precede the 1940 August Offer, and the Naval Revolt of February 1946 cannot precede the INA trial of November 1945. (Note: 1. The August offer 1940

2000PYQ 3(Prelims 2000)

Assertion (A): Lord Linlithgow described the August Movement of 1942 as the most serious revolt after the Sepoy Mutiny.Reason (R): There was a massive upsurge of the peasantry in certain areas.

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A. Linlithgow did make this comparison in correspondence to Churchill; the August 1942 Quit India Movement saw a massive peasant upsurge in Bihar, eastern UP, Bengal and coastal Andhra (parallel governments at Tamluk, Satara, Balia) — making it the most serious revolt since 1857.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — R is the explanation: it was the peasant upsurge — parallel governments at Tamluk, Satara and Ballia included — that made the 1942 revolt look like 1857 to Linlithgow.

(C) ✗ — R is not false: the peasantry's massive upsurge is recorded — "Peasants were the soul of the QIM(1942-45)".

(D) ✗ — A is not false: Linlithgow made exactly this comparison in correspondence with Churchill (see the ✓ entry above). (Note: Due to the involvement of peasants. the Quit India Movement became one of the biggest and most wide spread movement, thus led Linlithgow (1936-44) to call this movement most serious revolt after Sepoy Mutiny of 1857.)

2005PYQ 4(Prelims 2005)

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Per MG's Gowalia Tank speech: government servants were told Do not resign. Wrong options:

(Note: Mahatma Gandhi did not ask government servants to resign, but to show their allegiance towards Congress. Soldiers were not asked to leave their post but to not fire on their compaions.)

2009PYQ 5(Prelims 2009)

With which one of the following movements is the slogan 'Do or Die' associated?

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — at the Gowalia Tank meeting on 8 August 1942, MG gave his historic speech — including his famous call 'Do or Die' (Karo ya Maro). The slogan belongs to the Quit India Movement.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The Swadeshi Movement (1905–11) belongs to the boycott-Swadeshi era — decades before the Do-or-Die call of 8 August 1942.

(B) ✗ — The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) was built on withdrawal of cooperation; the Do-or-Die call is dated 8 August 1942.

(C) ✗ — CDM's signature act was the salt satyagraha of 1930; 'Do or Die' came twelve years later, at the QIM launch. (Note: On 8th August, 1942, the Congress in its meeting at Bombay passed a resolution known as 'Quit India' resolution, whereby Gandhiji asked the British to leave India as soon as possible and gave a call for 'Do or Die'.)

Chapter-derived Mains practice with the demand, guiding themes and a conclusion.

FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words The Quit India Movement (1942-45) was the most radical departure of the INM from constitutional politics. Examine its causes, course & long-term significance.

Demand of the question: "Examine" + "causes/course/long-term" = comprehensive treatment.

Suggested intro (~25 words): QIM was not just another mass movement — it was the INM's first declaration that British rule was illegitimate by definition, not merely reformable. Body guidance — 4 themes:

1. Causes (see 'Reasons for Launching the QIM(1942-45)'). Cumulative failure of negotiation: Linlithgow 1939 + August Offer 1940 + Cripps 1942. War-time British weakness: Singapore fall, Burma loss, Japanese threat. Inflation, food shortage, repression. Time pressure: Japanese advance meant Britain might collapse before INC could act.

2. Course (see 'Events During the QIM(1942-45) (1942)'). Bombay AICC resolution 8 Aug 1942 + Gandhi's "Do or Die" + Operation Zero Hour mass arrests + underground leadership (Jayaprakash Narayan, Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta) + Parallel Governments (Tamluk, Satara, Ballia, Talcher) + railway/telegraph sabotage. Largely leaderless, locally improvised within broad QIM framework.

3. British Response (see 'Events During the QIM(1942-45) (1942)'). Brutal repression: ~10,000 killed, ~60,000+ arrested, public floggings, machine-gunning of crowds. The movement was crushed militarily by mid-1943 but never declared formally suspended.

4. Long-term Significance (see 'Results and Significance of the QIM(1942-45)'). Established INC's mass legitimacy as the only viable INM voice. Demonstrated administrative ungovernability — Britain could not rule a hostile India indefinitely. Forced post-war Labour government to accelerate transfer-of-power negotiations (Cabinet Mission 1946). Provided ideological context for INA's parallel armed struggle.

Closing verdict: QIM did not "win" in the immediate sense — it was crushed. But it changed the British calculation. By 1945, the question was no longer "should we transfer power" but "to whom & on what terms". The strategic victory was structural — Britain accepted that continued rule was untenable, even before Japan was defeated.

Revise: 'Causes' + 'Events During the QIM(1942-45) (1942)' (Events During the QIM(1942-45) (1942))-4 (Events + Spontaneity Debate) + 'How Did the QIM(1942-45) End?'-6 (End + Significance).

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words The Parallel Governments formed during the Quit India Movement (1942-44) are a unique feature of Indian nationalism. Examine their character & limits.

Demand of the question: "Examine character & limits" demands both halves.

Suggested intro (~25 words): During QIM, four major Parallel Governments operated — Tamluk (Bengal), Satara (Maharashtra), Ballia (UP), Talcher (Odisha) — each a local non-British administration claiming legitimate authority. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Events During the QIM(1942-45) (1942)'):

1. Their Character. Local citizens organized their own taxation, justice, education, & welfare systems in defiance of British administration. Tamralipta Sarkar (Tamluk, Midnapore) lasted ~21 months — collected revenue, ran schools, judicial panchayats. Satara's "Prati Sarkar" under Y.B. Chavan + Nana Patil operated similarly.

2. Their Significance. Demonstrated the practical possibility of post-British self-government at the local level. Provided training in administrative self-rule for nationalist cadres. Showed the British that authority could be locally substituted, not just symbolically protested.

3. Their Limits. Geographically restricted to specific districts; could not function in cities (Bombay, Calcutta). Required British military weakness — once the British returned with reinforcement, each Parallel Government was crushed within weeks. They were episodic, not sustained alternatives to British rule.

Closing verdict: Parallel Governments were laboratory experiments in self-rule — proving administrative feasibility but unable to sustain against full British military repression. Their legacy lies in cadre experience, not in territorial achievement.

Revise: 'Events — Parallel Governments'.
