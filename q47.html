1995PYQ 1(Prelims 1995)

The word Adivasi was used for the first time to refer to the tribal people by

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Thakkar Bappa (Amrit Vithaldas Thakkar), a Servants of India Society member (founded by Gokhale, 1914) who worked in remote tribal areas, gave currency to the word 'Adivasi'. Outside the handout text; the companion key is followed.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Gandhi's coinage was 'Harijan' for the Depressed Classes, not 'Adivasi'.

(C) ✗ — No Adivasi coinage is recorded for Phule.

(D) ✗ — Ambedkar's vocabulary was Depressed Classes and Scheduled Castes (AIDCC 1930, AISCF 1942).

Answer: (C)

Codes

2002PYQ 5(Prelims 2002)

Codes

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Both are true and R explains A. CONFLICT: the companion dates this label to CPI 1925; the handout dates the line to the 6th Comintern Congress 1928. Handout followed.

(C) ✗ — R is not false; the communist leadership did call the INC bourgeois and reactionary.

(D) ✗ — A is not false; labour participation stayed limited.

Chapter-derived Mains practice with the demand, guiding themes and a conclusion.

FYQ 1 The INM, while presenting itself as a national movement, marginalised the voices of workers, capitalists, Dalits & women in different ways. Examine. (15 marks, 250 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine' + 'marginalised in different ways' demands tracing how each group was excluded from the INC's core agenda.

Suggested intro (~25 words): The INC's mass strategy was built on coalition-management; to hold it together it subordinated class, caste and gender questions to the national-political one.

1. Workers: tactically supported (AITUC 1920, Lajpat Rai) but never central to the INC economic platform; Gandhi's Trusteeship rejected class struggle; N.M. Joshi and later CPI autonomy tolerated, not integrated.

2. Capitalists: funded the INC (FICCI 1927, Bombay Plan 1944) but were marginalised in its public ideology; Gandhi's anti-industrial framing sat uneasily with them.

3. Dalits: Mahad 1927, Round Table advocacy and the 1932 Communal Award protest showed the INC could not accommodate them as autonomous actors; Poona Pact left reserved seats in the Hindu electorate as second-best; Ambedkar's 1956 conversion expressed his judgment.

4. Women: AIWC 1927 led; INC mobilised women (1930 salt march, 1942 QIM) but did not promote them to organisational or parliamentary leadership (Sarala Debi: 'lawbreakers not lawmakers').

Closing: 'Many Voices' was strategic absorption, not genuine representation; the post-1947 state inherited the incomplete integration.

Revise: Working Class + Capitalists + Dalits + Women.

FYQ 2 Examine the historical significance of the Ambedkar-Gandhi confrontation over the Communal Award (1932) for the politics of Dalit representation. (10 marks, 150 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine the significance' demands the structural meaning of the conflict.

Suggested intro (~25 words): The confrontation was not just about electorates; it was about whether Dalits could be politically autonomous from the Hindu fold.

1. The Award's framing: Communal Award (August 1932, Ramsay MacDonald) gave Depressed Classes separate electorates; Ambedkar's position was Dalits as an autonomous political community.

2. Gandhi's fast unto death: opposed separate electorates as a permanent split of the Hindu fold; Yerwada fast forced the Poona Pact (24 September 1932), reserved seats in joint electorates; Ambedkar agreed under moral pressure, not conviction.

3. The long shadow: Dalit representatives needed Hindu votes to win; Ambedkar's later ILP (1936), AISCF (1942) and 1956 Buddhist conversion rejected Hindu-mediated representation.

Closing: Gandhi won the immediate constitutional battle; Ambedkar's long-term diagnosis has shaped Indian politics since.

Revise: Voice of Dalits - Ambedkar + Poona Pact.

Past UPSC Mains questions with an answer skeleton.

PYQ 1 Mains (theme)Many voices had strengthened and enriched the nationalist movement during the Gandhian phase. Elaborate.

Demand of the question: 'Elaborate' asks for a detailed account of each constituency and how it added to the movement.

Suggested intro: The Gandhian phase widened the INC from an elite body into a coalition of workers, capital, Dalits, women and peasants.

1. Workers: AITUC, Ahmedabad, TLA (1920).

2. Capital: funds and boycott (FICCI, Birla, Bajaj).

3. Dalits: Harijan campaign, Poona Pact.

4. Women: sisterhood, charkha, picketing.

5. Peasants: CDM, AIKS, Karachi 1931.

Closing: the coalition gave the movement mass reach, though each group's own demands stayed partly unmet.

PYQ 2 Mains (theme)Discuss the role of women in the freedom struggle especially during the Gandhian phase.

Demand of the question: 'Discuss' asks for role plus limits.

Suggested intro: Gandhi's call turned women from reform subjects into participants.

1. Rowlatt call, NCM, Khilafat (Bi Amman).

2. CDM: salt march, picketing; Surya Sen group.

3. Organisations: AIWC 1927.

4. Limits: no organisational or parliamentary leadership (Sarala Debi: 'lawbreakers not lawmakers').

Closing: participation without power; the Constitution of 1950 gave political equality.

PYQ 3 Mains (theme)Mahatma Gandhi and Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, despite having divergent approaches and strategies, had a common goal of amelioration of the downtrodden. Elucidate.

Demand of the question: 'Elucidate' asks to explain the shared goal and the differences.

Suggested intro: Both sought dignity for the downtrodden but by different routes.

1. Common goal: dignity, end of untouchability.

2. Gandhi: varna ideal, penance, Harijan campaign, change of heart.

3. Ambedkar: annihilation of caste, separate electorates, reservation, law.

4. Meeting point: Poona Pact 1932; Article 17.

Closing: the synthesis lies in the Constitution.

PYQ 4 Mains (theme)Defying the barriers of age, gender, and religion, the Indian women became the torch-bearer during the struggle for freedom in India. Discuss.

Demand of the question: 'Discuss' asks for examples across age, gender and religion barriers.

Suggested intro: Women from varied backgrounds crossed social limits to join the struggle.

1. Religion: Bi Amman in the Khilafat movement.

2. Gender and class: Basanti Devi.

3. Armed and military roles: Rani Jhansi Regiment.

4. Peasant struggles: women in Tebhaga.

Closing: sacrifice was repeatedly demanded of women, yet power did not follow.
