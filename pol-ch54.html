1 · Introduction: the birth of a constitutional nation#

The making of the Constitution was a revolutionary act of self-determination, not merely a legal exercise. For nearly two centuries India had been governed by laws of the British Parliament, designed to rule subjects, not to empower citizens.

  • The Constituent Assembly was a body of representatives composed for the specific purpose of drafting the Constitution of India.
  • Formed in November 1946 under the Cabinet Mission Plan.
  • It served as the first Parliament of independent India and facilitated the transfer of sovereign power from British to Indian hands.
  • Its task: to stitch a diverse, fragmented subcontinent into a single, unified nation under the "Rule of Law", born amidst the turmoil of partition and the optimism of Independence.
Big idea

The formation of the Assembly in 1946 marked the transition from colonial rule to a sovereign democratic republic.

2 · Evolution of the demand (1934 to 1942)#

The idea of a Constituent Assembly was not sudden but the result of a gradual demand.

Steps of the demand

1934
M.N. Roy (pioneer of the Communist movement in India) put the idea forward for the first time
1935
The Indian National Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly
1938
Nehru's Declaration on behalf of the INC (sovereignty and democracy)
1940
August Offer by Viceroy Lord Linlithgow
1942
Cripps Mission (failed)
1946
Cabinet Mission Plan; Assembly formed in November 1946

1938: Nehru's Declaration

Jawaharlal Nehru added two non-negotiable conditions:

  • Adult franchise: the Assembly must be elected by the people (democratic legitimacy).
  • No outside interference: the British Parliament would have no say in the drafting (sovereignty).

1940: The August Offer

  • Context: World War II had begun (1939) and Britain desperately needed Indian cooperation; Viceroy Lord Linlithgow made the offer to win over Indian political parties.
  • The "principle" accepted: for the first time the British explicitly acknowledged the right of Indians to frame their own Constitution.
  • The catch: the new constitution would be framed "mainly" (not "solely") by Indians, implying continued British oversight.

1942: The Cripps Mission

  • Proposal: Sir Stafford Cripps (member of the British War Cabinet) proposed an elected body after the war to frame a constitution. It promised Dominion Status with the right to secede from the Commonwealth.
Why the Cripps Mission failed
WhoObjection
Opt-out clauseProvinces could reject the new constitution and form separate unions; the INC saw a blueprint for the Balkanization of India
Mahatma GandhiCalled it "a post-dated cheque on a crashing bank" (declining power of the British Empire in the war)
Muslim LeagueRejected it because it did not explicitly grant a sovereign Pakistan with two distinct Constituent Assemblies
Six steps of the demand
RINACC

Roy 1934, INC 1935, Nehru 1938, August Offer 1940, Cripps 1942, Cabinet Mission 1946: "Real Indians Never Accept Cheap Cheques."

Mainly vs solely

August Offer says "mainly" by Indians = British still watching. Cripps = opt-out for provinces = the post-dated cheque.

3 · The Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946#

The Mission rejected the demand for two assemblies and offered a compromise.

  • Members: Lord Pethick Lawrence (Secretary of State for India), Sir Stafford Cripps, and A.V. Alexander.
  • Rejection of two assemblies: the Mission studied the Muslim League's demand for a separate Pakistan and two Constituent Assemblies but rejected it, arguing it would not solve the communal minority problem.
  • The scheme (the compromise): to satisfy the Muslim League, a Grouping System.
Grouping System
GroupProvincesExamples
Group AHindu-majority provincesMadras, Bombay, UP
Group BMuslim-majority provinces in the North-WestPunjab, Sindh
Group CMuslim-majority provinces in the North-EastBengal, Assam
  • Outcome: both the INC and the Muslim League accepted the plan initially, though with different interpretations of the "Grouping" clause.
  • This agreement allowed elections in July to August 1946, leading to the formation of the Constituent Assembly in November 1946.
Mission members
PCA

Pethick Lawrence, Cripps, Alexander: "Please Call All."

Groups

A = Hindu-majority (Madras, Bombay, UP). B = Punjab, Sindh (North-West). C = Bengal, Assam (North-East). Letters run A, B, C in that order.

4 · Composition of the Assembly#

The Assembly was partly elected and partly nominated.

  • Elected members: indirectly elected by the members of the Provincial Legislative Assemblies, using Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote.
  • Nominated members: representatives of Princely States were nominated by their heads.
Seat allocation (total strength 389)
ComponentBefore PartitionAfter Partition
British India296229
Princely States9370
Total389299
  • The 296 British India seats were allocated among Muslims, Sikhs and General categories in proportion to population: 292 from eleven governors' provinces and 4 from the four Chief Commissioners' provinces.
  • The strength fell to 299 after Partition and the withdrawal of Muslim League members (from Pakistani territories).
Check the sums

292 + 4 = 296 and 296 + 93 = 389. After Partition 229 + 70 = 299.

Elected

  • Indirectly, by Provincial Legislative Assemblies
  • PR by STV
  • British India seats

Nominated

  • Representatives of Princely States
  • nominated by their heads
  • 93 seats at first
Numbers

389 = 296 + 93, then 299 = 229 + 70. British India lost 67, the States lost 23 (as the handout's figures give).

5 · Key timeline of the making#

The process took 2 years, 11 months and 17 days.

Timeline in the handout's order
DateEvent
9 Dec 1946First meeting (boycotted by the Muslim League); Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha temporary President
11 Dec 1946Dr. Rajendra Prasad elected President; H.C. Mookerjee and V.T. Krishnamachari Vice-Presidents; B.N. Rau appointed Constitutional Legal Advisor
13 Dec 1946Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution
22 Jan 1947Objectives Resolution unanimously adopted
22 July 1947National Flag adopted
Aug 1947Independence Act: Assembly became fully sovereign and also the Dominion Legislature (first Parliament)
Feb 1948Drafting Committee (formed 29 Aug 1947) published the first draft
26 Nov 1949Constitution passed and adopted
24 Jan 1950Final session
26 Jan 1950Commencement of the Constitution

After August 1947: two hats

  • As Constituent body: chaired by Dr. Rajendra Prasad.
  • As Legislative body (Dominion Legislature): chaired by G.V. Mavlankar.

26 Nov 1949 and 24 Jan 1950

  • 26 Nov 1949: citizenship, elections and provisional parliament provisions came into force immediately.
  • 24 Jan 1950 (final session): the Constitution was signed; the National Anthem and National Song were adopted; Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected the first President of India.
  • 26 Jan 1950: the date was chosen to commemorate the Purna Swaraj declaration of 1930.

6 · The Objectives Resolution#

The roadmap and guiding principles for framing the Constitution.

Basics
ItemDetail
Moved byPt. Jawaharlal Nehru
Introduced13 December 1946
Adopted22 January 1947 (unanimously)
SignificanceRoadmap and guiding principles; its modified version later became the Preamble

Key provisions: the 8 points

The eight principles
No.PointWhat it says
1SovereigntyFirm and solemn resolve to proclaim India an Independent Sovereign Republic
2Territorial UnionTerritories of British India, the Indian States and other parts willing to join form a Union of India
3Federalism and residuary powersTerritories keep "retained status" (autonomy); residuary powers (not assigned to the Union) vest in the units, making India federal
4Source of powerAll power and authority of Sovereign Independent India and its Constitution derive from the People
5Fundamental valuesJustice (social, economic, political); Liberty (thought, expression, belief, faith, worship); Equality (status and opportunity)
6Protection of vulnerable sectionsAdequate safeguards for minorities, backward and tribal areas, depressed and other backward classes
7Territorial integrityIntegrity of the territory of the Republic and its sovereign rights on land, sea and air maintained per justice and the law of civilized nations
8International peaceFull and willing contribution to world peace and the welfare of mankind
Eight points
STFSFPTI

Sovereignty, Territorial Union, Federalism, Source of power, Fundamental values, Protection of vulnerable sections, Territorial integrity, International peace: "Some Tall Folks Sang Fine Poems To Impress."

Evolution into the Preamble

The Resolution was not a temporary motion but a pledge.

  • After Partition and Independence (1947) the word "Independent" was retained, but the federal structure was made stronger (Unitary bias) because of fears of Balkanization.
  • The core ideals (Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity) were enshrined in the Preamble, which serves as the identity card of the Constitution.

Objectives Resolution (1946-47)

  • Federal, residuary powers with the units
  • "Independent Sovereign Republic"
  • guaranteed Justice, Liberty, Equality

As it evolved

  • "Independent" retained
  • federal structure made stronger (Unitary bias)
  • Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity in the Preamble
Resolution to Preamble

Resolution = pledge; Preamble = identity card. Federalism in the Resolution (units keep residuary powers), Unitary bias later.

8 · Criticism of the Assembly#

Scholars and critics raised several objections.

Six criticisms
CriticismPoint made
Not a representative bodyMembers not directly elected on universal adult franchise
Not a sovereign bodyCreated by the proposals of the British Government (Cabinet Mission)
Time-consumingTook too long compared with the American Constitution (4 months)
Dominated by CongressGranville Austin: "The Assembly was the Congress, and the Congress was India."
Lawyer-politician dominationLed to the complicated language of the Constitution
Hindu dominatedWinston Churchill: represented "only one major community in India"
Six faults
RSTCLH

Representative (not), Sovereign (not), Time-consuming, Congress-dominated, Lawyer-dominated, Hindu-dominated: "Real Students Take Careful Long Hours."

Who said what

Granville Austin made the Congress remark. Winston Churchill made the "one major community" remark.
