In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The idea of a Constituent Assembly began with M.N. Roy (1934)
  • The INC demanded it in 1935 and Nehru (1938) asked for adult franchise and no outside interference
  • The British moved step by step: the August Offer (1940), the Cripps Mission (1942) and finally the Cabinet Mission (1946), whose scheme created the Assembly.
  • The Assembly had 389 seats (296 British India, 93 princely states), indirectly elected by Provincial Assemblies through proportional representation (single transferable vote). After Partition it fell to 299.
  • Key dates: 9 Dec 1946 first meeting, 11 Dec Prasad elected President, 13 Dec Objectives Resolution moved, 26 Nov 1949 Constitution adopted, 26 Jan 1950 it began.
  • Work took 2 years, 11 months, 17 days. Main committees: Drafting (Ambedkar), Union Powers and Union Constitution (Nehru), Provincial Constitution and Advisory (Patel), Rules of Procedure and Steering (Prasad).
  • Critics said the Assembly was not representative, not sovereign, slow, Congress-dominated, lawyer-dominated and Hindu-dominated.

1 · Introduction: the birth of a constitutional nation#

  • The making of the Constitution was a revolutionary act of self-determination, not merely a legal exercise. For nearly two centuries India had been governed by laws of the British Parliament, designed to rule subjects, not to empower citizens.
  • The Constituent Assembly was a body of representatives composed for the specific purpose of drafting the Constitution of India.
  • Formed in November 1946 under the Cabinet Mission Plan.
  • It served as the first Parliament of independent India and facilitated the transfer of sovereign power from British to Indian hands.
  • Its task: to stitch a diverse, fragmented subcontinent into a single, unified nation under the "Rule of Law", born amidst the turmoil of partition and the optimism of Independence.
  • Big idea: The formation of the Assembly in 1946 marked the transition from colonial rule to a sovereign democratic republic.

2 · Evolution of the demand (1934 to 1942)#

  • The idea of a Constituent Assembly was not sudden but the result of a gradual demand.

Steps of the demand

  • 1934: M.N. Roy (pioneer of the Communist movement in India) put the idea forward for the first time
  • 1935: The Indian National Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly
  • 1938: Nehru's Declaration on behalf of the INC (sovereignty and democracy)
  • 1940: August Offer by Viceroy Lord Linlithgow
  • 1942: Cripps Mission (failed)
  • 1946:
    • Cabinet Mission Plan
    • Assembly formed in November 1946

1938: Nehru's Declaration

  • Jawaharlal Nehru added two non-negotiable conditions:
  • Adult franchise: the Assembly must be elected by the people (democratic legitimacy).
  • No outside interference: the British Parliament would have no say in the drafting (sovereignty).

1940: The August Offer

  • Context:
    • World War II had begun (1939) and Britain desperately needed Indian cooperation
    • Viceroy Lord Linlithgow made the offer to win over Indian political parties
  • The "principle" accepted: for the first time the British explicitly acknowledged the right of Indians to frame their own Constitution.
  • The catch: the new constitution would be framed "mainly" (not "solely") by Indians, implying continued British oversight.

1942: The Cripps Mission

  • Proposal: Sir Stafford Cripps (member of the British War Cabinet) proposed an elected body after the war to frame a constitution. It promised Dominion Status with the right to secede from the Commonwealth.

Why the Cripps Mission failed

WhoObjection
Opt-out clauseProvinces could reject the new constitution and form separate unions; the INC saw a blueprint for the Balkanization of India
Mahatma GandhiCalled it "a post-dated cheque on a crashing bank" (declining power of the British Empire in the war)
Muslim LeagueRejected it because it did not explicitly grant a sovereign Pakistan with two distinct Constituent Assemblies
  • Memory aid (Six steps of the demand): Roy 1934, INC 1935, Nehru 1938, August Offer 1940, Cripps 1942, Cabinet Mission 1946: "Real Indians Never Accept Cheap Cheques."
  • Shortcut: Mainly vs solely. August Offer says "mainly" by Indians = British still watching. Cripps = opt-out for provinces = the post-dated cheque.

Also in the handout

  • 1935: The Indian National Congress (INC) officially demanded a Constituent Assembly to frame the Constitution of India.

3 · The Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946#

  • The Mission rejected the demand for two assemblies and offered a compromise.
  • Members: Lord Pethick Lawrence (Secretary of State for India), Sir Stafford Cripps, and A.V. Alexander.
  • Rejection of two assemblies: the Mission studied the Muslim League's demand for a separate Pakistan and two Constituent Assemblies but rejected it, arguing it would not solve the communal minority problem.
  • The scheme (the compromise): to satisfy the Muslim League, a Grouping System.

4 · Composition of the Assembly#

  • The Assembly was partly elected and partly nominated.
  • Elected members: indirectly elected by the members of the Provincial Legislative Assemblies, using Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote.
  • Nominated members: representatives of Princely States were nominated by their heads.

Seat allocation (total strength 389)

ComponentBefore PartitionAfter Partition
British India296229
Princely States9370
Total389299
  • The 296 British India seats were allocated among Muslims, Sikhs and General categories in proportion to population: 292 from eleven governors' provinces and 4 from the four Chief Commissioners' provinces.
  • The strength fell to 299 after Partition and the withdrawal of Muslim League members (from Pakistani territories).
  • Check the sums: 292 + 4 = 296 and 296 + 93 = 389. After Partition 229 + 70 = 299.

Elected versus nominated

  • Elected:
    • Indirectly, by Provincial Legislative Assemblies
    • PR by STV
    • British India seats
  • Nominated:
    • Representatives of Princely States
    • Nominated by their heads
    • 93 seats at first
  • Shortcut: Numbers. 389 = 296 + 93, then 299 = 229 + 70. British India lost 67, the States lost 23 (as the handout's figures give).

6 · The Objectives Resolution#

  • The roadmap and guiding principles for framing the Constitution.

Basics

ItemDetail
Moved byPt. Jawaharlal Nehru
Introduced13 December 1946
Adopted22 January 1947 (unanimously)
SignificanceRoadmap and guiding principles; its modified version later became the Preamble

Key provisions: the 8 points

The eight principles

No.PointWhat it says
1SovereigntyFirm and solemn resolve to proclaim India an Independent Sovereign Republic
2Territorial UnionTerritories of British India, the Indian States and other parts willing to join form a Union of India
3Federalism and residuary powersTerritories keep "retained status" (autonomy); residuary powers (not assigned to the Union) vest in the units, making India federal
4Source of powerAll power and authority of Sovereign Independent India and its Constitution derive from the People
5Fundamental valuesJustice (social, economic, political); Liberty (thought, expression, belief, faith, worship); Equality (status and opportunity)
6Protection of vulnerable sectionsAdequate safeguards for minorities, backward and tribal areas, depressed and other backward classes
7Territorial integrityIntegrity of the territory of the Republic and its sovereign rights on land, sea and air maintained per justice and the law of civilized nations
8International peaceFull and willing contribution to world peace and the welfare of mankind
  • Memory aid (Eight points): Sovereignty, Territorial Union, Federalism, Source of power, Fundamental values, Protection of vulnerable sections, Territorial integrity, International peace: "Some Tall Folks Sang Fine Poems To Impress."

Evolution into the Preamble

  • The Resolution was not a temporary motion but a pledge.
  • After Partition and Independence (1947) the word "Independent" was retained, but the federal structure was made stronger (Unitary bias) because of fears of Balkanization.
  • The core ideals (Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity) were enshrined in the Preamble, which serves as the identity card of the Constitution.

Resolution versus Preamble

  • Objectives Resolution (1946-47):
    • Federal, residuary powers with the units
    • "Independent Sovereign Republic"
    • Guaranteed Justice, Liberty, Equality
  • As it evolved:
    • "Independent" retained
    • Federal structure made stronger (Unitary bias)
    • Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity in the Preamble
  • Shortcut:
    • Resolution to Preamble. Resolution = pledge
    • Preamble = identity card. Federalism in the Resolution (units keep residuary powers), Unitary bias later

Also in the handout

  • The Resolution proclaimed the following principles: - Sovereignty: It declared the Assembly's firm and solemn resolve to proclaim India as an Independent Sovereign Republic.

  • Cabinet Mission (members Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps, A.V. Alexander) arrived on 24 March 1946 and published its plan on 16 May 1946. The Assembly was to give about one seat per million people, divided among Muslims, Sikhs and General in each province.
  • Election results (July-August 1946, 296 seats): Congress 208, Muslim League 73, others 15. The 93 princely-state seats were not filled at first, as the states stayed away.
  • Only 207 members attended the first meeting. The Assembly had 15 women members and every important leader except Mahatma Gandhi.
  • After the Independence Act the Assembly also sat as the Dominion Legislature
  • On 17 Nov 1947 it first met so and elected G.V. Mavlankar as speaker. It ratified India's Commonwealth membership in May 1949 and continued as the provisional Parliament until the first elections of 1951-52
  • Totals:
    • 11 sessions
    • About 60 constitutions studied
    • The Draft Constitution was considered for 114 days
    • The Drafting Committee sat 141 days
    • Cost Rs 64 lakh
  • Drafting Committee had seven members:
    • Ambedkar (chairman), N. Gopalaswamy Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar, K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadullah, N. Madhava Rau (replacing B.L. Mitter) and T.T. Krishnamachari (replacing D.P. Khaitan). The first draft was open to the public for eight months
    • The second draft came in October 1948
  • Three readings: first reading from 4 Nov 1948 (five days of talk), second reading from 15 Nov 1948 to 17 Oct 1949 (7653 amendments proposed, 2473 discussed), third reading from 14 Nov 1949. Only 284 of 299 members were present to sign on 26 Nov 1949. The adopted text had a Preamble, 395 Articles and 8 Schedules.
  • Other major committees and chairmen:
    • Advisory Committee had sub-committees (Fundamental Rights under J.B. Kripalani, Minorities under H.C. Mukherjee)
    • The States Committee was chaired by Nehru. Congress's Experts Committee of 8 July 1946 under Nehru prepared material before the Assembly met

Traps#

Trap 1 · Three days in December 1946 9 Dec was the first meeting (Sinha as temporary President, Muslim League boycott), 11 Dec Prasad was elected President, 13 Dec Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution. Rule: 9 meet, 11 elect, 13 resolve.
Trap 2 · Adopted, signed and in force The Constitution was adopted on 26 Nov 1949, signed and the anthem and song adopted on 24 Jan 1950, and came into force on 26 Jan 1950. Some provisions (citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament) started on 26 Nov 1949. Rule: 26 Nov passes, 24 Jan signs, 26 Jan commences.
Trap 3 · 389 versus 299 The 1946 plan gave 389 seats (296 and 93). After Partition the strength fell to 299 (British India 296 to 229, princely states 93 to 70). Rule: 389 is the plan, 299 is after Partition.
Trap 4 · Who was who Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee, B.N. Rau was Legal Advisor, H.V.R. Iyengar was Secretary and S.N. Mukherjee was Chief Draftsman. Prasad chaired the Assembly as constituent body; Mavlankar when it sat as legislature. Rule: do not mix the chairman, advisor, secretary and chief draftsman.
