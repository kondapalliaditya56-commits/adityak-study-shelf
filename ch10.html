  • What MQK 17-27 allowed and refused; Dastak, Gomasta, interloper, free merchant

  • First Subsidiary Alliance = Hyderabad 1798, not Awadh; battle order Ambur, Plassey, Wandiwash, Buxar

Mains hooks

  • Conquest without rule (Plassey) against the legal right to rule (Buxar)
  • How Plassey began the Drain of Wealth and changed the trade structure
  • Why the EIC did not annex Bengal outright; how the Dual Government led to the 1770 famine (a paragraph for the recurrent-famines question)
  • Clive 57-67 as father of the empire-building toolkit; why the EIC could build an empire (many States, resources not united)

Plassey gave conquest and the Drain; Buxar and the Treaty of Allahabad turned that plunder into a legal right.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: Abuse, then conquest, then plunder, then legalise: each step makes the last step's abuse legal. Farman rights are abused through Dastaks (abuse); Plassey wins Bengal by conspiracy (conquest); the Bengal treasury is looted (plunder); Buxar and the Treaty of Allahabad 1765 make the loot a legal right (legalise).

  1. The Doctor's gift (1717). The EIC is just a trader with a few Farmans: 1613 a factory at Surat, 1618 factories across the empire (Thomas Roe), 1691 duty-free trade in Bengal for Rs 3,000 a year. Then the EIC surgeon Dr William Hamilton cures Emperor Farrukhsiyar 13-19, and the reward is the 1717 Farman, the Magna Carta of the Company: duty-free trade, the Royal Mint, and rent of 38 villages around Calcutta.
    → But the Nawab of Bengal has to honour it.
  2. The Nawab says yes, but. MQK 17-27 allows duty-free trade but no Dastak misuse; refuses the Royal Mint (it hurts sovereignty and his commission); allows renting villages but not buying them.
    → So the EIC's officers look for other ways to profit.
  3. The cheating begins. Officers use the Dastak, a duty-free pass meant for EIC goods, to do private trade. By 1750 private trade is 1,00,000 pounds a year, about 50 times their salaries, and Bengal loses duty revenue. Interlopers break the Royal Charter; free merchants sell to the EIC in India; Dastak misuse breaks Indian law.
    → Bengal's rulers must decide how long to tolerate this.
  4. A young Nawab says enough (1756-57). Alivardi Khan 40-56 had tolerated the EIC (he was pragmatic, and Adyar 1746 had shown the strength of European infantry). His grandson Siraj 56-57, young and disliked at court, stops the Dastak misuse. The EIC shelters Krishna Ballabh and adds fortifications, so Siraj seizes Kasimbazar and then Calcutta, which he renames Alinagar. The Black Hole story is born (146 locked in and 123 dead in Holwell's account; modern estimate 30 to 40).
    → The EIC calls Robert Clive back.
  5. Clive returns. Clive 57-67 arrives from Madras with the Royal military, recaptures Calcutta, signs the Treaty of Alinagar (all EIC privileges restored), destroys Hugli and beats the French at Chandarnagar (the Seven Years' War is on).
    → Now he needs a way to beat Siraj without a real fight.
  6. Plassey, 23 June 1757: the conspiracy. Mir Jafar 57-60, Siraj's Bakshi (bag = war paymaster, binoculars = head of military intelligence), makes a deal with Clive. At Plassey the bulk of the army does not fight; Siraj flees and is beheaded on the orders of Miran, Mir Jafar's son. The EIC has conquest, not rule.
    → Now comes the reward.
  7. The loot. Plunder 1757-65: 2,75,000 pounds to the Royal military, 2,34,000 pounds plus a jagir of 35,000 a year to Clive 57-67, Rs 2.25 crore to the EIC, and 2.5 million pounds of political gifts. Gomastas run untaxed private trade; Bengal's treasury buys Indian and Chinese goods and sends gold to Britain: the Drain begins; a large army is built. When Mir Jafar 57-60 cannot pay more, Mir Qasim 60-63 replaces him.
    → But Mir Qasim does not want to stay a puppet.
  8. The puppet rebels. Mir Qasim 60-63 shifts his base to Bihar, punishes zamindars who underreported revenue, abolishes all internal duties. He is defeated in 1763, escapes to Awadh, and forms a league with Shuja 54-75 and Shah Alam II 60-06 (who is in Awadh because he left Delhi fearing Imad-ul-Mulk).
    → Three armies now face the EIC.
  9. Buxar, 1764: the real battle. Seven years after Plassey a closely fought battle is won by Hector Munroe (Clive 57-67 is in London). Shuja 54-75 had attacked the fort against Mir Qasim's advice. This is the EIC's first real military victory.
    → Now the EIC dictates terms: the Treaty of Allahabad.
  10. Allahabad, 1765: Paper, Power, Pay. Paper: the Emperor gets promises (Rs 26 lakh tribute, help to retake Delhi) that are never kept, plus Allahabad and Kora. Power: the EIC gets the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa (Nizamat stays with the Nawab): the Dual Government, with Md Reza Khan. Pay: Awadh pays Rs 50 lakh, loses Benaras, and becomes the buffer (Ring Fence, mini Subsidiary Alliance). Indirect Rule, Ring Fence and the mini SA are Clive 57-67's toolkit.
    → Power without responsibility cannot last.
  11. The bill comes due (1770-73). The Great Bengal Famine 1770 kills about one-third of the population while neither the EIC nor the Nawab takes charge. Land revenue and law and order collapse and the EIC goes bankrupt (1772). The EIC takes over the Nizamat; W. Hastings 73-85 carries Direct Rule: the Company State stands.

In simple words#

  • The EIC began as a trader with trading privileges (the Farmans, above all the 1717 one) that its officials abused through Dastaks and private trade.
  • Siraj-ud-Daula 56-57 tried to stop the abuse
  • Plassey (1757) was a court conspiracy with Mir Jafar 57-60, not a real battle. It gave conquest without rule
  • After Plassey the EIC plundered Bengal (cash and gifts), started the Drain of Wealth, built a large army and kept a puppet Nawab.
  • Mir Qasim 60-63 tried to be independent and lost at Buxar (1764)
  • The Treaty of Allahabad (1765) gave the EIC the Diwani, making the plunder a legal right
  • The Dual Government (Diwani with the EIC, Nizamat with the Nawab) failed in the 1770 famine, and Direct Rule followed in 1772: the Company State.

1 · Plassey marks the beginning of conquest#

Story link

Story so far: the EIC is a trader. Plassey is where it first acts like a conqueror. Keep the spine in mind: abuse, conquest, plunder, legalise.

  • Plassey marks the beginning of the transformation of the EIC into a Company State.
  • Conquest means military domination and political supremacy. It began here, in Bengal.
  • But the EIC did not legally take over sovereign functions in Bengal after the Battle of Plassey 1757.
  • It focused on military control and on extracting wealth.
Key takeaway
  • Takeaway: Rule did not begin in 1757.
  • Conquest and the Drain of Wealth from India, however, did.
Teacher's board
  • Extracting wealth = PLUNDER, and plunder has two parts: ATTACK and LOOT.
  • The Plassey Plunder runs from 1757 to 1765 and it was illegal.
  • In 1765 the EIC became the FM (finance minister, that is the Diwan) of Bengal and so got the LEGAL RIGHT to use Bengal's wealth: the loot was legalised.
  • This is the thread that ties Plassey to Buxar.

2.2 What the 1717 Farman gave, and what the Nawab refused

  • The 1717 Imperial (Royal) Farman gave the EIC three rights:
  • The right to Duty-Free Trade (DFT) in Deccan, Gujarat and Bengal.
  • For Bengal this was a Royal Farman reiterating a pre-existing privilege, granted under the sign and seal of the Subahdar in 1691 (the slide calls the 1691 grant a provincial privilege or Parwana).
  • The right to use the Royal Mint.
  • The EIC could mint Indian coins and so save the cost of converting gold into Indian currency (sikkas) for purchasing Indian goods.
  • The right to rent 38 villages around Calcutta for expanding its trading settlement.
Teacher's board
  • Why the mint mattered. Within Indian territory one can use only Indian sikkas.
  • So the EIC took its gold and silver to the Royal Mint and got sikkas back, and here the Royal Mint earned a commission.
  • The right to mint its own coins would save the EIC that cost and take that commission away from the government.
  • Under the settlement clause the teacher noted two years: [1690] (under "expand the settlement") and [Presidency 1699] (they read as the year of the Calcutta settlement and the year it became a Presidency; the story is in Chapter 8).
  • The 1717 Farman became a source of conflict between the EIC and the Nawab of Bengal.
  • Bengal by 1717 was a successor State.
  • Recall: successor means uttaradhikari, one who comes from within the family.
  • Here the family is the political family of the Mughal State (the Mughal Emperor and his Mansabdars).
  • So certain powerful Mansabdars established their rule in certain provinces and took away the real powers of the Emperor, making him a symbolic authority.
  • They were MQK 17-27, Sadat Khan 22-39 and Nizam 24-48 in Bengal, Awadh and Hyderabad respectively (Chapter 3).
  • Nawab Murshid Quli Khan (MQK 17-27) dealt with the three rights like this:

3 · The Dastak problem#

Story link

Because the Nawab set conditions, officers found loopholes: the Dastak. Ask 'whose law is broken?' for each villain (intruder, friend, cheat).

3.1 Private trade and what a Dastak was

Teacher's board
  • Private trade (PVT TRADE) is when EIC officers purchase Indian goods for trade in their personal capacity, apart from purchasing goods on behalf of the EIC.
  • The reason is greed
  • it is corruption
  • it is a "stab in the back" of the EIC by its own employees.
  • It violates the Royal Charter, which gave the monopoly over British trade in the East Indies to the EIC: an employee should not become a competitor to his own company.
Teacher's board
  • A Dastak is a document issued by EIC officers
  • It is what "operationalised" duty-free trade.
  • The teacher drew one: "It is certified that these 100 goods are of the EIC.
  • Please allow DFT exit from port. (Signed)".
  • It is shown to the Indian customs officer, who then allows the container to leave India without duty payment.
  • Misuse = using a Dastak for private trade by MISDECLARING that the goods are the EIC's.
  • It led to a loss of duty [tax revenue] for the Indian State.

The teacher's board: how an interloper became a free merchant

  • Earlier: ryots and artisans sell to the Indian merchant, who sells to the EIC, which exports
  • Interloper:
    • Buys from Indian merchants and exports on his own
    • say his profit is Rs 100, which is a loss of Rs 100 to the EIC
  • The EIC is unable to stop it: the Charter is broken, and there are court cases in Britain
  • Free merchant:
    • The same man now sells to the EIC inside India and does not export himself
    • The Rs 100 is shared
  • No Charter is broken: the EIC earns some share, and the free merchant gains because there is no court case in Britain

4 · Siraj-ud-Daula 56-57 and the road to Plassey (1757)#

Story link

Because the cheating hurt Bengal's revenue, a young Nawab finally tries to stop it. Watch who tolerated it and who did not.

4.1 Why Alivardi Khan 40-56 tolerated the EIC's provocations

  • Afraid of the French, the EIC began adding fortifications in Calcutta, that is strengthening Fort William (thickening walls, filling gaps, raising the height), from 1755, without the permission of Alivardi Khan 40-56.
  • A fort is a military structure. Building or expanding one on another sovereign's territory without permission is a challenge to that sovereign's authority.
  • However, Alivardi Khan 40-56 was pragmatic and followed the path of diplomacy rather than military confrontation, since the EIC's trade was economically beneficial to Bengal.
  • He must also have noticed the Battle of Adyar 1746, which proved the military capabilities of European infantry.
Teacher's board
  • Why the EIC was fortifying. 1756: the Seven Years' Global War made the EIC anxious. 60% of the EIC's trade was Bengal, so Bengal had to be protected.
  • The EIC's reasoning, in the teacher's words: "We need to be ATMANIRBHAR (self-reliant), because Adyar 1746 tells us that the Nawab won't be able to help us" against the French. Fort = military structure.
  • Fortification means either building a fort or strengthening one: filling gaps in the walls, increasing the height, increasing the thickness.
  • The same Battle of Adyar 1746 (in the Carnatic Wars, which the teacher dates 1746-63) explains both sides: it made Alivardi Khan 40-56 cautious and it made the EIC rely on itself (Chapter 9).

4.2 Why Siraj 56-57 did not tolerate them

  • Siraj 56-57 was the precious grandson of Alivardi Khan 40-56 through his daughter, as Alivardi Khan 40-56 had no sons and Siraj 56-57 was the only grandson.
  • Siraj 56-57 was disliked in the Bengal court.
  • He had disrespected senior Mansabdars and had at one point imprisoned Jagat Seth, the powerful banker, for extortion.
  • He was young, impulsive, surrounded by enemies.
  • When he came to power he stopped all Dastak misuse, hurting the private trade fortunes of EIC officials.
  • The British then gave asylum to Krishna Ballabh (for money), a fugitive from Siraj 56-57's court, charged with fraud, and added new fortifications to Calcutta.
  • Siraj 56-57 first seized the EIC factory at Kasimbazar and then captured Calcutta. He renamed Calcutta Alinagar.

5 · Robert Clive 57-67, the conspiracy and the battle#

Story link

Because Siraj dared to act, the EIC sends Clive. Notice how he wins: by a deal, not by force.

Was the Battle of Plassey (23rd June 1757) really a battle?

5.1 Clive 57-67 recaptures Calcutta and neutralises the French

  • Robert Clive 57-67 arrived from Madras with Royal military forces and recaptured Calcutta.
  • The Treaty of Alinagar (Alinagar was the name Siraj 56-57 had given to Calcutta) restored all EIC privileges.
  • Clive 57-67 destroyed Hugli port, which was being used by the French, and defeated the French at Chandarnagar (the slide adds: a French settlement since 1690).
  • The reason: the Anglo-French conflict was live as part of the Seven Years' Global War (1756-63).

The Great Bengal Famine of 1770 (GBF 1770) and the end of the Dual Government

  • In 1772 the Dual Government was formally ended.
  • The Nizamat was taken over legally from the Nawab, and the EIC replaced Indian administrators with its own officers, establishing Direct Rule.
  • Thus the Company State was properly established in Bengal.
  • The reason was the Great Bengal Famine of 1770 (GBF 1770).
  • The famine killed one crore people, an estimated one-third of Bengal's population.
  • High land revenue demand and harsh tax collection methods continued even as crops failed and people starved.
  • Grain hoarding by speculators went unchecked, because neither the EIC nor the Nawab took responsibility for famine relief.
  • The EIC claimed that famine relief was the Nawab's domain, and the Nawab lacked the resources for it.
  • Due to the famine, land revenue collection and law and order collapsed, forcing the EIC to establish its own Direct Rule.
  • From 1772 the Nawab of Bengal became as powerless a figurehead as the Chhatrapati of the Marathas had become after the Treaty of Sangola 1750 (Chapter 4).

The Policy of Ring Fence (PoRF)

  • The Policy of Ring Fence was initiated with Awadh.
  • It is mainly associated with Warren Hastings 73-85, but Clive 57-67 planted its seed here.
  • It meant creating a military buffer around the EIC's core territory by signing military alliances with neighbouring States.
  • Under this military alliance with Awadh, if anyone attacked the EIC in Bengal, Awadh was treaty-bound to help.
  • Think of it like the US using NATO during the Cold War: a buffer of allied Western Europe protected the core, that is the USA, from the USSR.
Handout alert
  • Prelims match-the-following (the teacher's forecast). If "Policy of Ring Fence" is to be matched with a name, the correct pair is PoRF = Warren Hastings 73-85, even though Clive 57-67 planted the seed with Awadh in 1765.
  • Do not pick Clive 57-67 in a matching question.
Teacher's board
  • The Ring Fence analogy. Urban home:
    • A lower-middle-class house fixes broken glass on top of its boundary wall
    • a rich house puts coils of barbed wire there
  • Rural: a farmer rings his field with a thorny hedge so that cattle do not destroy the crop.
  • In each case the home or the field is the CORE interest, and the glass, wire or hedge is the Ring Fence.
  • The common idea: BUILD A BUFFER AROUND THE CORE.
Teacher's board
  • Applied to 1765: Bengal is the core interest of the EIC, and Awadh is the buffer.
  • An enemy of the EIC coming from the west must first pass through Awadh.
  • The fence is made not of wire but "via military alliance".
  • Simple definition: PoRF = military alliance with neighbours.
  • In the small target diagram, British India is the inner circle, the allied neighbours are the ring, and the enemies are outside it.

The mini Subsidiary Alliance

  • A mini Subsidiary Alliance was signed. Awadh was forced to partially outsource a core sovereign function, its own military, to the EIC.
  • The size of the Awadh army was reduced, and an EIC army was permanently stationed in Awadh and paid for by Awadh via an annual subsidy.
  • (The handout's joke: the EIC saying "haha, thank you Dupleix 42-54 for the 1751 idea at Hyderabad".)
  • Thus the EIC now partially controlled the military resources of Awadh.
  • Combined with the military alliance, it meant that these resources could be used directly by the EIC itself (under its command) for protecting Bengal.
  • The clauses relating to Awadh are a prelude to the full Subsidiary Alliance that Wellesley 98-05 would formalise three decades later (Chapter 11).
  • A formal and proper Subsidiary Alliance was signed with Awadh in 1801, when Awadh's own army was disbanded and its foreign policy was also taken over by the EIC.
  • But the architecture was drawn here in 1765.

Recall sheet#

Read the question, answer in your head, then tap to check.

What began at Plassey and what did not?Conquest (military domination and political supremacy) and the Drain of Wealth began; rule did not: the EIC took no sovereign function legally.
What did the four Farmans give?1613 (Jahangir 05-27): factory at Surat. 1618 (Jahangir 05-27): factories in the whole empire and liberal rights, Thomas Roe. 1691 (Subahdar of Bengal): duty-free trade in Bengal for Rs 3,000 a year. 1717 (Farrukhsiyar 13-19): duty-free trade, Royal Mint, 38 villages.
How was the 1717 Farman obtained?Medical diplomacy: Dr William Hamilton, an EIC surgeon, cured Farrukhsiyar 13-19 of a serious illness.
Three rights in the 1717 Farman?Duty-free trade in Deccan, Gujarat and Bengal; use of the Royal Mint; rent of 38 villages around Calcutta.
Why did MQK 17-27 refuse the Royal Mint?It hurt sovereign authority and government revenue: the government earned commission on converting gold into sikkas.
What is a Dastak?A permit issued by EIC officials certifying that goods belonged to the EIC, hence exempt from export duty.
How big was private trade by 1750?1,00,000 pounds a year, roughly 50 times the officials' salaries; so the primary motive was private trade, not salary.
Who curbed private trade later?Cornwallis 86-93, an honest Governor General (the teacher: by increasing salaries).
Interloper versus free merchant?Interloper: non-EIC British merchant trading with India, breaking the Royal Charter. Free merchant: one who sells to the EIC inside India, so no Charter is broken.
Why did Alivardi Khan 40-56 tolerate the fortification of Fort William?He was pragmatic: the EIC's trade benefited Bengal, and Adyar 1746 had shown the strength of European infantry.
Why was Siraj 56-57 disliked at court?He disrespected senior Mansabdars and had imprisoned the banker Jagat Seth for extortion; young, impulsive, surrounded by enemies.
What were the triggers for Siraj 56-57's attack on Calcutta?He had stopped Dastak misuse; the British gave asylum to Krishna Ballabh (for money) and added new fortifications. He seized Kasimbazar, then Calcutta.
What did the Treaty of Alinagar do?Restored all EIC privileges after Clive 57-67 recaptured Calcutta (Alinagar was Siraj 56-57's name for Calcutta).
Why was Plassey "no real battle"?Mir Jafar 57-60 ensured the bulk of the Bengal army did not fight; the victory came from court conspiracy.
How did Plassey start the Drain of Wealth?Bengal's own revenue paid for Indian and Chinese goods and gold was shipped to Britain: gold inflow turned into gold outflow.
What did Mir Qasim 60-63 do to become independent?Shifted from Murshidabad to Bihar, punished zamindars who underreported revenue, abolished all internal duties on domestic trade.
Why was Shah Alam II 60-06 in Awadh?He left Delhi fearing Wazir Imad-ul-Mulk (who blinded Ahmad Shah Bahadur 48-54 in 1754 and murdered Alamgir II 54-59 in 1759), failed against the EIC in Bengal in 1761, and made Shuja 54-75 his Wazir.
How was Buxar lost?Shuja 54-75 lost patience and attacked the fort directly against Mir Qasim 60-63's advice; Hector Munroe's final charge turned a close battle.
The teacher's "imp reason" why the EIC could build an empire?India had multiple States, not one State; they were rivals, so economic and military resources were not united. Indian kings, not Indians, were not united.
What did the Emperor get in ToA 1765?A promise of Rs 26 lakh annual tribute, a promise of military help to retake Delhi from the Rohillas, and Allahabad and Kora. The first two were never delivered.
What was the EIC's legal position after ToA 1765?A Mansabdar of the Mughal State, with Bengal as its Watan Jagir: absorbed into the Mughal legal-political structure.
Diwani versus Nizamat?Diwani (EIC): revenue administration, treasury, civil administration and civil justice. Nizamat (Nawab): law and order and criminal justice; military in practice with the EIC.
Why did the EIC not annex Bengal in 1765?A company works for profit, not for ruling; annexation meant administrative cost and responsibility; it was already safe militarily and now had a legal right over the finances.
Three elements of Indirect Rule?The Nawab is a puppet; a British Resident is stationed in court; the Resident controls the puppet and governs indirectly.
Why did the Dual Government end in 1772?The Great Bengal Famine of 1770 (1 crore dead, one-third of the population) collapsed land revenue and law and order; the EIC took the Nizamat and set up Direct Rule.
The teacher's concept of famine?Famine = a function of a nature factor (drought, floods) AND a human factor (people's savings; a caring State that keeps a buffer and imports).
What did Awadh lose in ToA 1765?Rs 50 lakh compensation; Benaras (annexed by the EIC in 1775); Kora and Allahabad to the Emperor; duty-free trade for the EIC; part of its military autonomy.
What is the Policy of Ring Fence?A military buffer around the EIC's core territory through military alliances with neighbouring States: Awadh protects Bengal. Analogy: barbed wire or broken glass on a wall; NATO in the Cold War.
Mini Subsidiary Alliance (1765) versus the formal one (1801)?1765: Awadh's army reduced, an EIC army stationed and paid by subsidy, foreign policy not taken. 1801: Awadh's army disbanded and foreign policy taken over.
Why is Clive 57-67 called the father of the empire-building toolkit?Indirect Rule, Ring Fence and the mini Subsidiary Alliance were all invented or operationalised by him in ToA 1765; Wellesley 98-05 built on it.
