1 · Plassey marks the beginning of conquest#

  • Plassey marks the beginning of the transformation of the EIC into a Company State.
  • Conquest means military domination and political supremacy. It began here, in Bengal.
  • But the EIC did not legally take over sovereign functions in Bengal after the Battle of Plassey 1757. It focused on military control and on extracting wealth.
Remember

Takeaway: Rule did not begin in 1757. Conquest and the Drain of Wealth from India, however, did.

Teacher's board

Extracting wealth = PLUNDER, and plunder has two parts: ATTACK and LOOT. The Plassey Plunder runs from 1757 to 1765 and it was illegal. In 1765 the EIC became the FM (finance minister, that is the Diwan) of Bengal and so got the LEGAL RIGHT to use Bengal's wealth: the loot was legalised. This is the thread that ties Plassey to Buxar.

2.2 What the 1717 Farman gave, and what the Nawab refused

The 1717 Imperial (Royal) Farman gave the EIC three rights:

  • The right to Duty-Free Trade (DFT) in Deccan, Gujarat and Bengal. For Bengal this was a Royal Farman reiterating a pre-existing privilege, granted under the sign and seal of the Subahdar in 1691 (the slide calls the 1691 grant a provincial privilege or Parwana).
  • The right to use the Royal Mint. The EIC could mint Indian coins and so save the cost of converting gold into Indian currency (sikkas) for purchasing Indian goods.
  • The right to rent 38 villages around Calcutta for expanding its trading settlement.
Teacher's board

Why the mint mattered. Within Indian territory one can use only Indian sikkas. So the EIC took its gold and silver to the Royal Mint and got sikkas back, and here the Royal Mint earned a commission. The right to mint its own coins would save the EIC that cost and take that commission away from the government. Under the settlement clause the teacher noted two years: [1690] (under "expand the settlement") and [Presidency 1699] (they read as the year of the Calcutta settlement and the year it became a Presidency; the story is in Chapter 8).

The 1717 Farman became a source of conflict between the EIC and the Nawab of Bengal.

  • Bengal by 1717 was a successor State. Recall: successor means uttaradhikari, one who comes from within the family. Here the family is the political family of the Mughal State (the Mughal Emperor and his Mansabdars). So certain powerful Mansabdars established their rule in certain provinces and took away the real powers of the Emperor, making him a symbolic authority. They were MQK 17-27, Sadat Khan 22-39 and Nizam 24-48 in Bengal, Awadh and Hyderabad respectively (Chapter 3).
  • Nawab Murshid Quli Khan (MQK 17-27) dealt with the three rights like this:

3 · The Dastak problem#

3.1 Private trade and what a Dastak was

Teacher's board

Private trade (PVT TRADE) is when EIC officers purchase Indian goods for trade in their personal capacity, apart from purchasing goods on behalf of the EIC. The reason is greed; it is corruption; it is a "stab in the back" of the EIC by its own employees. It violates the Royal Charter, which gave the monopoly over British trade in the East Indies to the EIC: an employee should not become a competitor to his own company.

Teacher's board

A Dastak is a document issued by EIC officers; it is what "operationalised" duty-free trade. The teacher drew one: "It is certified that these 100 goods are of the EIC. Please allow DFT exit from port. (Signed)". It is shown to the Indian customs officer, who then allows the container to leave India without duty payment. Misuse = using a Dastak for private trade by MISDECLARING that the goods are the EIC's. It led to a loss of duty [tax revenue] for the Indian State.

4 · Siraj-ud-Daula 56-57 and the road to Plassey (1757)#

4.1 Why Alivardi Khan 40-56 tolerated the EIC's provocations

  • Afraid of the French, the EIC began adding fortifications in Calcutta, that is strengthening Fort William (thickening walls, filling gaps, raising the height), from 1755, without the permission of Alivardi Khan 40-56.
  • A fort is a military structure. Building or expanding one on another sovereign's territory without permission is a challenge to that sovereign's authority.
  • However, Alivardi Khan 40-56 was pragmatic and followed the path of diplomacy rather than military confrontation, since the EIC's trade was economically beneficial to Bengal. He must also have noticed the Battle of Adyar 1746, which proved the military capabilities of European infantry.
Teacher's board

Why the EIC was fortifying. 1756: the Seven Years' Global War made the EIC anxious. 60% of the EIC's trade was Bengal, so Bengal had to be protected. The EIC's reasoning, in the teacher's words: "We need to be ATMANIRBHAR (self-reliant), because Adyar 1746 tells us that the Nawab won't be able to help us" against the French. Fort = military structure. Fortification means either building a fort or strengthening one: filling gaps in the walls, increasing the height, increasing the thickness. The same Battle of Adyar 1746 (in the Carnatic Wars, which the teacher dates 1746-63) explains both sides: it made Alivardi Khan 40-56 cautious and it made the EIC rely on itself (Chapter 9).

4.2 Why Siraj 56-57 did not tolerate them

  • Siraj 56-57 was the precious grandson of Alivardi Khan 40-56 through his daughter, as Alivardi Khan 40-56 had no sons and Siraj 56-57 was the only grandson.
  • Siraj 56-57 was disliked in the Bengal court. He had disrespected senior Mansabdars and had at one point imprisoned Jagat Seth, the powerful banker, for extortion. He was young, impulsive, surrounded by enemies.
  • When he came to power he stopped all Dastak misuse, hurting the private trade fortunes of EIC officials.
  • The British then gave asylum to Krishna Ballabh (for money), a fugitive from Siraj 56-57's court, charged with fraud, and added new fortifications to Calcutta.
  • Siraj 56-57 first seized the EIC factory at Kasimbazar and then captured Calcutta. He renamed Calcutta Alinagar.

5 · Robert Clive 57-67, the conspiracy and the battle#

Was the Battle of Plassey (23rd June 1757) really a battle?

5.1 Clive 57-67 recaptures Calcutta and neutralises the French

  • Robert Clive 57-67 arrived from Madras with Royal military forces and recaptured Calcutta.
  • The Treaty of Alinagar (Alinagar was the name Siraj 56-57 had given to Calcutta) restored all EIC privileges.
  • Clive 57-67 destroyed Hugli port, which was being used by the French, and defeated the French at Chandarnagar (the slide adds: a French settlement since 1690). The reason: the Anglo-French conflict was live as part of the Seven Years' Global War (1756-63).
  • The French threat in Bengal was neutralised.
Teacher's board

The teacher's sketch of the field: the Bengal Army with Siraj 56-57 standing on the higher ground, rain falling on it, and below it, advancing upward, Clive 57-67 + the EIC army + the British Royal military. Note the three separate forces on the British side: the Company's own army and the Royal (King's) military are different things (this matters again for the plunder figures in section 6). In his margin note (Lecture 14, slide 4) the teacher expands the code: Clive 57-67 was Governor of the Bengal Presidency of the EIC in two terms, 1757-60 and 1765-67.

5.2 Plassey, 23 June 1757: conspiracy, not a battle

What happened next was not war: it was conspiracy.

  • Mir Jafar 57-60 was the commander of Bengal's army. That is what the books say. Technically the Subahdar was the commander of the army. Mir Jafar 57-60 was the Bakshi (Bakhshi) at the province level, that is the war paymaster and head of military intelligence. Since Siraj 56-57 was young and inexperienced, in practice Mir Jafar 57-60 was the real commander.
  • He approached Clive 57-67 and made a deal with the EIC to remove Siraj 56-57.
  • At the Battle of Plassey (1757), Mir Jafar 57-60 ensured that the bulk of the Bengal army did not fight and turned back.
  • Siraj 56-57 was easily defeated and fled. He was later captured and beheaded on the orders of Miran, Mir Jafar 57-60's son.
  • Mir Jafar 57-60 became Subahdar (he was again Subahdar from 1763 to 1765). But he was now the EIC's puppet, as the EIC did not withdraw from the military domination established after the defeat of Siraj 56-57.
  • The political supremacy of the EIC in India had begun.
Remember

Takeaway: Plassey was no real battle. The EIC's victory in Bengal came from court conspiracy. The first real military victory for the EIC comes seven years later, at Buxar (1764).

6.2 Beyond the cash: Bengal's economy and polity, the EIC's trade structure and the EIC army

1. Private trade, international and now domestic too

  • After Plassey there was rampant misuse of Dastaks.
  • EIC officials made large fortunes not just from private international trade but also from private domestic trade.
  • This domestic trade was conducted through Indian agents called Gomastas, who procured goods directly from peasants and artisans, cutting out Indian merchants and traders.
  • They also did not pay domestic taxes on this trade, which hurt further the Indian merchants competing against them.
Teacher's board

The board: ryots and artisans used to sell to the Indian merchant, who sold to the EIC. Now the Indian merchant is crossed out, and the goods go "via Gomastas [Indian agents of the EIC]" straight to the EIC's officers. The teacher ticked "Private trade" and "Domestic trade" on the slide (Lecture 13, slide 44) and underlined "paid no domestic tax".

2. The structure of the EIC's international trade changed: the Drain of Wealth begins

  • Before Plassey, gold had to be shipped from Britain to buy Indian goods.
  • After Plassey, Bengal's own Diwani revenues funded the purchase of not just Indian but also Chinese goods: tea, silk, porcelain.
  • Further, gold from the Bengal treasury was also shipped to Britain.
  • Thus, from gold inflow before 1757, there was now gold outflow from India. Hence began the Drain of Wealth.

7 · Battle of Buxar (1764): the EIC gets the right to rule#

7.1 Why did Mir Qasim 60-63 rebel?

  • Mir Qasim 60-63 was an able man and had no intention of remaining a puppet.

8.2.2 Policy of Indirect Rule, the 1770 famine and Direct Rule

The Policy of Indirect Rule was now formally installed in Bengal's Nizamat. The three elements of Indirect Rule are:

The three elements of Indirect Rule (PoIR)
  1. The King or Nawab is a puppet.
  2. A British Resident is stationed in court.
  3. The Resident controls the puppet and thus governs indirectly.
  • All three were now in place.
  • The EIC also obtained the legal right to appoint the Deputy Nawab. This meant that if the Deputy Subahdar refused to follow informal EIC instructions in Nizamat matters, he could be removed by the EIC.
  • By 1772 the British Resident had established complete indirect control over the Nizamat.
Teacher's board

PoIR is a "3 step PROCESS", and a process "can take years". (a) The Nawab is a puppet; (b) a British Resident is stationed in the court, "to play the Nawab as a puppet"; (c) the Resident controls him. On the slide (Lecture 14, slide 15) he writes "Policy implementation takes time" and, under the row label, "Began in Bengal, ToA 1765". The Great Bengal Famine of 1770 (GBF 1770) and the end of the Dual Government

  • In 1772 the Dual Government was formally ended. The Nizamat was taken over legally from the Nawab, and the EIC replaced Indian administrators with its own officers, establishing Direct Rule. Thus the Company State was properly established in Bengal.
  • The reason was the Great Bengal Famine of 1770 (GBF 1770).
  • The famine killed one crore people, an estimated one-third of Bengal's population.
  • High land revenue demand and harsh tax collection methods continued even as crops failed and people starved.
  • Grain hoarding by speculators went unchecked, because neither the EIC nor the Nawab took responsibility for famine relief. The EIC claimed that famine relief was the Nawab's domain, and the Nawab lacked the resources for it.
  • Due to the famine, land revenue collection and law and order collapsed, forcing the EIC to establish its own Direct Rule.
  • From 1772 the Nawab of Bengal became as powerless a figurehead as the Chhatrapati of the Marathas had become after the Treaty of Sangola 1750 (Chapter 4).
Teacher's board

The teacher's chain beside the slide: GBF 1770 led to the collapse of L and O (law and order), which is the Nizamat side, and of LR (land revenue), which is the Diwani side. Both halves of the Dual Government failed together. The slide names who brought Direct Rule: W. Hastings 73-85. Teacher's board

Concept of famine: Famine = a function of [a NATURE factor AND a HUMAN factor]. Nature factor: drought or floods reduce the supply of food, so the price of food shoots up. That alone is not yet a famine. Human factor: if the people are prosperous and have good savings, they can still buy (food can be imported); and a caring, welfare State maintains a buffer stock and imports food. A famine happens when nature fails and the human side fails too. In Bengal in 1770 the human side failed completely: revenue demand stayed high, hoarding went unchecked, and neither authority took charge of relief. This is the paragraph for the Mains question.

Teacher's board

Concept of bankruptcy: one is unable to meet one's liabilities (loan repayment, employees' salaries, the price of goods and services purchased). In capitalism companies run on LEVERAGE: the teacher's table shows a company of 1 crore taking a loan of 0.4 crore, a company of 10 crore taking 4 crore, 100 crore taking 40, 1,000 crore taking 400, and so on. A bank gives a loan when it is confident that you will pay the EMI. BUT an income shock means the EMI cannot be paid, and the company goes bankrupt.

Teacher's board

"The EIC went BANKRUPT (1772)." The reasoning: the EIC had two incomes: trade and LR (land revenue). Since 1757 it was not bringing gold from Britain to buy goods; it used Indian (Bengal) money, that is the money in the Bengal treasury, which was the LR paid by the people. So when GBF 1770 hit the LR income, the trade income fell with it (no revenue, no purchases). Both incomes collapsed together: that is the income shock. This is the money side of why the Dual Government had to end in 1772.

  • The Policy of Ring Fence was initiated with Awadh. It is mainly associated with Warren Hastings 73-85, but Clive 57-67 planted its seed here.

  • Under this military alliance with Awadh, if anyone attacked the EIC in Bengal, Awadh was treaty-bound to help.

Recall sheet#

Read once, hide it, then rebuild the chapter from memory.

  1. Plassey = conquest (military domination and political supremacy) without rule; rule (L, E, J on territory) did not begin in 1757, but conquest and the Drain of Wealth did. Plunder 1757-65 was illegal; legalised in 1765.
  2. Farmans: 1613 Jahangir 05-27 (Surat, after Suvali 1612); 1618 Jahangir 05-27 (whole empire, liberal rights, Thomas Roe 1615-19); 1691 Subahdar of Bengal under Aurangzeb 58-07 (DFT Bengal, Rs 3,000 a year); 1717 Farrukhsiyar 13-19 (Magna Carta of the Company, Dr William Hamilton).
  3. 1717 Farman: DFT in Deccan, Gujarat and Bengal; Royal Mint; rent 38 villages around Calcutta. MQK 17-27: DFT yes but no Dastak misuse; mint refused (sovereignty and commission); rent yes, purchase no.
  4. Dastak = permit by EIC officials certifying goods as the EIC's, exempt from export duty. Misuse = private trade and selling Dastaks; by 1750 private trade 1,00,000 pounds a year, 50 times salaries; Cornwallis 86-93 curbed it.
  5. Interlopers broke the Royal Charter (British law, court cases in Britain); free merchants sold to the EIC inside India (artificially legal); Dastak misuse broke Indian law (customs duty evasion).
  6. Alivardi Khan 40-56: pragmatic, diplomacy, Adyar 1746; Fort William fortified from 1755. Siraj 56-57: grandson through daughter, disliked, jailed Jagat Seth, stopped Dastak misuse; Krishna Ballabh; Kasimbazar then Calcutta (Alinagar).
  7. Black Hole: Holwell's 146 locked, 123 dead; modern estimate 30 to 40; propaganda. Monument 1760, demolished 1821, Curzon 99-05 replica 1901, Bose agitation 1940, shifted to St. John's Church graveyard.
  8. Clive 57-67 from Madras with Royal military; Treaty of Alinagar; Hugli destroyed, French beaten at Chandarnagar (Seven Years' War 1756-63). Plassey 23 June 1757: Mir Jafar 57-60 (Bakshi) kept the army out; Siraj 56-57 beheaded on Miran's orders.
  9. Plunder: 2,75,000 pounds to the Royal military; 2,34,000 pounds plus a jagir of 35,000 pounds a year to Clive 57-67; Rs 2.25 crore to the EIC; 2.5 million pounds as political gifts 1757-65 (3.5% of Britain's 1760 GDP, multiplier about x32,000).
  10. After Plassey: Gomastas and untaxed domestic trade; Bengal's revenue buys Indian and Chinese goods (tea, silk, porcelain); gold outflow = Drain; standing army; Mir Jafar 57-60 replaced by son-in-law Mir Qasim 60-63.
  11. Mir Qasim 60-63: Murshidabad to Bihar (Patna), punished zamindars, abolished internal duties; defeated 1763; allied with Shuja 54-75 (3 crore, Bihar and its treasury) and Shah Alam II 60-06. Buxar 1764: Shuja 54-75 attacked against advice; Hector Munroe's final charge; Clive 57-67 in London.
  12. ToA 1765 @ Emperor: Rs 26 lakh tribute, help to retake Delhi from the Rohillas, Allahabad and Kora; the EIC as Mansabdar, Bengal as its Watan Jagir; all legal formality.
  13. ToA 1765 @ Bengal: Diwani (revenue, treasury, civil justice) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa to the EIC; Nizamat (law and order, criminal justice) with the Nawab; Md Reza Khan Naib Nazim and EIC's Diwan; Dual Government 1765-72; Indirect Rule (puppet, Resident, control); GBF 1770 (1 crore, one-third); Direct Rule 1772.
  14. ToA 1765 @ Awadh: Rs 50 lakh; Benaras separated (annexed 1775); DFT and Dastak misuse; Ring Fence (seed by Clive 57-67, credited to W. Hastings 73-85); mini Subsidiary Alliance (formal 1801; first SA was Hyderabad 1798). Battle order: Ambur 1749, Plassey 1757, Wandiwash 1760, Buxar 1764.
