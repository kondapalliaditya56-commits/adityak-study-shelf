In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Centre-State relations have three heads: legislative (Arts. 245-255), administrative (Arts. 256-263) and financial (Arts. 268-293).
  • The Seventh Schedule has the Union (98), State (59) and Concurrent (52) Lists. Union List prevails over both others and the Concurrent List over the State List. On a Concurrent subject central law prevails (Art. 254)
  • residuary powers go to Parliament (Art. 248)
  • Parliament can legislate on the State List in five ways: Rajya Sabha resolution (249), National Emergency (250), states' request (252), treaties (253) and President's Rule (356).
  • In administration, the Centre can direct states (Arts. 256-257). Defiance can lead to President's Rule through Art. 365. All-India Services, the Inter-State Council (Art. 263) and bodies like NITI Aayog link the two levels.
  • For money, Arts. 268-271 divide tax proceeds, GST came with the 101st Amendment (2016) and the GST Council (Art. 279A), and the Finance Commission (Art. 280) advises on sharing taxes and grants.
  • Friction produced the ARC (1966), Rajamannar (1969), Anandpur Sahib (1973), West Bengal (1977), Sarkaria (1983) and Punchhi (2007) reports.

Key terms

Repugnancy (Art. 254)
Clash of a central and a state law on a Concurrent subject: central law wins, unless the state law got Presidential assent (then it holds in that state until Parliament overrides)
Residuary powers
Subjects in no list; Parliament has them (Art. 248), following Canada, not the USA
Mutual delegation
President (Art. 258) with a state's consent gives Union functions to it; Governor (Art. 258A) with Union consent gives state functions to the Union
Net proceeds
Tax collected minus the cost of collection; the CAG certifies it and his certificate is final
Surcharge (Art. 271)
Extra levy kept wholly by the Centre; none can be levied on GST
Statutory grants
Art. 275 grants charged to the Consolidated Fund of India for needy states, on Finance Commission advice
Discretionary grants
Art. 282 grants for any public purpose, even outside legislative competence; the Centre is under no duty to give them
Finance Commission (Art. 280)
Constituted by the President every five years to advise on the sharing of taxes and on grants

1 · Framework of Centre-State relations#

  • India has a parliamentary government inside a federal framework: the executive is responsible to the legislature (parliamentary principle), and the Union and States have separate spheres (federal principle). Because laws, administration and money touch people directly, the Constitution sets detailed rules.

Three dimensions of relations

  • Legislative: Who makes laws on what (Arts. 245-255)
  • Administrative: Who executes and how the Centre supervises (Arts. 256-263 and others)
  • Financial: Who taxes, shares and spends (Arts. 268-293)
  • The legislative scheme is the three-fold division (Union List, State List, Concurrent List) in the Seventh Schedule
  • Arts. 245-255, especially Art. 246, explain the powers and the rules where both legislate on one subject. Residuary subjects go to the Union.
  • Memory aid (Three relations): Legislative, Administrative, Financial: "Law, Administration, Funds".
  • Shortcut: Article bands. 245-255 legislative. 256-263 administrative. 268-293 financial.

2 · Legislative relations: territory and the Lists#

  • Part XI (Arts. 245-255) is the core, with other Articles making exceptions. The handout gives four headings: (a) territorial reach
  • (b) distribution of subjects
  • (c) Parliament in the State field
  • (d) central controls over State laws

Territorial reach (Arts. 245-246)

  • Parliament may legislate for the whole or any part of India.
  • State legislatures may legislate for the whole or any part of their own State.
  • Parliament may make laws with extra-territorial effect, applying to Indian citizens and their property beyond India.

Exceptions to Parliament's reach

  • Art. 240: the President may make regulations for certain Union Territories, with the force of parliamentary enactments.
  • Fifth and Sixth Schedules (Art. 244):
    • Central laws may be adjusted or regulated in Scheduled Areas and tribal districts
    • The Governor or the President can make modifications or exceptions to protect local interests
  • Art. 246(4): Parliament can make laws on any subject (even State List) for territories not part of any State (Union Territories, acquired territories). Example in the handout: Ladakh laws made by Parliament or under Art. 240 regulations.

Hierarchy and conflict

  • The Union List prevails over both the State and Concurrent Lists
  • The Concurrent List prevails over the State List
  • Art. 254(1): on a Concurrent subject, the Central law overrides the State law.
  • Art. 254(2):
    • A State law that receives Presidential assent may prevail within that State
    • Parliament can still override it later
  • Example in the handout: the Kerala Professional Colleges Act (2006), though approved by the President, was later superseded by Central NEET regulations under a Union law.

Special points

  • GST:
    • The 101st Amendment (2016) inserted Art. 246A giving Parliament and States concurrent power on GST
    • Parliament is exclusive on GST in inter-state trade (Art. 269A)
    • The GST Council (Art. 279A) is the joint forum
  • Residuary powers: Parliament, Art. 248, includes law-making and taxation on unlisted subjects (digital currencies, space technology, cybersecurity named as examples).

Origin and comparison

  • The idea of lists comes from the Government of India Act, 1935: Federal, Provincial, Concurrent lists.
  • In 1935 residuary powers lay with the Governor-General
  • The Constitution gives them to Parliament, following the Canadian model
  • The US has a single enumeration of federal powers with residuary power to the States
  • Australia is similar. India has double enumeration plus a concurrent field
  • Memory aid (Lists): Union = national uniformity (defence, currency), State = local diversity (police, agriculture), Concurrent = cooperation: "Union Serves Country".

3 · Parliament in the State field: five routes#

  • Normally Parliament cannot touch the State List. Five exceptions exist, each with its own trigger, scope and checks.

The five routes

#RouteTriggerEffect and duration
1Art. 249Rajya Sabha resolution by a special majority that Parliament should legislate on a State List subject in the national interestParliament can legislate for the whole or part of India. Resolution lasts one year, renewable yearly. Laws cease six months after the resolution lapses. States may still legislate, but Parliamentary law prevails on direct inconsistency
2Art. 250 with Art. 352National EmergencyParliament can legislate on any State List entry for the Emergency's duration. Laws end six months after the Emergency ceases, unless Parliament provides otherwise. States not barred; Central law overrides on repugnancy
3Art. 252Two or more State legislatures pass resolutions requesting itParliament legislates for the requesting States. Other States may adopt by resolution. Only Parliament can amend or repeal it, not the adopting States. Examples: Prize Competitions Act, 1955; Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972
4Art. 253To implement treaties, conventions or international agreementsParliament can legislate on State List matters. Examples: privileges of international organisations, Geneva Conventions laws, anti-hijacking law, environmental statutes
5Art. 356President's Rule in a StateParliament can legislate on any State List subject for that State. Laws continue after President's Rule ends; the State Legislature may later repeal, amend or replace them

Art. 249 resolution life cycle

  • Rajya Sabha passes resolution:
    • National interest
    • Special majority
  • Valid for one year: Renewable year after year
  • Resolution lapses: Law stays for six more months, then ceases to operate
  • Memory aid (Five routes): Rajya Sabha (249), National Emergency (250), States' request (252), Treaties (253), President's Rule (356): "Red New Sofa Tops Pillows".
  • Shortcut: Duration rule. 249 and 250: laws end six months after the trigger ends. 356: laws continue until the State repeals them. 252: only Parliament can amend.
  • Constitutional logic: Each route is a compromise: the Union acts when uniform action is needed, but with checks (special majorities, State requests, a presidential proclamation). In practice these routes have enabled central laws on pollution control, wildlife protection, transplantation law, urban land regulation and implementation of international conventions.

5 · Administrative relations: executive power and directions#

  • The administrative framework lies in Arts. 256-263 (Part XI). It gives the Centre power to guide, coordinate and supervise State administration.

Distribution of executive authority

  • Union: extends to the whole of India on matters where Parliament has exclusive legislative authority (Union List), and to implementing treaties and agreements (Art. 73).
  • State: confined to its own territory, extends to the State List.
  • Concurrent List: Art. 73(1)(a) says the State's executive power generally extends to these subjects unless the Constitution or a parliamentary law gives responsibility to the Union.
  • Example: Right to Education Act (2009) and Environmental Protection Act (1986) are carried out jointly by Union and States.

Supervision and compliance

Compliance chain

  • Art. 256:
    • Every State must ensure compliance with Union laws
    • The Union may issue directions
  • Art. 257:
    • The State's executive power must not impede the Union's
    • The Union may direct on communications, railways and national schemes
  • Non-compliance: Art. 365: President may find the State cannot be run as per the Constitution
  • Art. 356: President's Rule can follow
Compliance chain
  • Examples cited: Art. 356 in Punjab (1980s) and Uttarakhand (2016).

7 · All-India Services and Public Service Commissions#

  • Three core services: IAS, IPS, IFS. Officers serve at both Union and State levels and rotate between them.
  • Recruitment, training and ultimate control with the Central Government
  • day-to-day supervision with the States
  • Origins:
    • Indian Civil Service (ICS) and Indian Police (IP) replaced by IAS and IPS after 1947
    • IFS established 1966
  • Art. 312: Parliament can create additional All-India Services if the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution (given as a two-thirds majority in one passage).
  • Members share common rights, uniform pay scales and equal status: a single integrated service.

From Lakshmikanth · Indian Polity, 8th ed. Ch 15, Centre-State Relations
  • Territory:
    • A state law does not apply outside the state unless there is a sufficient nexus. Only Parliament can make extra-territorial laws. The President makes regulations for Andaman and Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, and Ladakh
    • For Puducherry only when its Assembly is suspended or dissolved. Such a regulation can repeal or amend an Act of Parliament
  • Scheduled and tribal areas:
    • The Governor can direct that an Act of Parliament does not apply, or applies with changes, to a scheduled area. The Governor of Assam does this for tribal areas
    • The President does it for Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram
  • List numbering still shows old last entries:
    • Union List ends at 97 but has 98 entries (2A, 92A, 92B added; 33, 92, 92C omitted)
    • State List ends at 66 but has 59 (11, 19, 20, 29, 36, 52, 55 omitted)
    • Concurrent List ends at 47 but has 52 (11A, 17A, 17B, 20A, 33A added). Extra examples: Union - census, audit, insurance
    • State - prisons, fisheries, markets, theatres, gambling
    • Concurrent - civil procedure, economic and social planning, drugs, newspapers and printing presses
  • Art. 249 needs two-thirds of members present and voting (it also covers GST). Under Art. 252 the state legislature gives up its power on the matter, so the resolution works as abdication. More Art. 252 laws: Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974, Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) Act 1976, Transplantation of Human Organs Act 1994. Art. 253 examples: UN (Privileges and Immunities) Act 1947, Geneva Convention Act 1960, Anti-Hijacking Act 1982, TRIPS laws.
  • Other controls:
    • The Governor cannot make an ordinance without the President's instructions in certain cases (Art. 213). Executive power on Concurrent subjects stays with the states unless the Constitution or Parliament gives it to the Centre (for example the Essential Commodities Act). The Centre cannot delegate legislative power, and one state cannot ask Parliament to legislate on a state subject. The state's power to entrust functions to the Centre (Art. 258A) came by the 7th Amendment, 1956
    • Art. 257A (armed forces help) is repealed
  • Finance: Union taxes lie in 13 entries and state taxes in 18. Parliament used residuary taxation for gift tax, wealth tax and expenditure tax. The professional tax cap was Rs 250 until the 60th Amendment (1988) raised it to Rs 2,500. The 80th Amendment deleted Art. 272. GST Council advises on taxes to merge, goods exempted, model laws, turnover threshold, rates with floor and bands, and special rates in a disaster.
  • Finance Commission is a quasi-judicial body called the 'balancing wheel' of fiscal federalism. It also advises on measures to supplement panchayat and municipal resources based on State Finance Commission reports (added by the 73rd and 74th Amendments). Committee members: Rajamannar had Lakshmanswamy Mudaliar and P.C. Chandra Reddy
  • Sarkaria had B. Sivaraman and S.R. Sen. Lakshmikanth lists 14 tension areas, adding governor appointment and dismissal, enquiry commissions against Chief Ministers, centrally sponsored schemes, and CBI and ED
  • Punchhi extras:
    • Governors should be eminent, from outside the state, detached from local politics and not recently active in it
    • Impeachment of Governors like the President
    • Decide on assent or reservation within six months. For a hung assembly the order of preference is pre-poll alliance, largest single party, post-poll coalition of all, post-poll alliance with outside support. Bommai guidelines should enter Art. 356, a 'localised emergency' framework should be made, and the Inter-State Trade and Commerce Commission (Art. 307) should have appeal to the Supreme Court
  • Lakshmikanth differs:
    • Borrowing. The Centre can borrow inside or outside India within limits fixed by Parliament (no such law yet), and a state can borrow only within India, within limits fixed by its legislature
    • A state needs Centre's consent if a Central loan or guarantee is still outstanding. The handout says states may borrow abroad with Union consent. Use the handout in class
    • Remember LK's rule
  • Lakshmikanth differs: ARC (1969) made 22 recommendations and wanted Central forces deployed 'on request or otherwise', while the handout says mainly on state request. For Art. 268, LK gives post-GST stamp duties on bills of exchange, cheques and insurance policies, and its table puts coinage and currency under non-tax revenue without Art. 261, which is Full Faith and Credit (the handout wrongly ties Art. 261 to coinage).

Which Articles are the legislative relations?
Arts. 245-255 (Part XI).
Which Articles are the administrative relations?
Arts. 256-263 (with other Articles).

Arts. 268-293 (Part XII).
List counts in the handout?
Union 98 (originally 97), State 59 (earlier 66), Concurrent 52 (up from 47).
Which subjects did the 42nd Amendment move to the Concurrent List?
Education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, administration of justice (excluding SC and HC).
What does Art. 254 say?
Central law prevails on a Concurrent subject; a State law with Presidential assent may prevail in that State, but Parliament can override it later.
What does Art. 246(4) say?
Parliament may legislate on any subject for territories not part of any State.
Name the five routes for Parliament in the State field.
Art. 249 (RS resolution), Art. 250 (National Emergency), Art. 252 (States' request), Art. 253 (treaties), Art. 356 (President's Rule).
How long does an Art. 249 resolution last and what happens after?
One year, renewable; laws cease six months after it lapses.
Examples of Art. 252 laws?
Prize Competitions Act, 1955 and Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.
What do Arts. 200 and 201 do?
Governor may reserve State Bills for the President; President can assent, withhold (absolute veto) or return (not Money Bills).
What does Art. 304(b) require?
Prior Presidential approval to introduce State List Bills that restrict inter-state trade.
Art. 256 versus Art. 257?
256: States must comply with Union laws; 257: State power must not impede the Union (directions on communications, railways).
Art. 258 versus 258A?
258: President entrusts Union functions to States (with State consent); 258A: State functions to the Union.
What is Art. 262 and Art. 263?
262: Parliament may adjudicate inter-state river disputes (Act of 1956); 263: Inter-State Council (1990).
What is Art. 312?
Parliament can create new All-India Services on a Rajya Sabha resolution; IAS, IPS, IFS.
When was the IFS established?
1966.
Who appoints and removes State PSC members?
Governor appoints; President removes (Art. 316).
What did the 101st Amendment (2016) insert?
Art. 246A, Art. 269A, Art. 279A (GST Council); deleted Art. 268A and Entry 92C.
What is the 80th Amendment (2000)?
Alternative Scheme of Devolution: 29% of gross revenue from certain central taxes to States, from 1 April 1996 (10th FC).
Art. 268 versus 269 versus 270 versus 271?
268: levied by Union, collected and kept by States; 269: levied and collected by Union, assigned to States; 270: shared per Finance Commission; 271: surcharge kept by Centre.
What is the Art. 276 limit?
Rs 2,500 per person per year on professions, trades, callings and employments.
What are Art. 292 and 293?
292: Union borrowing (within and outside India); 293: State borrowing within India, Union consent needed beyond limits or from outside.
Statutory versus discretionary grants?
Statutory: Art. 275 on Finance Commission advice; Discretionary: Art. 282 for any public purpose.
Finance Commission facts?
Art. 280; President constitutes every five years; Chairperson plus at least one finance member; 15th FC (2020-25) recommended 41%.
Art. 285 versus Art. 289?
285: Union property exempt from State tax; 289: State property and income exempt from Union tax.
What did the 1963 advisory opinion say?
State immunity does not extend to customs and excise duties.
What changed after the 1967 elections?
Congress lost nine States; non-Congress States asserted autonomy.
ARC 1966: chair, study team head, report year?
Morarji Desai then K. Hanumanthayya; M.C. Setalvad; 1969.

DMK Tamil Nadu government (1969); report 1971.
Anandpur Sahib versus West Bengal memorandum?
Anandpur Sahib 1973 (Akali Dal): residuary powers to States; West Bengal 1977: "Federal" for "Union", 75% revenue to States, abolish AIS.

Set up 1983, report 1988, term extended four times; 247 recommendations, 180 implemented; Inter-State Council 1990.

April 2007; report April 2010; 1,456 pages; over 310 recommendations.
What did Punchhi say on Governors?
Eminent, neutral, from outside the State, fixed five-year tenure, no chancellorships; majority proved on the floor before dismissal.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Which law wins on a Concurrent subject Art. 254(1): the central law overrides the state law. Art. 254(2): a state law with Presidential assent prevails in that state, but Parliament can later override it (Kerala Professional Colleges Act, 2006, overtaken by NEET rules). Rule: Centre wins by default; the assent exception is itself overridable.
Trap 2 · Six months or for good Laws under Art. 249 and Art. 250 end six months after the resolution or emergency lapses. Laws made during President's Rule (Art. 356) continue until the State Legislature repeals or amends them. An Art. 252 law can be amended only by Parliament. Rule: 249 and 250 = six-month tail; 356 = stays; 252 = only Parliament changes it.
Trap 3 · Levied by whom, kept by whom Art. 268 duties are levied by the Union but collected and kept by the states. Art. 269 and 269A are levied and collected by the Centre and handed to states. Art. 270 taxes are shared on Finance Commission advice. Art. 271 surcharge stays with the Centre. Rule: ask three questions: who levies, who collects, who keeps.
Trap 4 · Appointed by Governor, removed by President The State PSC chairman and members, and the State Election Commissioner (Art. 243K), are appointed by the Governor but removed only by the President. JSPSC members are appointed by the President. Rule: state-level watchdogs are appointed by the Governor, yet removal power sits with the President.
