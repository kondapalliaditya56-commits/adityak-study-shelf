In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The Ombudsman idea began in Sweden (1809)
  • New Zealand (1962) and the UK (1967) followed. India's version is the Lokpal at the Centre and Lokayuktas in the States, first proposed by the First ARC (1966-70)
  • Ten Lokpal Bills failed between 1968 and 2011. The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act passed in December 2013 and became operational on 16 January 2014.
  • The Lokpal has a Chairperson and up to 8 Members:
    • at least 50% judicial and 50% from SC, ST, OBC, minorities or women. The President appoints them on a PM-led selection committee
    • Tenure is 5 years or age 70, with no reappointment
  • It covers the PM (with safeguards), Union Ministers, MPs and Group A, B, C, D officers. It works through an Inquiry Wing and Prosecution Wing, uses the CBI to investigate and has civil court powers. Timelines: inquiry 90 days, sanction 60 days, trial 1 year.
  • Lokayuktas are the State counterpart, appointed by the Governor, with structure and powers that differ from state to state. Their findings are recommendatory
  • The 2013 Act told states to set them up within one year

Key terms

Ombudsman
'Representative of the people': an independent authority that investigates complaints of maladministration and corruption; began in Sweden in 1809

1 · Introduction and global scenario#

  • The Lokpal and Lokayukta draw their idea from the Ombudsman system that began in Sweden in 1809. Ombudsman means "representative of the people": an independent authority to investigate complaints of maladministration and corruption against public officials.

Global adaptations (the handout's list)

Country and yearWhat it did
Sweden (1809)First country with an Ombudsman, as a parliamentary commissioner to ensure executive accountability
New Zealand (1962)First Commonwealth nation to adopt the Ombudsman model; focus on administrative fairness
United Kingdom (1967)Created the Parliamentary Commissioner for Administration for citizens' grievances against departments
OthersCanada, Australia and Denmark later adapted the model to strengthen citizen oversight
  • These models inspired India's effort to create an independent watchdog suited to its democratic and administrative structure.

2 · Evolution and objectives of the 2013 Act#

Road to the Lokpal

  • 1966-1970: First Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC) recommends Lokpal at the Centre and Lokayuktas in the States, including complaints against ministers
  • 1968 to 2011:
    • Ten Lokpal Bills introduced
    • All failed because the Lok Sabha dissolved or politics disagreed
  • 2011: Jan Lokpal movement (India Against Corruption, Anna Hazare) presses for a strong, independent authority
  • December 2013: Lokpal and Lokayuktas Bill passed
  • 16 January 2014: Became operational (as the handout states in this section)

Road to the Lokpal
  • Ten failed Bills: The years: 1968, 1971, 1977, 1985, 1989, 1996, 1998, 2001, 2005, 2011. Hook: the first four are 68, 71, 77, 85, then 89, 96, 98, and the last three are 2001, 2005, 2011.
Handout alert

Handout alert: Two dates are given for the Lokpal becoming operational. The Evolution section says the Act became operational on 16 January 2014 (the Bill was passed in December 2013). The Challenges section says the Lokpal was operationalised in March 2019. The handout does not reconcile them. Read 16 January 2014 as the Act's commencement and March 2019 as the Lokpal's working start, but quote the date given in the question.

Objectives of the Act

  • Independent anti-corruption institutions: a Lokpal at the Centre and Lokayuktas in the States for corruption by public functionaries.
  • Institutional accountability: political executives, bureaucrats and other public servants under a common scrutiny framework.
  • Speedy and fair investigation: a time-bound process for inquiry, prosecution and adjudication.
  • Protect whistleblowers and informers: disclosures in the public interest, with safety and anonymity.
  • Public trust: transparency, ethics and rule of law, aligned with Article 14 (equality before law) and Article 311 (safeguards for civil servants).
  • Harmonise with other vigilance bodies: coordinate with the CVC, CBI and state vigilance commissions to avoid overlap.
  • Memory aid (Six objectives): Institutions, Accountability, Speedy probe, Whistleblowers, Public trust, Harmonise with CVC and CBI: "I Always Seek Wise Public Help."

Also in the handout

  • It represents a legislative culmination of decades of public demand for transparency and accountability in governance.

Grounds for removal

  • Insolvency (adjudged insolvent)
  • Conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude
  • Paid employment outside official duties
  • Infirmity: physical or mental incapacity
  • Conflict of interest: acquiring interests prejudicial to duties
  • Proved misbehaviour or incapacity, after inquiry by the Supreme Court on reference from the President

Removal procedure

  • President refers: In a case of misbehaviour or incapacity the President sends it to the Supreme Court
  • Supreme Court reports: Confirms or rejects misbehaviour or incapacity
  • President may remove: Only on the Court's report confirming it
  • Memory aid (Six grounds): Insolvency, Conviction, Paid job, Infirmity, Conflict of interest, Proved misbehaviour: "I Can Pay If Court Permits."

4 · Jurisdiction and powers of the Lokpal#

  • The Lokpal is the apex anti-corruption ombudsman at the central level.

Who is covered

  • Public functionaries: Prime Minister, Union Ministers, MPs, and officers of Groups A, B, C and D of the Central Government.
  • Prime Minister, with limits: inquiry only under strict conditions (matters of national security, foreign relations or public order) and only with the approval of a full Bench. No inquiry if allegations relate to official acts done in good faith or decisions of the Council of Ministers.
  • PSUs and bodies: all public sector undertakings, statutory bodies, societies and trusts fully or partly funded by the Central Government.
  • NGOs and societies: NGOs receiving foreign contribution above Rs 10 lakh or government grants above Rs 1 crore.
  • Complainants:
    • Any citizen may complain of corruption, abuse of office or disproportionate assets
    • Whistleblower protection is integrated
Who the Lokpal covers
SUMMON
  • SSocieties, trusts, statutory bodies and PSUs funded fully or partly by the Centre
  • UUnion Ministers
  • MMembers of Parliament
  • MMost senior: the Prime Minister (only under strict conditions and with full-Bench approval)
  • OOfficers of Groups A, B, C and D of the Central Government
  • NNGOs with foreign contributions above Rs 10 lakh or government grants above Rs 1 crore

The Lokpal can SUMMON all six kinds of public functionaries and bodies in its jurisdiction. The order is rearranged for memory. (Memory aid, not from the handout.)

Coverage versus limits

  • Covered: PM (conditions), Ministers, MPs, Group A to D officers, PSUs, aided NGOs
  • Not covered:
    • MPs' speech or vote inside Parliament (Art. 105)
    • Matters pending before a court
    • Matters under the exclusive jurisdiction of State Lokayuktas
    • PM outside the three listed matters

Limitations on jurisdiction

  • Cannot inquire into a statement or vote by an MP inside Parliament (protected under Article 105).

5 · Working, procedure, hearing and decisions#

  • The Lokpal is a quasi-judicial institution combining inquiry, investigation and prosecution.

Complaint to conviction

  • Complaint:
    • In writing or on the online portal
    • In good faith, with declaration of truth, naming the public servant and the allegation, with supporting documents if available
  • Preliminary inquiry:
    • By the Inquiry Wing, or the CVC for Group A and B officers
    • Ordinarily within 90 days, extendable to six months with written reasons
    • Accused gets a fair chance to respond
  • Frivolous?: If frivolous or motivated, the Lokpal may dismiss it and impose costs on the complainant
  • Investigation:
    • Referred to the CBI
    • Case registered under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988
    • Investigation within six months, extendable by six with justification
  • Prosecution:
    • Sanction under Section 19 of the PC Act
    • Prosecution Wing files chargesheet in the Special Court
    • Or disciplinary action recommended
  • Trial: Special Courts to finish within one year (up to two for complex cases)
Complaint to conviction

Hearing and decision-making

  • Works in benches of the Chairperson and Members.
  • Hearings generally public unless confidentiality is needed
  • Natural justice and civil court procedure followed
  • Can summon witnesses, require records and record statements on oath.
  • Orders are binding on all concerned public authorities.
  • Annual report:
    • Complaints received, disposed and pending
    • Categories of officials
    • Recommendations and action taken. Submitted to the President, laid before both Houses. Special reports for urgent or high-profile cases

Safeguards and transparency

  • Whistleblower protection under the Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014 (no disclosure of informants' identity).
  • Confidentiality of proceedings and records until prosecution is sanctioned.
  • Digital workflow (post-2020): e-filing, case-tracking dashboards, video hearings.
  • Accountability:
    • Decisions recorded in writing with reasons
    • Officers liable for deliberate delay or manipulation

Also in the handout

  • Be filed in good faith with a declaration that the contents are true to the best of the complainant’s knowledge. - Clearly identify the public servant concerned and the nature of the allegation.
  • The aim is to determine whether a prima facie case exists for investigation.
  • The CBI is required to register a case under relevant sections of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988.

6 · Structure and organisation#

Seven parts of the Lokpal (the handout's table, rebuilt)

Wing or divisionFunctions and responsibilities
1. ChairpersonHeads the Lokpal; gives strategic direction; allocates cases, approves reports, coordinates wings; rank equal to the Chief Justice of India
2. MembersUp to eight; at least 50% judicial; each oversees specific domains (ministries, PSUs, categories of officials)
3. Inquiry WingHeaded by a Director of Inquiry; preliminary investigations to decide if a full investigation is warranted
4. Prosecution WingLed by a Director of Prosecution; files charge sheets and prosecutes in Special Courts under the PC Act, 1988
5. SecretariatAdministrative and logistical support; coordinates Lokpal, CVC and CBI; manages grievances, human resources, records
6. Technical and Forensic CellSet up post-2020 reforms; digital forensics, financial data analytics, blockchain-based tracking
7. Legal and Research DivisionDrafts advisories, comparative legal research, liaison with legislative committees on reforms

Linkages and coordination

  • CBI:
    • Primary investigative agency for referred cases
    • Lokpal supervises timelines and quality
  • CVC:
    • Preliminary inquiries for Group A and B officers
    • Findings go to the Lokpal for further action including prosecution
  • CAG: audit insights on financial irregularities.
  • Income Tax and Enforcement Directorate: trace unaccounted assets, freeze accounts and confiscate property under the PMLA, 2002.
  • Whistleblower channel: secure digital channel as per PIDPI Rules, 2022, with end-to-end encryption.

Administrative oversight and financial autonomy

  • Independent of any ministry
  • Coordinates with the DoPT for staffing and budget approvals
  • Charged on the Consolidated Fund of India, like constitutional bodies such as the CAG or Election Commission.
  • Rule-making: can frame Rules (with Presidential assent) for inquiry, investigation, complaint filing and witness protection.
  • Appoints its own Director of Inquiry, Director of Prosecution and officers from services including IPS, IRS and IA&AS.
  • Memory aid (Seven parts): Chairperson, Members, Inquiry Wing, Prosecution Wing, Secretariat, Technical and Forensic Cell, Legal and Research: "Cool Minds Investigate, Prosecute, Secure Truth, Legally."

7 · Role and challenges#

Key roles

  • Independent oversight: investigates high-ranking officials, including the PM (with safeguards).
  • Integration with the CBI, CVC and Enforcement Directorate to avoid overlap.
  • Whistleblower protection under the Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014.
  • Systemic vigilance: reforms in procurement, appointments and service delivery.
  • Public confidence: a legal forum for corruption grievances at the highest level.

Major challenges

Five groups of challenges (the handout's list)

GroupProblems
(a) Institutional and proceduralDelay in a fully functional Secretariat and Inquiry Wing; no exclusive investigation machinery, dependence on CBI and CVC; weak follow-up on recommendations
(b) Legal and jurisdictionalPM hedged by exceptions; judiciary and state-level officials excluded; NGOs and private contractors using public funds covered but enforcement unclear for lack of audit access
(c) Coordination and overlapOverlapping powers with CVC and Lokayuktas confuse reporting channels; no centralised vigilance database
(d) Resources and capacityInadequate manpower and budget; limited expertise in digital forensics, financial intelligence, international cooperation
(e) Political and administrative resistanceHesitation in referring sensitive cases; ministries and PSUs not cooperating on records
  • Memory aid (Five groups): Institutional, Legal, Coordination, Resources, Political: "I Like Clean Records, Please."

Recent reforms

  • Since operationalisation in March 2019: over 8,000 complaints, only a small fraction reached the investigation stage because of backlogs.
  • Digital Case Management System (2022): better tracking and transparency.
  • Joint investigation protocol with the CVC and CBI (2023): coordination gradually improved.
  • First prosecution sanctions under the Lokpal issued in 2024.

Also in the handout

  • Major Challenges Faced by the Lokpal Despite its statutory strength and promise, the Lokpal’s functioning faces several structural and operational hurdles that have limited its impact since inception.
  • Weak follow-up mechanism for ensuring implementation of Lokpal’s recommendations.
  • Absence of a centralised vigilance database restricts information sharing among agencies.
  • (e) Political and Administrative Resistance 9. Governments have shown hesitation in referring to politically sensitive cases.

9 · Lokayuktas: introduction, composition and appointment#

  • The Lokayukta is the State-level counterpart of the Lokpal: an independent ombudsman for corruption, maladministration and abuse of office by State public functionaries. The idea came from the ARC, 1966. Most States acted much later, through their own laws. The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 gave a uniform framework and directed every State to set up a Lokayukta within one year of its commencement. Structure, powers and effectiveness still vary widely with local laws, political will and capacity.

10 · Lokayuktas: jurisdiction, powers and working#

Jurisdiction (set by each State's law)

  • Chief Minister and Ministers: CM covered in some States (Karnataka, Kerala), excluded in others (Maharashtra).
  • State legislators: MLAs and MLCs covered in most States.
  • Public servants: bureaucrats, employees of state PSUs, municipal bodies, universities, cooperative institutions.
  • Local authorities: Panchayati Raj and municipal functionaries increasingly covered after the 73rd-74th Amendments.
  • Exclusions: Judges of High Courts, members of the State Public Service Commissions and the subordinate judiciary, to avoid overlap.

Powers and functions

Investigative and advisory

  • Investigative powers:
    • Inquire into corruption, abuse of power, maladministration
    • Summon witnesses and demand documents with civil court powers
    • Order preliminary inquiry through the state vigilance or anti-corruption bureau
    • Recommend prosecution or departmental action
  • Advisory and preventive:
    • Advise the State on reforms
    • Monitor ethical codes and conduct rules
    • Annual and special reports to the Governor, laid before the State Legislature
  • Whistleblowers: many States (like Karnataka and Kerala) assure confidentiality and protection from retaliation.

How a Lokayukta works

  • Complaint: Any citizen, written or digital, on corruption or maladministration
  • Preliminary scrutiny: Within jurisdiction and not frivolous?
  • Investigation: If it has merit, through the State Vigilance Department or special inquiry teams
  • Recommendation: Prosecution, disciplinary action or systemic reform
  • Follow-up: State Government to report action taken, usually within 90 days

12 · Lokayuktas: challenges and reforms#

Six challenges (the handout's list)

ChallengeDetail
Lack of uniformityState laws differ in jurisdiction, powers, procedures
Limited enforcement powerRole is recommendatory; government may accept or reject findings
Political resistanceAppointment delays and non-cooperation weaken independence
Resource constraintsStaff shortage, weak investigation machinery, poor digital infrastructure
Overlapping jurisdictionsCoordination issues with State Vigilance Commissions, Anti-Corruption Bureaus and the CVC
Low public awarenessCitizens unaware of complaint mechanisms or fear retaliation

Reforms and way forward

  • Uniform Model Law: a National Model Lokayukta Framework for powers, procedures and accountability.
  • Stronger enforcement: binding authority to initiate prosecution or recommend disciplinary action in proven cases.
  • Institutional autonomy: expenses charged to the State's Consolidated Fund.
  • Digital integration: a National Vigilance Database linking Lokayuktas, Lokpal, CVC and state agencies.
  • Citizen-centric reforms:
    • Awareness campaigns
    • Online complaints in vernacular languages
  • Periodic performance audit: annual review by the State Legislature or an independent committee.
  • Memory aid (Six challenges): Uniformity, Enforcement, Politics, Resources, Overlap, Low awareness: "Under Every Politician's Rule, Ombudsmen Languish."
Handout alert

Handout alert: The Lokpal challenges list says the Act excludes the judiciary and state-level officials, and the Limitations list says the Lokpal cannot touch matters under the exclusive jurisdiction of State Lokayuktas. Yet the Lokayukta section says the 2013 Act directs every State to set one up within a year, while powers are set by each State's own law. Use: Lokpal for the Centre, Lokayukta for the State, State law decides Lokayukta powers.

  • Ombudsman details:
    • 'ombud' is a Swedish word for a representative
    • Finland adopted it in 1919, Denmark in 1955 and Norway in 1962
    • New Zealand (1962) was the first Commonwealth country. It cannot reverse a decision or punish
    • It reports to higher authorities
  • Swedish Ombudsman matters: abuse of discretion, maladministration, administrative corruption, nepotism and discourtesy. Other grievance devices in the world: Administrative Courts (France) and the Procurator System (USSR and China).
  • First ARC (1966-70):
    • Lokpal would deal with complaints against ministers and secretaries (Centre and States), and Lokayukta with other higher officials
    • Judiciary kept outside. Lokpal would be appointed by the President after consulting the CJI, the Speaker of Lok Sabha and the Chairman of Rajya Sabha

    • The ten Bills. LK lists 1968, 1971, 1977, 1985, 1989, 1996, 1998, 2001 and two in 2011 (August and December)
    • It has no 2005 Bill. The handout lists 1968, 1971, 1977, 1985, 1989, 1996, 1998, 2001, 2005 and 2011. For the exam, use the handout
    • Both agree there were ten attempts and the 2011 one was enacted
  • Referral rule:
    • For Group A and B officers the CVC sends its preliminary report back to the Lokpal
    • For Groups C and D the CVC proceeds under its own Act, subject to the Lokpal's reporting and review
  • The Lokpal has superintendence and direction over any investigating agency, including the CBI, in cases it refers. It can attach property of public servants acquired by corrupt means even while prosecution is pending.
  • Punishment under the Prevention of Corruption Act:
    • Maximum raised from 7 to 10 years
    • Minimum 3 years under Sections 7, 8, 9 and 12, and 2 years under Section 15 (attempt)
  • Institutions financed fully or partly by Government come under the Lokpal
  • Institutions merely aided by Government are excluded. Foreign-source donations above Rs 10 lakh a year under FCRA also bring entities in
  • Seven drawbacks:
    • No suo motu action
    • Stress on form of complaint
    • Heavy punishment for false complaints may deter
    • No anonymous complaints
    • Legal help for the accused public servant
    • 7-year limitation period
    • Non-transparent procedure for the PM

Recall sheet#

Cover the right column and answer. Same questions as the flashcards.

What does Ombudsman mean?
"Representative of the people": an independent authority for complaints of maladministration and corruption against officials.
Which countries led the Ombudsman model?
Sweden (1809); New Zealand (1962, first in the Commonwealth); UK (1967).
What did the First ARC recommend?
Lokpal at the Centre and Lokayuktas in the States (1966-1970).
How many Lokpal Bills failed before 2013?
Ten, between 1968 and 2011.
Which movement pressed for a strong Lokpal in 2011?
The Jan Lokpal movement (India Against Corruption, Anna Hazare).
When was the Lokpal Bill passed and when operational?
Passed December 2013; operational 16 January 2014 (the handout also says March 2019 for working operations).
What is the Lokpal's composition?
Chairperson and up to eight Members; at least 50% judicial.
What reservation does the Lokpal have?
At least 50% for SCs, STs, OBCs, minorities or women.
Who sits on the Selection Committee?
PM (head), Speaker, LoP in Lok Sabha, CJI or SC Judge nominated by him, one eminent jurist.
What is the Search Committee's role and composition?
Formed by the Selection Committee to recommend names; at least 50% from SCs, STs, OBCs, minorities or women.
What is the Lokpal's tenure and pay?
Five years or age 70; pay equal to CJI (Chairperson) and SC Judge (Members); no reappointment.
How is a Lokpal member removed for misbehaviour?
President removes on the Supreme Court's report confirming proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
Who is covered by the Lokpal?
PM (with limits), Union Ministers, MPs, Group A to D Central officers, PSUs, funded bodies, NGOs above the thresholds.
What are the PM safeguards?
Only national security, foreign relations or public order; full Bench approval; no inquiry on good-faith official acts or Council of Ministers decisions.
What can the Lokpal not inquire into?
MPs' statements or votes in Parliament (Art. 105); matters pending before a court; matters under exclusive State Lokayukta jurisdiction.
What is the timeline for a preliminary inquiry?
90 days, extendable to six months with written reasons.
What is the CBI's timeline for investigation?
Six months, extendable by another six with justification.
How fast must Special Courts finish trials?
Within one year, extendable to two for complex cases.
Who leads the Inquiry and Prosecution Wings?
A Director of Inquiry and a Director of Prosecution.
How is the Lokpal financially independent?
Its expenses are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.
What does the Technical and Forensic Cell do?
Digital forensics, financial data analytics and blockchain-based tracking (post-2020).
Name three Lokpal challenges.
Dependence on the CBI and CVC; PM jurisdiction hedged by exceptions; judiciary and state officials excluded.
What is the Lokpal's 2024 milestone?
First prosecution sanctions issued.
Who appoints the Lokayukta and who sits on the committee?
The Governor; usually CM (Chairperson), Speaker, LoP and Chief Justice or nominee of the High Court.
Who is generally the Lokayukta?
A retired Chief Justice or Judge of a High Court.
How is a Lokayukta removed?
For misbehaviour or incapacity through a process like High Court judges: judicial inquiry and Governor's approval.
Which State Lokayukta excludes the CM?
Maharashtra (1971); Karnataka and Kerala include the CM.
Which Lokayukta was formed in 2019 under the 2013 Act?
Odisha.
Why are Lokayuktas weak?
Lack of uniformity, only recommendatory findings, political resistance, resource constraints, overlap, low awareness.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Lokpal panel versus CBI and CVC panels The Lokpal Selection Committee has five persons: PM, Speaker, Leader of Opposition, CJI (or nominee) and an eminent jurist. The CBI panel has three (PM, LoP, CJI); the CVC panel is PM, Home Minister and LoP. Rule: the Speaker and the eminent jurist appear only on the Lokpal panel.
Trap 2 · PM is covered, but with limits The Lokpal can inquire into the PM only on matters such as national security, foreign relations or public order, with approval of a full Bench; official acts in good faith and Council of Ministers' decisions are barred. Rule: PM is inside the Act, with strict conditions.
Trap 3 · Who investigates The handout notes the Lokpal depends on the CBI and CVC: it directs the CBI, supervises the work, and the CVC does the preliminary inquiry for Group A and B officers. Rule: Lokpal supervises and sanctions; CBI investigates; CVC helps on Groups A and B.
Trap 4 · Dates and counts The Bill was passed in December 2013 and operational on 16 January 2014. The handout names ten failed Bills (1968 to 2011) and elsewhere speaks of March 2019 for operationalisation of the Lokpal body. Rule: Act 2013, in force 16 January 2014; ten Bills between 1968 and 2011.
