Civil Services: UPSC and SPSC

Low priorityHandout M1 p.3-4
PYQ tags none quoted in the material (still a hook for Prelims facts).
In simple words
  • The Public Service Commissions are the Constitution's recruiters: the UPSC for the Centre and an SPSC for each state (Part XIV, Articles 308-323).
  • They began under British rule. The 1919 Act provided for a Federal PSC, which came in 1926 after the Lee Commission (1924). Madras (1929) set up the first provincial commission. The 1935 Act created three kinds: Federal, Provincial and Joint.
  • The Constitution protects their independence: service conditions cannot be cut after appointment, salaries are paid directly from the Consolidated Fund, and removal follows only the route the Constitution lays down.
  • In practice the shield is thinner. Governments set aside the Commission's advice (57 times in 1950-84, then 82 times in 1984-88). The UPSC has no separate oath and an easier removal process than the EC, CAG or Supreme Court. Recruitment is 'negative': it screens people out instead of finding the best fit.
  • The reform direction in the handout: turn the UPSC into a Central Personnel Agency that recruits positively and runs research on civil-service management.

Civil Services: Meaning and Lateral Entry

Medium priorityHandout M1 p.5-7
PYQ tags
2014 Has the cadre-based Civil Services organization been the cause of slow change in India? Critically examine.
In simple words
  • 'Civil Services' was a term coined by the East India Company around 1765 to separate combatant from non-combatant staff.
  • Civil servants sit inside the larger circle of public servants. Political executives, the judiciary, defence forces and PSU employees are public servants but not civil servants. Groups A and B are the management cadre; Groups C and D the staff side.
  • The Indian system has been closed and cadre-based: sharp grade divides, a narrow entry age band, exams that test general ability. The handout says this breeds conservative, status-quo, rule-bound officers and slows social change.
  • Lateral entry is the fix on offer: bring private-sector specialists into government. It answers Type I error (hiring the wrong person) and Type II error (rejecting the right one), but raises worries about field experience, reservation and favouritism.
  • The generalist-vs-specialist debate is the same story: holistic coordination on one side, techno-economic complexity on the other. The 2nd ARC wants broad orientation plus domain expertise.

All India Services

Low priorityHandout M1 p.7-9
PYQ tags none quoted in the material (still a hook for Prelims facts).
In simple words
  • All India Services are recruited and trained by the Union but serve under both Centre and States. India has three today: IAS, IPS and IFoS (25 cadres, two of them joint).
  • They shrank over time: 9 (1909), 4 (1924), 2 (1947). Abolishing one is called provincialisation.
  • In the Constituent Assembly some doubted them, but Sardar Patel (Father of the AIS) argued for them as the 'Steel Frame' that holds the country together; Article 312 gave them constitutional status.
  • Supporters say AIS bring the best talent, national unity and Centre-State coordination. States reply that the authority-responsibility link is broken: they have limited control over officers they must work with.
  • The same part of the handout gives the protections of civil servants: Articles 309, 310, 311, the Doctrine of Pleasure, and the T.S.R. Subramanian (2013) call for fixed tenure and Civil Services Boards.
Key termIn plain words
ProvincialisationAbolishing an All India Service.
Doctrine of PleasurePublic employment is not a private contract: termination is allowed within constitutional safeguards.
Steel FrameLloyd George's phrase for the Imperial Civil Service; the AIS were later called the Steel Frame of India.
Key points
  • AIS originated under British rule. Officers are recruited and trained by the Union but serve under both Centre and States.
  • 1909:
    • Nine AIS
    • 1924: four
    • 1947: two. Abolishing an AIS is called provincialisation. India has three today: IAS, IPS, IFoS
Constituent Assembly debate
  • Opposition:
    • Nehru Committee Report (1928) not keen
    • Regional interests feared Centre's influence
    • British policy had moved to reduce their number
    • Nehru initially not in favour
  • Retained and given constitutional status under Article 312 largely through Sardar Patel (Father of All India Services). Arguments: states lacked capacity to recruit
  • AIS the Steel Frame maintaining unity. David Lloyd George used 'Steel Frame' for the Imperial Civil Service
  • Article 312 provides for the creation of AIS. 25 cadres, including two joint cadres: AGMUT (Arunachal Pradesh, Goa, Mizoram, Union Territories) and Assam-Meghalaya.

Role of Civil Services in Democracy

Medium priorityHandout M1 p.9-12
PYQ tags
2017 · 250 words Initially Civil Services in India were designed to achieve the goals of neutrality and effectiveness, which seems to be lacking in the present context. Do you agree with the view that drastic reforms are required in Civil Services? Comment.
In simple words
  • The handout expects six qualities from civil servants: neutrality, commitment, anonymity, instrumentality, professionality, impersonality.
  • Neutrality has three layers: class (officers are mostly middle class), cultural (language, caste, religion, region) and political. Political neutrality rests on three confidences: the people's, the ministers' and the civil servants' own.
  • Political neutrality is not policy neutrality: civil servants advise on policy intellectually, never emotionally. They must understand politics, not play politics. The 4Cs (criminalisation, casteism, communalism, corruption) make this hard.
  • Bhaskar Ghose's three types show the choices: Neither Wilt nor Tilt (firm), Incorrigibly Corrupt (find godfathers) and Weather Report Readers (shift with circumstances).
  • Anonymity means the minister takes credit and blame, but RTI, social audits and Citizens' Charters are loosening it. Activism (media, leaks, courts, whistleblowing, the Lakhina Experiment) is a sign of the times, though the handout says it cannot be encouraged.

Working and Performance of Civil Services

Low priorityHandout M1 p.12-13
PYQ tags none quoted in the material (still a hook for Prelims facts).
In simple words
  • Civil services do four jobs: formulate policy, implement it, evaluate it, and adjudicate (quasi-judicial powers, e.g. Collectors in revenue courts). They also give continuity when governments change.
  • Evaluation has a built-in problem: officers judge programmes they themselves ran, a conflict of interest, which is why social audits matter.
  • Report card: relatively good at preserving the constitutional order and law and order; weaker on impartial rule of law (class bias, corruption, interference); mixed on development; shifting after 1991 from direct doer to facilitator.
  • Why performance suffers: political interference, dual control over AIS, arbitrary transfers and short tenures (a DC averages about a year), street-level corruption, weak training and appraisal, wage compression.
  • The 21st century adds globalised problems (COVID-19, terrorism, money laundering), new roles (doer, facilitator, regulator), global yardsticks, expanding rights and a global monoculture (the 3Ms).

In simple words

Medium priorityHandout M2 p.15-19
PYQ tags
2013 · 10 'A national Lokpal, however strong it may be, cannot resolve the problems of immorality in public affairs'. Discuss.
In simple words
  • The ombudsman idea: Sweden 1809, UK 1967. In India the first bill came in 1968, the Act in 2013, and the Lokpal was constituted in 2019 with Justice P.C. Ghose as Chairperson. Maharashtra had the first Lokayukta (1971).
  • State Lokayuktas often became 'paper tigers': recommendatory, misused, and some were abolished (Odisha 1992, Haryana 1999).
  • The Lokpal has nine members including the Chairperson; half judicial, half non-judicial; 50% reserved. Selection committee: PM, Lok Sabha Speaker, Leader of Opposition, CJI or nominee and an eminent jurist.
  • It covers PM (partly), ministers, MPs and Groups A-D, plus bodies funded above Rs 10 lakh. A preliminary inquiry runs within 60 days; a 7-year limit applies to complaints.
  • The PM is covered only partly: no inquiry into decisions on public order, security, atomic energy and space, and a full-bench two-thirds majority is needed.
  • The record is thin: complaints fell from 1,427 (2019-20) to 110 (2020-21) to 12 till August 2021-22.

Concept trap · Whistleblower and anonymity. No anonymous complaints under the 2014 Act; frivolous complaint: 2-year jail; competent authority depends on who is accused. Rule: no anonymity is the first answer to 'what is wrong with the Act'.
Prelims trap · PCA 2018 cuts both ways. Bribe-giving an offence (7-day complaint defence for coercion), jail 3 to 7 years, trial 2 years (max 4), Sec 17A covers retired servants; yet Sec 13(1)(d) was diluted and Sec 17A needs permission for inquiry.
