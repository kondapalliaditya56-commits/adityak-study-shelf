Exam lens#

Prelims hooks

  • Twelve PYQs (1998 to 2026) sit on this chapter: Cripps (1998, 2003, 2009 x2, 2010, 2016, 2022), ministries' resignation (2008, 2012), Individual Satyagraha (2009), Bose and the Forward Bloc (1999, 2026).
  • Cripps is the star: Dominion Status, solely Indian Constituent Assembly, right to secede, Defence stays with the Viceroy, Nehru and Azad as negotiators, Churchill as PM.
  • Know the order and numbers: first three Satyagrahis Bhave, Nehru, Brahma Dutt; VEC 3 of 8 to 8 of 12; 565 + 11 = 576.
  • The August Offer 1940 (advisory council, VEC expansion, CA 'mainly' Indians, Dominion Status, minority veto) is the base for all later offers.

Mains hooks

  • Theme-based answers use the four tags: ToP, Swaraj, COI, Unity. Judge every offer (AO1940, Cripps) on these four.
  • Skeleton for 'why India became independent': INM strong hero + weak colonial villain (economic: WWI, Great Depression, WWII; political; military: INA, Naval Mutiny).
  • Skeleton for 'why did INC and ML both reject AO1940 / Cripps': INC wanted Purna Swaraj and unity; ML wanted two Constituent Assemblies and Pakistan.
  • Skeleton for 'WWII and the INM': 1939 war without consent, ministries out, AO1940, Individual Satyagraha, Cripps failure, the road to Quit India.

Three players, four questions: every offer from 1939 to 1942 is graded on ToP, Swaraj, COI and Unity.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: In September 1939 Britain dragged India into the war without asking, and the Congress answered by pulling its ministries out. Britain tried to buy cooperation back with the August Offer (1940) and the Cripps Mission (1942), but each offer either kept power in the Viceroy's hands or planted seeds of partition. The INC wanted Purna Swaraj and a united India; the Muslim League wanted a veto, then Pakistan; Britain wanted to prevent decolonisation. Every offer failed on one of the four questions, and the failure of Cripps pushed the INM towards Quit India.

  1. A weak villain, a strong hero. Teacher opens the chapter with one question: why did India become independent? The INM was the strong hero. The colonial power was the weak villain, bled by three huge economic shocks in 31 years (WWI, the Great Depression, WWII) and shaken on the military front by the INA and the Naval Mutiny.
    → So to read 1939 to 1947 properly, you need to see who is playing and what they are fighting over.
  2. Three players, four questions. The British, the INC and the ML1906 bargain over four things: transfer of executive power (ToP), the kind of Swaraj, who drafts the Constitution (COI), and Unity (565 Princely States, Pakistan). Britain's best case is no ToP, no Dominion, COI by Britain and a united Indian colony. INC wants ToP to one centre, Purna Swaraj, one COI by Indians. ML wants two centres and two COIs.
    → But first Britain made a move nobody asked it to make.
  3. September 1939: India is taken to war. Britain declared war on Germany and declared India a belligerent. Viceroy Linlithgow36-43 did not consult a single Indian leader. This act of colonial arrogance set the tone for six years.
    → So the INC had to decide what to do, and it did not speak in one voice.
  4. Three views inside the INC. MG: unconditional support, because Britain was the victim of Axis aggression. Bose: Britain's weakness is India's chance, start a Civil Disobedience Movement now. Nehru: no cooperation without Purna Swaraj, but no mass movement either. Most leaders privately wanted an Allied win, since the Axis were dictatorships and India might become a Japanese colony.
    → But the INC did not take any one pole; it named a price for cooperation.
  5. The INC's price and Linlithgow36-43's no. The INC asked for responsible government at the Centre, a post-war Constituent Assembly and a declaration of war aims. In return it would help the war effort. Linlithgow36-43 rejected all three and offered only an advisory committee.
    → So the CWC met at Wardha and the INC walked out of the government.
  6. Wardha 1939: ministries resign. The CWC refused to support the war and directed the Congress ministries to resign, ending the 28-month rule. Linlithgow36-43 panicked and promised Dominion Status after the war. The ML1906 spotted its opening and passed the Lahore Resolution in March 1940.
    → Now Britain needed to bring the INC back to the table.
  7. August Offer 1940. Advisory War Council, a bigger VEC, a post-war Constituent Assembly of mainly Indians, Dominion Status after the war, and no constitution without the consent of minorities. The last clause handed the ML a veto over the Constitution.
    → But both sides said no, for opposite reasons.
  8. Both reject, then Individual Satyagraha. The INC rejected Dominion Status and the backdoor partition in the veto. The ML feared a Hindu-majority Centre would ignore its veto, leaving it with neither power nor Pakistan. MG, pressed to launch a mass movement, chose a limited Individual Satyagraha in October 1940: Bhave, Nehru, Brahma Dutt arrested in that order.
    → Then the war itself turned against Britain.
  9. 1941: the war turns. In 1941 the VEC was expanded to 8 Indians out of 12, but Defence, Home and Finance stayed out of Indian hands. Germany attacked the USSR in June, Japan hit Pearl Harbour in December, and the INC offered support for Purna Swaraj after the war plus immediate ToP.
    → So when Rangoon fell, Britain had to send someone to Delhi.
  10. Cripps 1942: the post-dated cheque. Japan took Rangoon in March 1942. Under US and USSR pressure, Churchill sent Sir Stafford Cripps. The offer: Dominion Status plus foreign policy, a solely Indian CA, a right to secede (565 + 11 = 576 entities) and Defence with the Viceroy during the war. The talks broke on that last point. MG called it a post-dated cheque.
    → The failure of Cripps closes the negotiation route and leads on to Quit India (Chapter 44).

In simple words#

  • The chapter is a three-player game: the British, the INC and the ML1906, all bargaining over four questions: ToP, Swaraj, COI, Unity.
  • September 1939: Britain declared war and declared India a belligerent without consent. The INC refused to support the war and its ministries resigned.
  • August Offer 1940 gave an advisory council, a bigger VEC, a CA of mainly Indians, Dominion Status after the war and a minority veto. INC and ML both said no, for opposite reasons.
  • Individual Satyagraha (Oct 1940) was a small, symbolic protest for freedom of speech: Bhave, Nehru, Brahma Dutt.
  • 1941: VEC became 8 Indians of 12, yet Defence, Home and Finance stayed out of reach.
  • Cripps (March 1942) offered Dominion Status, a solely Indian CA and a right to secede (576 possible entities), but kept Defence with the Viceroy. Talks broke there.
  • MG called it a post-dated cheque. The INC conceded only autonomy for Muslim Majority Provinces, during the talks.

0 · The chapter on one board#

Story link

Because the colonial power was weak and the national movement strong, India became free. Ask: what hit Britain, and what did the INM do with it?

Teacher's board
  • Why India became independent: INM is the strong hero; the colonial power is the weak villain.
  • The weak villain lost power on three fronts: Eco, Pol and MILITARY. Military means INA (1942-45) and the Naval Mutiny, Feb 1946.
  • The weak villain was hit by three huge negative economic events in three decades: WWI (1914-19), GD1929, WWII (1939-45). From 1914 to 1945 is 31 years.
  • Board boxes 'WH' and 'MIH' sit on the two branches; check your notebook for what the letters stand for.
Teacher's board
  • The period 1939-47 is organised on four issues:
  • 1 ToP = Transfer of (E) Executive Power at (C) Centre to Indians, i.e. Viceroy = powerless President + fully responsible government (ministers = representatives of the people + PM).
  • 2 Unity = accession and merger of 565 PS (Princely States) into British India, and Pakistan??
  • 3 After war, kind of Swaraj: Purna Swaraj or not. Reference point: Irwin26-31 statement, 31 Oct 1929: Dominion GRADUALLY.
  • 4 CoI: by Indians or not?
Teacher's board
  • Two viceroys at WW II 1939-45: Linlithgow36-43 and the soldier viceroy Wavell 43-47 (Wavell was the C-in-C under Linlithgow36-43).
  • Under Linlithgow36-43: AO 1940 (the August Offer), Cripps 1942, Bengal Famine 1943, QIM 1942-45, INA 1942-45.
  • Under Wavell 43-47: Wavell Plan, June 1945 (this ended QIM 1942-45), CMP 1946 (3 British Cabinet ministers), Dec 1945-46 elections, INA Trials (1945-46) + Naval Mutiny, Feb 1946.
  • Why it matters: this single board is the map of the next four chapters.
Handout alert
  • This chapter (handout) covers only September 1939 to Cripps 1942. QIM, Famine, INA, Naval Mutiny, Wavell Plan and CMP come in Chapters 44 to 46.

1 · Setting the Stage: Three Players, Four Questions#

Story link

Because three players pulled in three directions, every event 1939-47 is a move in one game. Ask: what does each player want on the four questions?

Teacher's board
  • Three-way politics: British, INC 1885 and ML1906. The board uses 4 parameters for organising content.
  • British aim: prevent decolonisation (national interest). Best case for Britain: no ToP, no Dominion also, COI by Britain, united Indian colony.
  • INC 1885 aim: Purna Swaraj (Swaraj = by Indians). Branches: ToP to 1 centre, Purna Swaraj, Unity, 1 COI by Indians.
  • ML1906 aim: 1940 Pakistan Resolution, which did not use the word Pakistan. Branches: full independence, Pakistan, ToP to 2 centres, 2 COI (one for Pak, one for the rest of India).

1.1 The three players

PlayerWhat it is doing, 1939-1947
BritishRuling India, at war from Sept 1939, negotiating, pressuring and manoeuvring around the same four questions
INCNegotiating, pressuring and manoeuvring for power at the Centre, and refusing to hand over cooperation for free
ML1906 (Muslim League)Manoeuvring for a separate settlement, using the INC's confrontation with the British as its own opening

1.2 The four questions

QuestionWhat it asks
@ToP Transfer of executive power at CentreViceroy a powerless constitutional head; real power with an Indian PM and Council of Ministers. Not just 'more Indians in the Viceroy's council': actual responsible government, ministers accountable to an elected legislature, not to London
@Swaraj What kind of freedom?Purna Swaraj (full independence) or merely Dominion Status, which keeps India tethered within the British Empire
@COI Constitution of IndiaWill Indians draft their own constitution through a Constituent Assembly composed entirely of elected Indians, or will the British keep a hand in it?
@Unity One India or many?A united India? Will the British create Pakistan? Will Princely States merge into India or float as separate entities?

2 · 1939: Where Did the INC Stand @WW II?#

Story link

Because Britain declared India a belligerent without asking, the INC had to choose a stand. Ask: did the INC speak with one voice?

  • When Britain declared war on Germany in September 1939, it simultaneously declared India, a country it ruled without consent, a belligerent.
  • Viceroy Linlithgow36-43 (1936-43) did not bother to consult Indian leaders. This single act of colonial arrogance set the tone for the next 6 years.
  • Inside the INC there was no single position: at least three distinct views.
Teacher's board
  • Board on the declaration: India's state, its economy, army and human resources, were committed to the war; the box also reads 'belligerent / participant'. (Check your notebook for the exact first symbol.)
Teacher's board
  • INC @ WW II (1939) hangs three points on the INC: Purna Swaraj after war, Immediate ToP, No CDM.
  • On one scale left to right: MG = unconditional support to Britain (Britain = victims); Nehru = no CDM, first give us Purna Swaraj, then India fights on the side of the Allied Powers vs the Axis Powers ('Like China'); Netaji Bose = no war support, start CDM.
  • The INC's actual line sits between MG and Nehru.

2.1 Mahatma Gandhi's position

  • Justice was on the Allied side: Nazi Germany and fascist Italy were aggressors; morally India should support Britain unconditionally.
  • To take advantage of Britain's difficulty would be wrong.

2.2 Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose

  • The opposite view: Britain's weakness was India's opportunity; the moment called for an immediate Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM).
Teacher's board
  • Bose's reasoning on the board: post WWI the victors took the colonies of the losers; therefore if the Axis win, India may become a colony of Japan = dictatorship, no self-government to its own people, so all progress may be lost, i.e. GoI 1935.
  • The label on the left reads Indian National Interest: Bose believes that a Japanese takeover of India will be prevented (by his path).
Teacher's board
  • Board on what could be lost: GoI 1935 = Centre: partially responsible government; Province: fully responsible government; plus the possibility of a united India (if more than 50% of Princes sign the IoA).
  • Also 1882 Local: LSG (local self-government). The title word reads 'Super Law' (check your notebook for the first letter).

Bose's road out of the Congress

Chain: Bose and the Forward Bloc
  • Why the Left could not hold Bose. The Left had united to elect him, but the coalition collapsed at Tripuri; the three-way fracture of the Left left Bose with no base.
FactionWhat it did at Tripuri
Communists (CPI), Socialists (CSP, JP Narayan), MN Roy's followersAll voted for Bose when electing him
Gandhian right wingPassed the Pant Resolution: Bose to form his CWC strictly per Gandhi's wishes
CSP (Jayaprakash Narayan) and MN Roy's supportersAbstained on the Pant Resolution, choosing Congress unity over Bose, fearing a permanent split would destroy the anti-imperialist movement
Communists (CPI)The only faction that stayed with Bose and voted against the resolution
  • Result: with the right wing hostile and the Left fragmented, Bose had no base left inside the Congress.
  • The Forward Bloc later became the political base from which Bose launched his international campaign and eventually the INA (Chapter 45).
Teacher's board
  • CPI 1925 was pro-USSR and against capitalist Britain plus against colonialism.
  • The USSR did not join the war on the Allied side until June 1941 (the board writes 'WWI'; read it as this war).
  • Therefore in 1939 the CPI wanted stronger action against Britain: no dilemma.
  • June 1941: USSR joins the Allied Powers, so the CPI becomes pro-USSR, pro-Britain. QIM 1942: CPI opposed.
Handout alert
  • PYQ Prelims 2026 (Forward Bloc): answer (b) 1, 2 and 4. Statement 1: Bose beat Gandhi's candidate and could not function without CWC support. Statement 2: the Left that elected him fractured. Statement 4: CSP (JP) and Royists abstained on the Pant Resolution. Statement 3 is backwards, because the CPI stood by Bose.

2.3 Nehru's position

  • More calibrated: no cooperation in the war unless India received actual independence, Purna Swaraj, but no mass movement during the war itself.

Why most INC leaders privately wanted an Allied victory

  • WWI precedent (1914-19): victors took over the colonies of the defeated; German colonies in Africa went to Britain and France; Ottoman territories became British and French mandates.
  • If the Axis won, all progress made by the INM could be lost and India could become a colony of Japan.
  • The Axis were dictatorships: when they did not give democracy to their own people, why would they give it to the people of their colonies?
  • Still, Britain had dragged India into war without consent, and the INC was not going to hand over cooperation for free.

The INC's price for cooperation

DemandWhat the INC asked Britain to do
i) @ToPImmediately establish Responsible Government at the Centre: Indian ministers, Indian accountability
ii) @COIPromise a Constituent Assembly after the war to frame India's own constitution
iii) @SwarajDeclare its war aims regarding India's future: tell Indians whether they fight for their own freedom or someone else's empire. Margin: this means Purna Swaraj after war
INC offered in returnIndian support for the war: INC asking Indians to join the army, prevent strikes by workers, stop revenue campaigns and protests
Teacher's board
  • The INC's position was a middle path: neither Gandhi's unconditional support, nor Bose's immediate confrontation, nor Nehru's hard 'Purna Swaraj first'. It was a negotiating position.

Linlithgow36-43's answer

  • Linlithgow36-43 rejected all three demands.
  • Offered instead only an advisory committee of Indian politicians from British India and representatives of the Princely States, so Indians could 'advise' the British on the war.
  • Read the device: Indians feel consulted without being given power, classic.
Handout alert
  • PYQ cluster (Prelims 1999): the 'godsent opportunity' leader is Bose. Rajagopalachari: no such view; Jinnah's opening was political (Lahore Resolution); Nehru's stand was no cooperation without Purna Swaraj and no mass movement. Handout alert names Prelims 1999 as 'war aims'; the slide question is the godsent-opportunity one.

8 · Cripps Mission (1942)#

Story link

Because Japan reached Burma, Britain had to show India was a willing partner. Ask: did Cripps offer anything that moved ToP?

8.1 Background

  • The war shifted in the second half of 1941: June 1941 Germany attacked the USSR; December 1941 Japan attacked the United States at Pearl Harbour in Hawaii. The Axis now appeared unstoppable and the fear of Japanese invasion of India became real.
  • December 1941: the INC, overriding the objections of both Nehru and MG, offered to support the British war effort if: (i) Purna Swaraj after the war and (ii) immediate transfer of power at the Centre (Viceroy a powerless President + Indian PM + Indian ministers).
  • March 1942: Japan occupied Rangoon (Burma); India's eastern border was exposed. Under pressure from the US and USSR, now Allied partners, Britain needed to show India was a willing partner, so it sent the Cripps Mission in March 1942.
Teacher's board
  • Board notes: June 1941 = 'Hitler was winning'. A sketch marks Hawaii as a dot off the coast of North, Central and South America.

8.2 Provisions / Offer

Teacher's mnemonic · Uncle Chips (Cripps)
565 + 11 = 576
A crispy chip snaps into smaller and smaller pieces: the Cripps Plan could crumble India into 565 + 11 parts. Crispy chip. Cripps Plan. Remember it.
565Princely States11Provinces576countries technically possible, all with Dominion Status inside the Empire
  • i) (@Swaraj) Dominion Status after the war, plus Indian control over foreign policy. Dominion Status was already in AO1940; now it added foreign affairs. Still Dominion Status, not Purna Swaraj.
  • ii) (@COI) A Constituent Assembly after the war, composed solely of Indians (AO1940: 'mainly'). It consists of: a) elected members from the PLAs after fresh elections under GOI Act 1935; b) nominees of the Princely States. Picture it like the Rajya Sabha model: indirectly elected plus nominated members.
Teacher's board
  • Board on ii: after GoI 1935 we demanded a UAF-based (universal adult franchise) elected CA. The Cripps CA is like the FA of GoI 1935: indirect elections in British India + nominees.
  • Why it matters: this is why the INC later objected to Princely State nominees.
  • iii) (@Unity) The most explosive provision. Once the constitution was framed by the CA, any Province of British India or Princely State that disagreed had the option to: a) not join the Union, and b) frame its own constitution or set up its own union within the British Empire.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher rewrites 'not join' as 'secede from' the Union and boxes the word 'framed': the option arises only once the constitution is framed.
Handout alert
  • Handout vs board: the handout says 'not join the Union'; the board says 'secede from the Union'. Both describe the same right of a dissenting Province or State to stay out; learn 'secede'.
  • Step-by-step logic: 1 CA frames a constitution for India. 2 Members representing a Province or Princely State pass a resolution that they disagree. 3 That Province or State is allowed to secede. 4 It can frame its own constitution or set up its own union within the Empire.
  • Hence technically 565 Princely States + 11 Provinces = 576 countries possible.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board on step 1: CA frames the constitution for India (Dominion), on no veto and a majority-vote basis, so a united India created: the contrast with the AO1940 veto.
  • Why it matters: the veto of 1940 stopped a united India; the Cripps majority rule would have allowed it, but the secession clause then broke it up.
  • iv) (@ToP) If power was transferred during the war itself, Defence would continue under the Viceroy and there would be no dilution of the Viceroy's powers over ministers; he would not become a powerless constitutional President acting on aid and advice of popular ministers; he would retain real power over Defence.
  • It was on this last point that the talks actually broke down. The British adopted a 'take it or leave it' approach on the Viceroy's war powers.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher adds under provision iv: the Viceroy can veto any decision of ministers.
