1 · Part XVIII and the three emergencies#

Part XVIII (Articles 352 to 360) holds the powers to respond to extreme threats: grave peril to the country or a part of it, breakdown of a state's constitutional machinery, or a serious threat to financial stability. The framers meant these as safety valves for sovereignty, unity and integrity, and added checks over time to prevent misuse.

Three emergencies
EmergencyArticleTrigger
National Emergency352War, external aggression, armed rebellion
President's Rule (State or Constitutional Emergency)356State's constitutional machinery has broken down
Financial Emergency360Financial stability or credit of India (or any part) is threatened
Three emergencies
NPF

National (352), President's Rule (356), Financial (360): "No Panic, Folks."

Anchor numbers

352 national, 356 state, 360 financial. 353 effects, 354 money sharing, 355 Union's duty, 357 Parliament legislates for state, 358 Art. 19, 359 enforcement of rights, 359A repealed.

2 · National Emergency: grounds and scope (Art. 352)#

The President may proclaim that the security of the whole nation or a part of it is endangered by war, external aggression or armed rebellion. It can be done also where there is imminent danger, so the Union can act pre-emptively.

Grounds and names
GroundNameNote
WarExternal EmergencyOriginal ground
External aggressionExternal EmergencyOriginal ground
Armed rebellionInternal EmergencyInserted by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978

The earlier phrase "internal disturbance" (used in 1975-77) was removed by the 44th Amendment because it was ambiguous and wide.

Amendments that shaped Art. 352 proclamations
AmendmentChange
38th Amendment Act, 1975Different proclamations on different threats allowed, even while an earlier one is in force; also tried to bar judicial review
42nd Amendment Act, 1976Emergency may cover the entire territory or be restricted to a region or state
44th Amendment Act, 1978Armed rebellion replaces internal disturbance; Cabinet's written recommendation; judicial review restored; stricter approval; Arts. 20 and 21 protected
Which Amendment
344

38th = multiple proclamations (and the bar on review that was later removed), 42nd = area-specific, 44th = the big safeguards: "38 many, 42 area, 44 safe."

3 · Judicial review and the Cabinet safeguard#

The 38th Amendment (1975) tried to bar judicial review of proclamations. The 44th Amendment (1978) removed this shield and restored accountability.

Safeguard on declaring the Emergency

Before 1978
Proclamation issued without prior Cabinet consultation in 1975
44th Amendment
Emergency can be declared only on the written recommendation of the Union Cabinet, not merely the Prime Minister's advice
Result
The Cabinet is the decision-maker; misuse of 1975 corrected

The Supreme Court held

  • Proclamations can be challenged in court
  • Emergency is no longer a political question immune from review

Review is allowed if

  • The decision is mala fide
  • based on irrelevant or non-existent facts
  • or absurd or perverse in reasoning
Grounds of review
MIA

Mala fide, Irrelevant or non-existent facts, Absurd or perverse: "My Inquiry Asks".

4 · Parliamentary approval, duration and renewal#

The 44th Amendment strengthened Parliament's control after the misuse of 1975-77.

Life of a National Emergency

Proclamation
Issued by the President on Cabinet's written recommendation
Within 1 month
Both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha must approve (earlier 2 months)
If Lok Sabha is dissolved
Proclamation continues until 30 days from the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha, provided the Rajya Sabha has approved
Approved
Lasts 6 months
Renewal
Extended 6 months at a time, with fresh approval each time; before 1978 it could last indefinitely
Special majority

Each resolution approving or extending the Emergency needs a special majority in both Houses: a majority of the total membership of the House AND two-thirds of members present and voting.

Before and after the 44th Amendment
FeatureBefore 44th AmendmentCurrent rule
Time for initial approval2 months1 month
ExtensionNo periodic approval neededEvery 6 months
VotingSimple majoritySpecial majority

These changes prevent unilateral and prolonged Emergency rule, reassert Parliament over the Executive, and protect democracy, federalism and civil liberties.

Numbers
1662/3

1 month approval, 6 months first period, 6 months each renewal, 2/3 of present and voting plus majority of total: "1-6-6 and two-thirds."

5 · Revocation (Art. 352(2) and (7))#

The President may withdraw the proclamation at any time by a later proclamation, and this needs no parliamentary approval. The 44th Amendment gave the Lok Sabha more power:

Ways Emergency can end

By President
Subsequent proclamation, no Parliament approval needed
By Lok Sabha
A resolution disapproving continuation obliges the President to revoke immediately
Special sitting
If one-tenth of total Lok Sabha membership gives written notice to the Speaker (in session) or the President (not in session), a special sitting within 14 days must consider a disapproval resolution
Disapproval versus approval of continuance
FeatureResolution for disapprovalResolution for approval of continuance
HouseLok Sabha onlyBoth Houses
MajoritySimple majority (more votes for than against)Special majority under Art. 352(4): majority of total membership and two-thirds of those present and voting
EffectMandatory revocation by the PresidentEmergency continues another 6 months
Shortcut

Disapprove = Lok Sabha alone + simple majority. Approve or extend = both Houses + special majority. One-tenth of the House can force a sitting within 14 days.

6 · Effects of a National Emergency#

Three domains change: Centre-State relations, tenure of the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies, and Fundamental Rights under Part III.

Effect on Centre-State relations
AreaRule during the Emergency
Executive (Art. 353(a))Union may direct any state on how to exercise its executive power, on any subject (normally limited to Arts. 256 and 257)
Legislative (Art. 353(b))Parliament may legislate on any State List subject; state legislatures continue but are subordinate; such laws stay valid up to six months after the Emergency ends; President may issue ordinances on State subjects (Art. 123) when Parliament is not in session; these powers apply to all states (42nd Amendment)
Financial (Art. 354 in the handout's article table)President may alter the sharing of finances (tax-sharing, cut or cancel grants); such orders continue until the end of the financial year in which the Emergency ends and must be laid before Parliament
Three relations
ELF

Executive (directions on any subject), Legislative (State List), Financial (tax-sharing and grants): "Every Law Flows to the Centre."

Life of the Lok Sabha and Assemblies (Arts. 83(2) and 172(1))

  • Parliament may extend the normal 5-year Lok Sabha term one year at a time, for as long as the Emergency continues.
  • The extension cannot go beyond six months after the Emergency is lifted.
  • Example in the handout: the 5th Lok Sabha (1971-77) was twice extended under the Emergency of 1975-77.
  • Same one-year rule and six-month limit for State Assemblies.

Fundamental Rights: Articles 358 and 359

Article 358 versus Article 359
FeatureArticle 358Article 359
ScopeOnly Article 19Any specified FR except Arts. 20 and 21
Automatic?Yes, automatic suspensionNo, needs a Presidential Order
GroundsOnly war or external aggressionWar, external aggression or armed rebellion
DurationEntire Emergency periodPeriod specified in the Order
AreaEntire countryWhole or part of India
EnforcementThe right itself is suspendedOnly the remedy (right to move court) is suspended
Similarity

Both protect only Emergency-related laws and matching executive actions from judicial challenge.

Post-44th safeguards

Art. 19 cannot be suspended in an Emergency based on armed rebellion (only war or external aggression). Arts. 20 and 21 (protection in respect of conviction; life and personal liberty) cannot be suspended under Art. 359. Only laws related to the Emergency are protected.

358 and 359
A19/2021

358 = Art. 19 automatically, war or aggression only; 359 = by Order, any FR except 20 and 21, remedy only: "358 Auto, 359 Order."

8 · President's Rule: grounds, approval and duration (Art. 356)#

Art. 355 makes it the Union's duty to protect states and ensure their governance runs according to the Constitution. When the state's constitutional machinery has broken down, the President may act under Art. 356: "a situation has arisen in which the government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution." It is exceptional, not routine.

Grounds
BasisDetail
(a) Report of the GovernorPresident acts on it
(b) Any other informationAlso sufficient
Art. 365If a state fails to comply with the Union's directions, that failure may justify Art. 356

The test is whether constitutional machinery has broken down, not a criminal standard.

Life of President's Rule

Proclamation
Issued by the President under Art. 356
Within 2 months
Both Houses must approve (simple majority of those present and voting)
If Lok Sabha dissolved
Rajya Sabha's approval keeps it alive up to 30 days from the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha
Approved
Lasts 6 months at a time
Extension
Renewed every 6 months with Parliament's approval; cumulative maximum 3 years
Revocation
Any time by President through a later proclamation; no parliamentary approval needed
Voting

Approval of President's Rule is not a constitutional amendment, so simple majority of those present and voting in each House is enough.

44th Amendment restriction

Extension beyond one year is allowed only if both conditions are met:

  • A National Emergency (Art. 352) is in force in the entire country or in the whole or any part of the state; AND
  • The Election Commission certifies that elections to the state Assembly cannot be held because of difficulties.
One year plus
EE

Emergency in force AND Election Commission certificate: "E and E, both needed after year one."

Numbers

2 months approval, 6 months each, 3 years max, beyond 1 year needs Art. 352 emergency + ECI certificate.

9 · Consequences of President's Rule#

The President acquires extraordinary executive and legislative powers over that state.

What changes
AreaConsequence
ExecutivePresident takes over state government functions and any powers of the Governor or other state authority; the Council of Ministers headed by the CM is dismissed; Governor administers for the President, helped by the Chief Secretary or advisors
LegislativeParliament exercises the state legislature's powers; the state legislature is suspended or dissolved; Parliament may delegate law-making to the President or another authority; delegated laws can confer powers and duties on the Centre and its officers (Art. 357)
Financial and ordinancesPresident may authorise expenditure from the State Consolidated Fund when Parliament is not in session (pending later sanction); may issue ordinances when Parliament is not sitting
Laws madeLaws by Parliament, the President or the specified authority stay in force after the Proclamation ends; the state legislature may repeal, amend or re-enact them
High CourtPresident cannot assume the High Court's powers or suspend its provisions

Not necessarily

  • Dissolution of the State Assembly (it may be suspended instead)
  • dissolution of local bodies

Always

  • Removal of the Council of Ministers
  • state legislature's powers passed to Parliament
  • High Court untouched
After PR
CPH

CM and ministers go, Parliament makes laws, High Court stays: "Council Packs, High court Holds."

Shortcut

FRs not affected by President's Rule (unlike a National Emergency). Laws made in PR outlive PR.

10 · Use, misuse and judicial review of Art. 356#

President's Rule has been imposed on about 134 occasions since 1950, roughly twice a year. The first was Punjab in 1951. Almost every state has seen it.

Political pattern
YearEvent
1977After general elections the Janata government imposed PR in nine Congress-ruled states (assemblies no longer represented the electorate)
1980Congress returned and did likewise in nine states
1992Congress government imposed PR in three BJP-ruled states: Madhya Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan (alleged non-implementation of a ban on certain religious organisations)
1994Bommai: SC upheld one such proclamation (secularism basic feature) but did not uphold Nagaland (1988), Karnataka (1989), Meghalaya (1991)

Ambedkar hoped Art. 356 would be a "dead letter", a last resort. A commentator later wrote: "Dr. Ambedkar is dead and the Articles are very much alive."

Judicial review

Judicial review of Art. 356

1975
38th Amendment made the President's satisfaction final and beyond court challenge
1978
44th Amendment removed that protection; review restored
1994
S.R. Bommai v. Union of India sets the principles
Bommai principles (S.R. Bommai v. Union of India, 1994)
PrincipleContent
ReviewableA proclamation can be examined by courts
Reasoned satisfactionMust rest on relevant material; extraneous factors, mala fides or perversity can strike it down
Burden on CentreUnion must show relevant material
ScopeCourts do not judge correctness or sufficiency of material, only its relevance
RemediesCourt may restore the dismissed state government and revive the legislature
ProcedureAssembly to be suspended, not dissolved, until Parliament approves; if Parliament refuses, reactivate it
SecularismA basic feature; anti-secular conduct can justify action under Art. 356
Floor testLoss of majority to be tested on the floor of the House, not assumed
Change at CentreA new party at the Centre cannot automatically dismiss other parties' state ministries
Exceptional powerUse sparingly, only where normal governance is impossible
Bommai principles
RRBSRPSFCE

Reviewable, Reasoned material, Burden on Centre, Scope (relevance only), Remedies (restore), Procedure (suspend), Secularism, Floor test, Change at Centre, Exceptional: "Right Rules Bind States; Revive Polity, Save Federal Constitution Every time."

11 · Proper and improper uses of Art. 356#

Drawing on the Sarkaria Commission and Bommai: Art. 356 is for clear, provable breakdowns only.

Proper grounds

  • Hung Assembly after elections
  • majority party declines to form government and no alternative
  • resignation after losing a confidence vote with no alternative
  • deliberate defiance of lawful Union directions
  • internal subversion (e.g. fomenting violent revolt)
  • physical breakdown (wilful refusal to discharge duties endangering security)

Improper uses

  • No exploration of alternatives
  • Governor's unverified assessment without a floor test
  • punishing a ministry for heavy defeat in Lok Sabha elections (1977 and 1980 alleged)
  • trivial internal disturbances
  • administrative failure or corruption alone
  • no prior warning (except sudden emergency)
  • intra-party disputes or extraneous purposes
Proper grounds
HMRDIP

Hung assembly, Majority refuses, Resignation with no alternative, Defiance of directions, Internal subversion, Physical breakdown: "Hard Moments Require Decisive Intervention, Provably."

Improper uses
FGPTAAI

Failure to explore alternatives, Governor's unverified view, Punitive after Lok Sabha defeat, Trivial disturbances, Administrative failure or corruption alone, Absence of warning, Intra-party or extraneous purposes: "Few Governors Play Tricks, And All Ignore rules."

Shortcut

Hung, Refuse, Resign, Defy, Subvert, Break = proper. Alternatives, Assessment, Punish, Trivial, Admin, Notice, Party = improper. Principle in brief: the Sarkaria and Bommai guidance confines Art. 356 to clear, provable breakdowns and bars use for routine political or administrative disagreements.

12 · National Emergency versus President's Rule#

Comparison (handout's table, rebuilt)
FeatureNational Emergency (Art. 352)President's Rule (Art. 356)
When applicableSecurity of India or any part threatened by war, external aggression or armed rebellionState government cannot be carried on per the Constitution (reasons need not be war, aggression or rebellion)
Effect on state institutionsState executive and legislature continue to function; Centre gets concurrent powers in administration and legislationState executive dismissed; legislature suspended or dissolved; Centre rules through the Governor and Parliament makes laws
Legislative powerParliament legislates on State List; cannot delegate this power to another bodyParliament may delegate law-making to the President or an authority he specifies ("President's Acts")

ScopeAlters Centre-State relations across all states while operativeAlters relations only between Centre and the state under PR
Voting thresholdSpecial majoritySimple majority of present and voting

Memory pairs

352: no limit, special majority, FRs hit, LS can revoke. 356: 3 years, simple majority, FRs safe, President revokes.

Handout alert

The comparison table says Art. 356 has no parliamentary revocation provision, yet the text of President's Rule says the President revokes by proclamation without approval. Treat this as consistent: the only way to end PR is a later Presidential proclamation, whereas a National Emergency can also be ended by a Lok Sabha disapproval resolution.

13 · Financial Emergency (Art. 360)#

The framers saw that economic instability can threaten the country like war. Art. 360 lets the President proclaim a Financial Emergency if the financial stability or credit of India or any part of its territory is threatened. This reflects India's quasi-federal character: federal in normal times, unitary under stress, especially in finance.

Grounds and approval
ItemRule
GroundFinancial stability of India endangered OR creditworthiness of India or any region compromised
Judicial review38th Amendment (1975) made satisfaction final; 44th Amendment (1978) removed that, so review is allowed
ApprovalLaid before both Houses; approval within 2 months
If Lok Sabha dissolvedRajya Sabha approval keeps it alive; Lok Sabha must approve within 30 days of re-constitution
MajoritySimple majority (present and voting)
DurationNo maximum; no recurring approval; continues until revoked
RevocationPresident, any time, fresh proclamation, no parliamentary approval needed

National Emergency

  • Approval in 1 month
  • 6-month renewals
  • special majority

Financial Emergency

  • Approval in 2 months
  • no renewal
  • simple majority
  • no maximum period

Effects

Fiscal federalism changes
AreaEffect
Central supervision of state financesUnion may direct states to follow financial propriety and budget discipline
Salaries and allowancesReduction in pay and perks of state government employees; Money Bills of the state legislature reserved for the President after passage
Union institutionsReduction in pay and allowances of Central government employees; salary cuts for judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts

Members of the Constituent Assembly like H.N. Kunzru warned about state autonomy; Ambedkar said such extraordinary powers mirror global precedents such as the US National Industrial Recovery Act, 1933, made to fight the Great Depression.

Never used

No Financial Emergency has ever been declared. Even the 1991 Balance of Payments crisis was managed through structural reforms, external borrowing (IMF) and fiscal consolidation without invoking Art. 360.

Art. 360 facts
2NSJ

2 months, No limit, Simple majority, Judges' pay can be cut: "Two No Simple Judges".

14 · How emergencies alter the order#

In a valid proclamation the Centre's reach widens: Parliament legislates on state subjects, the Centre gives binding directions to state executives, and certain FR protections can be cut. The working federal balance becomes more unitary without amending the Constitution; Ambedkar noted the Constitution is federal in normal times but able to work as unitary when necessity demands. Art. 356 especially has drawn judicial review and controversy; Sarkaria and Punchhi urged restraint and prior consultation.
