In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Part XVIII (Articles 352-360) gives the Union special powers in a crisis. There are three kinds: National (Art. 352), President's Rule (Art. 356) and Financial (Art. 360).
  • A National Emergency needs war, external aggression or armed rebellion (imminent danger also counts). The 44th Amendment (1978) replaced 'internal disturbance' and demanded the Cabinet's written recommendation.
  • Both Houses must approve within 1 month by a special majority
  • It lasts 6 months and is renewed 6 months at a time. The Lok Sabha alone can force revocation by a simple majority
  • During the Emergency the Centre commands the states, Art. 358 suspends Art. 19 automatically, and Art. 359 suspends the remedy for other FRs, but never Arts. 20 and 21.
  • President's Rule follows a breakdown in a state: approval in 2 months (simple majority), 6 months at a time, 3 years at most. The Bommai case (1994) made it reviewable.
  • A Financial Emergency needs approval in 2 months, has no time limit, can cut pay (even of judges) and has never been declared.

Key terms

Proclamation of Emergency
The President's formal order that starts an emergency under Art. 352, 356 or 360
External Emergency
National Emergency on the ground of war or external aggression
Internal Emergency
National Emergency on the ground of armed rebellion (44th Amendment, 1978)
Special majority
Majority of the total membership of the House AND two-thirds of members present and voting
Disapproval resolution
Lok Sabha only, simple majority; forces the President to revoke the National Emergency
President's Rule
Takeover of a state when its constitutional machinery breaks down (Art. 356); the Council of Ministers is dismissed
President's Acts
Laws for a state made by the President, because Parliament may delegate law-making during President's Rule
Floor test
Bommai rule: loss of majority must be proved on the floor of the House, not assumed

1 · Part XVIII and the three emergencies#

  • Part XVIII (Articles 352 to 360) holds the powers to respond to extreme threats: grave peril to the country or a part of it, breakdown of a state's constitutional machinery, or a serious threat to financial stability. The framers meant these as safety valves for sovereignty, unity and integrity, and added checks over time to prevent misuse.

Three emergencies

EmergencyArticleTrigger
National Emergency352War, external aggression, armed rebellion
President's Rule (State or Constitutional Emergency)356State's constitutional machinery has broken down
Financial Emergency360Financial stability or credit of India (or any part) is threatened
  • Memory aid (Three emergencies): National (352), President's Rule (356), Financial (360): "No Panic, Folks."
  • Shortcut: Anchor numbers. 352 national, 356 state, 360 financial. 353 effects, 354 money sharing, 355 Union's duty, 357 Parliament legislates for state, 358 Art. 19, 359 enforcement of rights, 359A repealed.

Also in the handout

  • This equips the Union to respond to changing security environments dynamically.

2 · National Emergency: grounds and scope (Art. 352)#

  • The President may proclaim that the security of the whole nation or a part of it is endangered by war, external aggression or armed rebellion. It can be done also where there is imminent danger, so the Union can act pre-emptively.

Grounds and names

GroundNameNote
WarExternal EmergencyOriginal ground
External aggressionExternal EmergencyOriginal ground
Armed rebellionInternal EmergencyInserted by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978
  • The earlier phrase "internal disturbance" (used in 1975-77) was removed by the 44th Amendment because it was ambiguous and wide.

Amendments that shaped Art. 352 proclamations

AmendmentChange
38th Amendment Act, 1975Different proclamations on different threats allowed, even while an earlier one is in force; also tried to bar judicial review
42nd Amendment Act, 1976Emergency may cover the entire territory or be restricted to a region or state
44th Amendment Act, 1978Armed rebellion replaces internal disturbance; Cabinet's written recommendation; judicial review restored; stricter approval; Arts. 20 and 21 protected
  • Memory aid (Which Amendment): 38th = multiple proclamations (and the bar on review that was later removed), 42nd = area-specific, 44th = the big safeguards: "38 many, 42 area, 44 safe."

Also in the handout

  • Importantly, the constitutional authority extends not only to events that have already occurred but also to situations where there is “imminent danger” of such threats. 3 · Judicial review and the Cabinet safeguard#

  • The 38th Amendment (1975) tried to bar judicial review of proclamations. The 44th Amendment (1978) removed this shield and restored accountability.

Safeguard on declaring the Emergency

  • Before 1978: Proclamation issued without prior Cabinet consultation in 1975
  • 44th Amendment: Emergency can be declared only on the written recommendation of the Union Cabinet, not merely the Prime Minister's advice
  • Result:
    • The Cabinet is the decision-maker
    • Misuse of 1975 corrected

Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)

  • The Supreme Court held:
    • Proclamations can be challenged in court
    • Emergency is no longer a political question immune from review
  • Review is allowed if:
    • The decision is mala fide
    • Based on irrelevant or non-existent facts
    • Or absurd or perverse in reasoning
  • Memory aid (Grounds of review): Mala fide, Irrelevant or non-existent facts, Absurd or perverse: "My Inquiry Asks".

Also in the handout

  • Result: Allows legislative continuity during national crises while maintaining time-bound democratic accountability.

4 · Parliamentary approval, duration and renewal#

  • The 44th Amendment strengthened Parliament's control after the misuse of 1975-77.

Life of a National Emergency

  • Proclamation: Issued by the President on Cabinet's written recommendation
  • Within 1 month: Both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha must approve (earlier 2 months)
  • If Lok Sabha is dissolved: Proclamation continues until 30 days from the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha, provided the Rajya Sabha has approved
  • Approved: Lasts 6 months
  • Renewal:
    • Extended 6 months at a time, with fresh approval each time
    • Before 1978 it could last indefinitely
Life of a National Emergency
  • Special majority: Each resolution approving or extending the Emergency needs a special majority in both Houses: a majority of the total membership of the House AND two-thirds of members present and voting.

Before and after the 44th Amendment

FeatureBefore 44th AmendmentCurrent rule
Time for initial approval2 months1 month
ExtensionNo periodic approval neededEvery 6 months
VotingSimple majoritySpecial majority
  • These changes prevent unilateral and prolonged Emergency rule, reassert Parliament over the Executive, and protect democracy, federalism and civil liberties.
  • Memory aid (Numbers): 1 month approval, 6 months first period, 6 months each renewal, 2/3 of present and voting plus majority of total: "1-6-6 and two-thirds."

Also in the handout

  • Once such a Proclamation is issued by the President, it cannot remain in operation indefinitely without democratic scrutiny.

5 · Revocation (Art. 352(2) and (7))#

  • The President may withdraw the proclamation at any time by a later proclamation, and this needs no parliamentary approval. The 44th Amendment gave the Lok Sabha more power:

Ways Emergency can end

  • By President: Subsequent proclamation, no Parliament approval needed
  • By Lok Sabha: A resolution disapproving continuation obliges the President to revoke immediately
  • Special sitting: If one-tenth of total Lok Sabha membership gives written notice to the Speaker (in session) or the President (not in session), a special sitting within 14 days must consider a disapproval resolution

Disapproval versus approval of continuance

FeatureResolution for disapprovalResolution for approval of continuance
HouseLok Sabha onlyBoth Houses
MajoritySimple majority (more votes for than against)Special majority under Art. 352(4): majority of total membership and two-thirds of those present and voting
EffectMandatory revocation by the PresidentEmergency continues another 6 months
  • Shortcut: Shortcut. Disapprove = Lok Sabha alone + simple majority. Approve or extend = both Houses + special majority. One-tenth of the House can force a sitting within 14 days.

Also in the handout

  • This ensures that even a minority of the House can initiate democratic scrutiny and veto power over emergency continuation.

6 · Effects of a National Emergency#

  • Three domains change: Centre-State relations, tenure of the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies, and Fundamental Rights under Part III.

Effect on Centre-State relations

AreaRule during the Emergency
Executive (Art. 353(a))Union may direct any state on how to exercise its executive power, on any subject (normally limited to Arts. 256 and 257)
Legislative (Art. 353(b))Parliament may legislate on any State List subject; state legislatures continue but are subordinate; such laws stay valid up to six months after the Emergency ends; President may issue ordinances on State subjects (Art. 123) when Parliament is not in session; these powers apply to all states (42nd Amendment)
Financial (Art. 354 in the handout's article table)President may alter the sharing of finances (tax-sharing, cut or cancel grants); such orders continue until the end of the financial year in which the Emergency ends and must be laid before Parliament
  • Memory aid (Three relations): Executive (directions on any subject), Legislative (State List), Financial (tax-sharing and grants): "Every Law Flows to the Centre."

Life of the Lok Sabha and Assemblies (Arts. 83(2) and 172(1))

  • Parliament may extend the normal 5-year Lok Sabha term one year at a time, for as long as the Emergency continues.
  • The extension cannot go beyond six months after the Emergency is lifted.
  • Example in the handout: the 5th Lok Sabha (1971-77) was twice extended under the Emergency of 1975-77.
  • Same one-year rule and six-month limit for State Assemblies.

Fundamental Rights: Articles 358 and 359

FeatureArticle 358Article 359
ScopeOnly Article 19Any specified FR except Arts. 20 and 21
Automatic?Yes, automatic suspensionNo, needs a Presidential Order
GroundsOnly war or external aggressionWar, external aggression or armed rebellion
DurationEntire Emergency periodPeriod specified in the Order
AreaEntire countryWhole or part of India
EnforcementThe right itself is suspendedOnly the remedy (right to move court) is suspended
  • Similarity: Both protect only Emergency-related laws and matching executive actions from judicial challenge.
Handout alert

Post-44th safeguards: Art. 19 cannot be suspended in an Emergency based on armed rebellion (only war or external aggression). Arts. 20 and 21 (protection in respect of conviction; life and personal liberty) cannot be suspended under Art. 359. Only laws related to the Emergency are protected.

  • Memory aid (358 and 359):
    • 358 = Art. 19 automatically, war or aggression only
    • 359 = by Order, any FR except 20 and 21, remedy only: "358 Auto, 359 Order."

Also in the handout

  • These effects are primarily classified into three significant domains: - Transformation of Centre–State Relations - Changes in tenure of the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies - Restrictions on Fundamental Rights under Part III Effect on Centre–State Relations During a National Emergency, the federal structure tilts strongly toward unitary control.
  • These changes were intended to make arbitrary exercise of Emergency powers far harder.

8 · President's Rule: grounds, approval and duration (Art. 356)#

  • Art. 355 makes it the Union's duty to protect states and ensure their governance runs according to the Constitution. When the state's constitutional machinery has broken down, the President may act under Art. 356: "a situation has arisen in which the government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution." It is exceptional, not routine.

Grounds

BasisDetail
(a) Report of the GovernorPresident acts on it
(b) Any other informationAlso sufficient
Art. 365If a state fails to comply with the Union's directions, that failure may justify Art. 356
  • The test is whether constitutional machinery has broken down, not a criminal standard.

Life of President's Rule

  • Proclamation: Issued by the President under Art. 356
  • Within 2 months: Both Houses must approve (simple majority of those present and voting)
  • If Lok Sabha dissolved: Rajya Sabha's approval keeps it alive up to 30 days from the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha
  • Approved: Lasts 6 months at a time
  • Extension:
    • Renewed every 6 months with Parliament's approval
    • cumulative maximum 3 years
  • Revocation:
    • Any time by President through a later proclamation
    • no parliamentary approval needed
Life of President's Rule
  • Voting: Approval of President's Rule is not a constitutional amendment, so simple majority of those present and voting in each House is enough.

44th Amendment restriction

  • Extension beyond one year is allowed only if both conditions are met:
  • A National Emergency (Art. 352) is in force in the entire country or in the whole or any part of the state
  • AND
  • The Election Commission certifies that elections to the state Assembly cannot be held because of difficulties.
  • Memory aid (One year plus): Emergency in force AND Election Commission certificate: "E and E, both needed after year one."
  • Shortcut: Numbers. 2 months approval, 6 months each, 3 years max, beyond 1 year needs Art. 352 emergency + ECI certificate.

Also in the handout

  • Government summaries and historical records refer to the Commission’s report and its role in shaping reform.

9 · Consequences of President's Rule#

  • The President acquires extraordinary executive and legislative powers over that state.

What changes

AreaConsequence
ExecutivePresident takes over state government functions and any powers of the Governor or other state authority; the Council of Ministers headed by the CM is dismissed; Governor administers for the President, helped by the Chief Secretary or advisors
LegislativeParliament exercises the state legislature's powers; the state legislature is suspended or dissolved; Parliament may delegate law-making to the President or another authority; delegated laws can confer powers and duties on the Centre and its officers (Art. 357)
Financial and ordinancesPresident may authorise expenditure from the State Consolidated Fund when Parliament is not in session (pending later sanction); may issue ordinances when Parliament is not sitting
Laws madeLaws by Parliament, the President or the specified authority stay in force after the Proclamation ends; the state legislature may repeal, amend or re-enact them
High CourtPresident cannot assume the High Court's powers or suspend its provisions

Not necessarily happens versus always happens

  • Not necessarily:
    • Dissolution of the State Assembly (it may be suspended instead)
    • Dissolution of local bodies
  • Always:
    • Removal of the Council of Ministers
    • State legislature's powers passed to Parliament
    • High Court untouched
  • Memory aid (After PR): CM and ministers go, Parliament makes laws, High Court stays: "Council Packs, High court Holds."
  • Shortcut: Shortcut. FRs not affected by President's Rule (unlike a National Emergency). Laws made in PR outlive PR.

Also in the handout

  • This takeover of executive functions is the concrete reason why an Article 356 proclamation is commonly termed ‘President’s Rule’.

10 · Use, misuse and judicial review of Art. 356#

  • President's Rule has been imposed on about 134 occasions since 1950, roughly twice a year. The first was Punjab in 1951. Almost every state has seen it.

Political pattern

YearEvent
1977After general elections the Janata government imposed PR in nine Congress-ruled states (assemblies no longer represented the electorate)
1980Congress returned and did likewise in nine states
1992Congress government imposed PR in three BJP-ruled states: Madhya Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan (alleged non-implementation of a ban on certain religious organisations)
1994Bommai: SC upheld one such proclamation (secularism basic feature) but did not uphold Nagaland (1988), Karnataka (1989), Meghalaya (1991)
  • Ambedkar hoped Art. 356 would be a "dead letter", a last resort. A commentator later wrote: "Dr. Ambedkar is dead and the Articles are very much alive."

Judicial review of Art. 356

  • 1975: 38th Amendment made the President's satisfaction final and beyond court challenge
  • 1978:
    • 44th Amendment removed that protection
    • Review restored
  • 1994: S.R. Bommai v. Union of India sets the principles

Bommai principles (S.R. Bommai v. Union of India, 1994)

PrincipleContent

Reasoned satisfactionMust rest on relevant material; extraneous factors, mala fides or perversity can strike it down

RemediesCourt may restore the dismissed state government and revive the legislature
ProcedureAssembly to be suspended, not dissolved, until Parliament approves; if Parliament refuses, reactivate it

11 · Proper and improper uses of Art. 356#

  • Drawing on the Sarkaria Commission and Bommai: Art. 356 is for clear, provable breakdowns only.

Justified versus unwarranted

  • Proper grounds:
    • Hung Assembly after elections
    • Majority party declines to form government and no alternative
    • Resignation after losing a confidence vote with no alternative
    • Deliberate defiance of lawful Union directions
    • Internal subversion (e.g. fomenting violent revolt)
    • Physical breakdown (wilful refusal to discharge duties endangering security)
  • Improper uses:
    • No exploration of alternatives
    • Governor's unverified assessment without a floor test
    • Punishing a ministry for heavy defeat in Lok Sabha elections (1977 and 1980 alleged)
    • Trivial internal disturbances
    • Administrative failure or corruption alone
    • No prior warning (except sudden emergency)
    • Intra-party disputes or extraneous purposes
  • Memory aid (Proper grounds): Hung assembly, Majority refuses, Resignation with no alternative, Defiance of directions, Internal subversion, Physical breakdown: "Hard Moments Require Decisive Intervention, Provably."
  • Memory aid (Improper uses): Failure to explore alternatives, Governor's unverified view, Punitive after Lok Sabha defeat, Trivial disturbances, Administrative failure or corruption alone, Absence of warning, Intra-party or extraneous purposes: "Few Governors Play Tricks, And All Ignore rules."
  • Shortcut: Shortcut. Hung, Refuse, Resign, Defy, Subvert, Break = proper. Alternatives, Assessment, Punish, Trivial, Admin, Notice, Party = improper. Principle in brief: the Sarkaria and Bommai guidance confines Art. 356 to clear, provable breakdowns and bars use for routine political or administrative disagreements.

Also in the handout

  • These are heavy constitutional tools and therefore are covered by clear textual rules and parliamentary oversight.
  • The following situations are identified as improper bases for imposing President’s Rule: - Failure to Explore Alternatives — where a ministry resigns or loses majority and the Governor recommends direct Central rule without first probing the possibility of an alternative majority government.

13 · Financial Emergency (Art. 360)#

  • The framers saw that economic instability can threaten the country like war. Art. 360 lets the President proclaim a Financial Emergency if the financial stability or credit of India or any part of its territory is threatened. This reflects India's quasi-federal character: federal in normal times, unitary under stress, especially in finance.

14 · How emergencies alter the order#

  • In a valid proclamation the Centre's reach widens:

  • Art. 358: when the Emergency ends, Art. 19 revives and inconsistent laws lapse, but no remedy lies for anything done during the Emergency. The same holds after an Art. 359 Order ends. An Art. 359 Order must be laid before each House.

The 5th Lok Sabha (1971-77).

Traps#

Trap 1 · Who revokes, and by what majority A resolution of disapproval needs the Lok Sabha only and a simple majority. A resolution approving or extending the Emergency needs both Houses and a special majority. Rule: disapprove = Lok Sabha alone + simple majority; approve or extend = both Houses + special majority.
Trap 2 · Emergency numbers do not match across types National Emergency: 1 month approval, 6-month renewals, special majority. President's Rule and Financial Emergency: 2 months, simple majority. Rule: only the National Emergency has the 1-month window and the special majority.
Trap 3 · Art. 358 versus Art. 359 Art. 358 is automatic, covers Art. 19 only and only for war or external aggression. Art. 359 needs a Presidential Order and suspends only the right to move court, and never for Arts. 20 and 21. Rule: 358 Auto, 359 Order; 20 and 21 are always safe.
Trap 4 · President's Rule does not touch FRs or the High Court Unlike a National Emergency, President's Rule does not affect Fundamental Rights, and the President cannot take over the High Court's powers. Dissolving the Assembly is also not automatic. Rule: Council of Ministers always goes; Assembly may be only suspended; High Court and FRs stay.

Book edition: simple words and memory words#

In simple words

  • Part XVIII (Articles 352-360) gives the Union special powers in a crisis. There are three kinds: National (Art. 352), President's Rule (Art. 356) and Financial (Art. 360).
  • A National Emergency needs war, external aggression or armed rebellion (imminent danger also counts). The 44th Amendment (1978) replaced 'internal disturbance' and demanded the Cabinet's written recommendation.
  • Both Houses must approve within 1 month by a special majority; it lasts 6 months and is renewed 6 months at a time. The Lok Sabha alone can force revocation by a simple majority.
  • During the Emergency the Centre commands the states, Art. 358 suspends Art. 19 automatically, and Art. 359 suspends the remedy for other FRs, but never Arts. 20 and 21.
  • President's Rule follows a breakdown in a state: approval in 2 months (simple majority), 6 months at a time, 3 years at most. The Bommai case (1994) made it reviewable.
  • A Financial Emergency needs approval in 2 months, has no time limit, can cut pay (even of judges) and has never been declared.

Key terms

Proclamation of Emergency
The President's formal order that starts an emergency under Art. 352, 356 or 360
External Emergency
National Emergency on the ground of war or external aggression
Internal Emergency
National Emergency on the ground of armed rebellion (44th Amendment, 1978)
Special majority
Majority of the total membership of the House AND two-thirds of members present and voting
Disapproval resolution
Lok Sabha only, simple majority; forces the President to revoke the National Emergency
President's Rule
Takeover of a state when its constitutional machinery breaks down (Art. 356); the Council of Ministers is dismissed
President's Acts
Laws for a state made by the President, because Parliament may delegate law-making during President's Rule
Floor test
Bommai rule: loss of majority must be proved on the floor of the House, not assumed
