Exam lens#

Prelims hooks

  • Portfolio System: introduced by Canning56-62 in 1859, legally recognised by ICA 1861 (Prelims 2002: not GOI 1858, ICA 1892 or ICA 1909)
  • VEC after 1861 = GG + 5 ordinary members (Home, Revenue, Finance, Law, Military) + C-I-C (extraordinary); the 5th member Finance 1861 (James Wilson, income tax 1860); the 6th member Public Works 1874
  • ILC = VEC + 6 to 12 additional members, half non-officials, all nominated; official majority guaranteed; first 3 Indians 1862 (Raja of Banaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Dinkar Rao of Gwalior)
  • ILC powers: no questions, no resolutions, no budget discussion or vote; discussion and bills only with prior approval; Viceroy's absolute veto and 6-month ordinance
  • Provinces: Bombay and Madras legislative powers restored (taken 1833); PLCs Bengal 1862, NWP 1886, Punjab 1897 (teacher: not imp); Viceroy's five powers and the Acts that ended them (1892, 1919, 1935)

Mains hooks

  • ICA 1861 as the Constitution of British India: a structure with no democracy (despotism controlled from home)
  • One council, two hats: the rigged official majority and the co-option of loyal elites (reward those loyal in 1857)
  • The long arc: centralisation 1773, peak 1833, decentralisation 1861, Provincial Autonomy 1935; the Act as giving with one hand and taking with the other
  • No Mains PYQ is on the slides or in the bank; these are the teacher's own mains hooks

ICA 1861 built a State structure but shared no power: a despotism controlled from home, wearing a legislative costume.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: The British restructured the State in 1861 to look consultative while staying a despotism controlled from home: one council wore two hats, a nominated and rigged legislative ring was added, some provincial law-making was handed back, and the Viceroy kept every real power.

  1. The despot at home. After 1857 and the Crown takeover of 1858, the Secretary of State for India sits at the top of the chain in London. Charles Wood calls the Government of India a 'despotism controlled from home', and this stays the operating principle until 1947. The teacher: the Constitution of British India runs from the Regulating Act 1773 to the GOI Act 1935, and it is not a democracy.
    → So how was the machinery rebuilt in 1861?
  2. Two laws, one year. In 1861 the State is overhauled: the ICA 1861 restructures the Legislature and the Executive (L and E), and the Indian High Courts Act 1861 reorganises the Judiciary (J). Out of the Governor-General's Council come the VEC for executive work and the ILC for law-making.
    → But these are not really two bodies.
  3. One room, two hats. The VEC expands by adding 6 to 12 additional members for law-making: the VEC opens the doors of its room and the additional members join, and the room becomes the ILC. Every VEC member sits in the ILC, so the executive keeps an official majority. The same pattern appears in the provinces as PLCs and GECs.
    → Who sat in the executive room?
  4. The cabinet takes shape. Canning56-62 starts the Portfolio System in 1859 (departments allotted to individual members, decisions final subject to the Viceroy's veto) and the ICA 1861 makes it law. The 1857 debt brings a 5th ordinary member for Finance (James Wilson, income tax 1860, he dies 1860; the tax returns in 1868), so the VEC is GG + 5 + C-I-C. The C-I-C stays extraordinary so that one man does not hold both the office and the battlefield; a 6th member for Public Works comes in 1874.
    → Now, the legislative ring.
  5. A grain of representation. Of the 6 to 12 additional members the Viceroy nominates, half must be non-officials: for the first time non-officers can be lawmakers, and in 1862 three Indians join (the Raja of Banaras, the Maharaja of Patiala and Dinkar Rao of Gwalior). The goal is to reward those loyal in 1857 and co-opt the elites. Only 45 Indians are nominated until 1892.
    → But the arithmetic is rigged.
  6. Seven beats six. The VEC alone (GG + 5 + C-I-C = 7) matches or outnumbers the most non-officials possible (6). In the teacher's case of 6 additional members, officials are 10 (7 + 3) against 3 non-officials. The majority of officers is guaranteed, and 'official' means bureaucrat, PRO-British.
    → So what could the ILC actually do?
  7. A legislature at the bottom of the ladder. Of the ladder of legislative powers (discussion, questions, resolutions, bills, budget, create the Executive, dismiss it, amend the Constitution) the ILC has almost none: discussion and introducing bills only with the Viceroy's prior approval, no questions, no resolutions, no budget vote. The Viceroy holds an absolute veto and a 6-month ordinance. It is 'not a modern Parliament', all nominated, no elections.
    → And the provinces?
  8. The first small step back. The Charter Act 1833 had taken the law-making of Bombay and Madras to the Centre. The ICA 1861 restores it and proposes PLCs for Bengal, Punjab and the NWP (Bengal 1862, NWP 1886, Punjab 1897, the delays due to raw 1857 memories and a frontier province). On the teacher's board the Centre's share falls only from 100% (1833) to 90% (1861).
    → Yet the Viceroy keeps the keys.
  9. BRASS: the Viceroy keeps the keys. The Viceroy needs to sanction the Governor's nominations (ended by ICA 1892), frames the rules for all councils (ended only by GOI 1935), must approve certain bills before introduction (relaxed 1919 and 1935), keeps a single budget for all of British India (ended by GOI 1919) and can create provinces and appoint Lt Governors (to the Crown under GOI 1935). Giving with one hand and taking with the other.
    → So where does the story go next?
  10. The long road to democracy. The arc runs: centralisation began 1773, peaked 1833, decentralisation began 1861, Provincial Autonomy in 1935. The ladder of elections runs 1861 non-officers, 1909 first proper indirect election, 1919 first direct elections and elected majority. The expanded-council model lasts until GOI 1919 (CoS and CLA). The teacher's test of responsible government is that every minister is an MP or MLA first; the ICA 1861 fails it, and the Moderates ask for an ideal Constitution.

In simple words#

  • Charles Wood called the Government of India a "despotism controlled from home"
  • The Secretary of State in London was the top boss, and the ICA 1861 is the Constitution of British India that was not a democracy
  • The Act made one council wear two hats: the Viceroy's Executive Council (VEC) for executive work and the Imperial Legislative Council (ILC) = VEC + 6 to 12 additional members for law-making, with the provinces getting PLCs and GECs.
  • It gave legal form to the Portfolio System (Canning56-62, 1859), added a Finance Member (1861) and later Public Works (1874), so the VEC = GG + 5 + C-I-C.
  • Half the additional members were non-officials (the first 3 Indians, 1862), but the official majority was rigged, and the ILC could not ask questions, pass resolutions or vote the budget
  • The Viceroy held the veto and a 6-month ordinance
  • Bombay and Madras got their law-making back and new PLCs followed (Bengal 1862, NWP 1886, Punjab 1897), but the Viceroy kept Budget, Rules, Approval, Sanction and States: decentralisation began, federalism did not.

2 · What the Act did to the Legislature and the Executive#

Story link

Because the British wanted a law-making body without sharing power, they made the executive wear a second hat. Ask: why are the ILC and the VEC 'not really two bodies'?

  • Out of the Governor-General's Council, the ICA 1861 effectively created two bodies: the Viceroy's Executive Council (VEC) for day-to-day executive functions, and
  • The Imperial Legislative Council (ILC) for law-making.
  • Both operated under the authority of the Viceroy/Governor-General.
  • At the province level, a parallel structure: Provincial Legislative Councils (PLCs) and Governor's Executive Councils (GECs).
Teacher's board
  • In layman terms ICA 1861 created four bodies:
    • ILC and VEC at the Centre
    • PLCs and GECs in the Provinces.
  • "By law," ILC and VEC are not really two separate bodies.
  • VEC = E (executive) = Cabinet
  • Its members are today's ministers.
  • ILC = the VEC opens the doors of its room and the additional members join (so ILC = VEC + additional members).
  • The blank he leaves in quote marks:
    • "Pre 1858 called Legal ''"
    • The word he means for the pre-1858 additional members is the legal members of 1833 and 1853 (Section 3), but the exact wording is .
  • However, be precise: the ICA 1861 did not create two separate, independent institutions.
  • It was simpler and cleverer: the VEC was expanded by adding "additional members" for law-making sessions.
  • These additional members (other British officials and a handful of nominated non-officials) formed an outer ring, brought in only when laws were being made.
  • So the same council wore two hats: 2.1) Day-to-day executive work: it was the VEC. 2.2) Making laws: it became the ILC (= VEC + Additional Members).
One council, two hats
  • Every VEC member was automatically part of the ILC.
  • The design guaranteed the British executive a built-in official majority in every legislative vote.

  • "Official" = Bureaucrat [PRO Br]: an official is a bureaucrat, and bureaucrats are PRO-British.
  • That is why a council with an official majority can never go against the Raj.

The expanded-council model, 1861 to 1919

  • ICA 1861:
    • VEC (executive) and ILC = VEC + Additional Members
    • official majority ensured
  • ICA 1909 (Morley-Minto): the outer ring grows and gains marginal new powers for non-officials
  • GOI Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford): the VEC-ILC model is abolished and replaced by a bicameral Central Legislature: the Council of State (CoS) and the Central Legislative Assembly (CLA), structurally distinct from the VEC

3 · The Viceroy's Executive Council (VEC): its evolution and the 1861 changes#

Story link

Because an executive council needs working departments, the Portfolio System was made law and the council grew. Ask: why a Finance Member in 1861 and Public Works in 1874?

  • Understanding the VEC means tracing it backward before seeing what 1861 changed.
  • 1833: the composition shifted from the 1793 status (GG + 3 Councillors + C-I-C, the extraordinary member) because a Law Member was added for law-making. 3.1) 1833 Executive meetings of the GG's Council = GG + 3 + C-I-C (extraordinary member appointed by the Court of Directors, CoD). 3.2) 1833 Legislative meetings = VEC + 1 legal member.
  • 1853: the composition shifted further: the Law Member was elevated to ordinary executive member and 6 legal members were added for law-making. 3.3) 1853 Executive meetings = GG + 3 + C-I-C (extraordinary, appointed by the CoD) + the 1 Legal Member of 1833 elevated to Executive Member. 3.4) 1853 Legislative meetings = VEC + 6 legal members.
Teacher's board

    • 1773 = GG + 4C
    • 1784 = GG + C1 + C2 + C-I-C
    • 1793 = GG + (C1 + C2 + C3) + C-I-C, the C-I-C "extra-ordinary, appointed by CoD"
    • 1813 = no change
    • 1833 = split into an E meeting and an L meeting (L = E + 1 legal member), "grain of SoP"
    • 1853 = E: GG + 4C + C-I-C, L: E + 6, "SoP introduced"
    • 1858 = no change, but the GG's Council = the Viceroy's Council.
  • SoP: 1833 gave the grain, 1853 introduced it by splitting the executive meeting from the legislative one.

4 · The Legislature (now called ILC)#

Story link

Because the ring of additional members was nominated, the British could afford half non-officials. Ask: how does 7 beat 6, and why did the elites get the seats?

Key takeaway

Whenever a question mentions the Indian Councils Acts (1861, 1892, 1909), split the government into two boxes in your mind: the Executive Council (VEC) and the Legislative Council (ILC) = VEC plus the "additional members" (a mix of officials and non-officials).

  • Under the ICA 1861, 6 to 12 additional law members could be nominated by the Viceroy for the ILC, of which half must be non-officers.
  • So ILC = VEC + 6 to 12 = (GG + 4 + 1 finance member added in 1861) + 6 to 12. Earlier, all were officers.
  • This introduced "a grain of representation in governance": a non-officer lawmaker could be an Indian, a tiny element of popular and representative governance, because law-making is an integral part of governance.

  • He crosses out "Indians" and writes "Non-officials, not unofficial".
  • Case of 6 additional: officials = 10 (the 7 of the VEC plus 3 officers), non-officials = 3 (a politician, a civil-society person or a businessman).
  • Therefore a majority of officers is guaranteed.

5 · Powers of the ILC#

Story link

Because the ILC was only a consultative add-on, it stood at the bottom of the ladder of powers. Ask: which rungs needed 'Viceroy, may I?' and which were barred?

  • The powers of any Legislature, in increasing order of significance: discussion, then questions + discuss answers + ask supplementary questions, then pass resolutions, then passing bills, then discussing and passing the Budget (voting on individual items and on the Budget as a whole), then creating the Executive, then dismissing the Executive, then amending and drafting the Constitution.
  • The ILC's status was at the very bottom of this ladder.

What the ILC could and could not do
Teacher's board
  • "ILC is not equal to a modern P (Parliament)" with a sad face.
  • Discussion and Introduce Bills are underlined: even those two needed the Viceroy's prior approval.
  • Everything above them was NOT allowed.
  • The ILC was not a modern Parliament in any meaningful sense: small membership, entirely nominated (no elections), and no control whatsoever over the Executive.
  • It was a consultative add-on to the Viceroy's own council: useful to the British because it looked like participation while delivering none.
  • Ordinance = law made by the Executive, bypassing the Legislature.

6 · At the province level#

Story link

Because the 1833 centralisation had gone too far, 1861 gave some law-making back. Ask: why did NWP and Punjab wait?

  • The provincial provisions mirrored the arrangement at the Centre. PLCs and GECs were created.
  • Under the ICA 1861, each PLC = Governor (or Lt Governor) + Executive Council members + additional members nominated for law-making (the same formula as ILC = VEC + additional members).
  • The bigger change was in the relationship between the Centre and the provinces.
  • Centralisation had peaked in 1833, when the legislative powers of Bombay and Madras were taken away and concentrated at the centre. ICA 1861 began reversing that: it restored the legislative powers of Bombay and Madras, and
  • It proposed new PLCs for Bengal, Punjab and the North-Western Provinces (NWP).
Teacher's board
  • "Charter 1833 took away (L) powers" (the legislative powers of Bombay and Madras): the Charter Act of 1833 is the peak of centralisation (see Chapter 17).
  • ICA 1861 restores them: "Decentralisation begins." On the long arc he adds "e.g. GG" with a line to "Centralisation" and "GG(Be) to GG(I)": the Governor General of Bengal became the Governor General of India (in 1833), the example of centralisation in a single title.
Handout note
  • Why Bengal needed a fresh PLC (handout only, no slide).
  • The handout adds why Bengal needed a fresh PLC: Bengal's provincial council had evolved into the GG(India)'s Council by 1833, became the Viceroy's/GG's Council in 1858, and was rebranded as ILC and VEC in 1861.
  • So the old Bengal body had moved up to the Centre, and Bengal needed a new one of its own.
  • The long arc: centralisation began 1773, peaked 1833, and decentralisation began 1861, finally peaking with Provincial Autonomy under the GOI Act 1935.

  • The board's rows look as if 1833 is "Unitary" and 1861 is "Federal"
  • Do not read it that way.
  • The words "Unitary" and "Federal" are the definitions (at least 75% against about 50%), and the dated figures are 100/0 (1833) and 90/10 (1861), which is nowhere near 50/50.
  • The handout summary (8.11) says "Not federal" and the title of the next slide (19) has the teacher's sad face on "Federalism".
  • Use: ICA 1861 = decentralisation, but not federal.
  • New PLCs were set up in: 6.1) Bengal: 1862. 6.2) NWP: 1886. (NWP was the half of Awadh annexed in the 1801 Subsidiary Alliance as permanent payment of subsidy, where Mahalwari was first applied in 1822; given province status in 1836 but without a legislature and under a Lt Governor.) Why the delay? Memories of 1857 were still raw, and the British were cautious about loosening their executive grip. 6.3) Punjab: 1897. (Annexed in 1849.) Why the delay? A frontier province: the British preferred strong executive control to the slower deliberation of a legislative body.

  • Beside this list he writes "not imp".
  • Learn the three years lightly (1862, 1886, 1897), and do not spend time on the NWP background.

Read the question, answer in your head, then tap to check.

What is the core philosophy of the ICA 1861?"Despotism controlled from home" (Charles Wood, SoS): the SoS in London is the dictator at the top; it stayed the operating principle until 1947.
Which two laws restructured the State in 1861, and which arms did each cover?ICA 1861: Legislature and Executive (L, E). Indian High Courts Act 1861: Judiciary (J). Teacher's letters.
Is ICA 1861 a democracy? Why not?No (teacher's circled No). The Top Boss = PM of Britain / SoS for India / Viceroy and GG; no intent of sharing power with Indians.
What is "CoBrI" in the teacher's words?Constitution of British India: RA 1773 to GOI Act 1935, with ICA 1861 at its centre; made by the British; may not be ideal; the Moderates wanted an ideal CoI.
What did ICA 1861 create at the Centre and in the Provinces?Centre: ILC and VEC. Provinces: PLCs and GECs.
Are the ILC and VEC two separate bodies?No. The VEC expanded by adding additional members for law-making: VEC = executive hat; VEC + additional members = ILC, the legislative hat. Every VEC member is in the ILC.
Teacher's picture of the ILC?The VEC opens the doors of its room and the additional members join.
How did the expanded-council model end?ICA 1861 built it, ICA 1909 gave the outer ring marginal new powers, GOI 1919 abolished it (CoS + CLA).
Council in 1833 and 1853?1833: executive GG + 3 + C-I-C; legislative = VEC + 1 legal member (grain of separation of powers). 1853: law member elevated; legislative = VEC + 6 legal members (SoP introduced).
Who appointed the C-I-C before 1861?The Court of Directors (CoD), as the extraordinary member (teacher's 1793 note); after 1861 the Secretary of State for India appoints him.
What is the Portfolio System and when was it introduced and recognised?Departments (ministries) allotted to individual VEC members; decisions final subject to the Viceroy's veto. Introduced by Canning56-62 in 1859; legal recognition in ICA 1861; cabinet-style administration at the Centre.
Prelims 2002: which Act brought the portfolio system?(a) ICA 1861. Wrong: GOI 1858 (Crown takeover), ICA 1892 and ICA 1909 (legislative outer ring).
Who was the first Finance Member and what did he do?James Wilson (1859): introduced income tax in 1860 and died in 1860; withdrawn under WEMC pressure; re-introduced in 1868. The post gained constitutional backing in 1861.
Why a Finance Member in 1861?The 1857 revolt left the British Indian economy heavily in debt.
Five ordinary members' portfolios in 1861?Home, Revenue, Finance, Law, Military.
Why is the C-I-C an extraordinary member?Checks and balances: the ordinary Military Member (Defence Minister) ran the office (logistics, defence budget, orders); the C-I-C (CDS) the battlefield. The Crown did not want one man wearing both hats.
What was added in 1874 and why?A 6th Ordinary Member for Public Works: massive investment in railways, canals and infrastructure.
VEC formula (teacher)?VEC = GG + C-I-C + (C1 to C6); ILC = VEC + 6 to 12 additional members.
ILC composition and what was new?VEC + 6 to 12 additional members nominated by the Viceroy, half must be non-officers (3 to 6); earlier all were officers; 1861 = first time non-officers can be lawmakers.
Why was the official majority guaranteed?VEC = GG + 5 + C-I-C = 7, which outnumbers or matches the maximum 6 non-officials; teacher's case: 10 officials (7 + 3) against 3 non-officials.
Teacher's correction on "half must be ...", and the trap?He strikes out "Indians" and writes "Non-officials, not unofficial": non-official does not mean Indian (Anglo-Indian and Indian could join).
Who were the first three Indians (1862)?Raja of Banaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Dinkar Rao of Gwalior (Sir Dinkar Rao in 1877): elites, some from Princely States.
Why these three, and what is the pattern?Loyal in 1857: reward them; co-opt the elites (Permanent Settlement 1793, Brown Sahib policy 1835): Divide and Rule. The goal on the board: reward those loyal in 1857.
How many Indians were nominated until ICA 1892?45 in total (31 years): BrIA (1851) zamindars and moneylenders, British businessmen, some rulers of Princely States; the teacher: nationalistic WEMC missing.
Ladder of legislative powers?Discussion, questions (discuss answers, supplementary), resolutions, bills, budget, create the Executive, dismiss the Executive, amend the Constitution.
Which ILC powers needed the Viceroy's prior approval, and which were barred?Approval: discussion and introducing bills. Barred: questions, resolutions, budget discussion or vote.
Viceroy's own powers over the ILC?Absolute veto over all bills, and an ordinance power (law by executive order, valid for 6 months).

Composition of a PLC?Governor (or Lt Governor) + Executive Council members + additional members nominated for law-making.

PLC years and the reasons for the delay?Bengal 1862; NWP 1886 (1857 memories still raw); Punjab 1897 (frontier province, strong executive). Teacher: not imp.
