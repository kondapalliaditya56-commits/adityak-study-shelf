The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: The Census, the religion-wise reports and low Western education made Muslims a separate, deprived group that British Divide & Rule could favour; Sir Syed gave it the qaum idea, the Simla Deputation 1906 and the League asked for separate electorates, and the ICA 1909 granted them inside a council that had no real power, so the seed of Partition was sown.

  1. The count that made a community. Census 1881 was the first full census of British India (the 1872 attempt was incomplete). It found Muslims roughly 20% overall, a majority in Punjab and about 50% in Bengal, and it told a Bengal weaver and a Punjab landlord that they were one community with one set of interests. Government reports on education, employment and poverty by religion then showed Muslims behind Hindus.
    → But why were Muslims behind in the new jobs?
  2. Behind in the new jobs. Western education was expensive and most Bengal Muslims were poor ryots. The Ulamas resisted it for fear that Western literature would erode their community control, some wished to preserve Persian, and rich landlords saw little use for middle-class jobs. So Hindus, who took to Western education more readily, gained the new professions.
    → The rulers noticed, and had a plan.
  3. The rulers' plan: appease and divide. The teacher's frame: the goal of imperialism is economic loot and the tool is Divide & Rule, co-opting one side to loot the other. W.W. Hunter's Indian Musalmans (1871) argued the British should appease Muslims, job reservation followed in 1897, and the 1905 Partition made East Bengal and Assam a Muslim Majority Province. All three favoured the elite.
    → Meanwhile a Muslim leader gave the plan an idea.
  4. Sir Syed's qaums. The Mohammadan Association (1855) was pro-British and opposed the 1857 revolts. Sir Syed Ahmed Khan (1817-98) said India was a summation of qaums and that the Muslim qaum, the former rulers, needed Western education to claim special status; he built MAO College (1875) and the Mohammadan Educational Congress (1886) as a rival to the INC. He was not a full separatist, but he drew the blueprint of the Two Nation Theory.
    → And Hindu revivalism widened the wedge.
  5. The Hindu wave and the Muslim withdrawal. The Arya Samaj (1875) turned aggressive after Dayanand died in 1883, and the Cow Killing Riots of 1893 followed; the INC, bound by its 1886 rule of political issues only, did not condemn them, and Muslim participation fell. The Hindi-Urdu controversy (success for Hindi in 1900) tied Hindi to Hindus and Urdu to Muslims, and the Swadeshi movement's symbols (Bande Mataram, Kali worship, Ganpati and Shivaji festivals) deepened the alienation.
    → Then the Partition of 1905 sent the elite to the Viceroy.
  6. Simla 1906. Elite Muslims led by the Aga Khan met Viceroy Minto 05-10 at Shimla. They asked for no annulment of the Bengal Partition, reservation proportional to population in the ILC, PLCs and jobs, and separate electorates in future council reforms. Separate electorates are the hinge: once Muslims must elect Muslims, the Two Nation Theory is institutionally accepted.
    → An organisation followed within months.
  7. The League is born. The All India Muslim League was set up at Dacca in December 1906 to hold the British to the Simla promises, promote Muslim interests and promote Muslim loyalty. Its Green Book was written by Mohsin-ul-Mulk and Mohammed Ali Jauhar, provincial Leagues came in 1907-09, and the London branch (1908) under Ameer Ali influenced ICA 1909. The PCCs became mass instruments only in 1920, so the League had a 13-year head start.
    → Meanwhile the Viceroy's side had a reform track.
  8. Two tracks: repression and reform. Swadeshi (1905-11), the Extremists and Revolutionary Activities (from 1908) made India restless, so the British ran repression and reform together; Morley, a Liberal, was open to reform. Move 1 split the INC: Morley's 1906 Budget speech hinted at council reforms, the Moderates feared Extremist agitation would kill them, and the INC split at Surat in 1907. Move 2 divided Hindus and Muslims through separate electorates.
    → What did the Act actually give?
  9. A bigger council, not a stronger one. ICA 1909 made the ILC 69 members (9 + 60: 27 elected, 33 nominated) but kept an official majority, 37 against 32. It used the word 'election' for the first time, with proper indirect elections, gave the PLCs a non-official majority (mostly nominated), put S.P. Sinha in the VEC, and let members vote on Budget items, ask supplementary questions and pass non-binding resolutions. Morley stated there was no intent of responsible government, only Limited Self-Government.
    → And for the Muslims?
  10. The separate electorate. Muslims got separate electorates in the ILC, PLCs and local bodies: 8 of 27 elected ILC seats, about 30% against a population of about 20% (the INC's own 1889 Minority Clause had asked for 20%). They also voted in the general seats, had a lower income threshold and direct elections in Muslim seats. Joint electorates give a candidate of the People; separate ones give a candidate of a Group, for a Group, by a Group.
    → So how did the story end?
  11. Failure and the long shadow. ICA 1909 lasted only 10 years, the shortest of the council reforms (30, 17, 10, 16, 15). The Moderates wanted real power, the INC's goal since 1906 was Swaraj, Muslims got separate electorates and the legislatures were powerless, so it was a Benevolent Despotism. Minto 05-10 became the Father of Communal Electorate, and organised communal politics, never reversed, hardened until 1947.

1 · Background: census, education and communal consciousness#

Story link

1.0 The teacher's frame: the goal of imperialism

Teacher's board

  • The tool is DnR (Divide & Rule), especially, as he draws it, against the leadership (the happy and the sad face in brackets: one group of leaders favoured, one not).

1.1 Census 1881 and communal consciousness

  • Census 1881 was the first full census of British India.
  • The first attempt, in 1872, was not completed for all of British India.
  • From 1881 it ran every 10 years: 1881, 1891, 1901...
  • Muslims were roughly 20% overall, with a majority in Punjab and roughly 50% in Bengal.
Teacher's board
  • He underlines the 20%, the majority in Punjab and the 50% in Bengal, and under "a majority in Punjab" writes "~54%" (the Punjab figure, about 54%; the handout only says "majority").
  • The reason to hold the three numbers: 20% overall is the base against which the 30% of ICA 1909 is later called appeasement (Section 7.4.6).

1.2 The Census as a Divide & Rule contribution

  • The Census had a deep DnR contribution: it created and reinforced a consciousness of being a homogeneous religious group among an otherwise heterogeneous Muslim community.
  • Before mass enumeration, a poor Muslim weaver in Bengal and a rich Muslim landlord in Punjab had very different daily realities.
  • The Census told both that they were part of one community with one set of interests, and that Hindus and Muslims were two separate communities with separate, potentially competing interests.
  • Shia-Sunni differences and Worker-Peasant class differences all came later
  • Muslim identity was put first by the census logic.

1.3 Religion-wise reports and relative deprivation

  • The British government published reports on education, employment and poverty religion-wise.
  • This created a sense of relative deprivation among Muslims: the data showed them behind Hindus in government jobs, education and the new middle-class professions.
  • Whether the cause was discrimination or other factors, the published comparison reinforced a feeling that Hindus were gaining at Muslims' expense.
Key takeaway
  • Communalism is the organisation of society on the basis of religion and the ideology that every religious group has its own separate interests, which may be mutually exclusive: that the two communities cannot co-exist happily, and that meeting one community's interests will necessarily hurt the other's.
  • This is the logic that underpinned the demand for Separate Electorates and the demand for Pakistan.

  • The low representation of Muslims in the new middle-class professions was partly the result of lower uptake of Western education.

3 · Post-1883: Hindu revivalism and Muslim alienation#

Story link

Because the Muslim elite was building a separate identity, Hindu revivalism widened the gap in three waves. Ask: which wave did the INC not condemn, and why?

  • Hindu cultural revivalism of the late 19th and early 20th centuries (the Shuddhi movement, the Cow Protection Movement, the Swadeshi period's use of Hindu religious symbolism) alienated Muslims from the Congress-led national movement in successive waves.

3.1 Wave i: Shuddhi and the Cow Protection Movement

  • The Shuddhi movement and the Cow Protection Movement were run by the Arya Samaj (1875) and became more aggressive after Swami Dayanand Saraswati died in 1883.
  • The result was the Cow Killing Riots of 1893.
  • The INC did not condemn these riots, because its 1886 rule was to take up only political issues.
  • This non-condemnation produced a sharp decline in Muslim participation in the INC.

3.2 Wave ii: the Hindi-Urdu Controversy (1880s to 1900)

  • The controversy was in the North West Provinces and Awadh (1877), led by the moderate Madan Mohan Malviya.
  • It demanded official language status for Hindi (spoken by the majority) equivalent to Urdu. Success came in 1900.
  • The unintended byproduct: Hindi became associated with Hindus and Urdu with Muslims.

  • Right-margin board on language: Punjabi and Bangla are the two big languages (two circles joined by a V).
  • Yet "Jinnah chose URDU" (circled), and the script is Arabic. This matches the handout's note: Pakistan was linguistically dominated by Punjabis and Bengalis, but Jinnah made Urdu the official language for an Islamic image, not for a linguistic majority.

3.3 Wave iii: Hindu symbolism in the Swadeshi movement (1905-11)

  • Bande Mataram, Kali worship, Prabhat Pheris, Ganpati and Shivaji festivals contributed further to Muslim alienation from the movement, leading to low participation (as already discussed).

7.2 Context: why this reform happened

  • India in 1905 was not quiet. The partition of Bengal in 1905 had set off the Swadeshi Movement (1905-11), which pushed Indian politics in a sharper, angrier direction.
  • 1905-1908 saw the rise of Extremist politics within the INC, and from 1908 Revolutionary Activities became a real and growing force that worried the British.
  • The British response had two tracks, repression and reform, and they ran simultaneously. ICA1909 was the reform track.
  • But reform, in British hands, always came packaged with a political strategy.
  • There was also a personal dimension: Morley was a Liberal in the British political tradition, less authoritarian than many predecessors and open to giving Indians more participation.
  • His disposition, with political necessity, shaped the reform.

7.3 The Divide & Rule (DnR) strategy behind ICA1909

  • The British wanted to split the Indian political movement, specifically to separate the Moderates from the Extremists within the INC.
  • The strategy played out in two moves.

Move 1: Split the INC.

  • In 1906, Morley gave a speech in the British Parliament during the debate on India's Budget and hinted strongly at upcoming council reforms.
  • The message landed exactly as intended. Moderate leaders began to worry that Extremist agitation would harden British opinion and kill the chances of reform.
  • This fault line widened until the Surat Split of 1907, where the Extremists were expelled from the INC.
  • With the Extremists outside the tent, the British moved to repress them directly.

Move 2: Divide Hindus and Muslims.

  • The vehicle was the introduction of separate electorates for Muslims, a demand formally placed before Minto 05-10 by a delegation of Muslim elites at the Shimla Deputation of 1906.
  • The Shimla Deputation: a group of Muslim leaders met Minto 05-10 and asked not just for reserved seats, but for a system where only Muslims could vote for Muslim representatives.
  • The British were happy to oblige: it suited their DnR strategy perfectly.

7.4 Provisions of ICA1909

7.4.1 Nature of government introduced

  • Morley stated explicitly: "There is no intent of Responsible Govt in India via ICA1909."
  • Fully Responsible Government means every minister must first be an elected member of the legislature: the Executive is the child of Parliament, and a child is always accountable to its mother.
  • That is, no minister had to be an MP or MLA first.
  • ICA1909 gave nothing of the sort.
  • What ICA1909 did introduce was Representative Government, also called Limited Self-Government (LTD SG): people get to elect representatives to sit in a council and talk and advise, meaning some partnership in domestic affairs, but not self-rule, not Self Government, not Responsible Government.
  • This had been the goal of the INC until 1906.
  • From the 1906 Calcutta Session onward the INC's goal shifted to Swaraj.
  • So even by the Moderates' own older standard, the reform arrived too late.
  • It was the second failure of the Moderate approach.

7.4.6 The Muslim separate electorate: communal appeasement

  • For the first time, separate electorates for Muslims were introduced, in the ILC, PLCs and local bodies.
  • In the ILC, 8 of the 27 elected seats were reserved for Muslims: about 30% of the elected seats.
  • Muslim population in India was about 20%.
  • The INC's own 1889 Minority Clause had asked for minority reservation proportional to population, that is 20% for Muslims.
  • The extra 10% beyond the population share was pure appeasement politics.
  • Due to separate electorates, only Muslims could vote in Muslim reserved seats.
  • However, Muslims also had the right to vote in general (unreserved) seats, so they were not excluded from unreserved constituencies.
  • Muslims effectively had a double advantage: they could influence both their own reserved seats and the unreserved seats.
  • Compare with today's India: reservation exists for Scheduled Castes (SCs) in Parliament and state legislatures, but with joint electorates: non-SC voters also vote in SC-reserved seats.
  • What ICA1909 gave Muslims was institutionally far more separatist.
  • The conceptual difference is crucial. Reservation with Joint Electorates = Candidate of the People, For the People, By a Community.
  • Reservation with Separate Electorates = Candidate of a Group, For a Group, By a Group.
  • Country = Sum of Constituencies: thus today it is our Constituencies and tomorrow it is Our Pakistan.

  • A mix of direct and indirect elections:

  • Minto 05-10 became known as the "Father of Communal Electorate", a title history has not handed down with pride.

Teacher's board

The immediate predecessor: selection not election, 10 to 16 additional members, a Budget that could be discussed; every ICA 1909 power in Section 7.4.7 is a step beyond ICA 1892.

Chapter 26: Moderates in INC: Ideology, Demands, Negatives

ICA 1909 is called the second failure of the Moderate approach; the Moderates' "mouth watered" at Morley's hint and they feared Extremist agitation would derail the reform.

Chapter 29: The Extremists and the Swadeshi Movement (1905-11)

The Swadeshi storm is why the reform came, the Hindu symbolism of that movement alienated Muslims, and the Extremists were expelled at Surat 1907; the handout lists ML1906 as a negative impact of Swadeshi.

Chapter 30: Revolutionary Activities and Repression of Extremists

Revolutionary Activities grew from 1908; the British ran repression and reform on two tracks, and ICA 1909 was the reform track.

Chapter 24: INC 1885 & English Political Reformers

Sir Syed's reason for the Mohammadan Educational Congress was that the INC was a Hindu organisation; the INC's 1886 rule of only political issues is why it did not condemn the Cow Killing Riots.

Chapter 33: Home Rule League Movement (1916-18) & Lucknow Pact (1916)

The Lucknow 1916 pact is the nationalism that "had just united Hindus and Muslims" in the Prelims 2026 answer slide; it is the result the British tried to prevent with DnR.

Chapter 34: Government of India Act, 1919

The 1919 Act retained and extended separate electorates (the Prelims 2026 question shown in Section 8) and replaced ICA 1909 after 10 years.

The Nagpur 1920 reorganisation of the PCCs on linguistic lines ends the 13-year head start of the Muslim League's provincial structures.

What did Census 1881 do for communal consciousness?The first full census of British India (1872 incomplete; then every 10 years) made a homogeneous Muslim group out of a heterogeneous community, told Hindus and Muslims they were two separate communities with competing interests, and put Muslim identity first.
What were the Muslim numbers in the Census 1881?About 20% overall; a majority in Punjab (teacher: about 54%); about 50% in Bengal.
Why did religion-wise reports matter?Government reports on education, employment and poverty by religion showed Muslims behind Hindus in government jobs, education and new professions: relative deprivation.
Define communalism.Organisation of society on religion and the ideology that every religious group has separate, perhaps mutually exclusive interests, so meeting one community's interests must hurt the other's; the logic of Separate Electorates and the demand for Pakistan.
Why was Western education uptake low among Muslims?Cost (poor Bengal ryots); the Ulamas resisted it (Western literature would erode community control); the wish to preserve Persian; rich landlords' low incentive. Consequence: Hindus gained the new middle-class jobs. Mnemonic: CLAP.
What did W.W. Hunter argue in Indian Musalmans (1871)?Neglect and economic decline of Muslims since Mughal rule had fed the Faraizi movement of the 1830s-70s, so the British should appease Muslims for political stability: the justification for DnR in favour of Muslims.
Three DnR measures towards Muslims?Hunter's book 1871 (the argument); 1897 reservation in government jobs for Muslims; 1905 Bengal Partition creating East Bengal and Assam as a Muslim Majority Province. Mnemonic: Hunter Justifies Partiality.
The teacher's goal of imperialism and its tool?The goal is economic: loot or drain the wealth of the colony. The tool is DnR: if the majority is co-opted, loot the minority; if the minority is co-opted, loot the majority (Rwanda: Belgium, Hutus, Tutsis).
Mohammadan Association (1855)?Pro-British; it opposed the 1857 revolts and sought benefits for Muslims through loyalty to the British, not through the nationalist (teacher: anti-colonial) cause.
What did Sir Syed Ahmed Khan (1817-98) argue?India was a summation of qaums; the Hindu and Muslim qaums were separate with separate interests (the early form of the Two Nation Theory); the Muslim qaum, as the former ruling community, deserved special status and needed Western education to claim it.
MAO College (1875) and the Mohammadan Educational Congress (1886)?MAO College (later Aligarh Muslim University) combined Western education with Islamic values; the Congress was set up as a rival to the INC, which Sir Syed thought a Hindu organisation, and he discouraged Muslim participation.
Was Sir Syed a separatist?Not in the full 1940s sense: he believed in a unity of qaums; but he gave later leaders the intellectual architecture of the Two Nation Theory.
Why did the Cow Killing Riots of 1893 reduce Muslim participation in the INC?Shuddhi and Cow Protection by the Arya Samaj (1875) grew aggressive after Dayanand died (1883); the INC did not condemn the riots because its 1886 rule was to take up only political issues.
What was the Hindi-Urdu controversy?In the NWP and Awadh (1877) Malviya led the demand for official status for Hindi equal to Urdu (success 1900); Hindi became associated with Hindus and Urdu with Muslims. Jinnah later chose Urdu for an Islamic image (teacher: script = Arabic), not because it was the majority language.
Swadeshi symbols that alienated Muslims?Bande Mataram, Kali worship, Prabhat Pheris, Ganpati and Shivaji festivals (1905-11). Mnemonic: BLAST.
Who led the Simla Deputation 1906 and to whom?Elite Muslims led by the Aga Khan, to Viceroy Minto 05-10 at Shimla.
Three demands of the Simla Deputation?No annulment of the Bengal Partition; reservation proportional to population in the ILC, PLCs and government jobs; separate electorates in future council reforms. Mnemonic: PEP (Partition, Electorates, Proportion).
Why is Separate Electorates the hinge of partition history?Accepting that Muslims must elect Muslims from Muslim-only constituencies is institutionally accepting the Two Nation Theory; the 1909 grant was the legislative seed of 1947.
Joint against Separate electorates in the handout's formula?Joint: candidate of the People, for the People, by a Community. Separate: candidate of a Group, for a Group, by a Group. Country = sum of constituencies: today our Constituencies, tomorrow Our Pakistan.
When was the Muslim League founded, where and with what goals?December 1906 at Dacca by elite Muslims, right after Simla; to hold the British to the Simla promises, promote Muslim interests, promote Muslim loyalty to the British.
Who wrote the Green Book?Mohsin-ul-Mulk and Mohammed Ali Jauhar, both of the Aligarh movement (the teacher marks them "main").
Why did ML have a head start over the INC's PCCs?Provincial Muslim Leagues (1907-09) had ground-level structures about 13 years before the PCCs became mass instruments in 1920.
What is the PCC trap?PCCs were not created at Nagpur 1920; they were reorganised on linguistic lines (teacher: set up 1886; handout: 1908 Constitution).
Who headed the League's London branch and what did it do?Ameer Ali, 1908; it influenced ICA 1909, which introduced Separate Electorates for Muslims. (Prelims 2001.)
Who were Morley and Minto 05-10?John Morley = Secretary of State for India (London, a Liberal); Lord Minto 05-10 = Viceroy and Governor-General (India). Their reform = ICA 1909.
How did ICA 1909 use Divide & Rule?Move 1 split the INC (Morley's 1906 Budget speech hinting at reform, then the Surat Split 1907 expelling the Extremists); Move 2 divided Hindus and Muslims by granting separate electorates.
Nature of government under ICA 1909?No Responsible Government (Morley said so); Representative or Limited Self-Government: some partnership in domestic affairs; too late for the Moderates, whose goal had been Swaraj since 1906.
ILC composition under ICA 1909?69 = 8 ex-officio from the VEC + 1 rotating Governor or Lt-Governor + 60 additional (27 elected and 33 nominated, of whom 28 officials and 5 non-officials). Officials 37 against non-officials 32.
How did the indirect election chain run?Taxpayers and rent payers elected local bodies; they elected an electoral college; it elected PLC members; they elected ILC members. Mnemonic: Tax Lifts Electors, Provinces, Imperial.
PLCs and the Bengal exception?First non-official majority, only in PLCs and mostly nominated; the Bengal PLC had an elected majority (first province), with 4 elected seats reserved for European Businesses.
Indians in the Executive?S.P. Sinha, Law Member, the first Indian in the VEC (a provision); K.G. Gupta and Syed Hussain Bilgrami to the India Council in London, August 1907 (a precursor, Not Imp).
What did ICA 1909 give Muslims?Separate electorates in ILC, PLCs and local bodies; 8 of 27 elected ILC seats (about 30% against about 20%); vote in both reserved and general seats; lower income threshold; direct elections for Muslim seats. Mnemonic: SIDE.
Powers of the legislature under ICA 1909 against ICA 1892?Budget: vote on items, not the whole (1892: discussion only); supplementary questions by the original questioner (1892: none); resolutions allowed but non-binding (1892: none). Executive: power of rejection (1892: selection). Mnemonic: SIR.
Lifespan of the council reforms?ICA 1861 30 years; ICA 1892 17; ICA 1909 10 (shortest); GOI Act 1919 16; GOI Act 1935 15. (Prelims 1999.)
The teacher's reasons for the failure of ICA 1909?Moderates wanted real power; the INC's goal since 1906 was Swaraj; Muslims got separate electorates; BUT powerless legislatures (he struck "Extremists never cared" and "long-term disaster"). Mnemonic: SPAM.
What is Benevolent Despotism?Benevolent: more democratic than ICA 1892 (participation, discussion, elections). Despotism: real power stayed with the British Executive, answerable to nobody in India. Teacher: Better than 1892 and dictatorship with no responsible government.
Mains practice: Why did ICA 1909 fail? (skeleton)Intro: shortest-lived, 10 years. Body: Moderates wanted real power; goal already Swaraj; separate electorates for Muslims; powerless legislatures; no responsible government. Close: its real legacy was the foundation of Partition; Minto the Father of Communal Electorate.
Mains practice: How did DnR work from the Census to ICA 1909? (skeleton)Intro: loot is the goal, DnR the tool. Body: Census 1881 and religion-wise reports; Hunter 1871, jobs 1897, Partition 1905; Simla 1906 and ML at Dacca; ICA 1909 splitting the INC and then Hindus from Muslims. Close: of a Group, for a Group, by a Group.
