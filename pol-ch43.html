In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The CVC is the apex vigilance institution of the Centre. It began in 1964 by a government resolution (Santhanam Committee), was pushed to statutory status by the Vineet Narain case (1998) and became statutory under the CVC Act, 2003.
  • It has a Central Vigilance Commissioner (chair) and up to two Vigilance Commissioners. The President appoints them on the advice of a committee of the PM, Home Minister and Leader of Opposition.
  • Tenure is 4 years or age 65, whichever is earlier
  • no reappointment (a Vigilance Commissioner may become the Commissioner). Pay equals the UPSC Chairperson and Members. Removal for misbehaviour or incapacity needs a Supreme Court inquiry
  • The CVC superintends the CBI in corruption cases, reviews progress, supervises CVOs in ministries, PSUs and banks, advises on discipline and promotes preventive vigilance. It assists the Lokpal for Group A and B officers.
  • Under the Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014 the CVC is the competent authority. Disclosures need good faith and documents
  • Anonymous ones get no action
  • False complaints face 2 years and Rs 30,000
  • The SPG is excluded

1 · Introduction and establishment#

  • The Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) is the apex vigilance institution of India. It promotes integrity, transparency and accountability in public administration and works as an independent statutory body against corruption in government organisations and public sector undertakings. It acts as a watchdog of the Central Government so that public officials act with honesty and efficiency.
  • Set up in 1964 by a Government of India Resolution, on the recommendations of the Committee on Prevention of Corruption (Santhanam Committee).
  • With no statutory backing, its effectiveness was limited.
  • The Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003 gave it statutory status and independence from executive control.
  • Today it is the nodal agency for vigilance administration, coordinating between ministries, the CBI and public sector entities.

From Resolution to statute

  • 1962: Santhanam Committee on Prevention of Corruption appointed
  • 1964: CVC set up by Government Resolution (non-statutory)
  • 1998: Supreme Court in Vineet Narain v. Union of India directs statutory status
  • 2003: CVC Act, 2003 gives legal status, powers, composition and functions
  • 2013-14: Integration with the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act framework
  • 2020-24: e-vigilance and preventive vigilance reforms

Appointing the CVC against the CBI Director

  • CVC and VCs: President appoints on a committee of PM, Home Minister, LoP
  • CBI Director: Selected by a committee of PM, Leader of Opposition and Chief Justice of India (post-Lokpal Act, 2013)

Removal (Section 6 of the CVC Act, 2003)

  • The President can remove the CVC or any VC on these grounds:
  • (a) Insolvency
  • (b) Conviction of an offence involving moral turpitude
  • (c) Engaging in paid employment during office
  • (d) Mental or physical infirmity
  • (e) Financial or other interest affecting official duties
  • (f) Proved misbehaviour or incapacity, after a Supreme Court inquiry and recommendation to the President
  • Memory aid (Six grounds): Insolvency, Conviction (moral turpitude), Paid employment, Mental or physical infirmity, Financial interest, Proved misbehaviour: "I Can't Pay My Fine Promptly."

Safeguard against arbitrary removal

  • Allegation: Proved misbehaviour or incapacity alleged
  • President refers: The matter must first go to the Supreme Court
  • Court inquires: Inquiry held on the cause for removal
  • Court upholds:
    • Only then can the President remove
    • This ensures independence and security of tenure
Safeguard against arbitrary removal
  • Pattern:
    • Grounds (a) to (e) lead straight to removal by the President
    • Only ground (f), misbehaviour or incapacity, needs the Supreme Court inquiry first

Also in the handout

  • The Act institutionalises citizen participation in anti-corruption efforts by providing legal protection to those who act in the public interest.

3 · Functions and powers#

  • The CVC combines advisory, supervisory, preventive and coordinating roles. Its functions derive primarily from Sections 8 and 11 of the CVC Act, 2003.
  • Memory aid (Six heads of functions): Supervisory, Advisory, Preventive and educational, Investigative and quasi-judicial, Coordinating and monitoring, Financial and administrative: "Sharp Auditors Prevent Illegal Cash Flow."

A. Supervisory and oversight functions

  • Superintendence over the CBI: over CBI investigations in cases under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, especially those involving senior public officials. This keeps corruption investigations impartial and free from political or bureaucratic pressure.
  • Review of progress and prosecutions:
    • Reviews investigations and prosecutions by the CBI, public sector undertakings and other central organisations
    • Monitors pending cases and disciplinary proceedings for time-bound action
  • Supervision of vigilance units:
    • Oversees Chief Vigilance Officers (CVOs) posted in each Ministry, Department and PSU
    • CVOs are the nodal officers for departmental vigilance, under the CVC's technical guidance

B. Advisory functions

  • Advice on disciplinary matters: advises the Central Government and public sector organisations on disciplinary proceedings against officials under investigation. Advisory in nature, but its recommendations carry high persuasive value and are normally accepted.
  • Policy advice: formulates and updates vigilance manuals, holds consultations, recommends policy changes for ethical conduct and preventive vigilance.
  • Consultation with other authorities: consulted on appointment, transfer and posting of CVOs and vigilance staff in major departments and public enterprises.

C. Preventive and educational functions

  • Promotion of integrity: campaigns such as Vigilance Awareness Week and Preventive Vigilance Frameworks.
  • Preventive vigilance audits: periodic reviews of systems and procedures in corruption-prone areas to find loopholes and recommend reforms.
  • Training and capacity building: with the Institute of Public Administration (IIPA) and other bodies, orientation programmes for vigilance officers and managers.
  • Public outreach: the CVC Portal and complaint mechanisms let citizens report corruption and track action taken (participatory vigilance).

D. Investigative and quasi-judicial functions

  • Inquiry into complaints: can inquire, or cause inquiry, into corruption, favouritism, abuse of position or lack of integrity against public servants.
  • Suo motu action: can start inquiries in serious irregularities even without a formal complaint, if there is prima facie evidence.
  • Summoning and inspection: powers equivalent to a civil court in inquiries: summoning witnesses, examining them on oath, requisitioning public documents.
  • Recommending action: recommends departmental proceedings, criminal prosecution or other corrective measures.

E. Coordinating and monitoring functions

  • Coordination with Lokpal: under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013, assists the Lokpal in preliminary inquiries involving Group A and B officers.
  • Inter-agency cooperation: nodal link among the CBI, the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) and departmental vigilance units for data sharing and coordinated action.
  • Annual vigilance report: compiled and submitted to the President, laid before both Houses of Parliament.

F. Financial and administrative powers

  • Budget is charged on the Consolidated Fund of India, ensuring independence from executive control.
  • Can appoint, transfer or assign duties to its officers and staff and decide procedures for handling vigilance cases.
  • Can delegate functions to subordinate authorities for case management and coordination.

Advisory versus investigative

  • Advisory side:
    • Disciplinary advice normally accepted
    • Vigilance manuals
    • Consulted on CVO postings
  • Investigative side:
    • Inquiry on complaint or suo motu
    • Civil court powers
    • Recommends prosecution or departmental action
  • Shortcut: Independence shortcut. Independent because of: statute (2003), bipartisan committee, SC-inquiry removal, UPSC-equal pay that cannot be reduced, and budget charged on the Consolidated Fund.

7 · Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014#

  • The Act gives a statutory mechanism to protect people who disclose corruption, abuse of power or wilful misuse of authority by public servants. It rests on the idea that citizens have a duty to report wrongdoing without fear of retaliation or victimisation.

Objectives

  • Memory aid (Three objectives): Protection to whistleblowers, Encouragement of public interest disclosures, Institutional mechanism for complaints (including against ministers): "Public Eyes Illuminate."
  • Protection: safeguard identity and safety of those who expose corruption or misuse of public office.
  • Encouragement: promote disclosure of corrupt acts, maladministration or abuse of discretion through a secure channel.
  • Institutional mechanism: an independent framework to receive and inquire into complaints of corruption or wilful misuse of power by public servants, including ministers.

Competent authority and coverage

  • The CVC is the competent authority for receiving and inquiring into disclosures. The Government may, by notification, designate any other body.
  • Applies to all public servants: employees of Central and State Governments, public sector undertakings and government-aided bodies, except the Special Protection Group (SPG).

Procedure for disclosure

Making a disclosure

  • Mode: Complaint in writing or by electronic communication (email), as prescribed
  • Evidence: Full particulars, with documents or material evidence supporting the allegations
  • Declaration: Personal declaration that the information is in good faith and believed to be substantially true
  • Anonymous: No action on complaints where the identity of the complainant or the public servant is not indicated or is found to be false

Protection and safeguards

  • Identity protection: confidentiality of the whistleblower's identity to prevent victimisation, harassment or physical harm.
  • No retaliation: a person disclosing under the Act cannot be subjected to punitive or disciplinary action by the concerned public authority.
  • False complaints: punishment of up to two years' imprisonment and a fine up to Rs 30,000 for wilful false or frivolous disclosures.

Limitations and exceptions

  • SPG personnel are excluded because of their sensitive security functions.
  • Disclosures prejudicial to national security or covered by the Official Secrets Act may be exempted from inquiry.

Protected versus punished

  • Protected:
    • Good-faith disclosure with identity and evidence
    • Confidentiality
    • No punitive or disciplinary action
  • Punished: Wilful false or frivolous disclosure: up to 2 years and Rs 30,000
  • Memory aid (Act in numbers):
    • CVC is competent authority, SPG excluded, 2 years, Rs 30,000: "CVC Shields
    • 2 years for lies, 30,000 fine."
Handout alert

Handout alert: The handout says the Act ensures anonymity and confidentiality of the whistleblower, yet also that no action is taken on anonymous complaints (where identity is not indicated or found false). Read them together: the whistleblower must give a verifiable identity to the authority, and the authority then keeps that identity confidential. The PIDPI portal with stronger anonymity and encryption is listed under the CVC's recent initiatives (Section 6).

  • Wider picture: The handout says the Act complements the CVC, the Lokpal and the CBI as part of India's integrity architecture, aiming to turn fear into civic courage and secrecy into transparency.

Recall sheet#

Cover the right column and answer. Same questions as the flashcards.

What is the CVC?
The apex vigilance institution of India: an independent statutory body promoting integrity, transparency and accountability in public administration.
When and how was the CVC first set up?
In 1964, by a Government of India Resolution on the Santhanam Committee's recommendation; it was non-statutory.
Which Act gave the CVC statutory status?
The Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003 (in force 2004).
Which case led to a direction for statutory status?
Vineet Narain v. Union of India (the handout says 1998 in one table and 1997 in another).
What is the composition of the CVC?
A Central Vigilance Commissioner (Chairperson) and not more than two Vigilance Commissioners: at most three members.
Who appoints the CVC and on whose recommendation?
The President, by a warrant under hand and seal, on the recommendation of PM (Chairperson), Home Minister and LoP in Lok Sabha.
What if there is no recognised LoP?
The leader of the single largest opposition party is deemed LoP.
What are the eligibility bars?
Cannot be an MP, a member of a Legislature, or hold any office of profit.
What is the tenure of the CVC and VCs?
Four years or until age 65, whichever is earlier (earlier five years).
Can a CVC be reappointed?
No. A Vigilance Commissioner may be appointed CVC within the same overall four-year limit.
To whom is the CVC's pay equal?
CVC: Chairperson of the UPSC. VCs: Members of the UPSC. Not variable to their disadvantage.
Name the six grounds of removal.
Insolvency; conviction involving moral turpitude; paid employment; mental or physical infirmity; financial or other interest affecting duties; proved misbehaviour or incapacity (after SC inquiry).
Under which Sections do the CVC's functions arise?
Sections 8 and 11 of the CVC Act, 2003.
What are the six heads of CVC functions?
Supervisory, advisory, preventive and educational, investigative and quasi-judicial, coordinating and monitoring, financial and administrative.
What powers does the CVC have in inquiries?
Powers equivalent to a civil court: summon witnesses, examine on oath, requisition public documents; suo motu inquiry if prima facie evidence.
How is the CVC financially independent?
Its budget is charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.
What is the CVC's relationship with the CBI?
Superintendence over CBI corruption investigations (PC Act, 1988) and monthly review; cannot direct the CBI to initiate prosecution.
Who selects the CBI Director (post-Lokpal Act, 2013)?
A committee of the PM, the Leader of Opposition and the Chief Justice of India.
How does the CVC relate to the Lokpal?
It assists in preliminary inquiries on Group A and B officers and reports progress back to the Lokpal.
What is a CVO?
Chief Vigilance Officer in each Ministry, Department, PSU and Bank: nodal point, appointed in consultation with the CVC, reports quarterly.
What is the CVC's equity test for CPSEs?
PSUs where the Central Government holds more than 51% equity.
What is the Integrity Index Framework?
Developed with IIM-Ahmedabad; ranks over 500 public organisations on transparency, ethical conduct and corruption-risk mitigation.
When was the AIVA Unit established?
2023: AI-based Vigilance Analytics for predictive analytics on procurement and financial data.
Who is the competent authority under the Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014?
The Central Vigilance Commission (the Government may notify another body).
Who is excluded from the Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014?
Personnel of the Special Protection Group (SPG).
What is the punishment for a false or frivolous disclosure?
Up to two years' imprisonment and a fine up to Rs 30,000.
What happens to anonymous whistleblower complaints?
No action where the identity of the complainant or public servant is not indicated or is found to be false.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Different panels for CBI and CVC The CVC panel is PM, Home Minister and Leader of Opposition. The CBI Director's panel is PM, Leader of Opposition and Chief Justice of India. Rule: Home Minister sits for the CVC; the Chief Justice sits for the CBI.
Trap 2 · Statutory only since 2003 The CVC began as a non-statutory body in 1964. The Vineet Narain judgement directed statutory status; the CVC Act, 2003 gave it. The handout gives the case year as 1998 in the evolution table and 1997 in one later line. Rule: write Vineet Narain as 1998; the Act is 2003.
Trap 3 · Superintendence has limits The CVC superintends the CBI only in Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 cases. It cannot direct the CBI to start prosecution; it can recommend inquiries and monitor progress. Rule: supervision and advice, not command of prosecution.
Trap 4 · Tenure and reappointment Tenure is now four years or age 65 (earlier five years). No reappointment, but a Vigilance Commissioner can become the Central Vigilance Commissioner within the same overall limit. Rule: 4 or 65, whichever earlier; no reappointment.

Book edition: simple words and memory words#

In simple words

  • The CVC is the apex vigilance institution of the Centre. It began in 1964 by a government resolution (Santhanam Committee), was pushed to statutory status by the Vineet Narain case (1998) and became statutory under the CVC Act, 2003.
  • It has a Central Vigilance Commissioner (chair) and up to two Vigilance Commissioners. The President appoints them on the advice of a committee of the PM, Home Minister and Leader of Opposition.
  • Tenure is 4 years or age 65, whichever is earlier; no reappointment (a Vigilance Commissioner may become the Commissioner). Pay equals the UPSC Chairperson and Members. Removal for misbehaviour or incapacity needs a Supreme Court inquiry.
  • The CVC superintends the CBI in corruption cases, reviews progress, supervises CVOs in ministries, PSUs and banks, advises on discipline and promotes preventive vigilance. It assists the Lokpal for Group A and B officers.
  • Under the Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014 the CVC is the competent authority. Disclosures need good faith and documents; anonymous ones get no action; false complaints face 2 years and Rs 30,000; the SPG is excluded.
