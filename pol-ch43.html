1 · Introduction and establishment#

The Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) is the apex vigilance institution of India. It promotes integrity, transparency and accountability in public administration and works as an independent statutory body against corruption in government organisations and public sector undertakings. It acts as a watchdog of the Central Government so that public officials act with honesty and efficiency.

  • Set up in 1964 by a Government of India Resolution, on the recommendations of the Committee on Prevention of Corruption (Santhanam Committee).
  • With no statutory backing, its effectiveness was limited.
  • The Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003 gave it statutory status and independence from executive control.
  • Today it is the nodal agency for vigilance administration, coordinating between ministries, the CBI and public sector entities.

  • President appoints on a committee of PM, Home Minister, LoP

  • Selected by a committee of PM, Leader of Opposition and Chief Justice of India (post-Lokpal Act, 2013)

Removal (Section 6 of the CVC Act, 2003)

The President can remove the CVC or any VC on these grounds:

  • (a) Insolvency
  • (b) Conviction of an offence involving moral turpitude
  • (c) Engaging in paid employment during office
  • (d) Mental or physical infirmity
  • (e) Financial or other interest affecting official duties
  • (f) Proved misbehaviour or incapacity, after a Supreme Court inquiry and recommendation to the President
Six grounds
ICPMFP

Insolvency, Conviction (moral turpitude), Paid employment, Mental or physical infirmity, Financial interest, Proved misbehaviour: "I Can't Pay My Fine Promptly."

Safeguard against arbitrary removal

Allegation
Proved misbehaviour or incapacity alleged
President refers
The matter must first go to the Supreme Court
Court inquires
Inquiry held on the cause for removal
Court upholds
Only then can the President remove; this ensures independence and security of tenure
Pattern

Grounds (a) to (e) lead straight to removal by the President; only ground (f), misbehaviour or incapacity, needs the Supreme Court inquiry first.

3 · Functions and powers#

The CVC combines advisory, supervisory, preventive and coordinating roles. Its functions derive primarily from Sections 8 and 11 of the CVC Act, 2003.

Six heads of functions
SAPICF

Supervisory, Advisory, Preventive and educational, Investigative and quasi-judicial, Coordinating and monitoring, Financial and administrative: "Sharp Auditors Prevent Illegal Cash Flow."

A. Supervisory and oversight functions

  • Superintendence over the CBI: over CBI investigations in cases under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, especially those involving senior public officials. This keeps corruption investigations impartial and free from political or bureaucratic pressure.
  • Review of progress and prosecutions: reviews investigations and prosecutions by the CBI, public sector undertakings and other central organisations; monitors pending cases and disciplinary proceedings for time-bound action.
  • Supervision of vigilance units: oversees Chief Vigilance Officers (CVOs) posted in each Ministry, Department and PSU; CVOs are the nodal officers for departmental vigilance, under the CVC's technical guidance.

B. Advisory functions

  • Advice on disciplinary matters: advises the Central Government and public sector organisations on disciplinary proceedings against officials under investigation. Advisory in nature, but its recommendations carry high persuasive value and are normally accepted.
  • Policy advice: formulates and updates vigilance manuals, holds consultations, recommends policy changes for ethical conduct and preventive vigilance.
  • Consultation with other authorities: consulted on appointment, transfer and posting of CVOs and vigilance staff in major departments and public enterprises.

C. Preventive and educational functions

  • Promotion of integrity: campaigns such as Vigilance Awareness Week and Preventive Vigilance Frameworks.
  • Preventive vigilance audits: periodic reviews of systems and procedures in corruption-prone areas to find loopholes and recommend reforms.
  • Training and capacity building: with the Institute of Public Administration (IIPA) and other bodies, orientation programmes for vigilance officers and managers.
  • Public outreach: the CVC Portal and complaint mechanisms let citizens report corruption and track action taken (participatory vigilance).

D. Investigative and quasi-judicial functions

  • Inquiry into complaints: can inquire, or cause inquiry, into corruption, favouritism, abuse of position or lack of integrity against public servants.
  • Suo motu action: can start inquiries in serious irregularities even without a formal complaint, if there is prima facie evidence.
  • Summoning and inspection: powers equivalent to a civil court in inquiries: summoning witnesses, examining them on oath, requisitioning public documents.
  • Recommending action: recommends departmental proceedings, criminal prosecution or other corrective measures.

E. Coordinating and monitoring functions

  • Coordination with Lokpal: under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013, assists the Lokpal in preliminary inquiries involving Group A and B officers.
  • Inter-agency cooperation: nodal link among the CBI, the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) and departmental vigilance units for data sharing and coordinated action.
  • Annual vigilance report: compiled and submitted to the President, laid before both Houses of Parliament.

F. Financial and administrative powers

  • Budget is charged on the Consolidated Fund of India, ensuring independence from executive control.
  • Can appoint, transfer or assign duties to its officers and staff and decide procedures for handling vigilance cases.
  • Can delegate functions to subordinate authorities for case management and coordination.

Advisory side

  • Disciplinary advice normally accepted
  • vigilance manuals
  • consulted on CVO postings

Investigative side

  • Inquiry on complaint or suo motu
  • civil court powers
  • recommends prosecution or departmental action
Independence shortcut

Independent because of: statute (2003), bipartisan committee, SC-inquiry removal, UPSC-equal pay that cannot be reduced, and budget charged on the Consolidated Fund.

7 · Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014#

The Act gives a statutory mechanism to protect people who disclose corruption, abuse of power or wilful misuse of authority by public servants. It rests on the idea that citizens have a duty to report wrongdoing without fear of retaliation or victimisation.

Objectives

Three objectives
PEI

Protection to whistleblowers, Encouragement of public interest disclosures, Institutional mechanism for complaints (including against ministers): "Public Eyes Illuminate."

  • Protection: safeguard identity and safety of those who expose corruption or misuse of public office.
  • Encouragement: promote disclosure of corrupt acts, maladministration or abuse of discretion through a secure channel.
  • Institutional mechanism: an independent framework to receive and inquire into complaints of corruption or wilful misuse of power by public servants, including ministers.

Competent authority and coverage

  • The CVC is the competent authority for receiving and inquiring into disclosures. The Government may, by notification, designate any other body.
  • Applies to all public servants: employees of Central and State Governments, public sector undertakings and government-aided bodies, except the Special Protection Group (SPG).

Procedure for disclosure

Making a disclosure

Mode
Complaint in writing or by electronic communication (email), as prescribed
Evidence
Full particulars, with documents or material evidence supporting the allegations
Declaration
Personal declaration that the information is in good faith and believed to be substantially true
Anonymous
No action on complaints where the identity of the complainant or the public servant is not indicated or is found to be false

Protection and safeguards

  • Identity protection: confidentiality of the whistleblower's identity to prevent victimisation, harassment or physical harm.
  • No retaliation: a person disclosing under the Act cannot be subjected to punitive or disciplinary action by the concerned public authority.
  • False complaints: punishment of up to two years' imprisonment and a fine up to Rs 30,000 for wilful false or frivolous disclosures.

Limitations and exceptions

  • SPG personnel are excluded because of their sensitive security functions.
  • Disclosures prejudicial to national security or covered by the Official Secrets Act may be exempted from inquiry.

Protected

  • Good-faith disclosure with identity and evidence
  • confidentiality
  • no punitive or disciplinary action

Punished

  • Wilful false or frivolous disclosure: up to 2 years and Rs 30,000
Act in numbers
CS23

CVC is competent authority, SPG excluded, 2 years, Rs 30,000: "CVC Shields; 2 years for lies, 30,000 fine."

Handout alert

The handout says the Act ensures anonymity and confidentiality of the whistleblower, yet also that no action is taken on anonymous complaints (where identity is not indicated or found false). Read them together: the whistleblower must give a verifiable identity to the authority, and the authority then keeps that identity confidential. The PIDPI portal with stronger anonymity and encryption is listed under the CVC's recent initiatives (Section 6).

Wider picture

The handout says the Act complements the CVC, the Lokpal and the CBI as part of India's integrity architecture, aiming to turn fear into civic courage and secrecy into transparency.

Recall sheet#

Read once, hide it, then rebuild the chapter from memory.

  1. CVC: apex vigilance body; statutory under the CVC Act, 2003 (in force 2004); started 1964 by Resolution on Santhanam Committee advice; statutory status directed in Vineet Narain.
  2. Composition: 1 Central Vigilance Commissioner plus not more than 2 VCs; max 3.
  3. Appointment: President, on committee of PM, Home Minister, LoP in Lok Sabha.
  4. Tenure: four years or age 65 (earlier five years); no reappointment; VC can become CVC within the four-year limit.
  5. Pay equal to UPSC Chairperson (CVC) and Members (VCs); cannot be reduced during tenure.
  6. Removal by President on six grounds; misbehaviour or incapacity only after Supreme Court inquiry (Section 6).
  7. Functions under Sections 8 and 11: supervisory, advisory, preventive, investigative, coordinating, financial.
  8. Budget charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.
  9. Jurisdiction: departments, CPSEs (over 51% equity), autonomous bodies, banks, Group A officers, PC Act cases.
  10. CBI: superintendence, monthly review; cannot direct prosecution.
  11. Lokpal: CVC assists on Group A and B officers and reports back.
  12. CVO: nodal point, quarterly report; DVUs keep case registers.
  13. Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014: CVC competent authority; SPG excluded; false complaint up to 2 years and Rs 30,000.
