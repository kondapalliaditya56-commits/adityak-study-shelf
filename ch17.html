Mains hooks

  • "1857 Revolt was only a final nail in the coffin of EIC's empire in India. Discuss." Each Act stripped the EIC of powers and gave them to the Br State
  • Why the Br State regulated the Company State in 1773 instead of ending it (two incomes, burden of ruling, empire and geopolitics against the five worries)
  • Dual Government against Dual Control; centralisation in the GG (peak 1833) and the start of a separate legislature

The sovereignty moved to the Crown by 1793 and the Company's body was left in place until the Crown could take it over in 1858.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: London first let the Company rule, then took its powers one by one: it regulated the political (CMR) side in 1773, controlled it in 1784, took the head in 1793, the trade in 1813 and 1833, the jobs and the Budget in 1853, and the body in 1858. Each Act moved power from the EIC to the Br State, so 1857 was the final nail, not the first wound.

  1. A trader turned ruler (1765). The EIC has done business since 31 December 1600; since the Treaty of Allahabad in 1765 it also runs Civil, Military and Revenue (CMR) affairs, a Company State. The Br State has no problem with a trader but 'problem and anxiety' with a Company State, since only a State holds power over territory. Yet it does not end it: the EIC brings two incomes (trade and land revenue), bears the burden of ruling far-away India, and raises Britain's empire and geopolitical power indirectly. So from 1773 the State regulates the EIC's political matters.
    → But why was London so nervous?
  2. Five worries. Money: in 1767 the State demanded 400,000 pounds a year. America: the revolution of 1765-83 ended with the USA in 1783, so what if EIC men did the same? The taxpayer: the EIC went bankrupt in 1772 and was lent 1.4 million pounds. Conscience: the Great Bengal Famine of 1770 and the Nabobs. Careers: Clive 57-67 and Hastings 73-85 wanted a Crown link. The Select Committee of 1772 kept the EIC's trade monopoly but made the Court of Directors accountable for its political functions.
    → So Parliament passes the Regulating Act of 1773.
  3. The Regulating Act 1773 in India. The Governor of Bengal becomes GG(Bengal) and is made senior to Bombay and Madras in war and peace, except in emergency; commerce is untouched. A Supreme Council of GG + 4 decides by majority and the GG has only a casting vote, so he needs at least two councillors: a check on an empire-builder. Law-making for Bengal and on war and peace goes to the council.
    → And in London the State gets its own hooks.
  4. The Regulating Act 1773 in London. For the first time Parliament's right to regulate the EIC's political affairs is recognised. The GG sends information to the Court of Directors and the Directors send political matters to the State. A Supreme Court at Calcutta (a Chief Justice and three puisne judges, all chosen by the Crown, Elijah Impey first in 1774) is created, officials may not take gifts, and the tenures are 4, 5 and 4 years.
    → But the Act leaks.
  5. The leaks of 1773. The Supreme Court clashes with the GG's council over officials' official acts; distance blunts supervision; Bombay signs the Treaty of Surat in 1775 without the GG, starting the 1st Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82). With no veto, the GG is blocked by factions: Philip Francis opposes Hastings 73-85 and Edmund Burke leads an impeachment, which ends in acquittal. The Amending Act of 1781 fixes the Supreme Court's jurisdiction.
    → Having lost America, London moves from supervision to control.
  6. Pitt's India Act 1784. Britain lost 13 colonies in 1783, so the State fears an Indian declaration of independence. A Board of Control (six members, a ministry for Indian affairs) takes political affairs while the Court of Directors keeps commerce: Double Government 1784-1833. The BoC can also order the GG directly: Dual Control 1784-1858. The council shrinks to GG + 2 + CIC and the GG gets a regular and a casting vote. Conquest is declared 'repugnant'. The Amending Act of 1786 gives a veto and lets Cornwallis 86-93 hold both GG and C-in-C.
    → Still, the Company appoints its own top men.
  7. 1793: the Crown takes the head. Subjects of the Crown cannot acquire sovereign power except on behalf of the Crown, so the EIC is an agent. Principal Servants (GG, Governors, C-in-C, Councillors) are appointed by the EIC but only after Crown approval, and Parliament renews the Charter for the first time. The head of the Company State belongs to the State while the body stays with the EIC. In India the GG can supersede Governors, the CIC becomes an extra-ordinary member, and Cornwallis 86-93's Code makes law written.
    → Next, free-trade ideas knock on the door.
  8. 1813: the monopoly breaks. Adam Smith (1776), Liberals, Utilitarians and Evangelists, and lobbying manufacturers attack the monopoly; the Industrial Revolution makes Britain want India's raw material and an export market. The India trade monopoly ends (Tea, Opium and China stay), Crown sovereignty is stated as undoubted, missionaries enter with a licence and Rs 1 lakh a year goes to education. The Act contributes to 1857.
    → The rest of the trade follows in 1833.
  9. 1833: one India, one law. The St Helena Act ends the EIC's commercial function: no more business, only a political agent. The BoC supervises all administration and its President is renamed Minister for Indian Affairs. The GG(Bengal)-in-Council becomes GG(India)-in-Council (Bentinck 28-35 first), the Governors of Bombay and Madras lose law-making, Macaulay joins as Law Member, the Law Commission of 1834 begins codification and the UCS opens to Indians. Centralisation peaks.
    → 1853 takes the jobs and the purse.
  10. 1853: no term, no jobs, no Budget. The Charter lasts only 'till Parliament specifies otherwise'; a Civil Services exam (first held in London in 1855) ends the EIC's patronage and opens the CCS to Indians; the Budget goes to Parliament. Six Legal Members (four provincial, a Chief Justice and a judge) make a legislative council of twelve, and in 1854 Dalhousie 48-56 appoints Halliday as first LG of Bengal.
    → Then 1857 arrives.
  11. 1858: the Crown takes the body. The EIC is blamed for 1857; the public, free traders and the western-educated middle class want direct rule. The GOI Act 1858 ends Company rule: a Secretary of State with a 15-member India Council replaces the BoC, Dual Control ends, and Canning 56-62 is first Viceroy. Mughal sovereignty is ended (Zafar 37-57 goes to Rangoon) and the princes of the 565 PS live under Paramountcy. The Proclamation promises no annexation, Crown administration, religious non-interference and amnesty; the Royal Titles Act 1876 makes Victoria Kaesar-i-Hind.
    → But who was responsible for the conquest itself?
  12. Whose conquest? Britain claimed no official policy of conquest; Sub-Imperialism by EIC officials drove it, and Pitt's India Act 1784 banned it. Yet the State gave the Charter (the tools), sent the Royal Military at Plassey and in October 1857, financed the EIC, followed the money, controlled the empire-builders and used the EIC as a foreign-policy tool (the 1st Afghan War, the Great Game).

In simple words#

  • The Br State let the EIC rule as a Company State, but from 1773 it regulated only the EIC's political (CMR) work, because trade is private business and territory is a State matter.
  • 1773 (Regulating Act): GG(Bengal), a council of GG + 4, a Supreme Court at Calcutta. 1784 (Pitt's India Act): the Board of Control, Double Government and Dual Control.
  • 1793: the Crown took the head (sovereignty, Principal Servants after Crown approval). 1813 ended the India trade monopoly, 1833 ended all trade and made the GG of India, 1853 took patronage and the Budget.
  • After 1857, the GOI Act 1858 replaced the EIC with the Crown: a Secretary of State, the India Council, a Viceroy and Paramountcy over the princes.
  • Read every Act through two lenses, @GG (the Company State in India) and @Br State (London over the EIC). Each Act moved power from the EIC to the State, so 1857 was only the final nail.

Act by act: what London took

1 · Why the Br State wanted to regulate the EIC#

Story link

Because the EIC grew rich and powerful after 1765, London grew anxious. Ask: which of the five drivers were about money, which about fear, which about conscience?

Key takeaway
  • The 1857 revolt is often seen as why EIC rule ended, but the laws Parliament passed from 1773 show the EIC's autonomy declining nearly a century earlier.
  • Each Charter Act and Regulating Act stripped the EIC of powers and transferred them to the British State. 1857 was the final nail, not the first wound.

1.1 The five drivers

DriverDetail
1. MoneyAfter Plassey (1757) and the ToA 1765, the EIC got both plunder and Bengal's land revenue. In 1767 the Br State demanded 400,000 pounds a year from the EIC, arguing the State had helped the EIC in conquest. The State wanted its share
2. Fear of another AmericaThe American Revolution began in 1765 (the Stamp Act crisis marks the start of the political revolution) and by 1783 the USA was created. The armed War of Independence began in 1775. What if EIC officials, growing rich and powerful in India, did the same?
3. Protecting the taxpayerIn 1772 the EIC went bankrupt and was bailed out with a 1.4 million pound loan from the State ("too big to fail"). The State wanted oversight to prevent reckless war expenditure
4. White Man's Burden betrayedThe Great Bengal Famine of 1770 and the "Nabobs" (corrupt EIC officers who returned to Britain filthy rich) embarrassed the State and went against the civilising mission narrative
5. Career ambitions of EIC menSenior officials such as Robert Clive 57-67 and Warren Hastings 73-85 also wanted a formal relationship with the State: the Crown gave legitimacy and career advancement

1.2 Select Committee of Parliament (1772)

  • Parliament set up a Select Committee to study the EIC's affairs in India and to advise on the relationship between the British State and the EIC with regard to the EIC's territorial possessions.
  • Only political functions were examined (in capitalism the State does not interfere in a company's business).
  • Result: the EIC kept its monopoly over trade with India.
  • But the Court of Directors (CoD) in London was now made accountable for the EIC's political functions in India.
  • This led directly to the Regulating Act of 1773.

Why regulation, and why in 1773

  • 1765 ToA: the EIC becomes a Company State, with two incomes, trade and land revenue
  • 1767:
    • The State demands 400,000 pounds a year
    • 1765-83: the American Revolution
    • 1770: famine and Nabobs
  • 1772:
    • The EIC goes bankrupt
    • The State lends 1.4 million pounds
    • Select Committee of Parliament
  • Result:
    • Trade monopoly kept
    • The CoD made accountable for political functions
  • Regulating Act 1773: the State regulates the EIC's CMR matters, not its trade

2 · Regulating Act of 1773#

Story link

Because the five worries peaked in 1772, Parliament regulated in 1773. Ask: what did the Act do in India (@GG) and in London (@Br State), and where did it leak?

2.1 @GG: provisions shaping the Company State in India

Teacher's board
  • Reporting chain, RA 1773. GG(Be) gives orders on war and peace to G(Bo) and G(Ma), who send all information back.
  • GG(Be) sends all information (6 months) on Commerce and CMR to the CoD of EIC, which passes CMR information to the Br STATE [circled E and P: unclear, probably Executive and Parliament
  • Check your notebook]
  • GG(Be)'s council = Supreme Council of Bengal = 4 Councillors (C1 to C4).
  • 1. The Governor of the Bengal Presidency was elevated to Governor-General of the Bengal Presidency (GG/Bengal).
  • He stayed Governor of Bengal but was now a General over the Governors of Bombay and Madras in specific matters.
  • 2. The Governors of Bombay and Madras were subordinated to GG(Bengal) in matters of war and peace, except in emergency circumstances.
  • They could be suspended if they disobeyed.
  • The logic:
    • High war expenditure caused the 1772 bankruptcy, so the GG was made accountable
    • and accountability without power is meaningless.
  • 3. Commercial affairs were untouched. In commerce the Governors stayed independent regional managers.
  • 4. A Supreme Council of Bengal was created with four councillors (GG + 4).
  • All decisions were by majority vote of the four councillors.
  • The GG had only a casting vote, a tiebreaker.
  • This was a check on any empire-building ambitions of the GG.
  • 5. Legislative function was given to the GG's Council: it could make laws governing Bengal and on matters of war and peace.
Teacher's board
  • "GG is not boss in commerce matters" (row 3): in commerce the Governors stayed independent.
  • "[Treaty = Law]" (row 5): a treaty on war and peace is made by law of the GG's Council.
  • In "Governor-General", Governor = ruler of the Bengal Presidency, in charge of making laws and implementing them
  • General = senior to G(Bo) and G(Ma)
Teacher's board
  • Decision making in the Supreme Council: a check against the empire-builder GG. (1) The GG proposes.
  • (2) Only the four councillors C1 to C4 vote.
  • (3) On a tie, the GG's casting vote decides.
  • So the GG needs the support of at least 2 councillors (2 for and 2 against is a tie he breaks).

2.2 @Br State: provisions shaping the EIC-State relationship

  • 1. For the first time Parliament's right to regulate the EIC's political affairs was legally recognised. Commercial affairs stayed outside supervision.
  • (Since 1600 the EIC had commercial functions; since 1765 also political functions, civil, military and revenue.)
  • 2. The GG collects information on commercial and political matters and sends it to the Court of Directors (CoD) in London.
  • The CoD submits information on political matters to the British State.
  • 3. A Supreme Court in Calcutta: one Chief Justice and three puisne judges ("puny", junior judges), all chosen by the Crown.
  • The first Chief Justice was Sir Elijah Impey (1774).
  • It heard cases involving British-born subjects and their Indian servants in the Bengal Presidency, under British law.
  • Similar courts followed at Madras in 1800 and Bombay in 1823 (earlier cases involving British subjects went to Mayor's Courts): thus 3 Supreme Courts.
  • 4. To curb corruption, EIC officials were barred from accepting gifts from Indians.
  • 5. Tenures:
    • Directors of the EIC 4 years, not immediately re-eligible
    • GG(Bengal) 5 years, re-appointable
    • Councillors 4 years
    • all first appointees were named in the Act itself, future ones by the CoD
Teacher's board
  • "[not today's SC]": a British-law court at Calcutta, not today's Supreme Court of India.
  • "(not BY EIC)": judges chosen by the Crown, not the EIC, so the court was the State's instrument.
  • "Hastings 73-85 [GG's terms]": the first GG's 5-year tenure.

2.3 Impact of the 1773 Act

  • 1. The Supreme Court was set up at Calcutta in 1774.
  • (The Act was passed in 1773; Warren Hastings 73-85 assumed office as the first GG of Bengal in 1774.) Conflict arose between the Supreme Court and the GG's Council over cases arising from acts done by EIC officials in their official capacity.
  • 2. Poor supervision: the huge distance between India and London and no modern communication.
  • 3. Centralisation of power in the GG began: the journey of India's central executive and central legislature starts here.
  • (Three equal Governors become one GG over two Governors.)
  • 4. Bombay and Madras still acted independently because of the vague phrase "emergency circumstances." Example: the Treaty of Surat (1775) was signed by the Governor of Bombay without the GG's approval, leading directly to the 1st Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82).

3.2 @Br State: provisions shaping the EIC-State relationship

  • 1. The Act used the phrase "British territorial possessions in India" (for the first time) but left it ambiguous whether British India belonged to the EIC or the British State.
  • 2. Double Government of EIC affairs (1784-1833): political affairs went under the supreme control of the Br State through a new Board of Control (BoC) in London, effectively a ministry for Indian affairs.
  • On commercial affairs the top authority remained the CoD of the EIC
  • on political affairs it was now the BoC.
  • 3. The BoC had six members: one Secretary of State (a Cabinet minister) as President of the BoC, the Chancellor of the Exchequer (the Finance Minister) and four members of the Privy Council.
  • (The Privy Council is the Crown's advisory body; earlier it worked like the Mughal Darbar or the Chhatrapati's Ashtapradhan.) Its role: to superintend, direct and control all acts relating to Civil, Military and Revenue government of the British territorial possessions in the East Indies.

  • 7. Dual Control over GG (1784-1858): the BoC could pass binding orders both to the CoD and directly to the GG, bypassing the CoD.

3.3 @GG: provisions shaping the Company State in India

Aim: resolve the indecision problem of 1773.

  • 1. GG(Bengal)'s council cut from 4 councillors to 3.
  • One seat was mandated for the Commander-in-Chief (CIC) of the EIC army as an ex-officio ordinary member.
  • (Ordinary = mandated in the Act; ex-officio = member because he is the CIC; extra-ordinary = the CoD chooses whether to appoint him.) Council of 1784 = GG + 2 councillors + CIC.
  • 2. The GG was given both a regular vote and a casting vote.
  • With just one councillor's support the GG could create a tie and break it in his favour.
  • (Earlier he needed at least two.
  • In effect one councillor was removed and his vote was given to the GG.)
  • 3. Bombay and Madras Governors were now subordinated to GG(Bengal) in war, peace and revenue (earlier only war and peace).
  • This is how the Permanent Settlement of 1793 could extend to parts of Madras (the Northern Sarkars; see Chapter 16).
  • 4. Responsibilities of the GG and the Governors were more clearly defined, removing the vagueness of "except in emergency circumstances" (RA 1773).
  • 5. A Council of 3 was created under each of the Governors of Bombay and Madras (the beginning of today's State assemblies and Council of Ministers, without separation of powers).
  • 6. "Conquest in India is repugnant to the wish, policy and honour of this nation." The Act thus banned imperial expansion, a response to the heavy war expenditure of the 1st Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82) and the 2nd Anglo-Mysore War (1780-84).
  • (The handout's aside: in reality only fiscal decline was repugnant, not empire-building.)
Teacher's board
  • Why the 1784 council helps the GG.
  • Before (1773):
    • 2 for, 2 against, a tie
    • The GG casts and wins 3 against 2.
  • After (1784): council C1, C2, C3 and the GG also votes: with only C3 for him, the votes are 2-2 (C3 + GG against C1 + C2), a tie, he casts: 3 against 2.

3.4 Impact of the 1784 Act and the Amending Act of 1786

  • 1. Dual control in practice meant no effective control: the GG could play his two masters (CoD and BoC) against each other, given the distance and no modern communication.
  • 2. The GG still had no veto over his council: if all three councillors opposed him, the indecision problem continued.
  • (Indecision continued, BUT the position improved because he needed only one councillor.)
  • 3. Conflict between the GG and the C-in-C: the decision of war and peace rested with the GG, but the right to use the army rested with the C-in-C.
  • 4. The Amending Act of 1786 amended Pitt's Act by:
    • (i) giving the GG a veto over his council in extraordinary circumstances
    • (ii) letting the EIC appoint the same person to both posts, GG and C-in-C, to avoid the rift (the posts were not merged, and it was not compulsory, just permitted)
  • This was done on the insistence of Cornwallis 86-93 (GG 1786-93), who became the first person to hold both posts.

9 · Conquest in India: policy versus drift#

Story link

Because the 1784 Act banned conquest yet the empire grew, the State's role needs explaining. Ask: was conquest policy or drift, and where was the State involved?

Handout note
  • Section 9 rests on typed slides without board work
  • the handout gives the same points
  • It is the "Br State responsibility for conquest" Mains angle.

9.1 No official policy of conquest

  • On the surface, Britain had no official policy of conquest.
  • Imperial expansion was largely driven by Sub-Imperialism: the policies, actions and personal ambitions of senior EIC officials in India, many of whom built political careers back home after their India tenures.
  • Pitt's India Act (1784) explicitly banned conquest in India, a response to the heavy expenditure of the 1st Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82) and the 2nd Anglo-Mysore War (1780-84).

9.2 British State responsibility

  • However, the British State was responsible for conquest, for these reasons:
  • a) Tools of conquest given by the State:
    • The EIC's Charter gave it the right to wage war, acquire and administer territory
    • the Charter was issued first by the Crown and later by Parliament.
  • b) Military aid at critical junctures:
    • Plassey (1757) was won with the help of the Royal British Military (Chapter 10)
    • When the 1857 revolt nearly overthrew Company rule, the arrival of the Royal Military in October 1857 turned the tide
  • The State helped both the initiation and the survival of conquest.
  • c) The State financed the Company: trade monopoly over Britain's East Indies trade (1600-1813).
  • King Charles II 60-85 rented Bombay (received as a dowry from the Portuguese in 1661) to the EIC for just 10 pounds a year in 1668.
  • Thomas Roe was sent as a State representative to Jahangir 05-27's court to lobby.
  • The State bailed out the EIC with a 1.4 million pound loan in 1772.
  • d) Follow the money trail:
    • Over 1,50,000 pounds to the Monarch in the 17th century for Charter renewals
    • In 1698 Parliament gave the monopoly to a "New Company" because it offered a 2 million pound loan against the EIC's 7,00,000
    • in 1767 the EIC had to pay 4,00,000 pounds a year
    • many Parliamentarians and even Queen Elizabeth I 58-03 were shareholders receiving dividends

Five drivers of regulation?Money (1767: 400,000 pounds a year); fear of another America (1765-83); taxpayer (1772 bankruptcy, 1.4 million pound loan); White Man's Burden betrayed (GBF 1770, Nabobs); career ambitions of Clive 57-67 and Hastings 73-85.
What did the Select Committee of 1772 conclude?The EIC kept its trade monopoly; the CoD was made accountable for the EIC's political functions; this led to the Regulating Act 1773.
What did the RA 1773 do @GG?GG(Bengal) over Bombay and Madras in war and peace (except emergency); Supreme Council GG + 4, majority vote, casting vote only; law-making for Bengal and war and peace; commerce untouched.
What did the RA 1773 do @Br State?Recognised Parliament's right to regulate political affairs; information to the CoD and the State; Supreme Court at Calcutta (Crown-chosen, 1 CJ + 3 puisne, Impey 1774); no gifts; tenures 4, 5, 4.
What went wrong after 1773?Conflict between the SC and the GG's council; distance; Bombay and Madras acted independently (Treaty of Surat 1775, 1st Anglo-Maratha War); no veto, factionalism and the indecision problem (Philip Francis, Burke's impeachment).
What did the Amending Act of 1781 do?Fixed the SC's jurisdiction: Calcutta, British subjects in Bengal (not official acts of EIC officials), and EIC property in Fort William.
What was the Board of Control?Created by PIA 1784: a ministry for Indian affairs of 6 (a Secretary of State as President, Chancellor of the Exchequer, 4 Privy Councillors) to superintend, direct and control Civil, Military and Revenue government in the East Indies.
Double Government versus Dual Control?Double Government (1784-1833): the EIC's work split, commerce to the CoD, CMR to the BoC. Dual Control (1784-1858): the GG has two bosses in CMR matters, CoD and BoC.
What was the Secret Committee?Three directors within the CoD, through whom the BoC could send confidential military and diplomatic orders to India bypassing the full CoD.
How did PIA 1784 change the GG's council?From GG + 4 to GG + 2 + CIC (ordinary, ex-officio); the GG got a regular and a casting vote, so one councillor's support was enough; Governors subordinated in war, peace and revenue; Councils of 3 under the Governors.
What does PIA 1784 say about conquest?"Conquest in India is repugnant to the wish, policy and honour of this nation": a reaction to the 1st Anglo-Maratha War 1775-82 and the 2nd Anglo-Mysore War 1780-84.
What did the Amending Act 1786 do?GG got a veto in extraordinary circumstances; the same person may hold GG and C-in-C (not merged, not compulsory); on Cornwallis 86-93's insistence, he was first to hold both.
Ordinary versus extra-ordinary member?Ordinary: the law itself says the person will be a member (CIC in 1784). Extra-ordinary: the law lets someone, e.g. the CoD, appoint him (CIC in 1793).
The six firsts?Hastings 73-85 (GG Bengal 1773), Bentinck 28-35 (GG India 1833), Canning 56-62 (Viceroy 1858), Mountbatten (partitioned India 15 Aug 1947), Rajagopalachari (first Indian GG), Rajendra Prasad (first President).
What did Charter 1793 do @Br State?Crown sovereignty (EIC = agent); BoC cut to a President + 2; BoC salary by the EIC; first Parliament renewal (20 years); Principal Servants after Crown approval; patronage kept for lesser posts.
What was the hallmark of Charter 1793 @GG?The Cornwallis 86-93 Code: a code of regulations (written law and judgement, in the vernacular, Hindu and Islamic laws, the Permanent Settlement Act); CIC became extra-ordinary (GG + CIC + 3); GG could supersede Governors.
Why was the Charter Act 1813 passed?Adam Smith 1776 (laissez faire); Liberals, Utilitarians, Evangelists; lobbying by manufacturers and non-EIC traders; the Industrial Revolution (raw material and export market); Great Ancient India a myth.
What did Charter 1813 do?Undoubted Crown sovereignty; India trade monopoly ended (Tea, Opium, China kept); missionaries with a CoD or BoC licence; Rs 1 lakh a year on education; 20 years; contributed to 1857.
What did Charter 1833 do @Br State?Commercial function fully ended (Double Government ends), only a political agent; BoC supervises all administration; President of BoC renamed Minister for Indian Affairs; St Helena taken over.
What did Charter 1833 do @GG?GG(India)-in-Council, executive and legislative head; Governors of Bombay and Madras lose law-making (restored 1861); Law Member Macaulay; Law Commission 1834; UCS and judicial posts opened; centralisation peaks.
What did Charter 1853 do?No fixed term; patronage lost (Civil Services exam 1855, London); CCS opened to Indians; Budget to Parliament; 6 Legal Members (4 provincial, CJ, 1 judge) = 12; LG of Bengal (Halliday 1854).
Governor versus Lieutenant-Governor (post 1858)?Governor: presidencies, Crown-appointed, aristocrat, direct to the SoS, council. LG: provinces, GG-appointed, ICS, through the GG, sole executive (the teacher: curiosity, not imp).
What did the GOI Act 1858 do @Br State?Direct Crown rule; Subordinate Union and Paramountcy (Sov, D, C, FA); Mughal sovereignty ended, Zafar 37-57 to Rangoon; SoS; 15-member India Council (7 from the CoD); Dual Control ended; Viceroy and GG, Canning 56-62 first.
What did Queen Victoria's Proclamation 1858 promise?No annexation of Indian States; administration under the Crown; religious non-interference, fair treatment and equal public service; amnesty to the 1857 rebels (limited exceptions).
What did the Royal Titles Act 1876 do?Let Victoria take the title Kaesar-i-Hind (Empress of India); the 1877 Delhi Darbar under Lytton 76-80 coronated her.
Why was the 1853 council of 12?[GG + CIC + 4 ordinary] = 6 executive members plus 6 Legal Members (4 provincial + CJ + 1 judge of Calcutta SC) = 12.
How did PIA 1784 and the Prelims 2002 match line up?A Charter 1813 = 2 (trade ended); B Regulating 1773 = 4 (Directors present documents); C Act 1858 = 3 (power to the Crown); D Pitt's 1784 = 1 (Board of Control): option (a).
