1996PYQ 1(Prelims 1996)

Who among the following leaders did not believe in the drain theory of Dadabhai Naoroji?

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — Sir Syed Ahmed Khan stood apart: he was loyal to the British and opposed the INC's politics of self-rule — he did not subscribe to the Drain Theory, unlike the Moderate economists who built it. names the theory's architects: R.C. Dutt, Dadabhai Naoroji, and M.G. Ranade.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Tilak stood inside the nationalist camp that embraced the economic critique of British rule; the question seeks the one leader OUTSIDE it — Sir Syed, who opposed the INC and its politics altogether.

(B) ✗ — R.C. Dutt is one of the theory's ARCHITECTS — His work gave the Drain Theory its historical depth and empirical backbone.

(C) ✗ — M.G. Ranade likewise built the theory — the record lists him among its architects and calls him "the father of Indian economics." (Note: Sir Syed Ahmed Khan did not believe in the Drain Theory. He was loyal to British and was opposed to Congress' idea of self rule and end of British empire.)

1997PYQ 2(Prelims 1997)

M.C Setalvad, B.N. Rao and Alladi Krishnaswamy Iyer were distinguished members of the

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — M.C. Setalvad, B.N. Rau, and Alladi Krishnaswamy Iyer were distinguished members of the All India National Liberal Federation, the post-1918 Moderate splinter party from the INC (founded after the Surat Split aftermath; many Moderates left INC to form the Federation alongside Surendranath Banerjea and Tej Bahadur Sapru). All three were noted constitutional lawyers; B.N. Rau later drafted the initial Indian Constitution; Alladi Krishnaswamy Iyer was a Constituent Assembly member. (Note: Chapter Ch 27 currently lacks an explicit section on the All India National Liberal Federation — Chapter update recommended.)

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The Swaraj Party (1923) was the Council-entry party of C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru — a post-Non-Cooperation formation, not the Moderates' Liberal Federation where these three constitutional lawyers belonged.

(D) ✗ — The Servants of India Society (1905) was Gokhale's order of full-time social workers — not the political home of Setalvad, Rau, and Alladi Krishnaswamy Iyer, whom the record places in the All India National Liberal Federation (1918).

(C) ✗ — The Madras Labour Union (1918) was a workers' trade union — no connection to these three jurists of the Liberal Federation.

1999PYQ 4(Prelims 1999)

There was no independent development of industries in India during British rule because of the

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — The record account of why no independent industry grew: there was no industrialisation of India — no Indian factories came up to replace the handicrafts — British rule built no capital-goods (heavy-industry) base, so independent industrial development was impossible.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — Capital scarcity is not the record explanation — the drain shipped India's own surplus OUT (Home Charges, dividends, interest); what India lacked was the factory base itself.

(C) ✗ — Resources were never the obstacle — India was Britain's raw-material source, exporting cotton, indigo, jute, and opium in bulk; a resource-scarce country could not have played that role.

(D) ✗ — The drift back to land was the CONSEQUENCE of having no factories to absorb displaced artisans (the record "disguised unemployment in agriculture"), not the cause of missing industrial development. (Note: The absence of heavy industries during British period in India was due to the negligence and high-handedness of British regime.)

2008PYQ 5(Prelims 2008)

Who among the following used the phrase 'Un-british' to criticise the English colonial control of India?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — The phrase is Dadabhai Naoroji's: his book Poverty and Un-British Rule in India (1901) was "the most systematic presentation" of the Drain Theory — exploitation indicted as conduct unworthy of Britain's own professed principles.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Anandamohan Bose was a Bengal Moderate organiser, not the economist of the drain; the 'Un-British' coinage is fixed in the title of Naoroji's 1901 book.

(B) ✗ — Badruddin Tyabji's place in the era is the INC presidency (first Muslim President, Madras 1887); the 'Un-British' indictment belongs to Naoroji's economic critique.

(D) ✗ — Pherozeshah Mehta was an INC founder-figure of the Bombay school, not the author of the drain critique — the phrase comes from Naoroji's Poverty and Un-British Rule in India. (Note: Dadabhai Naoroji (1865-1917) in his book "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India" described an economic critique of British polices by alleging them of home charges. He was the first Indian to be elected to the British House of Commons.)

2008PYQ 6(Prelims 2008)

Who among the following rejected the title of 'Knighthood' and refused to accept a position in the Council of the Secretary of State for India?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Gokhale declined both the Knighthood and a seat on the Council of the Secretary of State — consistent with the ethic of self-denying public service he institutionalised in the Servants of India Society (1905).

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Motilal Nehru's renunciations belong to a LATER era — he gave up his legal practice during Non-Cooperation (1920–22); the Knighthood-and-Council refusal is Gokhale's story.

(B) ✗ — Ranade, as a sitting judge, HELD government office his whole career — the opposite of refusing a position; the refusal belongs to his protégé Gokhale.

(D) ✗ — Tilak was never offered such honours — the Raj prosecuted and jailed him rather than courted him; only a Moderate the British wished to co-opt could refuse a Knighthood, and that was Gokhale. (Note: In 1905, Gopal Krishna Gokhale had established the Servants of India Society. He had also rejected the title of 'Knighthood' and refused to accept a position in the Council of the Secretary of State for India.)

2011PYQ 7(Prelims 2011)

With reference to the period of colonial rule in India, 'Home Charges' formed an important part of drain of wealth from India. Which of the following funds constituted 'Home Charges'? 1. Funds used to support the Indian office in London. 2. Funds used to pay salaries and pensions of British personnel engaged in India. 3. Funds used for waging wars outside India by the British. Select the correct answer using the codes given below

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — All three sat in the drain's London ledger: Home Charges covered goods and services supposedly provided to India by Britain or the Secretary of State — the India Office establishment (statement 1); salaries, pensions, and training costs of British officials and soldiers (statement 2); and India was billed even for Britain's campaigns beyond India — its army fought imperial wars like the Boxer Rebellion (1899–1901) at Indian expense (statement 3).

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — "Only 1" undercounts severely: officials' salaries, pensions and training costs (statement 2) head the record own Home-Charges list.

(B) ✗ — "1 and 2" stops short: India was also charged for wars waged outside India — the Boxer Rebellion deployment is the record own example of Indian money spent on Britain's external campaigns.

(C) ✗ — "2 and 3" drops statement 1 — but the India Office in London was the most direct of the charges India paid "for goods and services supposedly provided" by Britain. (Note: All wars waged in defence of India was put under Home Charges. In fact, the suppression of the 1857 Revolt itself was put under the 'Home Charges'.)

2013PYQ 8(Prelims 2013)

Consider the following statements The most effective contribution made by Dadabhai Naoroji to the cause of Indian freedom Movement was that he 1. exposed the economic exploitation of India by the British. 2. interpreted the ancient Indian texts and restored the self-confidence of Indians. 3. stressed the need for eradication of all the social evils before anything else. Which of the statement(s) given above is/are correct?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Statement 1 is Naoroji's exact contribution: the Drain Theory exposed how Britain used political power to extract India's wealth. Statements 2 and 3 describe other streams: textual revivalism was not his work, and "social evils first" inverts his record — Naoroji's 1886 rule kept social questions OFF the INC agenda.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — Contains neither of Naoroji's actual contributions: he was the economist of the drain, not a textual revivalist (statement 2), and his 1886 rule deliberately excluded social questions from the Congress platform (statement 3).

(C) ✗ — Statement 3 directly contradicts Naoroji's record: he laid down the informal rule that the INC would take up only political questions, not social or class questions.

(D) ✗ — Overcounts: only statement 1 survives — statements 2 (revivalism) and 3 (social-evils-first) belong to other streams of the movement, not to the economist of the drain. +. (Note: Dadabhai Naoroji was the first Indian to show that India was being drained of its wealth under the British rule and thus was fast succumbing to poverty. In 1876, he published a paper titled 'Poverty of India'.)

2015PYQ 9(Prelims 2015)

Who of the following was/were economic critic/critics of colonialism in India? 1. Dadabhai Naoroji 2. G Subramaniam Iyer 3. RC Dutt Select the correct answer using the codes given below:

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — All three were economic critics of colonialism: names R.C. Dutt (Economic History of India) and Dadabhai Naoroji among the Drain Theory's architects, and G. Subramania Iyer — the nationalist journalist who founded The Hindu — pressed the same critique in the press.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — "Only 1" drops two genuine critics: R.C. Dutt gave the theory "its historical depth and empirical backbone", and Subramania Iyer carried the critique into nationalist journalism.

(B) ✗ — "1 and 2" wrongly excludes R.C. Dutt — the record names him among the theory's architects.

(C) ✗ — "2 and 3" wrongly excludes Naoroji — the theory's principal author, whose Poverty and Un-British Rule in India (1901) was its most systematic presentation. (Note: The National Movement was built around a very sophisticated ideological discourse, most of which was developed by the first generation of nationalists, simultaneously called Moderates.)

2018PYQ 10(Prelims 2018)

Economic result of British rule in 19th century:

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Indian agriculture was reoriented away from food crops and towards cash crops and raw materials meant for export to British factories — the commercialisation of agriculture, the record named economic result of the 19th century.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Reversed: handicraft exports COLLAPSED — artisans "lost both domestic and export markets" as India turned from net exporter of finished goods into a raw-material supplier.

(B) ✗ — The record is categorical: "no Indian factories came up to replace the handicrafts" — industrial growth was precisely what 19th-century British rule denied India.

(D) ✗ — Displaced artisans were forced BACK onto agriculture — rural disguised unemployment, not urban growth, was the century's drift.

2020PYQ 11(Prelims 2020)

Which of the following statements correctly explains the impact of Industrial Revolution on India during the first half of the nineteenth century?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — The Indian handicrafts sector was destroyed in this process. Indian artisans lost both domestic and export markets — the Industrial Revolution flooded India with machine-made British goods and ruined the handicrafts.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — No machine age came to India in that half-century: there was no industrialisation of India — no Indian factories came up to replace the handicrafts.

(C) ✗ — The transport revolution the record describes — steam railways, modern ports, the Suez Canal (1859–69) — belongs to the SECOND half of the century, and it deepened the drain rather than industrialising India.

(D) ✗ — Backwards: Britain kept India an OPEN market for its manufactures — finished goods "flooded back in as imports"; heavy duties on British imports are the opposite of the era's policy. (Note: (a) The decline of Indian handicraft correctly explains the impact of Industrial revolution on India during the first half of nineteenth century. It was direct consequence of Industrial revolution in Britain.)

FYQ 1

At the very first INC session in 1885 at Bombay, the Congress declared which of the following as its first goal?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — at the first INC session in 1885 in Bombay, the Congress declared its goal as "building national unity," because India in 1885 had no pre-existing sense of a single political community.

Why others wrong: (a) Swaraj as a goal came much later (1906 Calcutta), not in 1885; (c) Separate electorates were a colonial concession, not an INC goal — the INC's own 1889 Minority Clause demanded reserved seats, not separate electorates; (d) Social-reform questions like untouchability were consciously kept off the INC agenda under the 1886 Naoroji rule.

Memory hook: 1885 Bombay first call — not Swaraj, just "unity above all."

FYQ 2

The informal INC rule that the Congress would take up only political questions and not social or class questions was laid down in 1886 by:

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — The record states that in 1886, Dadabhai Naoroji laid down what became an informal INC rule — that the Congress would take up only political questions, not social or class questions, because social issues could divide Indians against each other.

Why others wrong: (a) Banerjea was a leading Moderate but is not credited with this 1886 rule; (c) Bonnerjee presided over the 1885 founding session but did not author the political-only rule; (d) Mehta was a Bombay Moderate but the 1886 rule is attributed specifically to Naoroji.

Memory hook: 1886 Naoroji's filter — Indians vs British only, no in-house quarrels.

FYQ 3

Consider the following statements regarding the early INC's effort at building Hindu–Muslim unity: 1. The 1888 INC decision held that no resolution would be passed if the majority of Hindu or Muslim members disagreed. 2. The 1889 Minority Clause demanded that the British government reserve seats in the Imperial and Provincial Legislative Councils for religious minorities in proportion to their population. 3. The demand for minority representation in legislative councils was first formally raised by the Muslim League. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — Statements 1 and 2 are taken verbatim from the record. Statement 3 is wrong because the 1889 Minority Clause was raised by the INC itself, not by the Muslim League.

Why others wrong: (b) Statement 3 is wrong — it was the INC, not the Muslim League, that first formally demanded minority representation; (c) Same reason — Statement 3 is wrong;

(d) Statement 3 inverts the record actual point.

Memory hook: 1888 veto + 1889 reservation — Moderates, not Muslim League, opened the minority-seat door.

FYQ 4

Why did the INC make it a structural rule that the President of each annual session would not be from the host region?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — The record explains the logic in plain words: a Bengali leader presiding in Calcutta builds nothing new, but a Marathi leader presiding in Calcutta — speaking to Bengali concerns — builds an all-India leadership and creates inter-regional bonds that would not exist otherwise.

Why others wrong: (a) No such British requirement is on record; (b) Logistical convenience is not the reason given — the rule is explicitly about nation-building, not workload; (d) The purpose was internal nation-building, not appearance management for British observers.

Memory hook: Marathi in Calcutta, Bengali in Bombay — each session stitched another inter-regional bond.

FYQ 6

The Drain Theory gained particular prominence after the bitter disappointment with which of the following measures?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — The record states that the Drain Theory gained particular prominence after the bitter disappointment with the Indian Councils Act of 1892 (ICA1892), which failed to deliver any meaningful reform.

Why others wrong: (a) ICA 1861 pre-dates the INC itself and is not linked to the Drain Theory's rise; (c) GOI 1858 only transferred power from EIC to Crown — it was not the proximate trigger; (d) ICA 1909 came much later and is associated with separate electorates, not the rise of Drain Theory.

Memory hook: 1892 ICA flopped — Drain Theory took the stage.

FYQ 7

Which of the following best captures the core argument of the Drain Theory in one sentence, as articulated in the Moderates' framework?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — This is the record one-line statement of Drain Theory: The misuse of political power over the State of the Colony for the economic benefit of the Colonial Power.

Why others wrong: (a) Free trade is one mechanism (Absence of Protective Tariffs) — not the whole core argument; (b) Monetisation versus barter is not the record framing of the drain;

(d) the record speaks of surplus extraction, not banking-channel routing as the central definition.

Memory hook: Political control to economic plunder — one sentence, the whole theory.

FYQ 8

Consider the following statements about the impact of British industrial capitalism on India: 1. India shifted from being a net exporter of finished goods to a net exporter of raw materials. 2. Indian agriculture was reoriented from food crops towards cash crops and raw materials for British factories. 3. Indian artisans who lost their trade were absorbed quickly into newly built Indian factories. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — Statements 1 and 2 reflect the record account of the commercialisation of agriculture and the inversion of India's trade structure. Statement 3 is the opposite of the truth — the record says explicitly that there was no industrialisation of India, no Indian factories came up to replace the handicrafts, and artisans were forced back onto agriculture.

Why others wrong: (b) Statement 3 inverts the record actual account of artisan displacement;

(c) Same reason — Statement 3 is wrong; (d) Statement 3 is the opposite of what the record records.

Memory hook: Finished-goods exporter to raw-material exporter — and the artisan fell back to the field, not forward to the factory.

FYQ 9

Which of the following infrastructural change, in the second half of the 19th century, cut the sea distance between India and Britain by about 4,500 miles and intensified the drain by making raw-material extraction and finished-goods import cheaper and faster?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — The construction of the Suez Canal between 1859 and 1869 cut the sea distance between India and Britain by 4,500 miles, making it cheaper and faster to extract raw materials from India and ship finished goods back.

Why others wrong: (a) Steam locomotives helped extraction internally but the 4,500-mile sea-distance cut specifically belongs to the Suez Canal; (b) Port modernisation is part of the broader transport revolution but not the specific 4,500-mile fact; (d) The telegraph is not cited for the 4,500-mile distance figure.

Memory hook: Suez 1859 to 1869 — 4,500 miles shaved off, drain accelerated.

FYQ 15

(B)
