The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: None of these three was ruled by the Mughals in the middle path, and each built State capacity in its own way: the Rajputs by bargaining, Travancore by trade, Mysore by a war machine; each ended up under EIC pressure.

  1. Outside the middle path. These are the third class of regional States from Chapter 3: Already Autonomous States, where even when the Mughal was powerful he did not rule. The Rajputs were brought into the Mughal system but kept their homes, Travancore was never part of the Mughal Empire, and Mysore grew out of the Vijayanagar world.
    → Start in the north, with Akbar's deal.
  2. Akbar's Rajput bargain. Rajputana had no single centre, only roughly 20 major clans (a multi-polar world). Defeating them all would be expensive and uncertain, so Akbar 56-05 co-opted the powerful chiefs as Mansabdars with Vatan Jagirs, and in return they accepted the Emperor, paid tribute and sent troops. They formed a Hindu bloc of about 15% of the Mansabdars, and Akbar helped them subdue smaller chiefs: a partial consolidation.
    → That held for most of the 17th century, until Aurangzeb.
  3. 1680. Mewar under Raj Singh began expanding aggressively, and Aurangzeb 58-07 grew uneasy at one Rajput power dominating Rajputana. In 1680 a child came to the throne of Marwar, which lay astride the trade route from Delhi and Agra to Ahmedabad, and Aurangzeb interfered. Marwar and Mewar revolted, but the other chiefs refused to join for fear of Mewar, and the revolt collapsed.
    → After 1707 the Rajputs went their own way.
  4. Own terms, then new masters. After Aurangzeb's death in 1707 the Rajputs increasingly acted on their own terms, most prominently Sawai Jai Singh 99-43 of Amber. From 1751 the Marathas began regular raids for tribute but never fully conquered the region. After the Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803-05) the EIC replaced them and signed Subordinate Alliances: the ruler keeps his throne but surrenders foreign policy and defence.
    → One Rajput of that age deserves a close look.
  5. Four hats. Sawai means 1.25 times more able than any contemporary ruler, a title from Aurangzeb 58-07. Jai Singh was a Mughal Mansabdar, state-builder, astronomer and town planner, Subedar of Malwa (1729-37) and Agra. His Hurda conference of 1734 collapsed, and the Treaty of Bhopal (1738) gave Malwa to the Marathas. He founded Jaipur in 1727 (Vidyadhar Bhattacharya, Vastu Shastra, grid, the first planned city of modern India), and was the last Indian ruler to perform the Ashvamedha and Vajapeya Yajnas.
    → His other passion was the sky.
  6. Stone and sky. Five Jantar Mantars stand at Delhi (1724), Jaipur (1734), Ujjain, Mathura and Varanasi; Jaipur's is the largest, a UNESCO site (2010), and its Samrat Yantra measures time to 2 seconds. The old tables (Zij-i-Ulugh Begi, Siddhantic) had accumulated errors, so fresh observations gave the Zij-i-Muhammad Shahi (1733), dedicated to Muhammad Shah 19-48, and a delegation went to Portugal (1730). The Samrat, Jai Prakash and Ram Yantras were stone, not brass, for accuracy.
    → Now go south, where the Mughals never reached.
  7. Travancore builds on trade. Marthanda Varma 29-58 subjugated local chiefs, built a centralised State and a European-style military, and held a State monopoly over the trade of the Malabar coast. The revenues went into the military, irrigation works and transport. In 1741 his army crushed the Dutch East India Company at Colachel, leaving them as mere merchants in Cochin; Bedara (1759) and the 1795 expulsion finished the Dutch.
    → But his successor chose art over arms.
  8. Malabar slips, the Company arrives. Under Rama Varma 58-98 Travancore became a centre of art and scholarship but its military declined. In 1766 Haider Ali 61-82 annexed Malabar and Calicut, the heart of the spice trade. After 1798 British influence grew; in 1805 the Nair troops mutinied over unpaid salaries and Travancore signed a Subsidiary Alliance. In 1809 Velu Thampi Dalawa issued the Kundara Proclamation, and when it was suppressed he took his own life.
    → The man who took Malabar came from Mysore.
  9. From Vijayanagar to Haider Ali. Mysore was part of the Vijayanagar Empire (1336-1646), which Deccan Sultanates shattered at Talikota in 1565. The Wodeyars then made Mysore autonomous, and Chikka Devaraja 72-04 Wodeyar made it militarily powerful. By 1761 a corrupt Prime Minister, NanjRaj, had made the Wodeyars figureheads, and Haider Ali 61-82, a junior officer, overthrew him and kept the Wodeyar as nominal head.
    → He needed an army and the money to pay it.
  10. The machine and the money. With French help Haider Ali 61-82 built Risalas on a European model and spent roughly twenty years subjugating the Poligars and Deshmukhs. To pay for it Mysore practised Military Fiscalism: Khalisa land expanded, Jagirdari minimised, Deshmukhs removed so State officials collected directly, field surveys, canals, cash crops and sericulture.
    → His son took the machine abroad.
  11. Tipu looks abroad. Tipu 82-99 ran a government-owned trading company with 30 centres including Muscat, and used indigenous iron-cased rockets (range 2 km) that inspired Congreve's. In 1785 he declared independence and asked the Khalifa for a sanad; he set up embassies in France, Turkey and Egypt (Prelims 2001). He also planted a Tree of Liberty and joined the Jacobin Club, but the French alliance gave no military support.
    → Ambition then met a bill it could not pay.
  12. Why Mysore lost. Mysore wanted to dominate all of South India and its trade (Vijayanagar 2.0), but constant war drained the State faster than Military Fiscalism could refill it. The 1766 annexation of Malabar and Calicut triggered the First Anglo-Mysore War (1767-69), and four wars (1767-69, 1780-84, 1790-92, 1799) ended with Tipu 82-99's death at Seringapatam.

In simple words#

  • These are Already Autonomous States: even when the Mughals were strong they did not rule here (the Rajputs, Travancore, Mysore).
  • Akbar 56-05 co-opted powerful Rajput chiefs as Mansabdars with Vatan Jagirs
  • The 1680 revolt failed, and Sawai Jai Singh 99-43 built Jaipur (1727) and the Jantar Mantars
  • Travancore under Marthanda Varma 29-58 used a trade monopoly to build a centralised State and crushed the Dutch at Colachel (1741)
  • It signed a Subsidiary Alliance in 1805 and rose in 1809 (Velu Thampi, Kundara)
  • Mysore under Haider Ali 61-82 took power in 1761, built French-assisted Risalas and Military Fiscalism, and Tipu 82-99 added a trading company, iron-cased rockets and embassies.
  • Mysore overreached (Vijayanagar 2.0), constant war drained it, and Tipu 82-99 died at Seringapatam in 1799.

4 · Travancore: Marthanda Varma 29-58#

Story link

Because the Mughals never reached the far south, a small State could build itself on trade. Ask: where did the trade money go?

  • Travancore was never part of the Mughal Empire.
  • The Mughal State simply never expanded far enough south to include it.
  • By the 1740s, Marthanda Varma 29-58 had built a strong, centralised State by subjugating local chiefs across the region, and a European-style military: modern weapons, trained soldiers.
  • Takeaway: Travancore established a State monopoly over the trade of the Malabar coast.
  • The revenues were reinvested into the military, irrigation works and transport infrastructure: a classic example of a small State using trade income to build State capacity.

5 · Travancore against Mysore and the EIC#

Story link

Because the military declined after Marthanda Varma, the neighbours and then the Company moved in. Ask: which year did Malabar go?

  • Under Rama Varma 58-98 Travancore became a centre of art and scholarship, but military strength declined.
  • The consequences were quick: in 1766 Haider Ali 61-82 of Mysore annexed Malabar and Calicut.
  • (Malabar was the heart of the spice trade: pepper, cardamom, ginger.
  • Whoever controlled Malabar's ports controlled a significant share of Indian Ocean commerce, which is why both Mysore and the British were so interested.)
  • After 1798 Travancore came steadily under British influence.

6 · Mysore: from Vijayanagar to Haider Ali 61-82#

Story link

Because Vijayanagar broke at Talikota, Mysore became autonomous, and a strongman took it over. Ask: how did Haider Ali rise?

  • Mysore was part of the Vijayanagar Empire (1336-1646).
  • The Empire's effective power was shattered at the Battle of Talikota (also called Rakkasa-Tangadi) in 1565, when a coalition of Deccan Sultanates decisively defeated Vijayanagar.
  • The dynasty lingered until 1646, but 1565 marks the operational end of Vijayanagar as a major power.
  • After Vijayanagar's decline Mysore gradually became autonomous under the Wodeyar dynasty.
  • Under Chikka Devaraja 72-04 Wodeyar (1672-1704) it was militarily powerful and centrally governed.
  • By 1761 the Wodeyars had been reduced to figureheads by a powerful and corrupt Prime Minister, NanjRaj.
  • Haider Ali 61-82 ended that. He began as a junior military officer, his early career made possible partly because the Wodeyars had helped pay off his family's debts.
  • Capable, ambitious and strategic, in 1761 he overthrew NanjRaj and seized effective power, keeping the Wodeyar as a nominal head of State.
Handout alert
  • Pattern at the 18th century: the king becomes a puppet as personality weakens.
  • Recall the Peshwa and Chhatrapati (Chapter 4).

6.1 Haider Ali 61-82's military machine

  • Haider Ali 61-82 took French assistance for military training, technology transfer and organisational reform.
  • The army was now on a European model, organised into Risalas (regiments): Each Risala's commander was directly appointed by the King, ensuring personal loyalty upward. Each unit had a clear chain of command. Each was self-sufficient: its own weapons, transport and supplies (recall the Campoo system of Scindia, Chapter 4). Result: a fast, efficient military with regiments capable of independent action.
  • He then spent roughly twenty years subjugating the Poligars (local chiefs and landlords who controlled land, the wealth of temples and had their own armed men, the real rulers of the countryside since the Vijayanagar era) and the Deshmukhs (revenue collectors who functioned like primary zamindars). Non-State power was systematically dismantled.

  • (The handout's analogy: replacing a commission-based agent with a salaried employee; the State kept far more of the revenue.)
  • Khalisa land = land whose revenue went directly to the State treasury.
  • Jagirdari land = land whose revenue was assigned to nobles or commanders.

7 · Tipu 82-99 Sultan (1782-99): innovations and why he still lost#

Story link

Because his father built the machine, Tipu took it abroad and also overreached. Ask: which innovation answers Prelims 2001?

  • Modern navy and trading company: a government-owned trading company modelled on the EIC, trading in sandalwood, sulphur, rice, pepper, silk and cardamom, with 30 trading centres inside and outside India, including Muscat, Oman.
  • (Before the age of machines a "factory" meant a warehouse or trading depot, not a production facility; a trading settlement meant warehouses, homes, offices and sometimes a fort. Details in Chapter 8.)
  • Iron-cased rocket artillery:
    • Developed by Haider Ali 61-82 and Tipu 82-99, 100% indigenous, range 2 km
    • the iron cylinder casings did not burst and could take high pressure compared with cardboard-cased rockets (fireworks), which existed for centuries in India and China
  • The British studied them closely: William Congreve's rockets, deployed in the early 19th century, drew directly from Tipu 82-99's designs.
  • 1785: Tipu 82-99 formally declared independence from both the Wodeyars and the Mughal Emperor, and requested a sanad from the Khalifa recognising him as independent of Mughal authority.
  • Modern embassies in France, Turkey and Egypt, alongside new coinage and the Mauludi Lunisolar Calendar.

7.1 Tipu 82-99 and Revolutionary France

  • In the 1790s Tipu 82-99 sent an ambassador to France seeking military technology and a formal alliance against the British.
  • Since 1789 the French Revolution had set France on a collision course with the monarchies of Europe, including Britain (the 23-year wars, 1792-1815).
  • In 1798 Napoleon attacked Egypt and the Middle East, partly to disrupt British trade in the Arabian Sea.
  • Tipu 82-99 exchanged communications with Napoleon and planted a Tree of Liberty in Mysore (a symbol of the French Revolution), and joined the Jacobin Club.
  • The alliance did not materialise into military support.
  • The British were alarmed, and it became a reason for the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War of 1799.
Handout alert
  • Prelims 2001: who established embassies in foreign countries on modern lines? (d) Tipu 82-99 Sultan.
  • Embassies in France, Turkey and Egypt, plus new coinage and the Mauludi Lunisolar Calendar.
  • Jacobin membership and the Tree of Liberty made him an ideological adversary to the British, not merely a military one.

7.2 Why Mysore lost

  • Mysore's long-term ambition was to dominate all of South India and control its international trade (Vijayanagar 2.0).
  • But constant warfare against neighbouring Indian powers and the EIC drained the State's resources faster than Military Fiscalism could replenish them.
  • The ambition was real
  • The capacity, ultimately, was not enough
  • 1766: Haider Ali 61-82 annexed Malabar and Calicut, triggering the First Anglo-Mysore War (1767-69), as the EIC feared losing coastal trade access through Malabar.
  • Four Anglo-Mysore Wars: 1767-69, 1780-84, 1790-92, 1799.

Mysore's rise and fall

  • 1761 Haider Ali 61-82 overthrows NanjRaj
  • The Wodeyar stays nominal
  • French-assisted Risalas: self-sufficient regiments under commanders loyal to the King
  • Military Fiscalism: Khalisa expanded, Deshmukhs removed, surveys, canals, sericulture: wealth for war
  • Tipu 82-99: navy, government trading company, iron-cased rockets, 1785 independence, modern embassies, French link
  • Overreach:
    • Ambition to dominate all South India (Vijayanagar 2.0)
    • Constant war drains the State faster than fiscalism refills it
  • 1799:
    • Fourth Anglo-Mysore War, Tipu 82-99 dies at Seringapatam
    • Mysore's power broken

Recall sheet#

Read the question, answer in your head, then tap to check.

Why is this chapter about "already autonomous" States?Even when the Mughals were powerful they did not rule here.
Akbar 56-05's approach to the Rajputs?Co-opt the powerful chiefs as Mansabdars with Vatan Jagirs; help them subdue smaller chiefs.
Why did Aurangzeb 58-07 interfere in Marwar (1680)?Uneasy about Mewar's expansion; a child came to the throne of a kingdom on the Delhi-Agra-Ahmedabad route.
What is a Subordinate Alliance?Like a protectorate: the ruler keeps his throne but gives up foreign policy and defence; EIC troops are stationed and paid for by the ruler.
What does "Sawai" mean?1.25 times more able than any contemporary ruler; conferred by Aurangzeb 58-07.
What was the Hurda conference?1734, Rajput rulers' united front against the Marathas; collapsed from rivalries.
Who designed Jaipur?Vidyadhar Bhattacharya, on Vastu Shastra principles with a grid pattern; founded 1727.
Why is Jaipur pink?Sawai Ram Singh painted it in 1876 to welcome the Prince of Wales.
Where are the five Jantar Mantars?Delhi (1724), Jaipur (1734), Ujjain, Mathura, Varanasi.
What do the three yantras do?Samrat: time (sundial); Jai Prakash: celestial mapper; Ram: altitude of the Sun.
Why stone over brass?Greater accuracy.
Why was Travancore never Mughal?The Mughals never expanded far enough south.
Marthanda Varma 29-58's State?Centralised by subjugating local chiefs; European-style army; trade monopoly funding military, irrigation and roads.
Colachel 1741?Marthanda Varma 29-58's army crushed the Dutch East India Company; Dutch became mere merchants in Cochin.
Bedara 1759?The EIC defeated the Dutch in Bengal (Battle of Chinsura).
What happened to Travancore under Rama Varma 58-98?Art and scholarship flourished, military declined; Malabar and Calicut lost in 1766.
Why did Malabar matter?Spice trade (pepper, cardamom, ginger); control of ports meant a share of Indian Ocean commerce.
Dalawa versus Dewan?Dalawa: PM and Chief of Armed Forces; Dewan (under British): head of all administration but not the military.
What was the Kundara Proclamation?1809 call by Velu Thampi to rise against British control.
Why did Mysore matter after Vijayanagar?Part of it; Wodeyars gained autonomy after Talikota (1565); Chikka Devaraja 72-04 made it powerful.
How did Haider Ali 61-82 rise?Junior officer; overthrew PM NanjRaj in 1761, keeping the Wodeyar nominal.
Risala versus Campoo?Both self-sufficient regiments on the European model; Risala (Haider Ali 61-82), Campoo (Scindia).
Who were Poligars?Local chiefs and landlords with land, temple wealth and armed men; subdued by Haider Ali 61-82 over about 20 years.
What changed for Deshmukhs in Mysore?Removed as intermediaries; State officials collected taxes directly on salary.
What were Tipu 82-99's rockets?Indigenous iron-cased rockets, range 2 km; inspired Congreve's rockets.
Tipu 82-99's 1785 step?Declared independence from the Wodeyars and Mughals; asked the Khalifa for a sanad.
Tipu 82-99's French link?Ambassador to France, Tree of Liberty, Jacobin Club; no military support; alarmed the British.
Why did Mysore lose?Over-ambition (Vijayanagar 2.0) and constant war draining resources faster than fiscalism refilled them.
Anglo-Mysore wars?1767-69, 1780-84, 1790-92, 1799.
