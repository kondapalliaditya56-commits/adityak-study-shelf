1998PYQ 1(Prelims 1998)

Which one of the following defines extremist ideology during the early phase of Indian freedom movement?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — The Extremists' break with Moderate method is the record opening theme: where the Moderates' method was "petition, prayer, and press," the Extremists sought Swaraj through aggressive agitation — boycott, Swadeshi, passive resistance. +.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Preferring indigenous goods defines SWADESHI — one method the movement used, not the defining ideology of Extremism, which was about HOW self-government should be won.

(C) ✗ — National education was likewise one of the four Calcutta-1906 methods, not the ideology itself.

(D) ✗ — Military coups belong to the REVOLUTIONARY stream, not to the Extremists, whose framework was passive resistance — non-cooperation, boycott, civil disobedience — with violence only as an implied last resort. (Note: The extremist groups of the early phase of Indian freedom movement had believed in obtaining self government by aggressive means in place of petitions and constitutional ways.)

1998PYQ 2(Prelims 1998)

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — A is false: the Partition did not end the Moderates' role — they themselves passed the Boycott Resolution at Calcutta Town Hall (7 August 1905) and stayed in the movement; the INC stayed divided only from Surat 1907 to the 1916 reunion. R is true: the Surat Session (1907) did separate the Extremists from the Moderates. +.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Requires A to be true — but the Moderates led the movement's first phase themselves (Boycott Resolution, Calcutta Town Hall, 7 August 1905); their role did not end in

1905.

(B) ✗ — Same defect: any both-true reading fails because A is false — Moderate politics continued after 1905, inside and beyond the Swadeshi phase.

(C) ✗ — Exactly inverted: it is R that is true (Surat separated the factions) and A that is false. (Note: Moderates continued to participate in the struggle for freedom even after 1905, Bengal Partition. Later in 1916 both the groups re-united in 1916 at Lahore session.)
