The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: After the Extremists fell by 1908, a small committed group chose armed struggle: repeat 1857 in the long run, heroic action now. Their secret societies and centres abroad fed a string of attacks (1907-12), the British answered with the CID and the Cellular Jail, and the phase ended repressed but not futile: it gave pride, helped bring 1911, and its Rash Behari Bose line runs to the INA.

  1. Pen, People, Guns. By 1908 the Swadeshi Movement was leaderless: Tilak was deported to Mandalay, Aurobindo retired, B.C. Pal retired temporarily and L.L. Rai went abroad. The politically radicalised asked what next if mass politics had failed. The teacher's chain says it all: when the Pen failed, People came out; when People failed, Guns came out; when Guns failed, MG came out (1920+).
    → So the Revolutionaries gave two answers at once.
  2. Two answers: a long game and a quick strike. The long-term goal was to repeat 1857: a civil rebellion and disaffection within the army. The immediate method was individual heroic action: assassinate hated officials to strike fear in British hearts, remove fear from Indians and arouse nationalism. If caught, the trial became a stage and a death sentence a supreme sacrifice.
    → Heroic action needed structure, so they built societies.
  3. Bengal builds the Anushilan. In 1902 Pramathanath Mitra founded the Anushilan Samiti in Calcutta for physical training and character building, with armed resistance as its inner purpose. By 1906 an impatient inner circle formed around the newspaper Jugantar of Barindra Kumar Ghosh, Bhupendranath Datta and Abinash Bhattacharya. The British replied in 1903 with the CID, after the Fraser Police Commission of 1902.
    → But Bengal was only one stream, and the two streams soon diverged.
  4. Jolt now, drill for later. Jugantar was a loose, decentralised confederation that believed in propaganda of the deed and assassination to show the British were not invincible. The Dacca Anushilan Samiti of Pulin Behari Das (1906) was rigidly hierarchical and built for long-term military preparation, the teacher's 'Repeat 1857'. In the west the Savarkar brothers turned Mitra Mela (1899, Nasik) into Abhinav Bharat (1904, Pune), copying Mazzini's Young Italy.
    → Now the Revolutionaries looked beyond India's borders.
  5. Why go abroad? VOICE. Abroad they could speak freely in newspapers, procure weapons despite the Arms Act 1878, assassinate officers at Home in England, win diplomatic pressure by highlighting colonialism and raise awareness among NRIs. Savarkar sat in London, Hardayal reached the USA, Shyamji Krishna Varma ran the IHRS and India House (1905) before moving to Paris in 1907, and Madame Cama unfurled the first Indian flag at Stuttgart in August 1907.
    → With arms and money needed, the actions began.
  6. 1908: Barrah, Muzzafarpur and the bomb that missed. The Dacca Anushilan looted the Zamindar of Barrah to fund the revolution, since dacoity targeted collaborators. At Muzzafarpur Prafulla Chaki and Khudiram Bose bombed Magistrate Kingsford's carriage, but he was not inside and two English women died. Chaki killed himself and Khudiram, 18, was executed and became an instant martyr, the opposite of what the British intended.
    → The bomb alarmed the British into a wider crackdown.
  7. Alipore and the Andamans. The Alipore Case brought mass arrests, including Aurobindo from Murari Pukur Road. C.R. Das defended him and B.C. Pal refused to testify, so he could not be convicted; he fled to Pondicherry and spiritualism. Barindra Kumar Ghosh and Ullaskar Dutta were sentenced to death, commuted to transportation for life in the Cellular Jail, which broke the early Jugantar.
    → Even a newspaper could now be sedition.
  8. Tilak, Kesari and Jinnah. In Kesari, Tilak condemned the Muzzafarpur violence but defended Chaki and Bose on the ground that sustained oppression breeds violence, in an article titled 'Arrival of Bomb'. He was charged with sedition, sentenced to six years and sent to Mandalay, Burma, with Jinnah, a young constitutional lawyer, conducting his defence.
    → Meanwhile Abhinav Bharat struck at home and abroad.
  9. 1909: Dhingra, Nasik and Savarkar's sentence. In London in 1909 Madan Lal Dhingra killed Lt. Col. William Curzon-Wyllie, who was not Curzon 99-05 but worked with the Secretary of State for India. In Nasik, Anant Laxman Kanhere killed District Magistrate Jackson with a pistol that Savarkar had sent from London. In 1910 Savarkar was sentenced to transportation for life in the Cellular Jail.
    → Repressed, the phase still left its mark.
  10. Pride, reform and Delhi. The Revolutionaries gave back pride and dignity, and many felt the Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909 were a reform offered to buy peace. In 1911 the Partition was annulled and Delhi became the capital at the Durbar of December 1911, reducing Bengal's outsized influence; in 1912 Bihar and Orissa and a full province of Assam followed. Curzon 99-05 lost the map but won the plan.
    → One more bomb fell in the new capital, and its thrower's story did not end.
  11. Rash Behari Bose to the INA. In 1912 Rash Behari Bose threw a bomb from a balcony at Viceroy Hardinge 10-16 on an elephant in Chandni Chowk; Hardinge was wounded but survived. He led the Ghadar Movement (1913-15), escaped to Japan in 1915 and in 1942 convened the Tokyo and Bangkok conferences forming the IIL. Under it Captain Mohan Singh organised the first INA; after Mohan Singh's arrest Rash Behari Bose took over and, for old age, handed it to Netaji in 1943.

In simple words#

  • With the Extremists arrested or exiled by 1908, a small committed group chose armed struggle: the long-term goal was to repeat 1857, the immediate method was individual heroic action.
  • They built secret societies at home (Anushilan 1902, Jugantar 1906, Dacca Anushilan 1906, Abhinav Bharat 1904) and centres abroad (IHRS 1905, India House, Paris, USA) where speech and arms were freer.
  • Jugantar was loose and wanted propaganda of the deed now
  • The Dacca Anushilan was disciplined and built for long-term preparation
  • The actions ran Barrah, Muzzafarpur, Alipore, Tilak (1908), then Dhingra and Nasik (1909) and Delhi (1912)
  • The British answered with the CID (1903), trials and the Cellular Jail
  • The phase was repressed but gave pride and dignity, helped bring Morley-Minto 1909 and the 1911 Delhi move, and Rash Behari Bose carried the line from Delhi 1912 to the INA 1943.

2.2 The British intelligence response: CID (1903)

Teacher's board

  • The British were not passive observers. Recognising the need for a specialised intelligence apparatus beyond the foundational Indian Police Act of 1861, the colonial government formally established the Criminal Investigation Department (CID) in 1903, following the recommendations of the Fraser Police Commission of 1902.
  • What it was: a professionalised network of spies and informers, embedded within Indian society, designed to track, infiltrate and dismantle political dissent and secret societies.

Teacher's board
  • "Narrative" is boxed and marked "imp" beside Savarkar's book.
  • Savarkar's The Indian War of Independence 1857 (1909) recast the 1857 Revolt as a planned war of national liberation, not a mere "mutiny" (it was banned before publication, yet copies circulated underground and became essential revolutionary reading).
  • The teacher's point: a Revolutionary movement also needs a narrative, a story of why it fights.
  • He repeats this idea for the IHRS ("Building Narrative in favor of Home Rule") and The Indian Sociologist ("Narrative for ideology of Nationalism"), Section 2.5.
  • Abhinav Bharat (1904): the Savarkar brothers, Vinayak (Veer Savarkar) and Ganesh, set up a secret society called Mitra Mela in 1899 in Nasik.
  • It was renamed Abhinav Bharat, or Young India, in 1904 in Pune.
  • When Veer Savarkar went to London to study law in 1906, he opened a branch of Abhinav Bharat there and compiled Mazzini Charitra (1906), a translation of Mazzini's writings.
  • Savarkar also wrote The Indian War of Independence 1857 (1909), which recast 1857 as a planned war of national liberation rather than a mere "mutiny." The British banned it before publication, but copies circulated underground.
Key takeaway
  • Why "Young India"? Mazzini's "Young Italy." Mazzini's Young Italy was active in the revolts of 1830 in Italy to overthrow Austrian colonialism in northern Italy and unify the Italian kingdoms
  • Italian unification finally succeeded in 1870. Mazzini's method was guerrilla warfare.
  • Savarkar saw a direct parallel between Austrian colonialism over Italy and British colonialism over India.

  • But the larger political design of 1905 (divide Hindus and Muslims, undercut Bengal's dominance, shift capital) was substantially achieved: Curzon was successful on the strategic questions.

Recall sheet#

Read the question, answer in your head, then tap to check.

What were the two answers of the Revolutionaries?Long term: repeat 1857 (civil rebellion plus disaffection in the army). Immediate: individual heroic action (assassinate hated officials) to strike fear into the British, remove fear from Indians and arouse nationalism.
How did the Revolutionaries use the courtroom?A public trial was a platform for propaganda; a death sentence was a supreme sacrifice that could inspire others.
The teacher's chain of failed approaches?When Pen failed, People came out; when People failed, Guns came out; when Guns failed, MG (Mahatma Gandhi) came out [1920+].
Mass politics versus armed struggle on the teacher's board?Mass politics = Extremists, method = people (NC, CD, violence only if repression: stones and burning, not AK-47). Armed struggle = Revolutionaries, method = a committed small group (AK-47).
Who founded the Anushilan Samiti, and when?Pramathanath Mitra (P. Mitra), in Calcutta in 1902; Satish Chandra Basu ran the Calcutta body. Outer form: physical training; inner purpose: armed resistance.
Who founded the Jugantar and what is it?Barindra Kumar Ghosh (Aurobindo's younger brother), Bhupendranath Datta and Abinash Bhattacharya, around the Jugantar newspaper (1906), 'New Era'; the network became the Jugantar party.
Jugantar versus Dhaka Anushilan?Jugantar: loose, decentralised, propaganda of the deed, assassination, NOW. Dhaka (Pulin Behari Das, 1906): rigidly hierarchical, long-term military preparation, slow mass action, LATER (the teacher: Repeat 1857).
What was the CID (1903)?The Criminal Investigation Department: a professionalised network of spies and informers, set up after the Fraser Police Commission of 1902, beyond the Indian Police Act of 1861, to track, infiltrate and dismantle dissent.
Abhinav Bharat: dates and founders?Savarkar brothers Vinayak and Ganesh: Mitra Mela 1899 (Nasik), renamed Abhinav Bharat or Young India in 1904 (Pune); London branch 1906. Inspiration: Mazzini's Young Italy.
What did Savarkar write?Mazzini Charitra (1906, a translation of Mazzini's writings, compiled in London) and The Indian War of Independence 1857 (1909), which recast 1857 as a planned war of national liberation; banned before publication; the teacher: "Narrative: imp".
The teacher's five reasons for centres abroad?VOICE: diplomatic pressure (highlighting colonialism), free speech and newspapers (Voice), procure weapons (Arms Act 1878), assassinate officers at Home in England, NRIs' awareness (letters to relatives); weapons and money from enemies of Britain.
Who set up centres abroad, and where?Savarkar (London), Hardayal (USA), Shyamji Krishna Varma (London, Paris from 1907), Madame Cama (Paris), Ajit Singh. Mnemonic: Some Heroes Stayed Clear Abroad.
What did Madame Cama do in 1907?In August 1907 she unfurled the first version of the Indian national flag at the International Socialist Congress in Stuttgart, Germany.
IHRS 1905: who and what?Founded in London by Shyamji Krishna Varma with Cama, Naoroji (not a revolutionary) and S.R. Rana; self-rule and propaganda in Britain; all practicable means; only Indians; written constitution; India House hostel; The Indian Sociologist.
IHRS versus Home Rule League?IHRS = 1905, London, Shyamji Krishna Varma; Indian Home Rule League = 1916, Tilak (the teacher's warning).
Who took the lead at India House after 1907?The Abhinav Bharat Mandal (Savarkar's secret society), after Shyamji shifted to Paris.
The teacher's three routes to arms?Dacoity at an armoury (Chittagong 1930-33, Surya Sen), dacoity at the Government or loyalists, e.g. zamindars and moneylenders (Barrah 1908, Kakori 1925), alliance with enemies of Britain (WWI, Zimmerman Plan, Ghadar). Mnemonic: ARM.
Barrah Dacoity 1908?A Swadeshi dacoity in Dhaka, East Bengal by the Dacca Anushilan Samiti under Pulin Behari Das; the Zamindar of Barrah's house was looted to fund the revolution. Prelims 1995: East Bengal.
Muzzafarpur 1908?Target Magistrate Kingsford; Prafulla Chaki and Khudiram Bose bombed his carriage but he was not inside and two English women died; Chaki killed himself (revolver, or cyanide); Khudiram, 18, was executed and became a martyr.
Alipore Case 1908: names and outcome?Also Murari Pukur or Manick Tolla; Aurobindo arrested, defended by C.R. Das, B.C. Pal refused to testify, so no conviction; he fled to Pondicherry and spiritualism. Barindra and Ullaskar Dutta: death, commuted to the Cellular Jail.
Why can Barindra not be in the Ghadar Movement?He was transported to the Cellular Jail from 1908, cut off from all revolutionary activity on the mainland (Ghadar is 1913-15).
Why was "transportation for life" worse than death?Isolation, brutal prison conditions, permanent removal from the mainland; it eliminated leaders without making martyrs by public hanging. Savarkar got it in 1910.
Tilak's 1908 trial?Kesari (Marathi) article "Arrival of Bomb": condemned the violence but defended Chaki and Bose (oppression breeds violence); charged with sedition; six years, Mandalay; defended by Jinnah.
Jinnah in 1908?A young constitutional lawyer with nationalist sympathies, not yet Muslim League leader; he took Tilak's case on principle; same side then, 1940 Pakistan demand later.
1909 Abhinav Bharat actions?Dhingra killed Lt. Col. Curzon-Wyllie in London (an adviser to the Secretary of State, not Curzon 99-05); Kanhere killed District Magistrate Jackson at Nasik with a pistol Savarkar sent; Savarkar to the Cellular Jail in 1910.
Results of the Revolutionary phase?Pride and dignity (active agents, not passive subjects); Morley-Minto 1909 as a reform to buy peace; 1911 Partition annulled and Delhi the capital; Rash Behari Bose's line to the INA.
What happened in 1911 and 1912?1911: Partition annulled, Delhi the capital (Durbar, December, George V). 1912: Bihar and Orissa separated from Bengal, Assam a full province (Chief Commissioner Province 1874), Delhi Conspiracy.
Annulment versus capital shift?Annulment = the political concession; capital shift = the strategic correction that reduced Bengal's outsized influence.
Curzon 99-05's contradictory legacy?The Partition was reversed (1911) and the capital moved from Calcutta, so Swadeshi succeeded narrowly; but the larger design of 1905 (divide Hindus and Muslims, undercut Bengal, shift capital) was substantially achieved.
Delhi Conspiracy 1912?Rash Behari Bose threw a bomb from a balcony at Viceroy Hardinge 10-16 on an elephant in Chandni Chowk, during the capital-shift procession; Hardinge was wounded and survived.
Rash Behari Bose to the INA?Ghadar leadership (1913-15), Japan (1915), IIL at Tokyo and Bangkok (1942), under which Mohan Singh organised POWs into the first INA; after Mohan Singh's arrest he took over, then handed to Netaji (1943), who made it the Azad Hind Fauj.
Prelims 1999 journals?Varma = The Indian Sociologist; Cama = The Talwar; Besant = Commonwealth; Aurobindo = Bande Mataram. Only Varma's is in the handout.
Why did Tripura enter the freedom story (Prelims 2000)?The Bengal revolutionaries took shelter in Tripura.

Body: organisation, actions and escalation, repression (CID, Cellular Jail), results. Closing: repressed but not nothing; resurfaced in more organised forms (RB Bose to INA).
