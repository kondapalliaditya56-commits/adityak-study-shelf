The Preamble · exact text of the Constitution of India

“WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:

JUSTICE, social, economic and political;
LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;

and to promote among them all

FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;

IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.”

In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The Preamble is the short introduction to the Constitution. It begins 'We, the People of India' and ends with the adoption date, 26 November 1949.
  • It comes from Nehru's Objectives Resolution (moved 13 Dec 1946, adopted 22 Jan 1947).
  • It tells you two things. The nature of India: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic. The goals: Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity.
  • Courts changed their view in three steps: Berubari (1960) said not a part, Kesavananda (1973) said part, LIC (1995) said integral part.
  • It is non-justiciable: it gives no power and puts no limit on the legislature. It was amended only once, in 1976 (42nd Amendment), and the basic structure cannot be touched.

Key terms

Preamble
The introduction to the Constitution; the American Constitution was the first in the world to begin with one.
Objectives Resolution
Moved by Nehru on 13 Dec 1946 and adopted unanimously on 22 Jan 1947; a modified version became the Preamble.
Sovereign
Fully independent in internal and external matters; UN or Commonwealth membership does not limit it.
Democratic socialism
India's socialism: reduce inequality with a mixed economy; inspired by the Avadi Resolution (1955).
Sarva Dharma Sambhava
Equal respect for all religions; the Indian (positive) form of secularism.
Popular sovereignty
Power rests with the people, shown through universal adult suffrage and periodic elections.
Non-justiciable
Cannot be enforced in a court of law; the Preamble gives no rights and no duties.
Basic structure
Features of the Constitution that Parliament cannot amend away, even when it amends the Preamble.
Remember it as

Nature = S-S-S-D-R; goals = J-L-E-F; cases = Berubari No, Kesavananda Yes, LIC Yes; amended once (1976): Socialist, Secular, Integrity.

1 · The text and where it came from#

  • The Preamble opens with "WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA" and resolves to constitute India into a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic, securing to all citizens Justice, Liberty, Equality and promoting Fraternity "assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation". It ends: "In our Constituent Assembly this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION."
  • Prelims fact: The American Constitution was the first in the world to begin with a Preamble. "Preamble" comes from the Latin praeambulum, "to walk before".

The Objectives Resolution

  • The Preamble is based on the Objectives Resolution, drafted and moved by Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 December 1946. The Assembly adopted it unanimously on 22 January 1947, and a modified version became the Preamble.

What the Objectives Resolution declared (eight points)

  • 1 Resolve: Proclaim India an Independent Sovereign Republic and draw up a Constitution for her governance
  • 2 Union: British India, the Indian States and other willing territories form a Union of them all
  • 3 Autonomous units: Territories keep the status of autonomous units with residuary powers, except what is vested in or implied for the Union
  • 4 People's power: All power and authority of sovereign independent India and its organs derive from the people
  • 5 Guarantees:
    • Justice, social, economic and political
    • Equality of status and opportunity and before the law
    • Freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith, worship, vocation, association and action, subject to law and public morality
  • 6 Safeguards: Adequate safeguards for minorities, backward and tribal areas, depressed and other backward classes
  • 7 Integrity: The territory's integrity and its sovereign rights on land, sea and air, as per justice and the law of civilized nations
  • 8 World role: This ancient land attains its rightful and honoured place and contributes to world peace and the welfare of mankind

5 · Is the Preamble part of the Constitution?#

  • It does not confer powers or impose limits. Its role is interpretative: it shows the framers' intent when a provision is unclear. Courts moved in three steps.

Three cases, three positions

  • Berubari Union Case (1960):
    • Not a part of the Constitution. A guiding light for the framers' intent only
    • No independent legal force
    • Cannot be a source of power or rights
  • Kesavananda Bharati (1973): Is a part. The Constitution must be read in the light of its ideals. Also gave the basic structure doctrine: sovereignty, democracy, liberty, equality and justice guide what Parliament cannot amend away
  • LIC of India Case (1995):
    • Reaffirmed: an integral part
    • Creates no independent rights but guides interpretation and protects the spirit of the Constitution
  • Memory aid (Case order): Berubari = No → Kesavananda = Yes → LIC = Yes again. Hook: "Bad Kind Law" (bad first, kind later, law settled).
  • Constituent Assembly's view: The Preamble was adopted after the rest of the Constitution, to ensure full conformity. Dr Rajendra Prasad put the question "that the Preamble stands part of the Constitution" to a vote and it was adopted, showing the framers' intent.
  • Important takeaway: The Preamble is neither a source of power to the legislature nor a prohibition upon its powers. It is non-justiciable: its provisions are not enforceable in courts.
Status in law: three cases
BKL

Barred, Kept, Locked

  • KKesavananda Bharati (1973): is a part

Barred at first, then kept in, then locked in as an integral part. (Memory aid, not from the handout.)

Five limitations

  • Cannot override explicit provisions. A clear article prevails over the Preamble's broad ideals.
  • Not enforceable in court. Its objectives are aspirational
  • Fundamental Rights are enforceable
  • Cannot confer rights or duties. It is a statement of vision to be turned into reality by articles, laws and policies.
  • Ambiguous scope. Justice, liberty, equality and fraternity lack precise definitions, so courts must interpret them.
  • Depends on operative provisions. It is not self-executory: Fundamental Rights, DPSP and welfare policy deliver its ideals.
  • Memory aid (Limitations): Override (cannot), Enforce (cannot), Rights (cannot confer), Ambiguous, Depends on others. Hook: "Only Even Rich Aunts Donate."

6 · Can it be amended?#

How the amendment question was settled

  • 1960: Berubari: Preamble is not a part, so it cannot be amended
  • 1973: Kesavananda overrules that view: it is a part and can be amended under Article 368, but only if the basic structure is respected
  • 1976: Amended only once: 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act added Socialist, Secular and Integrity
  • Later judgments: The Supreme Court upheld the amendment: changes are permissible under Article 368 so long as the basic structure is not damaged
How the amendment question was settled
  • Remember: Opponents of amendability argued that even an amendable Preamble could not have its basic features destroyed. That safeguard is the basic structure.
  • Memory aid (Words added): Socialist, Secular, Integrity: "two S and an I, once, in 1976".

Also in the handout

  • This safeguard ensures that the Preamble retains its guiding role without being undermined by arbitrary changes.

7 · Recent issue: should "Socialist" and "Secular" go?#

The removal debate

  • For removal:
    • Emergency-era insertion in 1976, not in the 1950 Preamble, so politically motivated
    • Changed times: after liberalisation and 1991, rigid socialism may constrain economic reform
    • Ideological neutrality: the State should not be tied to a doctrine
  • Against removal:
    • They affirm India's identity as welfare, plural and inclusive
    • Basic structure: Minerva Mills (1980) and SR Bommai (1994) protect features like secularism
    • Secularism protects minorities and prevents majoritarian dominance
    • Preamble is the "identity card" (Palkhivala), removing words risks its coherence

Way forward (handout's five points)

  • Balance:
    • Retain inclusive principles while allowing economic liberalisation
    • Dialogue: Parliament, states, civil society and judiciary together
    • Judicial safeguards: keep "secular" and "socialist" as essential features
  • Practice over semantics:
    • Enforce secularism and welfare in practice
    • PM's "5S": Samman, Samvad, Sahyog, Shanti, Samriddhi

Also in the handout

  • Together, these establish India’s pursuit of inclusive development.
  • Changing times: Supporters of removal say that India’s socio-economic conditions have substantially evolved since 1976. After liberalisation and global integration post-1991, they argue that “socialism” as a rigid concept may constrain economic flexibility and inhibit reforms aligned with market dynamics.
  • Its retention is seen as essential to prevent majoritarian dominance and ensure that all faiths receive equal treatment under the law. 4.Symbolic importance: The Preamble functions as the “constitutional compass” or “identity card” of the Constitution (as famously described by N.A.
  • Removing these words risks undermining the symbolic coherence of India’s foundational document and its guiding ethos.
  • Strengthen Practice, Not Semantics: Instead of debating terminology, focus on practical enforcement of secularism and welfare.

8 · Conclusion#

  • The Preamble is the soul and guiding light of the Constitution: a solemn declaration of India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic and Republic nation committed to justice, liberty, equality and fraternity.
From Lakshmikanth · Indian Polity, 8th ed. Ch 5, Preamble of the Constitution
  • The Preamble has four ingredients: source of authority (the people), nature of the State, objectives, and the date of adoption (26 Nov 1949).
  • India was a British dominion from 15 Aug 1947 to 26 Jan 1950
  • It ceased to be one by declaring itself a sovereign republic
  • Commonwealth membership (declared 1949) and UN membership (since 1945) do not limit India's sovereignty.
  • Indian socialism is democratic socialism with a mixed economy, not communistic (state) socialism, which nationalises all means of production and abolishes private property.
  • The Supreme Court said in 1974 that the framers wanted a secular State even before the word was added
  • Arts. 25-28 show this. States can be atheistic, theocratic or secular
  • Democracy is direct (Switzerland:
    • Referendum, initiative, recall, plebiscite) or indirect
    • India has representative parliamentary democracy, where the executive answers to the legislature
  • Social plus economic justice together make distributive justice. The justice ideal comes from the Russian Revolution (1917)
  • Liberty, equality and fraternity from the French Revolution (1789-1799)
  • Dr Ambedkar (25 Nov 1949): political democracy cannot last without social democracy, and liberty, equality and fraternity must not be divorced from each other.
  • Berubari was a Presidential reference under Art. 143. Even there the Court called the Preamble a key to the minds of the makers, useful when an article is ambiguous.
  • Hidayatullah:
    • The Preamble resembles the US Declaration of Independence but is more than a declaration
    • Sir Ernest Barker called it the 'key-note' of the Constitution

Traps#

Trap 1 · Case order Berubari (1960) said the Preamble is NOT a part; Kesavananda (1973) said it IS a part; LIC (1995) said it is an integral part. Rule: the first case says No, the next two say Yes.
Trap 2 · Amended only once The only amendment is the 42nd (1976); it added Socialist, Secular and Integrity. Democratic, Republic and Fraternity were already there. Rule: two S and an I, once, in 1976.
Trap 3 · Power and enforceability The Preamble is non-justiciable and is neither a source of power to the legislature nor a prohibition on it (Prelims 2020). Rule: it guides interpretation; a clear article prevails over it.
Trap 4 · Nature versus objectives Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic describe what the State is. Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity are what it promises. Economic justice sits in the Preamble and the DPSP (Prelims 2013). Rule: keep the two lists apart; Republic is not an objective and Fraternity is not an adjective.
