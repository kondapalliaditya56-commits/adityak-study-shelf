2004PYQ 1Prelims 2004

Statements Consider the following statements: 1. In the Third Battle of Panipat, Ahmed Shah Abdali defeated Ibrahim Lodi. 2. Tipu Sultan was killed in the Third Anglo-Mysore War. 3. Mir Jafar entered into conspiracy with the English for the defeat of Nawab Siraj-ud-Daula in the Battle of Plassey. Which of the statement(s) given above is/are correct?

Answer: (B)

Why (b) is correct: Only Statement 3 is correct — and it sits in 'Robert Clive, the Conspiracy & the Battle'. Mir Jafar57-60 — technically the Bakhshi (war paymaster + head of military intelligence), but the de facto commander since Siraj56-57 was young — entered into a conspiracy with Robert Clive57-67 and the EIC. Jafar wanted the Subahdarship for himself; he made a deal with EIC to remove Siraj56-57. At the Battle of Plassey (1757), Mir Jafar ensured that the bulk of the Bengal army did not fight and turned back. Siraj56-57 was easily defeated, fled, and was later captured and beheaded on Mir Jafar's son Miran's orders.

Why the other options are wrong:

  • Statement 1 wrong — In the Third Battle of Panipat (1761), Ahmed Shah Abdali defeated the Marathas (not Ibrahim Lodi). Ibrahim Lodi was defeated at the First Battle of Panipat (1526) by Babur26-30.
  • Statement 2 wrong — Tipu Sultan was killed in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1798–99) at Srirangapatna, not the Third Anglo-Mysore War (1789–92). ⚠ ⚠ Partial match note — only Statement 3 is from the present material. Statements 1 and 2 belong to other topics (Panipat; the Anglo-Mysore Wars). Listed here because the correct answer (option b — Only 3) is derivable from the present material alone.
2005PYQ 2Prelims 2005

Sequencing Which of the following is the correct chronological order of the battles fought in India in the 18th century?

Answer: (B)

Why (b) is correct: The correct chronological order is Ambur (1749) → Plassey (1757) → Wandiwash (1760) → Buxar (1764). You will find the full chronology table in 'Chronology

Box: 18th-Century Battles' — and the analytical synthesis: two French battles frame two English ones (French high at Ambur, French out at Wandiwash; EIC political breakthrough at Plassey, EIC sovereign breakthrough at Buxar).

Why the other options are wrong:

  • (a) wrong — places Wandiwash (1760) before Buxar (1764) and before Ambur (1749), which is impossible.
  • (c) wrong — places Wandiwash (1760) before Plassey (1757), which is impossible.
  • (d) wrong — places Buxar (1764) before Wandiwash (1760), which is impossible. ⚠ ⚠ Partial match note — Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764) belong here; Ambur (1749) and Wandiwash (1760) belong to the Carnatic Wars. Listed here because the chronology sits in 'Chronology Box: 18th-Century Battles'.

FYQ 1

Consider the following statements about the Royal Farman of 1717: 1. The Farman was secured by the EIC from Mughal Emperor Farrukhsiyar13-19 after Dr. William Hamilton successfully treated the Emperor for a serious illness. 2. The Farman granted the EIC three rights — Duty-Free Trade in Bengal, Gujarat and Deccan; the right to use the Royal Mint; and the right to rent 38 villages around Calcutta. 3. Nawab Murshid Quli Khan17-27 accepted the Farman and granted all three rights without any pushback. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (A)

(a) 1 and 2 only:

Statement 1 ✓ — secured from Farrukhsiyar13-19 after Dr. William Hamilton's medical services.

Statement 2 ✓ — three rights: Duty-Free Trade in Bengal/Gujarat/Deccan + Royal Mint + 38 villages around Calcutta.

Statement 3 ✗ — MQK17-27 accepted the Farman but pushed back on each right: allowed DFT institutionally but refused to let officials misuse Dastaks; refused Royal Mint; allowed renting of villages but not purchase.

FYQ 2

With reference to the Dastak Problem, consider the following statements: 1. By 1750, private trade by EIC officials was worth roughly fifty times their annual salaries. 2. Misuse of Dastaks by EIC officials violated the Royal Charter, which was British law. 3. Cornwallis86-93 was a notable exception — one of the rare honest Governors of that era. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (C)

(c) 1 and 3 only:

Statement 1 ✓ — by 1750, private trade by EIC officials was worth 1,00,000 pounds per annum, roughly fifty times their annual salaries.

Statement 2 ✗ — misuse of Dastaks by EIC officials violated Indian law (tax evasion under Bengal's customs framework), NOT British law. The Royal Charter was violated by Interlopers (non-EIC British merchants), a different category.

Statement 3 ✓ — Cornwallis86-93 was a rare honest exception among Governors of that era.

FYQ 3

Match the following terms with their correct description: List I (Term) 1. Interlopers 2. Free merchants 3. Dastak misuse List II (Description) A. EIC officials fraudulently passing personal goods as company goods — violated Indian law (customs duty evasion under Bengal's customs framework). B. Non-EIC British merchants who traded with India, violating the Royal Charter — faced court cases in Britain. C. Non-EIC British merchants stationed in India who agreed to sell Indian goods to the EIC inside India rather than exporting independently — artificially legal in the eyes of British law. Codes:

Answer: (B)

(b) 1-B, 2-C, 3-A: Term 1 (Interlopers) — non-EIC British merchants trading with India, violated the Royal Charter (British law) and faced court cases in Britain (B). Term 2 (Free merchants) — British merchants stationed in India who agreed to sell to the EIC inside India rather than export independently — artificially legal in EIC's eyes (C). Term 3 (Dastak misuse) — EIC officials fraudulently passing personal goods as company goods, also selling Dastaks; violated Indian customs framework (tax evasion) (A).

FYQ 4

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

FYQ 5

With reference to the Treaty of Alinagar (February 1757), which one of the following is correct?

Answer: (B)

(b): "Alinagar" was the new name Siraj-ud-Daula56-57 had given to Calcutta after capturing it. The Treaty of Alinagar (February 1757) was therefore signed at Calcutta-renamed-Alinagar and restored all EIC privileges. The place name in the treaty title is the same city, not a different town.

(a) ✗ — No battle at any "Alinagar" in Bihar; Calcutta was the city.

(c) ✗ — Treaty of Alinagar was 1757, not 1764; the 1764 turning point was the Battle of Buxar; Diwani came via the Treaty of Allahabad in 1765.

(d) ✗ — French settlements neutralised by Clive57-67 included Hugli port and Chandarnagar in Bengal, not any "Alinagar."

FYQ 6

Consider the following statements regarding the Battle of Plassey (1757): 1. Mir Jafar, the commander of Bengal's army, conspired with Clive57-67 and ensured the bulk of the Bengal army did not fight. 2. Siraj-ud-Daula56-57 was vastly outnumbered in effective combatants and was easily defeated. 3. Mir Jafar57-65 became Nawab and the EIC's puppet, with a break — he ruled 1757–60 and 1763–65. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (D)

(d) 1, 2 and 3:

Statement 1 ✓ — Mir Jafar, commander of Bengal's army, approached Clive57-67 and ensured the bulk of the army did not fight.

Statement 2 ✓ — Siraj-ud-Daula56-57 was vastly outnumbered in effective combatants (his nominal army was much larger but most of it stood down per Mir Jafar's arrangement).

Statement 3 ✓ — Mir Jafar57-65 (with a break, 1757–60 and 1763–65) became puppet Nawab; the EIC reinstalled him for the second period after Mir Qasim60-63 rebelled.

FYQ 7

With reference to the Plassey Plunder (1757–65), consider the following statements: 1. The immediate financial settlement included Rs 2.25 crore to the EIC, 2,34,000 pounds to Robert Clive57-67 personally, and 2,75,000 pounds to the Royal British military. 2. Between 1757 and 1765, a further 2.5 million pounds were taken by the EIC as political gifts from Bengal. 3. After Plassey, EIC officials conducted private domestic trade through Indian agents called Gomastas who procured goods directly from peasants and artisans, cutting out Indian middlemen. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (D)

(d) 1, 2 and 3. (The Plassey Plunder (1757–65):

Statement 1 ✓ — immediate settlement: Rs 2.25 crore to EIC + 2,34,000 pounds to Clive57-67 personally + 2,75,000 pounds to Royal British military, plus a personal jagir of 35,000 pounds per annum to Clive57-67.

Statement 2 ✓ — between 1757 and 1765, a further 2.5 million pounds were taken as political gifts.

Statement 3 ✓ — Gomastas were Indian agents who procured directly from peasants and artisans, cutting out Indian middlemen entirely.

FYQ 8

Which one of the following best describes how the Drain of Wealth began after Plassey?

Answer: (B)

(b). (The Plassey Plunder (1757–65): Drain of Wealth = Bengal's own Diwani revenues used by the EIC to buy Indian goods for export. India was paying for its own plunder; surplus gold flowed from India to Britain (gold-outflow replacing the pre-1757 gold-inflow). This is the substantive meaning of the Drain of Wealth — not Crown tribute, not customs duty, not parliamentary tax.

(a) ✗ — there was no annual tribute paid by Indian rulers to the British Crown until much later; the EIC promised tribute to Shah Alam II60-06 (not the reverse).

(c) ✗ — customs duty was being evaded via Dastak misuse, not collected to London.

(d) ✗ — No parliamentary tax began in 1765; what began was the Diwani-funded extraction.

FYQ 9

Consider the following statements regarding Mir Qasim60-63's reforms: 1. He shifted his base from the capital Murshidabad to Bihar. 2. He abolished all internal duties on domestic trade — creating a level playing field for Indian merchants who had been squeezed out by EIC officials. 3. The autonomous behaviour was tolerated by the EIC as long as financial demands were met. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (A)

(a) 1 and 2 only:

Statement 1 ✓ — Mir Qasim60-63 shifted his base from Murshidabad to Bihar to escape EIC supervision.

Statement 2 ✓ — abolishing internal duties was clever; it created a level playing field for Indian merchants squeezed by EIC officials' untaxed Gomasta-mediated domestic trade since

1757.

Statement 3 ✗ — the autonomous behaviour was intolerable to the EIC; Mir Qasim60-63 was attacked, defeated in Bihar, and forced into the Buxar coalition.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

FYQ 11

With reference to the Battle of Buxar (1764), which one of the following is correct?

Answer: (B)

(b): The allied coalition (Mir Qasim60-63 + Shuja54-75 + Shah Alam II60-06) had prepared a defensive strategy — laying siege to the fort. Shuja54-75 lost patience, switched to offensive direct attack against Mir Qasim's explicit advice — handing the advantage to the EIC. Hector Munroe's final charge turned the tables. (Clive57-67 was in London at the time.)

(a) ✗ — The coalition lost.

(c) ✗ — Clive57-67 was in London; Munroe commanded.

(d) ✗ — Shah Alam II60-06 was deliberately spared and treated with respect — the EIC needed his sovereign legitimacy as legal cover.

FYQ 12

Consider the following statements regarding the Treaty of Allahabad (1765) clauses on the Emperor: 1. The EIC promised to pay Shah Alam II60-06 an annual tribute of 26 lakh rupees. 2. The EIC promised to militarily help Shah Alam II60-06 recapture Delhi from the Rohilla Afghans, who had held Delhi since 1761. 3. Shah Alam II60-06 was given Allahabad and Kora — taken from Awadh — as a seat of dignity. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (D)

(d) 1, 2 and 3.

Statement 1 ✓ — annual tribute of 26 lakh rupees promised to Shah Alam II60-06.

Statement 2 ✓ — military help promised to recapture Delhi from the Rohilla Afghans, who had held Delhi since 1761.

Statement 3 ✓ — Allahabad and Kora given (taken from Awadh) as a seat of dignity for the Emperor whose Red Fort was under Rohilla control. (Important caveat: in practice the EIC paid neither the Rs 26 lakh nor supplied an army to recapture Delhi — it was all legal formality. What mattered was the Diwani.)

FYQ 13

With reference to the Diwani granted by the Treaty of Allahabad (1765), consider the following statements: 1. The Diwani included revenue administration, control of the revenue/treasury, and control of Civil Courts. 2. The Diwani granted to the EIC covered Bengal, Orissa and Bihar. 3. The Diwani included welfare duties such as health and education. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (A)

(a) 1 and 2 only.

Statement 1 ✓ — Diwani = revenue administration + control of revenue/treasury + control of Civil Courts.

Statement 2 ✓ — Diwani of Bengal Suba covered Bengal, Orissa and Bihar.

Statement 3 ✗ — Diwani did not include modern welfare duties (health/education), because those were traditionally handled by private/religious patronage and local Zamindars, not the Mughal government.

FYQ 14

With reference to the Dual Government in Bengal (1765–72), which one of the following is correct?

Answer: (B)

(b). The EIC controlled the Diwani while the Nawab controlled the Nizamat, with the EIC appointing a Deputy Nawab (Naib Nazim — Md Reza Khan) as its Diwan in practice. The Dual Government (1765–72) was wound up in 1772 under Warren Hastings73-85, triggered by the catastrophic Great Bengal Famine of 1770 (one crore people / one-third of Bengal's population dead) which the Dual Government's split-responsibility had supercharged.

(a) ✗ — reverse arrangement; the Diwani was with the EIC, not the Nawab.

(c) ✗ — Direct Rule (both Diwani + Nizamat with EIC) only began in 1772, not from 1765.

(d) ✗ — The Dual Government was ended in 1772, not 1858.

FYQ 16

With reference to the 1717 Imperial Farman of Farrukhsiyar13-19, consider the following statements: 1. It was issued by Mughal Emperor Farrukhsiyar13-19 (reigned 1713–1719). 2. It is often called the "Magna Carta of the Company" because of the sweeping privileges it granted. 3. The EIC received dastaks — duty-free trade passes — across Bengal, along with extensive commercial rights. 4. The Farman was obtained through a remarkable piece of diplomacy: Dr. William Hamilton, an English surgeon, successfully treated the Emperor for a serious illness. 5. The 1717 Imperial Farman was a provincial-level farman issued by the Subahdar of Bengal, like the 1691 farman of Ibrahim Khan. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (C)

(c) 1, 2, 3 and 4 only. — Statements (1)–(4) all correct: Farrukhsiyar13-19 issued + "Magna Carta of the Company" + dastaks across Bengal + Dr. William Hamilton's medical treatment of the Emperor. Statement (5) is the trap — the 1717 Farman was the truly imperial farman issued directly by the Emperor; it is distinct from the 1691 provincial-level farman issued by Ibrahim Khan, the Subahdar of Bengal, under approval of Aurangzeb58-07. Confusing the two is the recurring exam trap. Subsidiary Alliance of Wellesley98-05 (1798–1805)

FYQ 1 Why does Plassey 1757 mark the beginning of British conquest in India? 150 words · 10 marks "The Battle of Plassey (1757) was the start of the British conquest of India — not because it was a great military victory, but because of what it gave the EIC. Examine."

Demand of the question: Show WHY Plassey began the conquest — even though it was militarily a small affair, it gave the EIC the financial and political base from which to fight every later war.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Plassey 1757 was not a great battle. But it gave the EIC Bengal — the richest province of India — and turned a trading company into a territorial State.

Guiding themes (pick 4-5 for a 150-word answer):

1. The conspiracy, not the battle — Clive57-67 won Plassey through pre-arranged defections (Mir Jafar, Rai Durlabh, Jagat Seths), not by overwhelming force (see 'Robert Clive, the Conspiracy & the Battle [ ▲ Index ]').

2. Mir Jafar as puppet — the EIC installed its own Nawab who paid huge sums to Clive and the EIC; the Dupleix blueprint perfected (see 'Robert Clive, the Conspiracy & the Battle [ ▲ Index ]' and 'The Plassey Plunder (1757–65) [ ▲ Index ]').

3. The Plassey Plunder (1757–65) — systematic extraction of Bengal wealth: nazarana, free trade exemptions, currency manipulation; transferred to England as the Drain (see 'The Plassey Plunder (1757–65) [ ▲ Index ]').

4. From trader to ruler — the EIC now had territorial revenue from Bengal that funded its later wars: Anglo-Mysore, Anglo-Maratha, Subsidiary Alliance era (see 'Plassey Marks the Beginning of Conquest [ ▲ Index ]').

5. Setup for Buxar 1764 — Plassey gave the EIC Bengal; Buxar would give it the legal right to rule. The two together made the EIC a sovereign State in India (see 'Battle of Buxar (1764): EIC Gets the Right to Rule [ ▲ Index ]' and 'Treaty of Allahabad (*ToA1765): [ ▲ Index ]').

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): Plassey 1757 was the start of conquest not by force of arms but by conspiracy + revenue — a trading company became a State.

FYQ 2 Compare the Battle of Plassey 1757 with the Battle of Buxar 1764. Which mattered more for British conquest? 150 words · 10 marks "While Plassey 1757 began British conquest, the Battle of Buxar 1764 was the decisive turning point. Examine both battles and their consequences."

Demand of the question: Cover BOTH battles — the conspiracy at Plassey + the pitched battle at Buxar — and assess which gave the EIC the deeper foundation.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Plassey 1757 gave the EIC Bengal’s wealth through conspiracy. Buxar 1764 gave the EIC the legal right to rule Bengal through a pitched military victory.

Guiding themes (pick 4-5 for a 150-word answer):

1. Plassey 1757 — conspiracy-led victory; Mir Jafar installed as puppet Nawab; EIC gained financial access to Bengal but no legal sovereignty (see 'Robert Clive, the Conspiracy & the Battle [ ▲ Index ]').

2. Buxar 1764 — pitched battle against a confederation of Mir Qasim60-63 (Nawab of Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daula54-75 (Nawab of Awadh), and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II60-06; the three combined armies of ~50,000 lost to Hector Munro’s ~7,000 EIC force (see 'Battle of Buxar (1764): EIC Gets the Right to Rule [ ▲ Index ]').

3. Treaty of Allahabad 1765 — the legal consequence of Buxar: Mughal Emperor granted Diwani (revenue collection rights) of Bengal-Bihar-Orissa to the EIC — legalising what Plassey had only made possible (see 'Treaty of Allahabad (*ToA1765): [ ▲ Index ]').

4. Why Buxar mattered more — it converted the EIC from a de facto power into a de jure ruler under Mughal sanction; the Mughal Emperor’s seal made EIC rule legitimate in Indian law (see 'Treaty of Allahabad (*ToA1765): [ ▲ Index ]').

5. Awadh as buffer — the Treaty also turned Awadh into a buffer State paying tribute, securing the EIC’s northern flank without annexation (see 'Treaty of Allahabad (*ToA1765): [ ▲ Index ]').

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): Plassey gave the EIC Bengal’s wealth; Buxar gave it Bengal’s law. Together they made the EIC a sovereign in India.
