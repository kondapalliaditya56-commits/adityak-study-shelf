In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Parliamentary committees are small bodies that do the detailed work Parliament cannot do on the floor. The Constitution only hints at them
  • The rules of the Houses (Art. 118) fix composition, tenure and powers
  • A body counts as a parliamentary committee if it is appointed or nominated by the House or Speaker, works under the Speaker/Chairman, reports to the House or Speaker, and has a secretariat from the House.
  • There are two kinds: standing (permanent, often renewed every year) and ad hoc (temporary; Inquiry and Advisory such as Select or Joint Committees on Bills). Standing ones fall in six families: Financial, Department-related, Inquiry, Scrutiny and control, Day-to-day business, House-keeping.
  • Financial trio: PAC (22 members, 15 LS + 7 RS, Opposition chairman since 1967, helped by the CAG), Estimates (30, only Lok Sabha) and COPU (22, set up in 1964). All are elected yearly by STV, no ministers, and their advice is only advisory.
  • 24 DRSCs (16 LS + 8 RS), started in 1993, have 31 members each (21 + 10) for one year and study Demands for Grants and Bills ministry by ministry.
  • Forums are not committees: no statutory sanction, no power to compel records, no Action Taken Report, and ministers may join. Consultative Committees are chaired by the minister and last as long as the Lok Sabha.

Key terms

Standing committee
Permanent or recurring body, usually constituted every year, working on a continuing basis
Ad hoc committee
Temporary body for one purpose; ends when its task is done (Inquiry and Advisory kinds)
Select / Joint Committee
Advisory ad hoc committee on a Bill: clause-by-clause study, expert evidence, then a report; Joint means both Houses
Minute of dissent
Note by a member who disagrees with the majority report
Post-mortem scrutiny
Checking spending after it is done; the handout's main complaint against PAC, Estimates and COPU
Action Taken Report
Government's reply to a committee report; needed for committees, not for forums
DRSC
Department-Related Standing Committee: 24 of them, ministry-wise oversight, 31 members each
Consultative Committee
Non-statutory informal group of MPs chaired by the minister; 10 to 30 members; lasts for the life of the Lok Sabha

1 · Why committees and what makes one#

  • Parliament is a large House facing legislation, policy, budgets and oversight in huge volume and technical detail, so smaller focused bodies do the detailed scrutiny and preparatory work: they sift evidence, examine Bills clause by clause and prepare reports for the House.

Constitutional position

  • The Constitution refers to committees at various places but does not prescribe composition, tenure or functions.
  • Those details come from the rules of the two Houses (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha): formation, term, powers, reporting.

The four conditions

  • A body is a parliamentary committee if it:
  • (a) is appointed or elected by the House, or nominated by the Speaker/Chairman;
  • (b) functions under the direction of the Speaker or Chairman;
  • (c) submits its report to the House or to the Speaker/Chairman;
  • (d) is supported by a secretariat provided by the Lok Sabha or the Rajya Sabha.
  • Memory aid (Four tests): Appointed or nominated, Functions under the Speaker/Chairman, Reports to House or Speaker, Secretariat from the House: "Always Follow Rules, Sir." These four give the committee its formal identity, accountability to the House and administrative support. Reports give the House researched findings, recommendations and proposed amendments, a job not feasible in full sittings.

2 · Classification of committees#

Two broad types

  • Standing committees:
    • Permanent or recurrent
    • Constituted periodically (often every year)
    • Continuing functions
  • Ad hoc committees:
    • Temporary
    • Set up for one purpose or inquiry
    • Cease to exist when the task is done
  • The standing committees fall into six categories by the nature of their work.

3 · Ad hoc committees#

  • Ad hoc committees serve one purpose and cease when it is done. There are two types: Inquiry Committees and Advisory Committees.

1. Inquiry Committees

  • Set up either by a motion adopted in the House(s) or by the Speaker/Chairman, to examine and report on particular matters of public importance or administrative concern.

2. Advisory Committees

  • These are Select Committees and Joint Committees on Bills: they deal only with legislative business, unlike inquiry committees.

How a Bill goes to a Select or Joint Committee

  • Bill introduced and discussed: In the House
  • Motion moved and adopted: Refers the Bill to a Select Committee of that House or a Joint Committee of both Houses
  • Joint Committee approved: The other House is asked to nominate members
  • Clause-by-clause scrutiny:
    • Amendments allowed
    • Expert evidence from associations, public bodies, individuals
  • Report to the House: Members who disagree may submit minutes of dissent
How a Bill goes to a Select or Joint Committee
  • Procedure is governed by the Rules of Procedure and Directions of the Presiding Officers.
  • Memory aid (Ad hoc split): Inquiry (matters of public or administrative concern) and Advisory (Bills only): "I Ask, Advise".

4 · Public Accounts Committee (PAC)#

PAC at a glance

FeatureDetail
First established1921, under the Government of India Act, 1919
Members22: 15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha
ElectionAnnually, by proportional representation by single transferable vote (all parties represented)
MinistersCannot be members
ChairmanAppointed by the Speaker from among Lok Sabha members; since 1967, by convention an Opposition member
  • Core job: Scrutinises the annual CAG audit reports, presented to Parliament by the President under Article 151, to ensure financial propriety and efficiency of spending.

CAG reports and what the PAC examines

  • CAG submits three reports: Appropriation Accounts, Finance Accounts, Public Undertakings.
  • Appropriation and Finance Accounts: spending must match grants voted through the Appropriation Act.
  • Use of funds: disbursed for authorised purposes.
  • Re-appropriation of funds: complies with the rules.
  • Accounts of State corporations and autonomous bodies: audit findings.
  • Audit of receipts and stores: losses, wastage, inefficiency.
  • Excess expenditure: spending beyond parliamentary grants.
  • The CAG acts as "guide, friend, and philosopher" to the PAC.
What the PAC examines
ASSURE
  • AAppropriation and Finance Accounts
  • SState corporations and autonomous bodies
  • SStores and receipts: audit of receipts and stores
  • UUse of funds for authorised purposes
  • RRe-appropriation of funds

The PAC works to ASSURE that money is spent as Parliament authorised. The order is rearranged for memory (the six areas are unordered). (Memory aid, not from the handout.)

Limitations

  • Post-expenditure scrutiny, not policy formulation.
  • Cannot intervene in day-to-day administration.
  • Recommendations are advisory, not binding on ministries.
  • Cannot disallow expenditure or issue executive orders.
  • Former CAG Ashok Chanda called the PAC a "powerful force in the control of public expenditure".
  • Memory aid (PAC facts): CAG as friend, Opposition chair since 1967, STV election of 22 members (15 + 7): "Cats Often Sleep."

5 · Estimates Committee#

FeatureDetail
OriginEvolved from the Standing Financial Committee of 1921; formally constituted 1950 on the recommendation of Finance Minister John Mathai
MembersInitially 25, now 30, all from Lok Sabha (no Rajya Sabha representation)
ElectionAnnually, PR by single transferable vote
MinistersBarred
ChairmanAppointed by the Speaker, usually from the ruling party

Functions

  • Suggest economies in public expenditure and administrative efficiency.
  • Recommend alternative policies for better use of resources.
  • Ensure money is well spent within the approved policy framework.
  • Recommend reforms in the presentation of estimates.
  • It works through the financial year and may report periodically, but Parliament need not wait for its report before voting on Demands for Grants.

Limitations

  • Examines estimates after Parliament votes them.
  • Cannot question the policy behind estimates.
  • Recommendations are advisory.
  • Reviews only a few ministries each year.
  • No CAG expert help, unlike the PAC.
  • Mostly retrospective: a post-mortem, not preventive oversight.
  • Memory line:
    • PAC chair = Opposition (since 1967)
    • Estimates chair = ruling party
    • Estimates is Lok Sabha only

6 · Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)#

  • Constituted in 1964 on the recommendation of the Krishna Menon Committee for sustained scrutiny of public sector undertakings. Its working follows the Houses' Rules of Procedure (Article 118 lets each House frame its own rules).

COPU at a glance

FeatureDetail
Original strength15 members (10 Lok Sabha + 5 Rajya Sabha)
Increased in 197422 members (15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha)
ElectionAnnually by Parliament from its members, PR by single transferable vote
TermOne year
MinistersIneligible
ChairmanAppointed by the Speaker from among its members

Functions

  • Examine reports and accounts of specified public undertakings.
  • Examine CAG reports on public undertakings (laid under Article 151).
  • Judge whether undertakings are run on sound commercial principles and prudent business practice, with autonomy and efficiency.
  • Do other work the Speaker allots, including tasks like those of the PAC and Estimates Committee.

Outside its remit

  • Major government policy distinct from business or commercial functions.
  • Day-to-day administration of the undertakings.
  • Matters under special statutory machinery set up by the Act creating the undertaking.

Practical constraints

  • Cannot examine more than ten to twelve undertakings a year.
  • Mostly post-facto (post-mortem) scrutiny.
  • Members are not technical experts.
  • Recommendations are advisory.

  • Established in 1993 on the recommendation of the Lok Sabha Rules Committee and expanded in 2004 to 24 committees, to strengthen Parliament's oversight and financial accountability of the Executive. They enable detailed ministry-wise scrutiny and act as "miniature Parliaments".

Advantages of the DRSC system

  • Non-partisan discussion, free of party bias.
  • Flexible procedure compared with the Lok Sabha.
  • Continuous oversight of the executive.
  • Financial discipline: ministries prepare demands more carefully.
  • Wider participation of Members from all parties.
  • Expert opinion through invited experts and eminent persons.
  • Stronger Opposition and Rajya Sabha in financial oversight.

13 · Parliamentary forums#

  • The idea came from Lok Sabha Speaker Somnath Chatterjee (UPA-1 period).

Why

  • He found Parliament's matters were specialised and technical, and MPs' contributions were ill-informed on technicalities.
  • Forums give MPs a platform to interact with departmental experts and outside domain experts.
  • They sensitise MPs to the latest information so they can raise matters effectively.
  • They gather information from government and non-government entities and build an integrated database on a topic.

Structure

  • Each forum has 31 members: 21 Lok Sabha + 10 Rajya Sabha.
  • Membership lasts 5 years, or the member may resign any time.
  • Around eight forums exist.
  • All are presided over by the Lok Sabha Speaker, except the Forum on Population and Public Health, presided over by the Rajya Sabha Chairman.

Forums against committees

  • Parliamentary committees:
    • Statutory sanction
    • Ministers cannot be members
    • Legal right to enforce production of records and documents and to summon and question civil servants
    • Government must prepare an Action Taken Report on their reports
  • Parliamentary forums:
    • Created on the Speaker's initiative in UPA-1
    • Ministers can be members
    • No formal powers to enforce production or summon
    • No Action Taken Report required
  • Memory aid (Four differences): Sanction, Ministers, Powers, Action Taken Report: "Some Members Push Ahead."
Handout alert

Handout alert: The forums list ("Presently there are around eight such forums:") is blank in the handout and the Lok Sabha figure is garbled as "2 1". Read it as 21 + 10 = 31. No forum names are given, so none are added here.

Also in the handout

  • He found that the matters being brought before the Parliament were specialized and technical in nature and the questions raised by the MPs on these matters, the kind of contribution the MPs were making to Parliamentary debates were totally ill informed and innocent of technicalities. The MPs were not able to understand the complexity of these issues. - Hence, he suggested to come up with Parliamentary Forums as a platform for MPs to call for interaction with experts from concerned departments or domain experts from outside to enhance their knowledge.

14 · Advantages and critical evaluation#

  • 1. More effective deliberation: small committees (PAC with 22 members) go deeper than the Houses of 543 and 245. 2. Technical and domain expertise: outside experts can be called. 3. Stakeholder participation: citizens, interest groups, industry, civil society can present views. 4. Inadequate sittings: Parliament meets about 67 days a year, committees work all year. 5. Inter-party consensus: PR membership and closed-door, non-televised meetings help cross-party agreement.
  • Memory aid (Five advantages): Deliberation, Experts, Stakeholders, Inadequate sittings, Forge consensus: "Deep Expert Studies Improve Final laws."

Critical evaluation

  • 1. Inadequate expert assistance:
    • Most lack regular technical support, unlike PAC with CAG. 2. Floating membership: frequent reconstitution blocks continuity. 3. Members' background ignored: MPs placed without matching sectoral expertise. 4. Party lines: partisan behaviour weakens objectivity. 5. Declining referrals: 71% of Bills went to committees in the 15th Lok Sabha
    • Only about 16% were examined in the 17th
  • Memory aid (Five flaws): Inadequate experts, Floating membership, Background ignored, MPs on party lines, Declining referrals: "I Find Bills Missing Debate."
Handout alert

Exam trap: The 71% and 16% figures compare Bills sent to committees (15th LS) and Bills examined by committees (17th LS). Quote the handout wording.

Also in the handout

  • Provide for technical considerations and domain expertise: Committees can call external experts and specialists to enrich their analysis and reports.
  • Floating membership: Frequent reconstitution of committees prevents MPs from building continuity and fully using the experience gained.

15 · Consultative Committees attached to ministries#

  • Non-statutory advisory bodies, joint in nature (both Houses), that give ministers and MPs an informal forum on a ministry's policies and programmes. The Minister or Minister of State in charge is the chairperson. Discussions are non-partisan and non-binding, supplementing formal oversight such as the DRSCs.

Railway zones

  • Informal Consultative Committees exist for all Railway Zones, made of MPs from constituencies in that zone, to review railway operations, finances and projects. The handout links railways to Article 77 (executive action of the Government of India).

History

  • Railway Convention Committee, 1949:
    • An early post-independence joint committee
    • First set the rate of dividend payable by Railways to General Revenues
    • From 1971 widened to working and financial performance of Railways
  • DRSC system formally introduced in 1993, evolving from 1989 experimental committees on Agriculture, Science and Technology, and Environment and Forests.
  • Inaugurated on 31 March 1993 by Vice-President and Rajya Sabha Chairman K.R. Narayanan: a way to ensure accountability through detailed, constructive scrutiny, to strengthen administration, not weaken it.
  • 13th Lok Sabha: 45 members per DRSC (30 Speaker + 15 Chairman). July 2004: reduced to 31 (21 + 10) for focus and efficiency.
  • Memory aid (Consultative facts): Minister chairs, Ten to thirty members, Life of the Lok Sabha: "Ministers Talk Locally."

  • By safeguarding parliamentary privileges, the committee ensures that members can perform their duties without fear, obstruction, or undue influence, preserving the independence of the legislature.
  • Accordingly, they stand dissolved automatically upon the dissolution of the Lok Sabha and are re-formed after every new House is constituted.

  • A joint committee has members of both Houses. Consultative Committees, though made of MPs, are not parliamentary committees because they fail the four tests (appointment, Speaker's direction, report, secretariat).
  • DRSC count in steps: 17 were set up in 1993 and 7 more in 2004, making 24. Three earlier DRSCs of 1989 (Agriculture, Science and Technology, Environment and Forests) were replaced in 1993.
  • The 8 Rajya Sabha DRSCs:
    • Commerce
    • Home Affairs
    • Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports
    • Industry
    • Science and Technology, Environment, Forest and Climate Change
    • Transport, Tourism and Culture
    • Health and Family Welfare
    • Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and Justice. The other 16 are Lok Sabha committees (for example Finance, Defence, External Affairs, Railways)
  • DRSC reports on Demands for Grants must not suggest anything of the nature of cut motions. They also consider ministry annual reports and national basic long-term policy documents. Lok Sabha members are nominated by the Speaker and Rajya Sabha members by the Chairman.
  • Lakshmikanth says ministries have by and large accepted DRSC recommendations. It judges DRSCs on four yardsticks: holding officials to account, publication of information, effect on the House and public, and influence on government decisions.
  • The PAC checks spending for economy, prudence, wisdom and propriety, not just legal slips, to expose waste, loss, corruption, extravagance, inefficiency and nugatory expenses. Until 1966-67 its chairman came from the ruling party.
  • In scrutinising appropriation accounts the PAC must be satisfied that the money was legally available for the purpose, that spending followed the governing authority and that every re-appropriation followed the rules. It is not an executive body: only Parliament takes the final decision on its findings.
  • Estimates Committee:
    • Described as a 'continuous economy committee'
    • Strength raised from 25 to 30 in 1956
    • It need not examine the entire estimates of one year, and Demands for Grants can be voted even if it has made no report
  • Indian Parliamentary Group (IPG):
    • Formed in 1949 as an autonomous body
    • Speaker of Lok Sabha is ex officio President, Deputy Speaker and Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman are vice-presidents, and the Secretary-General of Lok Sabha is its Secretary-General. All MPs can be members
    • Former MPs can be associate members
  • The IPG acts as the National Group of the Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU) and as the India Branch of the Commonwealth Parliamentary Association (CPA). Only MPs who have been IPG members for six months can join delegations abroad
  • The Outstanding Parliamentarian award began in 1995
  • IPU had 179 parliaments

Recall sheet#

Cover the right column and answer. Same questions as the flashcards.

What four conditions make a body a parliamentary committee?
Appointed or elected by the House or nominated by the Speaker/Chairman; works under the Speaker/Chairman; reports to the House or Speaker/Chairman; supported by a House secretariat.
Name the six families of standing committees.
Financial; Department-related; Inquiry; Scrutiny and control; Day-to-day business; House-keeping (service).
What are the two kinds of ad hoc committees?
Inquiry Committees and Advisory Committees (Select and Joint Committees on Bills).
How many members does the PAC have and who chairs it?
22 (15 LS + 7 RS); chairman appointed by the Speaker, an Opposition member by convention since 1967.
What is the CAG's role with the PAC?
"Guide, friend, and philosopher"; PAC scrutinises CAG audit reports presented under Article 151.
Name the PAC's limitations.
Post-expenditure only; no day-to-day intervention; advisory recommendations; cannot disallow expenditure or issue orders.
Estimates Committee: origin, size, chair?
Roots in 1921 Standing Financial Committee; formed 1950 (John Mathai); 25 then 30, all Lok Sabha; chair usually from the ruling party.
Estimates Committee limitations?
Works after voting; cannot question policy; advisory; few ministries yearly; no CAG help; retrospective.
COPU: year, recommendation, size?
1964, Krishna Menon Committee; 15 members raised to 22 (15 LS + 7 RS) in 1974; one-year term.
What is outside COPU's remit?
Major policy, day-to-day administration, matters under special statutory machinery.
COPU's practical constraints?
10 to 12 undertakings a year; post-mortem; non-expert members; advisory.
When were DRSCs set up and how big are they?
1993 (Lok Sabha Rules Committee), expanded in 2004 to 24; 31 members each (21 LS + 10 RS); one-year tenure.
What does a DRSC do with Demands for Grants?
Examines them in the adjournment after general budget discussion; reports within time with no extension; one report per ministry.
Which Bills do DRSCs examine?
Only introduced Bills, within a fixed time frame.
Who has the right to be on the Committee of Privileges and what is its nature?
15 LS and 10 RS members; semi-judicial; protects dignity of Parliament.
When was the Ethics Committee formed?
Rajya Sabha 1997, Lok Sabha 2000.
Which two scrutiny committees came in 1953?
Government Assurances and Subordinate Legislation.
Strength of SC/ST Welfare and Women committees?
30 each: 20 LS + 10 RS; Women's committee constituted 1997.
BAC strength and chairs?
LS 15 (Speaker chairs); RS 11 (Chairman, i.e. Vice-President).
Who chairs the Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions?
The Deputy Speaker (Lok Sabha only, 15 members).
After how many days of absence does the Absence Committee examine a case?
60 or more consecutive days without permission.
Library Committee strength?
9: 6 Lok Sabha + 3 Rajya Sabha.
Four differences between forums and committees?
Statutory sanction; ministers (not allowed in committees); enforcement powers; Action Taken Report (only for committees).
Who proposed Parliamentary Forums and how big are they?
Speaker Somnath Chatterjee (UPA-1); 31 members (21 + 10); 5-year membership.
What fall in Bills referred to committees did the handout cite?
From 71% (15th Lok Sabha) to about 16% (17th Lok Sabha).
Who chairs a Consultative Committee and how large is it?
The Minister or Minister of State in charge; 10 to 30 members.
Who constitutes Consultative Committees?
The Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs; tenure is the life of the Lok Sabha.
When and by whom were DRSCs inaugurated?
31 March 1993 by K.R. Narayanan, Vice-President and Rajya Sabha Chairman.
Mains 2018: Why are committees useful for parliamentary work, and what is the role of the Estimates Committee?
Useful for deeper deliberation than 543 and 245 members, expert and stakeholder input, year-round scrutiny given about 67 sitting days, cross-party consensus. Estimates Committee: suggests economies and efficiency, alternative policies, better presentation of estimates; limits: after voting, no policy challenge, advisory, no CAG help.
Mains 2021: Do DRSCs keep administration on its toes?
Yes: 24 committees give ministry-wise year-round scrutiny of Demands for Grants and Bills, with experts, non-partisan debate and a role for the Opposition and Rajya Sabha; limits: advisory, no day-to-day administration, one-year tenure, falling referrals (about 16% in 17th LS).
Mains 2023: Structure of the committee system and the financial committees' help?
Standing and ad hoc; six families; PAC, Estimates and COPU scrutinise spending, estimates and undertakings; limits: post-mortem, advisory, few ministries or undertakings a year, less expertise than the PAC gets from the CAG.
Mains 2017: Discuss the role of Public Accounts Committee in establishing accountability of the government to the people.
Intro: The PAC (1921, under the Government of India Act 1919) is the main parliamentary check on how public money is spent Body: 22 members (15 LS + 7 RS) elected annually by STV and no ministers; chairman an Opposition member by convention since 1967; scrutinises CAG audit reports on Appropriation Accounts and Finance Accounts under Article 151; checks spending against grants and use for authorised purposes; examines excess expenditure and losses in receipts and stores; CAG acts as guide, friend and philosopher; limits: post-expenditure only and advisory, cannot disallow expenditure. Close: Ashok Chanda called it a powerful force in the control of public expenditure, but its reports only advise.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Three financial committees look alike PAC has 22 members including 7 from Rajya Sabha, an Opposition chairman since 1967 and CAG help. Estimates has 30, all from Lok Sabha, a chairman usually from the ruling party and no CAG help. COPU has 22 (15 + 7) and was set up in 1964. Rule: Estimates = only Lok Sabha; PAC = Opposition chair + CAG; PAC and COPU both 22.
Trap 2 · 15 and 10 is a habit, not a rule Petitions, Privileges, Ethics, Government Assurances and Papers Laid have 15 LS and 10 RS. But Subordinate Legislation has 15 in each House, SC/ST Welfare and Women have 30 (20 + 10), Offices of Profit and Salaries are joint with 15 (10 + 5), Business Advisory is 15/11, Rules is 15/16, House is 12/10 and Library is 9 (6 + 3). Rule: use 15/10 only for the five named committees; learn the exceptions.
Trap 3 · Lok Sabha-only committees Private Members' Bills and Resolutions (chaired by the Deputy Speaker) and Absence of Members (60 or more days) exist only in the Lok Sabha. Rajya Sabha's Business Advisory Committee handles private members' business, and the House itself handles absence. Rule: if the question says 'no Rajya Sabha counterpart', think Private Members and Absence.
Trap 4 · Ministers: committees versus forums Ministers cannot be members of committees such as DRSCs and the financial three. They can be members of Parliamentary Forums, and the minister chairs the Consultative Committee of his ministry. Rule: committees = no ministers; forums and Consultative Committees = ministers allowed.
