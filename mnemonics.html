Chapter 1: The Advent of the Mughals (51)open chapter

Power and its six forms

Memory hook

Six forms of power.

"Every Pandit Makes Sweet Tea Slowly."

Decode: Economic, Political, Military, Social, Tech (Intellectual/Technological), Spiritual, in the chapter's order.

Why it sticks: a pandit slowly stirring sweet tea is easy to picture, and the six words keep the order.

It is in the nature of power to centralise in a strong personality

Memory hook

Vertical vs horizontal (when power leaves).

Vertical = the Vault door opens downward; Horizontal = a Heir steps in beside.

Decode: Vertical = power flows down, so decentralisation (grandparents to the family). Horizontal = another personality replaces the weak one at the same level (Kodak → smartphones).

Why it sticks: V goes Down (V points down), H goes Across (H has a crossbar).

Country, nation, state

Memory hook

Country, Nation, State: C-T, N-P, S-O.

"Country = Territory, Nation = People, State = Organisation."

Decode: Country (Desh) is the land; Nation (Rashtra) is the people who are one, equal and supreme; State (Rajya) is the organisation that rules both.

Why it sticks: the State is the only one that does something (rules); the other two just are.

Types of State

Memory hook

Five types of State.

"My Police Keep Calm Nightly."

Decode: Military, Police, Kingdom/Empire (bloodline; an empire = a sum of kingdoms), Company (EIC, 1765–1858), Nation State (set up by the people).

Why it sticks: the list runs from force (Military, Police) to bloodline to business to the people, in the chapter's order.

Sovereignty

Memory hook

External functions: D, C, FA.

"Defence fights, Communications and Foreign Affairs talk and trade at the MEA."

Decode: D (Defence) → Defence Minister → war. C (Communications) + FA (Foreign Affairs) → MEA → diplomacy + trade. Symbolic sovereignty = real power 0; L, E, J are the three powers over these and over domestic affairs.

Why it sticks: one minister fights, the other talks, and you only need two ministries for three letters.

Modern INM: the phases

Memory hook

INM phases: Pen, People, Gun, then Gandhi.

"The Pen wrote (1857–1905), the People marched (1905–20), the Gun fired (1907–47), then Gandhi led (1920–47)."

Decode: Moderates = Pen; Extremists = People; Revolutionaries = Gun; Gandhian Era 1920–47; the fifth phase is the Many Voices (working class, women, Dalits).

Why it sticks: Revolutionaries start in 1907 (overlapping the Extremists) and run to 1947, the same end year as the Gandhian Era.

How the teacher says to remember battles and wars

Memory hook

Remember any war: G-D-P-R.

"Great Drummers Play Rhythms" (Geography, Duration, Participants, Reason).

Decode: for every war ask where (Geography/place), how long (Duration, e.g. Seven Years' War), who (Participants), why (Reason, e.g. Austrian War of Succession 1740–48). A war = the sum of its battles.

Why it sticks: use it as a four-box grid every time a new battle appears in later chapters.

Passages: how regions connect

Memory hook

Passages: natural = P-S, man-made = B-T-C.

"Nature gives a Pass and a Strait; people build Bridges, Tunnels and Canals."

Decode: Natural land = pass (Khyber, India's North-West entry); natural water = strait (Gibraltar joins Europe and Africa; the Dardanelles). Man-made land = bridges and tunnels; man-made water = canals (Suez, Panama; Panama 1914 = key to the rise of the USA).

Why it sticks: Khyber is the only pass you need to name, Gibraltar the first strait.

2 · The Mughal monarchs: a quick reference

Memory hook

The baton relay in the reign codes.

Each code's second number is the next ruler's first number, like a baton passed on the track: Babur 26-30 → Humayun 30-40 ... Akbar 56-05 → Jahangir 05-27 → Shahjahan 27-58 → Aurangzeb 58-07 → Bahadur Shah 07-12 → Jahandar Shah 12-13 → Farukhsiyar 13-19 → Muhammad Shah 19-48 → Ahmad Shah Bahadur 48-54 → Alamgir II 54-59.

Decode: only two dropped batons. Humayun 30-40 to Akbar 56-05 (the Suri gap), and Alamgir II 54-59 to Shah Alam II 60-06 (starts 60). Then Shah Alam II 60-06 → Akbar II 06-37 → Bahadur Shah Zafar 37-57 is smooth again.

Why it sticks: if your next code does not start where the last one ended, you have found a gap.

Memory hook

The first six: B-H-A-J-S-A.

"Bhaji, Sa-ab?" = Babur, Humayun, Akbar, Jahangir, Shahjahan, Aurangzeb.

Decode: Babur 26-30, Humayun 30-40, Akbar 56-05, Jahangir 05-27, Shahjahan 27-58, Aurangzeb 58-07 are #1 to #6. The Later Mughals (#7 to #15) begin with Bahadur Shah 07-12 in 1707, the year Aurangzeb 58-07 died.

Why it sticks: 6 great + 9 later = 15 monarchs.

Memory hook

Humayun 30-40 = "Hum-do" (two reigns).

"Hum do reigns: 30-40, then back for 1555–56."

Decode: Humayun 30-40 is the only Mughal with two reign periods. Overthrown by Sher Shah 40-45 in 1540, in exile, back on the throne in 1555, dead the following year.

Why it sticks: "Hum-ayun" literally contains "hum", and he is the one who came back ("ayun").

Memory hook

The Mughal see-saw: up, down, up, down, up.

1526 UP (set up), 1540 DOWN (demolished), 1555 UP (re-established), Jan 1556 DOWN (Humayun 30-40 dies), 1556 UP (re-established).

Decode: the State is 1526–1858 with an intermission 1540–55 (the Suri gap, "a gap in the Mughal movie"). The handout's monarch list ends 1857; the teacher writes 1858, when the Company State ended.

Why it sticks: five beats on a see-saw; the two DOWNs are Kanauj-era 1540 and the death in Jan 1556.

Who were the Mughals?

Memory hook

Mother Mongol, Father Timur.

"Ma = Mongol (Genghis Khan 1206–27), Pa = Timur (1370–1405). The child chose Papa's name."

Decode: maternal side = Genghis Khan, Mongol Empire (1206–1368); paternal side = Timur (Tamerlane), Timurid Empire (1370–1507). They took greater pride in Timur, who captured Delhi in 1398, so they called themselves Timurids. "Mughal" = Persian corruption of "Mongol".

Why it sticks: pride goes to the ancestor who actually took Delhi.

Why Babur 26-30 turned to India

Memory hook

Babur 26-30's road: 83, 04, 12, 19, 26.

Born 83; Kabul 04 (poor); Fergana lost 12; attacks start 19; Panipat 26.

Decode: 1483 born in Fergana; 1504 captured Kabul (economically poor); 1512 Central Asian ambitions collapse; 1519 begins attacking the Delhi Sultanate with Ottoman gunners; 1526 First Panipat.

Why it sticks: after Kabul 04, the gaps are +8, +7, +7 (04 → 12 → 19 → 26).

First Battle of Panipat (1526)

Memory hook

First Panipat 1526: small gun, big result.

"A dozen thousand men, but bronze guns go BOOM: Lodi falls, a Kingdom is born."

Decode: Babur 26-30 had about 12,000 men and small bronze cannons (Ottoman gunners) against Ibrahim Lodi, last of the Lodis (1451–1526). The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526) ended. Tech power beat numbers.

Why it sticks: Babur 26-30 = Kingdom (K), Akbar 56-05 = Empire (E), Aurangzeb 58-07 = greatest Extent (E): "K-E-E".

Why three great battles happened at Panipat

Memory hook

Three Panipats: 26, 56, 61.

"Twenty-six, fifty-six, sixty-one: Babur 26-30's gun, Bairam's win, Kala Amb for the last one."

Decode: 1526 (First, Babur 26-30 beats Lodi), 1556 (Second, Bairam Khan beats Hemu), 1761 (Third; the Kala Amb memorial in Haryana is for this one, not 1526). The first two share "15"; the third jumps to 17.

Why it sticks: Kala Amb = the late one, 61.

Memory hook

Why Panipat? Three locks: K-T-Y.

"Khyber door, Thar-Himalaya tunnel, Yamuna wall."

Decode: close to the Khyber Pass (invasion route); a narrow corridor between the Thar Desert (south) and Himalayan foothills (north); the Yamuna to the east rules out flanking.

Why it sticks: the invader enters the door, walks the tunnel and hits the wall right at Panipat.

4 · Humayun 30-40 and Sher Shah 40-45 Suri 40-45

Memory hook

Humayun 30-40's battles go A-B-C: C, K, S.

Alphabetical = chronological: Chausa 1539 (lose), Kanauj 1540 (lose), Sirhind 1555 (win).

Decode: Chausa and Kanauj were his two defeats by Sher Shah 40-45; Sirhind was his win over the Suris with Safavid help. The Suri dynasty (1540–55) fills the gap between K and S.

Why it sticks: C, K, S never swap places, so the quiz order is just the alphabet. Two Ls then one W.

Sher Shah 40-45's three reforms

Memory hook

Sher Shah 40-45's three Rs.

"Records, Rupiya, Road."

Decode: Records = Patta + Qabuliyat (revenue; precursor to Akbar 56-05's Zabti). Rupiya = silver coin of fixed weight and purity. Road = Grand Trunk Road, Peshawar to Sonargaon (Bengal).

Why it sticks: "P to S" for the road, Peshawar to Sonargaon. He ruled the Suri dynasty only 1540–55, fifteen years.

Memory hook

Patta vs Qabuliyat: "Qabool hai!"

The State hands over the Patta; the ryot says "Qabool hai" and signs the Qabuliyat.

Decode: Patta = title deed from State to ryot (his field, his name, the LR to pay; a check on over-extraction). Qabuliyat = acceptance deed signed by the ryot and given back, promising to pay the LR on the Patta. Sher Shah 40-45 was first to make it two-sided.

Why it sticks: like a wedding, both sides must say yes.

Humayun 30-40 returns, with Safavid help

Memory hook

Persia's bill: Shirt, Key, Poem (S-K-P).

Humayun 30-40 pays for Safavid help with a Shirt, a Key and a Poem.

Decode: Shirt = adopt Shia forms; Key = cede Kandahar to Iran; Poem = promote Persian culture (so an Irani group of Mansabdars follows). The help: 14,000 Iranian troops, win at Sirhind 1555.

Why it sticks: three things to hand over, one picture each.

5 · How the Mughal Kingdom was re-established (Panipat 1556)

Memory hook

1556: one year, three events, one code.

"Fifty-six: Humayun 30-40 falls (Jan), Hemu crowns himself, Bairam wins, and Akbar 56-05's code starts with 56."

Decode: Jan 1556 Humayun 30-40 falls from his library stairs; Hemu (from Rewari, ex-Suri commander) declares himself Samrat; Second Panipat 1556, Bairam Khan (guardian of 13-year-old Akbar 56-05) defeats him.

Why it sticks: Akbar 56-05's code begins with the year of the battle, and "Bairam = Guardian" because the boy was only 13.

Rank: Zat + Sawar

Memory hook

Zat = Zaat (status), Sawar = Sawari (riders).

"Zat is who you are; Sawar is who rides for you."

Decode: Mansab = post/rank, Mansabdar = holder. Zat = personal rank (seniority, status). Sawar = number of horsemen to maintain. Net rank = Zat + Sawar (2,000 + 3,000 = 5,000).

Why it sticks: "Sawari" is the Hindi word for a ride, so Sawar = horse-riders.

Memory hook

Anshu = Army, Piyush = Paper.

Anshu (the emperor's favourite) is high on Sawar, the army; Piyush (the distrusted) is high on Zat, rank only.

Decode: the teacher's two outliers on the Zat-Sawar graph. Most nobles lie on the rising line (more Zat, more Sawar). Favourite = Anshu (high Sawar); distrusted = Piyush (high Zat).

Why it sticks: the trusted get the Army, the distrusted get Paper.

How Mansabdars were paid: Jagir, Khalisa, Inam

Memory hook

Four land types: J-K-I-M.

Jagir = Jagirdar's; Khalisa = King's; Inam = I'm-chosen gift; Mashrut = Mission bonus.

Decode: Jagir land: revenue to Mansabdars. Khalisa: royal land, revenue straight to the King, pays Naqdi Mansabdars. Inam: gifted land for a beneficiary (exceptional talent). Mashrut Jagir: a temporary top-up for a difficult assignment, revoked when it ends.

Why it sticks: J and K are the big two; I and M are the small extras.

Memory hook

Four fingers and a thumb: 4/5 Jagir, 1/5 Khalisa.

The Mansabdars hold four fingers of the revenue; the King holds only the thumb.

Decode: four-fifths of all LR came from Jagirs, only one-fifth from Khalisa land (teacher's 80 to 20). So the richest single person was still the King.

Why it sticks: the thumb is one finger, and the King is one person.

Three rules for Tankha Jagirs

Memory hook

Tankha Jagir rules: T-C-C.

"Tossed around, Capped, Confiscated."

Decode: Transferable (King can rotate it or turn the holder into a Naqdi Mansabdar); Capped (collect only the fixed LR, not a rupee more); Confiscated on death (Jagir and wealth both). Result: Mansabdars spent lavishly.

Why it sticks: a tenant who knows the house will be taken back spends everything on parties.

Watan Jagir

Memory hook

Tankha = Temporary, Watan = Waris (heir).

"Tankha is Taken back; Watan is Willed on."

Decode: Tankha Jagir = salary grant, rotated at will, confiscated on death; Jagirdar takes 100% of the LR. Watan = hereditary, permanent, not rotated; given to Rajput chiefs and submitted rulers; holder keeps 90% and pays 10% as Peshkash (tribute).

Why it sticks: Tankha's salary split (teacher's example) is 30 for self, 70 for the army: "thirty for me, seventy for my men".

What held the system together, and its fatal weakness

Memory hook

Patron gives, client pledges, ghosts appear.

King gives rank + Jagir; Mansabdar gives loyalty + army. If the King cannot deliver the desired Mansab and Jagir, loyalty melts, and half the army becomes a ghost.

Decode: patron-client bond; loyalty is personal, not institutional (to the King, not the State or law). Teacher's end state: on paper a 2 lakh army, in reality 1 lakh. After 1707, Mansabdars who did not get their desired posts turned disloyal.

Why it sticks: "2 on paper, 1 in reality" = half is a ghost army.

Who collected revenue, and how was it supervised

Memory hook

Revenue relay: write, file, assess, audit, collect.

The Patwari writes (village), the Qanungo files (pargana), the Amil assesses (pargana), the Amalguzar audits (sarkar), the Muqaddam collects (village).

Decode: Patwari = village ledger keeper, non-state, no vatan right. Qanungo = pargana, state official, hereditary keeper of old rates (has vatan right). Amil = pargana assessor; Amalguzar = supervises the Amil at sarkar level. Muqaddam = village headman who collects.

Why it sticks: P-Q alphabetical (record keepers), then A-A (assessors), and the Muqaddam closes at the bottom with the money.

Memory hook

Zamindar collects, Jagirdar consumes.

"Z Zips it up; J Just eats it."

Decode: Zamindar = right to collect LR (non-state actor, keeps about 10% Nankar / Malikana). Jagirdar = right to consume LR from a land (Mansabdar).

Why it sticks: the collector is the middleman; the consumer is the master.

The zamindar's position

Memory hook

The power ladder in six vehicles.

Sky to ground: Helicopter, Ferrari, Fortuner, Honda City, Luna, Legs.

Decode: King = helicopter; Jagirdar = Ferrari; Intermediary Zamindar = Fortuner; Primary Zamindar = Honda City; Khudkasht = cycle / Luna; Pahikasht = on foot.

Why it sticks: both Zamindars drive family cars you see on Indian roads; the top two are showpieces and the bottom two have no engine.

Memory hook

Primary 100-300, Intermediary 1000-3000: add a zero.

The Intermediary Zamindar is the Primary Zamindar with one more zero.

Decode: Primary Zamindar = smaller, 100 to 300 villages. Intermediary = big, 1000 to 3000 villages. Each keeps about 10% (Nankar / Malikana). Intermediaries pass Khalisa LR to the King and Jagir LR to Jagirdars.

Why it sticks: ten times the villages, ten times the status, but the same 10% commission rate.

Two kinds of ryot

Memory hook

Khudkasht = "khud ka khet"; Pahikasht = "paon-paon" wanderer.

The Khudkasht stays on his own field (occupancy, not ownership); the Pahikasht goes on foot from field to field.

Decode: Khudkasht = same land year after year, cannot be removed, holds a Patta, but cannot sell or mortgage (King owns all land). Pahikasht = different lands each season, no occupancy rights, landless and rightless (vagrant / Banjara).

Why it sticks: "khud" = self, and the teacher's own picture gives Khudkasht a cycle and Pahikasht his feet.

Revenue farming: Muqatai, Ijaradari, Farming System

Memory hook

Why Ijaradari arises: Need or Greed.

"Need runs dry, Greed runs wild."

Decode: Need = war, bad harvest, State's LR falling short. Greed = a Jagirdar who wants to maximise earnings, or a weak Mughal State. Both want more LR (↑LR).

Why it sticks: two words, one reason each: pressure from outside (Need), appetite from inside (Greed).

Memory hook

The painting auction: 1 crore in, 1 crore profit.

The right to collect revenue = the painting; the Ijaradar = Bhaskar, who buys at ₹1 crore to sell at ₹2 crore.

Decode: auction, money paid in advance, best price through competitive bidding. Ijaradar's motive is profit: the bid is the investment, and the LR he squeezes must cover bid plus profit. He farms revenue, not land.

Why it sticks: seller smiles (advance), buyer smiles (profit), the peasant is the canvas that gets squeezed.

Memory hook

Who auctions, how long: "J or K; 3 to 5, teacher 3-4."

The auctioneer is the Jagirdar (on Jagir land) or the King/State (on Khalisa land).

Decode: contract length is 3 to 5 years in the handout; the teacher writes 3-4. Learn "3 to 5 (teacher 3-4)": both start at 3. Bidders are merchants, moneylenders, bankers; the Zamindar temporarily loses his Watan right to collect.

Why it sticks: Jagir and Khalisa matches the letters J and K, just like the four land types.

Memory hook

Revolt needs a Leader and Followers: L + F.

Both unhappy = strong (a general with an army). Only the leader unhappy = none (a general with no army). Only followers unhappy = weak (an army with no general).

Decode: under Ijaradari the Zamindar (leader) lost his Watan right and earnings, and the ryots (followers) have no Patta: both are unhappy, so revolt is strongest. King/Jagirdar (advance, best price) and Ijaradar (profit) are the happy ones.

Why it sticks: the revolt is the product of two things; set either to zero and it fails.

Memory hook

Churaman 95-21 Jat: the code starts with 95, the revolt is in 1695.

"Ninety-five in his code, sixteen-ninety-five on the calendar."

Decode: a Zamindar-led peasant revolt (about 1695) over high LR that led to the Jat Kingdom.

Why it sticks: his code's first number is the revolt's year ending.

Memory hook

Zamindar plants a banyan; Ijaradar sells the timber.

The Zamindar's family lives with the peasants forever, so he caps himself at about 10%; the Ijaradar signs a short contract and strips everything.

Decode: Zamindar = hereditary, permanent, capped (Nankar / Malikana, about 10%). Ijaradar = auction winner, 3 to 5 years, no cap. Example: pays the State a fixed 1 lakh, extracts 3 lakh, keeps the 2 lakh profit.

Why it sticks: "1 in, 3 out, 2 kept" is the Ijaradar's arithmetic.

Memory hook

Zabti = Zameen survey; Ijaradari = auction, profit, no Patta.

"Zabti: Zameen is measured, Zamindar collects, Patta protects. Ijaradari: bid, bleed, no bill."

Decode: Zabti fixes LR by detailed survey, settles with the ryot (Patta), collection by the Zamindar for a State-mandated commission. Ijaradari fixes LR by auction, settles with the revenue farmer, collection by the Ijaradar for profit, and the peasant has no protection.

Why it sticks: survey vs auction is the single contrast that explains every other row.

The Ijaradari story across history: rise and decline

Memory hook

The door story: strong shuts it, weak opens it.

Akbar 56-05 slams it; Jahangir 05-27 leaves it ajar; Shahjahan 27-58 lets in only ruined land (banjar); Aurangzeb 58-07 opens the window (wars); Jahandar Shah 12-13 flings it open in Punjab and Bengal (fertile, richest); Farukhsiyar lets it into the King's Khalisa; Hastings 73-85 gives it a name (1772 Farming System).

Decode: before all that, the Delhi Sultanate began it as Muqatai. Rule: abolished when the State was strong, revived when it was weak. Successor states Awadh and Hyderabad continued it.

Why it sticks: the door opens a bit wider with each weaker ruler.

Memory hook

The floor stays at one-third; the ceiling climbs.

Akbar 56-05: 1/3. Shahjahan 27-58 and Aurangzeb 58-07: 1/3 to 1/2. Later Mughals: 1/3 to 2/3.

Decode: the lower figure never moves; only the top rises: third, half, two-thirds. Wars need money, so the demand rises. Always a share of gross produce (cost of cultivation was subtracted only by a later British reform).

Why it sticks: picture a lift: the floor button never changes, the top floor keeps going up.

Memory hook

Hastings 73-85: 72, 73, 74 on three steps.

1772 Farming System; 1773 Regulating Act makes him GG; 1774 he formally assumes office as GG.

Decode: Governor of Bengal Presidency from 1772; the Farming System (formalised revenue farming) = Ijaradari. His code 73-85 starts at the Regulating Act year.

Why it sticks: one step per year, 72-73-74.

8 · Akbar 56-05's religious vision: Sulh-i-Kul, Ibadat Khana, Mahzar, Din-i-Ilahi

Memory hook

Akbar 56-05's religion ladder: 75, 79, 82, then peace.

"I Must Do Sulh": Ibadat Khana 1575, Mahzar 1579, Din-i-Ilahi 1582, Sulh-i-Kul.

Decode: Ibadat Khana (Fatehpur Sikri, debate; goal: mid-path, less conflict) → +4 → Mahzar (Emperor is the final word on faith) → +3 → Din-i-Ilahi (syncretic religio-political order, not a movement) → Sulh-i-Kul (universal peace, equal respect for all faiths).

Why it sticks: the gaps are 4 then 3, and the last step has no year because it is the principle that stays.

9 · Who actually introduced Jizya in India?

Memory hook

Jizya: Qasim Started, Aibak Systematised.

"Qasim Started it (8th century, Sindh); Aibak made it a System (13th century, Delhi Sultanate)."

Decode: Muhammad bin Qasim first imposed Jizya in Sindh during the 8th-century Arab conquest. Qutb-ud-din Aibak 06-10 and successors institutionalised it from the early 13th century.

Why it sticks: "first imposed" and "institutionalised" are the two exam traps; Q is first in the line, A second.

Founder vs expanders, land vs coast.

Babur 26-30 = Built the base, Barren of coast. Akbar 56-05 Added coastal trade, Aurangzeb 58-07 Added more territory.

11 · The teacher's revision boards on the land revenue (LR) system (Lecture 4)

Memory hook

Admin ladder: Suba, Sarkar, Pargana (S-S-P).

"Sab Sarkari Paper": Province = Suba, District = Sarkar, Block = Pargana, then Villages.

Decode: Centre → Suba (province) → Sarkar (district) → Pargana (block) → Villages. Inside a Jagir the Zamindar has his Milkiyat and the Khudkashts sit on the rest.

Why it sticks: the S-S-P order matches the Amalguzar (sarkar) sitting above the Amil (pargana).

Memory hook

Field survey = P-R-L.

"Production, Rain, Land records."

Decode: (1) past trends of Production and LR, area-wise; (2) average Rainfall, droughts, floods and their frequency; (3) Land records (which ryot cultivates which field, field size, LR paid before). Kept by the Patwari (village) and Qanungo (block); used by the Amil and Amalguzar to assess.

Why it sticks: the three P-R-L boxes are what an assessor needs before naming a number.

Memory hook

Any tax system: A-S-C-D.

"A Smart Collector Deposits."

Decode: Assessment (how much) → Settlement (Patta with the ryot, Qabuliyat with the State, both parties) → Collection (mostly Zamindars, audited by Amil and Amalguzar) → Deposit (King's Khalisa or the Jagirdar Mansabdar).

Why it sticks: Settlement (the two documents) sits between the numbers and the cash, so nobody pays what was never agreed.

Memory hooks: chapter in one breath

Memory hook

The Mughal story in one breath.

Babur 26-30 comes through the Khyber with small bronze guns: Panipat 1526, a kingdom is born.

Humayun 30-40 loses at Chausa 39 and Kanauj 40 to Sher Shah 40-45 (Records, Rupiya, Road), flees, returns with Persian troops at Sirhind 55, and falls from his library stairs in Jan 1556.

Hemu crowns himself, but Bairam Khan wins Second Panipat 1556 for 13-year-old Akbar 56-05.

Akbar 56-05 builds the Mansabdari machine (Zat + Sawar, Jagir, Khalisa, Inam, Mashrut), runs revenue by Zabti (Patwari to Muqaddam, Patta and Qabuliyat, Zamindar 10%), and climbs his religion ladder 75, 79, 82, then Sulh-i-Kul.

But loyalty is personal: when the King cannot deliver the desired Mansab and Jagir, loyalty melts, the army turns ghost (2 lakh on paper, 1 lakh real), and weak rulers open the Ijaradari door, ending with Hastings in 1772.

Decode: every year and name above is in the chapter's own order: 1526 → 1539 → 1540 → 1555 → 1556 → 1575 → 1579 → 1582 → 1772.

Why it sticks: it is one family story: a conqueror, a runaway son, a boy emperor with a guardian, a machine, and its slow rot.

Chapter 2: The Decline of the Mughal State (1707 onwards) (34)open chapter

The five-part equation

Memory hook

Five parts of State power: P-E-M-S-T.

"People Eat Many Sweet Treats."

Decode: Political, Economic, Military, Social, Technological. Mughal decline hit all five at once.

Why it sticks: if you can taste five sweets at once, you can feel five powers weaken at once.

Two analytical tools (apply to every decline question)

Memory hook

Vertical drops, horizontal swaps.

"Vertical = Vacate downward; Horizontal = Hand over sideways."

Decode: Vertical = decentralisation: after 1707 power moved from the weak emperors down to the Mansabdars. Horizontal = replacement at the same level: Delhi Sultanate replaced by the Mughals at Panipat 1526; British super-power replaced by US-USSR at the end of WW2 (1945).

Why it sticks: use both lenses on every decline question: one for who lost power downward, one for who took the seat.

The teacher's components board (what each form of power means)

Memory hook

Military power's four parts: S-T-C-C.

"Sena, Takneek, Clean hands, Commander."

Decode: Size of the army; Technology; no military Corruption; strong military Commander (leadership). Teacher's examples: 1945, USSR had the largest army; 1945, atomic bomb (USA) = technology.

Why it sticks: two numbers-and-guns items (Sena, Takneek), two people items (Clean, Commander); the Mughals later failed all four.

The teacher's list of factors

Memory hook

Six factors: "A Weak Mughal Is Just Lost".
