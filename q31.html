1999PYQ 1(Prelims 1999)

The most short lived of all of Britain's constitutional experiments in India was the

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — ICA1909 had the shortest life of the constitutional experiments: in force only from 1909 until the Government of India Act 1919 replaced it — barely a decade, against ICA 1861's thirty-one years, ICA 1892's seventeen, and GOI 1919's sixteen.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — ICA 1861 was the LONGEST-lived of the series — three decades passed before ICA 1892 replaced it.

(B) ✗ — ICA 1892 ran seventeen years (1892–1909) — longer than its successor; the shortest span belongs to ICA1909.

(D) ✗ — GOI 1919 operated for sixteen years before the Government of India Act 1935 superseded it — well beyond ICA1909's single decade. (Note: The Indian Councils Act served as the governance structure of India, famously known as 'Minto Morley Reforms' was the most short lived.)

(Note: A London branch of the All India Muslim League was established in 1908 under the presidency of Ameer Ali to put pressure on British Government for Muslim's educational cause.)

FYQ 1

Who led the Simla Deputation of 1906 that met Viceroy Lord Minto to place a memorandum of Muslim political demands?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — In 1906, the elite Muslim delegation that met Viceroy Minto at Shimla was led by the Aga Khan; this deputation demanded no annulment of the Bengal Partition, population-proportional reservation in councils and jobs, and separate electorates in future council reforms.

Why others wrong: (a) Nawab Salimullah hosted the founding session of the Muslim League at Dacca in December 1906, he did not lead the Shimla delegation; (c) Sir Syed Ahmed Khan had died in 1898, well before the Deputation; (d) Mohsin-ul-Mulk drafted the Muslim League's constitution but did not head the Simla delegation.

Memory hook: Aga at Shimla, Salimullah at Dacca — two stops, one separatist road.

FYQ 2

Who was the first Indian to be inducted into the Viceroy's Executive Council under the Indian Councils Act, 1909?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — Satyendra Prasanna Sinha (S.P. Sinha) was inducted into the Viceroy's Executive Council as Law Member, becoming the first Indian on the VEC under the 1909 Act.

Why others wrong: (b) K.G. Gupta was appointed to the Secretary of State's India Council in London in August 1907 (a precursor action, not a 1909 Act provision, and not on the VEC); (c) Syed Hussain Bilgrami likewise went to the India Council in London in August 1907, not the VEC; (d) Romesh Chandra Dutt is not mentioned by the record in the 1909 induction.

Memory hook: Sinha sits at the Viceroy's table; Gupta and Bilgrami sit in London.

FYQ 3

Which Act introduced separate electorates for Muslims in India for the first time?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — The Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms) introduced separate electorates for Muslims for the first time, in the Imperial Legislative Council, the Provincial Legislative Councils, and local bodies.

Why others wrong: (a) ICA 1861 dealt with the addition of non-official members and provincial legislative councils, not communal electorates; (b) ICA 1892 used the word 'selection' and allowed discussion of the budget but introduced no separate electorates; (d) GOI Act 1919 extended the principle but did not introduce it for the first time.

Memory hook: 1909 plants the seed, 1947 reaps the partition.

FYQ 4

The All India Muslim League was founded in December 1906 at which city?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(D) ✓ — The All India Muslim League was set up by elite Muslims at Dacca in December 1906, immediately following the Simla Deputation, with Mohsin-ul-Mulk authoring its constitution.

Why others wrong: (a) Lahore is associated with the League's later 1940 Resolution, not its founding; (b) Lucknow is linked to the 1916 Lucknow Pact, not the League's founding; (c) Aligarh was the seat of the Mohammadan Anglo Oriental College set up in 1875 but not where the League was founded.

Memory hook: Dacca delivered the League; Lahore later demanded Pakistan.

FYQ 5

Who founded the Mohammadan Anglo Oriental College in 1875?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — Sir Syed Ahmed Khan (1817–98) set up the Mohammadan Anglo Oriental College in 1875 (later Aligarh Muslim University) to combine Western education with Islamic values and build a Muslim middle class able to compete for government positions.

Why others wrong: (a) Mohsin-ul-Mulk wrote the Muslim League's 1906 constitution but did not found the MAO College; (c) Nawab Wakar-ul-Mulk is not credited with founding the College; (d) Ameer Ali was associated with the Muslim League's London branch in 1908, not with founding the College.

Memory hook: Sir Syed builds Aligarh's bricks; Mohsin-ul-Mulk later drafts the League's rules.

FYQ 6

Who was the Secretary of State for India whose name is attached to the reforms enacted by the Indian Councils Act, 1909?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — John Morley was the Secretary of State for India, sitting in London, when the Indian Councils Act, 1909 was enacted; together with Viceroy Lord Minto (1905–1910), his name became attached to what is formally called the Indian Councils Act, 1909.

Why others wrong: (b) Lord Minto was the Viceroy in India, not the Secretary of State in London; (c) Lord Curzon was the Viceroy who carried out the 1905 Bengal Partition, not the Secretary of State of 1909; (d) Lord Hardinge succeeded Minto as Viceroy and is not associated with the 1909 reforms.

Memory hook: Morley in London, Minto in India — Liberal pen, viceregal seal.

FYQ 7

Consider the following statements about the Indian Councils Act, 1909: 1. It introduced separate electorates for Muslims in the Imperial Legislative Council. 2. It introduced a non-official majority in the Imperial Legislative Council. 3. The word 'election' was used officially for the first time in this Act. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — Statements 1 and 3 are correct. The Act introduced separate electorates for Muslims in the ILC, PLCs and local bodies; the word 'election' was used officially for the first time in ICA 1909. Statement 2 is wrong because the ILC retained an official majority (37 officials vs 32 non-officials); non-official majority appeared only in the PLCs.

Why others wrong: (a) wrongly includes Statement 2 — official majority continued in the ILC;

(b) wrongly includes Statement 2 and drops Statement 1; (d) wrongly accepts Statement 2.

Memory hook: ILC keeps officers in charge; PLCs let non-officials breathe.

FYQ 8

With reference to the Indian Councils Act, 1909, consider the following statements: 1. Members could vote on individual items of the Budget but could not vote on the Budget as a whole. 2. Members could ask supplementary questions, with the supplementary restricted to the member who had asked the original question. 3. Resolutions moved by members were binding on the government. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(D) ✓ — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Voting was allowed on individual budget items but not on the budget as a whole (this would have amounted to a No Confidence Motion); supplementary questions were allowed but only by the original questioner. Statement 3 is wrong — resolutions could be initiated and passed but were recommendatory only, not binding on the government.

Why others wrong: (a) wrongly treats resolutions as binding; (b) wrongly treats resolutions as binding and drops the budget point; (c) wrongly treats resolutions as binding.

Memory hook: Vote a line, ask a follow-up, suggest a resolution — but the Executive may yawn.

FYQ 9

Consider the following statements regarding the Simla Deputation of 1906: 1. The delegation demanded that the Partition of Bengal should not be annulled. 2. The delegation demanded separate electorates for Muslims in future council reforms. 3. The delegation demanded full Responsible Government for India. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The Shimla Deputation asked for no annulment of the Bengal Partition, reservation proportional to population in the ILC, PLCs and government jobs, and separate electorates in future council reforms. Statement 3 is wrong — the Deputation sought elite Muslim safeguards, not Responsible Government.

Why others wrong: (b) wrongly attributes a Responsible-Government demand to the Deputation; (c) wrongly attributes a Responsible-Government demand; (d) same error as (b) and (c).

Memory hook: Keep the Partition, give us the seats, fence the votes — that was Shimla's ask.

FYQ 10

Consider the following statements about the All India Muslim League founded in 1906: 1. Its constitution was written by Mohsin-ul-Mulk of the Aligarh movement. 2. A London branch of the League was set up in 1908. 3. It was established with the goal of promoting Muslim loyalty to the British and securing the promises of the Simla Deputation. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — All three statements are correct. The League's constitution was drafted by Mohsin-ul-Mulk of the Aligarh movement; a London branch was set up in 1908; and its stated goals were to ensure the British did not retreat from the Simla Deputation promises, to promote Muslim interests, and to promote Muslim loyalty to the British.

Why others wrong: (a) drops the correct loyalty/Simla goal in Statement 3; (c) drops Mohsin-ul-Mulk's authorship; (d) drops the London branch fact.

Memory hook: Mohsin pens it, Dacca launches it, London branches it — loyalty all the way.

FYQ 11

Consider the following statements about Sir Syed Ahmed Khan: 1. He argued that India was a summation of qaums and that the Hindu and Muslim qaums had separate interests. 2. He founded the Mohammadan Educational Congress in 1886 as a rival to the Indian National Congress. 3. He actively encouraged Muslim participation in the Indian National Congress. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Sir Syed argued that India was a summation of qaums with the Hindu and Muslim qaums forming separate communities with separate interests (the early form of the Two Nation Theory), and he set up the Mohammadan Educational Congress in 1886 as a rival to the INC. Statement 3 is wrong — he actively discouraged Muslim participation in the INC, which he believed was a Hindu organisation.

Why others wrong: (a) wrongly says he encouraged INC participation; (b) wrongly says he encouraged INC participation and drops the qaum framing; (d) wrongly accepts Statement 3.

Memory hook: Two qaums, one rival Congress — Sir Syed's three-line manifesto.

FYQ 12

The Indian Councils Act, 1909 is often described as the shortest-lived among Britain's council reforms in India primarily because:

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — ICA 1909 lasted only 10 years — the shortest of all council reforms — because it satisfied no one. Moderates wanted real legislative power and got a toothless body; the INC's stated goal since 1906 was Swaraj, and the Act fell far short; Extremists had never cared for council reforms; and the grant of separate electorates planted long-term communal trouble.

Why others wrong: (b) factually wrong — the Act did not transfer executive power to elected Indian ministers; the Executive remained non-responsible; (c) factually wrong — the ILC was expanded, not abolished; (d) factually wrong — the Lucknow Pact was a Congress–League agreement, not a constitutional repeal.

Memory hook: Pleased nobody, planted partition — ten years and out.

FYQ 13

The British grant of separate electorates to Muslims under the Indian Councils Act, 1909 is regarded as the legislative seed of Partition because:

Answer: (D)

(D)

(D) ✓ — Once it was institutionally accepted that Muslims must elect Muslim representatives from Muslim-only constituencies, the Two Nation Theory had been accepted in legislation; the 1909 grant of separate electorates therefore became the legislative seed of 1947 — Minto came to be called the Father of Communal Electorate.

Why others wrong: (a) wrong — 8 of 27 elected ILC seats (about 30%) were reserved for Muslims, not a numerical majority of the ILC; (b) wrong — joint electorates continued for unreserved seats; Muslims could also vote in those; (c) wrong — the partitioning of Bengal happened in 1905 under Curzon, separate from ICA 1909.

Memory hook: Separate seat, separate ballot, separate nation — the chain that broke India.
