2010PYQ 3Prelims 2010

What was the immediate reason for Ahmad Shah Abdali to invade India and fight the Third Battle of Panipat?

Answer: (A)

(a) ✓ — The immediate provocation was the Maratha thrust into the north-west: the Marathas had seized Lahore and driven out Abdali's son and viceroy Timur Shah from the Punjab, so Abdali invaded to recover the province and avenge that expulsion — the campaign that culminated in the Third Battle of Panipat (1761). (b) ✗ — Adina Beg Khan invited the Marathas (not Abdali) into the Punjab against the Afghans; the option inverts who was invited. (c) ✗ — Mughal non-payment of tribute was not the trigger; Mughal authority was by then nominal and the real contest was Maratha versus Afghan. (d) ✗ — A wish to annex the Punjab plains is a general ambition, not the immediate reason, which was the specific expulsion of Timur Shah.

FYQ 13

Consider the following statements regarding why the Marathas lost (4-factor diagnostic): 1. Factionalism at every stage — Surat vs Purandhar treaties of 1775–76; Bajirao II96-18's flight to Bassein 1802; Holkar–Scindhia split during the 2nd war; united too late in the 3rd. 2. Absence of unified command — Scindhia, Holkar, Bhonsle and Gaikwad of Baroda never coordinated effectively. 3. The Marathas had military superiority over the EIC throughout the three wars; their defeat came purely from political betrayal. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (A)

(a) 1 and 2 only:

Statement 1 ✓ — factionalism at every stage (Surat-Purandhar 1775-76 + Bassein 1802 + Holkar-Scindhia 2nd war split + united too late in 3rd).

Statement 2 ✓ — absence of unified command among Scindhia + Holkar + Bhonsle + Gaikwad of Baroda.

Statement 3 ✗ — opposite of chapter analytical thesis ('Why Did the Marathas Lose?' ◆ Takeaway). The Marathas had strong individual military (Pollilur-style at the unit level + Battle of Assaye reputation) but political organisational gaps. Their defeat was political (factionalism + no unified command + EIC financial depth + naval/logistics gap), NOT a "purely political betrayal" of an otherwise-superior military force. The chapter explicitly says: "Mysore fought well and modernised seriously. It lost because it fought largely alone" — same diagnostic applies to Marathas.

FYQ 1 Why did the Marathas lose the three Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775–82, 1803–05, 1817–19) despite their numerical strength? 150 words · 10 marks "The Maratha Confederacy was the largest indigenous power in India at the start of the Anglo-Maratha Wars. Yet by 1819 it was completely broken. Examine the reasons."

Demand of the question: Show why the Marathas’ numerical and territorial advantage failed against the EIC. Cover structural, leadership, and tactical reasons.

Suggested intro (~25 words): The Marathas were militarily strong but politically fragmented. The EIC exploited their internal disunity, financial weakness, and tactical inferiority to win all three wars.

Guiding themes (pick 4-5 for a 150-word answer):

1. Internal disunity — the Maratha Confederacy was nominally led by the Peshwa, but real power was split among four Sardars (Scindia, Holkar, Bhonsle, Gaekwad). EIC isolated them one at a time (see 'Why Did the Marathas Lose?').

2. Peshwa Bajirao II96-18’s blunder — signed the Treaty of Bassein (December 1802) after losing to Holkar; this sold out the Confederacy and triggered the 2nd Anglo-Maratha War (see '2nd Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05) — Wellesley98-05's War').

3. Tactical inferiority — the Marathas relied on heavy cavalry; EIC infantry squares + artillery (Wellesley98-05 at Assaye 1803) outclassed them on the field (see '2nd Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05) — Wellesley98-05's War').

4. Financial weakness — Maratha treasury was small relative to EIC’s Bengal revenue base; sustained war drained them faster than they could replenish (see 'Why Did the Marathas Lose?').

5. Pindari menace — the Pindaris (irregular raiders attached to Maratha Sardars) gave the EIC a pretext for the 3rd Anglo-Maratha War; Francis Hastings13-23’s paramountcy policy converted the war into final destruction of Maratha sovereignty (see 'The Pindaris, Francis Hastings 13-23 (1813-23) & his Policy of Paramountcy (1813-58)' and '3rd Anglo-Maratha War + Pindari War (1817–19)' and 'Consequences of 3rd Anglo-Maratha War (1817-19)').

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): Maratha defeat was political, not military. The EIC won because the Marathas were not one polity but many quarrelling ones.

Revise: '2nd Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05) — Wellesley98-05's War' + '2nd Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05) — Wellesley98-05's War' and 'The Pindaris, Francis Hastings 13-23 (1813-23) & his Policy of Paramountcy (1813-58)' (The Pindaris, Francis Hastings 13-23 (1813-23) & his Policy of Paramountcy (1813-58)) + '3rd Anglo-Maratha War + Pindari War (1817–19)' and 'Consequences of 3rd Anglo-Maratha War (1817-19)' + 'Why Did the Marathas Lose?'.

FYQ 2 Compare Francis Hastings13-23’s Policy of Paramountcy (1813–58) with Wellesley98-05’s Subsidiary Alliance — how did the EIC’s frontier doctrine evolve? 150 words · 10 marks "Wellesley98-05’s Subsidiary Alliance and Francis Hastings13-23’s Policy of Paramountcy were both designed to extend British control over Indian States. Compare the two doctrines and explain how the EIC’s frontier policy evolved."

Demand of the question: Show what each doctrine did + how Paramountcy extended/replaced SA. Conclude with the trajectory: SA → Paramountcy → direct annexation.

Suggested intro (~25 words): SA promised protection; Paramountcy demanded subordination. Each phase of EIC policy made Indian States more dependent and the EIC more sovereign.

Guiding themes (pick 4-5 for a 150-word answer):

1. Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley98-05, 1798–1805) — Indian ruler surrendered foreign policy + external defence; EIC stationed troops paid by the ruler ('2nd Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05) — Wellesley98-05's War' background; Ch 11 detail).

2. Policy of Paramountcy (Francis Hastings13-23, 1813–58) — EIC was declared the paramount power over ALL Indian States; principalities now had to accept EIC supremacy in both internal and external matters (see 'The Pindaris, Francis Hastings 13-23 (1813-23) & his Policy of Paramountcy (1813-58)').

3. Pindari pretext — Francis Hastings used the Pindari raids as justification to break Maratha sovereignty in the 3rd Anglo-Maratha War (1817–19); annexation and direct control followed (see '3rd Anglo-Maratha War + Pindari War (1817–19)' and 'Consequences of 3rd Anglo-Maratha War (1817-19)').

4. EIC Army overhyping — 'Wars due to EIC Army overhyping threat to borders' notes that the EIC Army repeatedly inflated border threats to justify wars; this was the structural pattern across multiple decades (see 'Wars due to EIC Army overhyping threat to borders').

5. Trajectory — SA (soft control) → Paramountcy (declared supremacy) → Doctrine of Lapse (Dalhousie48-56, outright annexation on heirless succession). Each step removed more Indian sovereignty.

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): SA was the bait; Paramountcy was the trap; Lapse was the kill. The EIC’s frontier doctrine ratcheted up through three phases.

Revise: '2nd Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05) — Wellesley98-05's War' and 'The Pindaris, Francis Hastings 13-23 (1813-23) & his Policy of Paramountcy (1813-58)' + '3rd Anglo-Maratha War + Pindari War (1817–19)' and 'Consequences of 3rd Anglo-Maratha War (1817-19)' + 'Wars due to EIC Army overhyping threat to borders'.
