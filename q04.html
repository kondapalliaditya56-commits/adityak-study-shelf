2017PYQ 1 Fragmented polity in mid-18th centuryMains 2017 · 10 marks · 150 words

"Clarify how mid-eighteenth-century India was beset with the spectre of a fragmented polity."

Demand of the question: Show how India was a fragmented polity by the mid-eighteenth century. The verb " clarify " means make the case clearly with evidence — give multiple regional examples. The phrase " spectre of a fragmented polity " tells you the question accepts the premise as a recognised condition; your job is to show how, not to debate whether it was fragmented.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Mid-eighteenth-century India was indeed beset by the spectre of a fragmented polity — Mughal authority had broken apart into many separate regional powers across the subcontinent.

Guiding themes (pick 4–5 for a 150-word answer):

1. Successor States — Bengal, Awadh, Hyderabad (see Chapter 3, Successor States).

2. Maratha Confederacy — top regional power by mid-18C — empire was a confederacy (Peshwa + four Sardars: Gaikwad, Holkar, Scindia, Bhonsle), not a unified empire.

3. Jats + Afghans — three rebel states — Bharatpur + Farrukhabad + Rohilkhand (see Chapter 5 — 'The Jat Kingdom (1695-1763)' + 'Farrukhabad (est. 1714)' + 'Rohilkhand (est. 1737–40)').

4. Sikh State — Banda Bahadur08-15 → Misl Confederacy → Ranjit Singh01-39 (see Chapter 6 — 'How did Banda Bahadur08-15 establish the first Sikh State — and why did it collapse?' + 'What kept the Sikh State alive after Banda Bahadur08-15's defeat?' + 'How did Ranjit Singh01-39 unify the Misls and build a powerful Sikh State?' + 'What were the terms of the 1809 Treaty of Amritsar with the EIC?').

5. Mysore + Travancore + Rajputs (see Chapter 7 — 'Akbar56-05 and Rajputs' through 'What made Tipu82-99 a different kind of ruler — and why did he still lose?').

6. Post-Panipat 1761 power vacuum.

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): The fragmented polity that haunted mid-eighteenth-century India was already a settled reality — the EIC's later expansion only used this break-up; it did not cause it.

2014PYQ 2 Why were so many empire-shaking battles fought at Panipat?Mains 2014 · 10 marks · 150 words

“The third battle of Panipat was fought in 1761. Why were so many empire-shaking battles fought at Panipat?”

Full answer: see 'Wars, Treaties & Territories — through to the 3rd Battle of Panipat 1761 & beyond' — the ⚠ Alert: Mains 2014 sub-section directly carries the full Mains-grade answer.

Key themes to highlight:

1. Geography — Delhi gateway: any power advancing from Central Asia / Afghanistan towards Delhi had to come through the Khyber Pass → the Panipat–Karnal–Kurukshetra–Thanesar staging belt → the Yamuna–Ganga doab.

2. Three empire-deciding battles: 1st Panipat 1526 (Babur26-30 vs Ibrahim Lodi → birth of Mughal Empire); 2nd Panipat 1556 (Akbar56-05 vs Hemu → consolidation of Mughal rule); 3rd Panipat 1761 (Abdali vs Marathas → peak-and-break of Maratha imperial expansion).

3. Terrain and logistics: flat plains suited large-army manoeuvring; fertile doab supported prolonged campaigns; martial-recruitment base; proximity to Delhi made it the natural staging ground before any final push to the throne.

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): The empire-shaking battles fought at Panipat — 1526, 1556, 1761 — happened because controlling Panipat meant controlling the Delhi throne.

FYQ 5

Answer: (A)

(a) Svarajya = direct rule + culturally homogeneous original kingdom inside Maharashtra; Samrajya = territory added beyond Maharashtra from 1719 onwards, governed by Maratha Sardars. ✓ — 'Svarajya vs Samrajya': Svarajya — the original kingdom inside Maharashtra, capital Satara, Peshwa's headquarters at Poona — was under direct rule and culturally homogeneous. FYQ 1 Shivaji74-80’s Integrated Land-and-Sea Military System 150 words · 10 marks "Shivaji74-80 built an integrated military system combining light cavalry, navy and forts. Examine how the three parts worked together, and why the system weakened after his death."

Demand of the question: Show how cavalry + navy + forts worked together as one system + explain why it weakened after Shivaji74-80.

Suggested intro (~25 words): "Shivaji74-80 did not build a strong army alone. He built three parts — light cavalry, navy and forts — that worked together as one system."

Guiding themes:

1. Light cavalry + guerrilla warfare — fast-moving, low-cost; used the Western Ghats land for surprise attacks.

2. Saranjami land grants — paid soldiers through land instead of cash; kept the cavalry self-funded.

3. Navy + Jaladurg fort doctrine — sea forts (Jaladurg) stopped Portuguese, Siddi and Mughal naval pressure on the Konkan coast. Shivaji74-80 is called the Father of the Modern Indian Navy.

4. After Shivaji74-80 — the three parts split: the navy stayed alive under Admiral Kanhoji Angre but was no longer linked to a central army; the land army drifted under the Sardars.

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): "The system worked while Shivaji74-80 held the centre. Once the centre weakened, the three parts ran on their own — strong in pieces, weak as a whole."

Revise: 'Military Tactics — Army, Navy, Forts' + 'Military Tactics — Army, Navy, Forts' + 'Military Tactics — Army, Navy, Forts'.

FYQ 2 Maratha Confederacy: The Same Features That Built It Also Broke It at Panipat 1761 150 words · 10 marks "The structural features that made the Maratha Confederacy the leading power in mid-18th-century India also led to its collapse at Panipat 1761. Discuss."

Demand of the question: Show the same Confederacy features (Peshwa + 4 Sardars + Chauth) acting as strengths in expansion + weaknesses at Panipat.

Suggested intro (~25 words): "The Maratha Confederacy was a power-sharing model — a Peshwa at the centre plus four Sardars. The same model that built the empire also broke it at Panipat 1761."

Guiding themes:

1. Peshwa + four Sardars (Gaikwad, Holkar, Scindia, Bhonsle) — a working power-sharing model that let the Marathas push north fast in the 1740s-50s.

2. Chauth + Sardeshmukhi — a tax system that funded expansion without holding territory. Light footprint, fast spread.

3. The same features break at Panipat 1761 — the Sardars who built the empire did not coordinate well under Sadashivrao Bhau; Surajmal (Jats) withdrew militarily, supply lines failed, Abdali’s horse-mounted guns broke Maratha armor.

4. After Panipat — the Sardars went their own way; Maratha rule continued in pieces but never as a unified all-India power.

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): "The Confederacy worked as long as the Peshwa held the centre. Panipat 1761 showed what happened when the centre asked too much of the Sardars at once."
