September 2026#

6.1.1 · UK recognises India's CCTS under UK CBAM#

Prelims + MainsEnvironment

The UK recognised India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme as a qualifying overseas carbon-pricing scheme under its Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism.

  • UK CBAM applies from 1 January 2027 to carbon-intensive imports (iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen); charge based on embedded emissions; carbon price paid in the exporting country can be credited.
  • CCTS: market-based mechanism creating the Indian Carbon Market with tradable Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs); notified June 2023 under the Energy Conservation Act 2001 as amended in 2022; targets emission intensity in energy-intensive industries and India's NDC.
  • Institutions: Central Government; National Steering Committee (NSCICM); BEE as administrator (rules, targets, accreditation, issues CCCs); Grid India (registry); CERC (regulates trading on power exchanges); Accredited Carbon Verification Agencies. Obligated entities meet targets or buy CCCs; offset mechanism lets non-obligated projects earn CCCs.
  • Challenges: relief conditional on proof and verification; only carbon price actually paid counts (not free allowances, rebates); compliance burden on MSMEs; UK can review recognition. Way forward: stronger MRV, credible price signal, MSME support, policy stability.
  • India's first soil carbon payments (September 2026): over Rs 2.9 crore by DBT to 2,550 small farmers in Punjab and Haryana; Aadi Programme (Grow Indigo since 2019, ICAR support); Verra VM0042 methodology.

  • Keywords: CBAM; Carbon pricing; MRV

6.1.2 · UNEP: 1.5°C overshoot unavoidable#

Prelims + MainsEnvironment

UNEP 2026 report "Limiting Overshoot: Navigating Exceedance of 1.5°C and Pathways Towards Return" says long-term warming will likely cross 1.5°C within a few years.

  • Overshoot = long-term warming rises above 1.5°C, peaks, then returns to or below it; assessed over multi-decadal average, not a single year.
  • Even full implementation of current national plans plus net-zero pledges gives peak near 1.8°C; current policies about 2.6°C by 2100. 1.5°C remains the goal; focus on limiting size and duration of overshoot.
  • Dangers: no safe overshoot; glaciers could lose over a quarter of mass by 2100; tipping-point risk (Greenland, West Antarctic, AMOC, Amazon, coral reefs); food output could fall up to 14% by 2050; some damage persists after cooling.
  • Response: rapid cuts in CO2, methane and short-lived pollutants; carbon dioxide removal; adaptation; equity concerns.
Prelims fodder
  • UNEP: established 15 December 1972 (UNGA Res. 2997) after Stockholm Conference; HQ Nairobi; governing body UNEA; reports: Emissions Gap, GEO, Adaptation Gap, Frontiers, Food Waste Index.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: carbon dioxide removal, climate tipping points, mitigation-adaptation nexus, net-negative CO2 emissions.

6.1.3 · World Ozone Day 2026#

PrelimsEnvironment

16 September; theme "Global Action for a Cooler Planet"; 10 years of the Kigali Amendment. India launched the Net Zero Portal and the NAPCC Dashboard.

  • Vienna Convention and Montreal Protocol were the first UN treaties with universal ratification (2009); about 99% of controlled ODS phased out. Kigali Amendment (2016) phases down HFCs, avoiding about 0.3-0.5°C by 2100.
  • India: party since 1992; phased out CFCs, CTC, halons for controlled uses; met 2015, 2020, 2025 HCFC targets; ratified Kigali September 2021; HFC phase-down from 2032 to 85% reduction by 2047.

Prelims PYQ on control and phasing out of ozone-depleting substances (Montreal Protocol).

6.2.1 · El Niño 2026-27 and the Niño regions#

PrelimsEnvironment

WMO warned of a very strong El Niño persisting into early 2027.

  • Niño 1+2 (0-10°S, 90-80°W); Niño 3 (5°N-5°S, 150-90°W); Niño 3.4 (5°N-5°S, 170-120°W) key for ENSO; Niño 4 (5°N-5°S, 160°E-150°W).
  • ONI uses 3-month mean in Niño 3.4; El Niño when at or above +0.5°C for five overlapping periods. RONI adjusts for tropical ocean warming (20°N-20°S); NOAA CPC adopted it from February 2026.
  • Intensity: weak +0.5 to <1.0; moderate 1.0 to <1.5; strong 1.5 to <2.0; very strong 2.0 or more. "Super El Niño" is informal.

6.2.2 · Hanging glaciers#

PrelimsEnvironment

After the August 2026 Nepal glacier collapse, an April 2026 study found 219 hanging glaciers in Uttarakhand's Alaknanda basin.

  • Unstable ice masses on steep slopes; warming changes velocity and stability; ice-rock avalanches can trigger river blockage, flash floods, GLOFs.

6.3.1 · Wildlife trafficking: India as destination#

Prelims + MainsEnvironment

Five infant orangutans found in Odisha suggest India is becoming a destination, not just source or transit.

  • Trends: exotic pets (orangutans, gibbons, lemurs, marmosets); routes via Myanmar and Bangladesh land borders, Thailand and Malaysia by air, sea routes emerging; social media demand; links to narcotics, arms, hawala; urban demand (macaws, Amazon parrots).
  • Orangutans: Borneo and Sumatra only; three species (Bornean, Sumatran, Tapanuli); largest arboreal mammals, about 97% genes shared with humans; frugivores; tool use; all Critically Endangered (IUCN); CITES Appendix I.
  • Law: Wild Life (Protection) Act 1972 as amended 2022; Schedule IV covers CITES species; Sec 49M registration; Living Animal Species (Reporting and Registration) Rules 2024; PARIVESH 2.0 portal.
  • Gaps: investigations stop at seizure, adaptive routes, weak traceability. Way forward: network-based investigation, cross-border cooperation, digital surveillance, DNA forensics, demand reduction.
  • Kathmandu Declaration 2026 (SAWEN): intelligence sharing, financial investigation, cyber-enabled trade, biopiracy.
PYQ connect

2017 Prelims: TRAFFIC (mission correct; it is not a UNEP bureau - joint WWF/IUCN). 2023 Prelims: orangutan uses a stick tool for insects.

6.4.1 · Soft coral slumping (Jeju)#

PrelimsEnvironment

Widespread slumping seen again around Jeju Island.

  • Slumping = loss of internal fluid pressure so colonies droop; physical collapse, unlike bleaching (loss of symbiotic algae). Causes: marine heat, typhoons, salinity changes (Yangtze outflow), compound stress.

6.4.2 · Robots vs water hyacinth in Vembanad; NPCA#

PrelimsEnvironment

Kumarakom one-month pilot of robots to remove water hyacinth and convert it into products under NPCA.

  • NPCA: Centrally Sponsored Scheme for wetlands; Integrated Management Plans; State/UT Wetlands Authorities implement, MoEFCC guides; National Wetlands Committee (advisory) and Technical Appraisal Committee.
  • Water hyacinth (Eichhornia crassipes): South American, introduced under British rule for flowers; chokes waterbodies; uses in biofertiliser, handicrafts, phytoremediation.

6.4.3 · MEE-TR 2026 (tiger reserves)#

PrelimsEnvironment

NTCA with Wildlife Institute of India launched the 6th cycle of Management Effectiveness Evaluation for all 58 tiger reserves.

  • IUCN-WCPA framework: Context, Planning, Inputs, Processes, Outputs, Outcomes. New criterion: solid waste management. Independent multidisciplinary teams.
  • MEE-TR (every 4 years) evaluates management; AITE (quadrennial) counts tigers and density; M-STrIPES is the digital daily-patrol data tool.

6.5.1 · India's green steel transition; steel slag roads#

Prelims + MainsEnvironment

Proposed Rs 5,000-crore support scheme, green steel certification and hydrogen pilots; capacity targets 300 MT by 2030-31 and 400 MT by 2035-36.

  • Steel emissions are 32% above global average, nearly 12% of India's GHG; about 160 MT produced in FY 2025-26; coal dependence; CBAM pressure.
  • Steel slag road: Guinness record for longest road with processed steel slag aggregates, 1.85 km at Raigarh (CSIR-CRRI with Jindal Steel); claims 40% lower cost, up to 4x strength, major maintenance after about 15 years.
  • Measures: National Green Hydrogen Mission (four pilot projects in DRI and blast furnace); Steel Scrap Recycling Policy 2019.

6.5.2 · Urban mining of e-waste#

MainsEnvironment

Recovery of copper, aluminium, gold, palladium and critical minerals from discarded electronics.

  • Value chain: collection, segregation, secure data destruction, recovery, compliant processing. Benefits: critical mineral security, lower import dependence, reduced mining pressure, data security, domestic capability.
  • E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022 effective 1 April 2023 with mandatory registration of manufacturers, producers, refurbishers and recyclers (EPR).

6.6.1 · Transboundary cooperation in Himalayan disasters#

MainsEnvironment

The August 2026 Nepal disaster showed glacier and flood hazards crossing borders within hours.

  • Why: hazards ignore borders; upstream data decides downstream preparedness; shared basins; cascading disasters.
  • Gaps: poor real-time data sharing; fragmented monitoring; weak institutions; mistrust. Way forward: data-sharing protocols, joint scientific monitoring, common early-warning systems.

  • Keywords: GLOF; Early warning

6.6.2 · AI in disaster management#

MainsEnvironment

Nepal and India disasters showed AI, drones, satellite imagery and crowdsourcing across the disaster cycle.

  • Across prevention, mitigation, preparedness (Google Flood Hub), response (thermal drones, crowdsourced data), recovery, rehabilitation.
  • GraphCast (DeepMind): global forecasts up to 10 days. Google Flood Hub: AI flood forecasts up to 7 days in 150+ countries. DisasterAWARE: multi-hazard early warning platform. SKAI: AI plus satellite imagery for building damage mapping.

6.7.1 · Deep-sea mining: India's third ISA contract#

Prelims + MainsEnvironment

India holds three ISA exploration contracts covering about 95,000 sq km; commercial mining rules still under negotiation.

  • 2002: polymetallic nodules, 75,000 sq km, Central Indian Ocean Basin (nickel, copper, cobalt, manganese). 2016: polymetallic sulphides, 10,000 sq km, Central and Southwest Indian Ridge. 2025: sulphides, 10,000 sq km, Carlsberg Ridge.
  • Deep Ocean Mission: MATSYA-6000; NIOT tested mining-system components at about 5,270 m.
  • Concerns: limited ecological knowledge, habitat loss, near-irreversible damage, strategic need vs ecological cost. Approach: exploration before exploitation.

6.7.2 · Environmental costs of data centres#

MainsEnvironment

Capacity rose from about 520 MW (2020) to nearly 1.5 GW (mid-2026); could reach 6.5 GW by 2030.

  • Power use about 13 TWh (2024) to 57 TWh by 2030 (S&P Global). A 100 MW centre uses about 2 million litres of water a day (CEEW); national use could reach 358 billion litres a year by 2030. Clusters in water-stressed Mumbai, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, NCR. A 2026 Cambridge study links data centres to about 2°C higher land-surface temperature within 10 km.
  • Gaps: no common standards, no water disclosure, grid assessment not mandatory; Gujarat requires 51% green energy; Maharashtra cut from 100% to 51%.

6.8.1 · PRAMAAN: first forest and wood certificates#

PrelimsEnvironment

First PRAMAAN certificates under the Indian Forest and Wood Certification Scheme (IFWCS): 33,000 ha of plantations in Andhra Pradesh and a bamboo farmer in Odisha.

  • IFWCS launched by MoEFCC in December 2023; PRAMAAN is its operational framework for verifiable sustainability credentials for deforestation-free timber markets.
  • Categories: Forest Management (assessed against Indian Forest Management Standard under National Working Plan Code 2023, third-party audit), Trees Outside Forests Management, Chain of Custody.
  • Bodies: Indian Forest and Wood Certification Council (apex); IIFM Bhopal (operating agency); certification bodies accredited by NABCB (Quality Council of India).

6.8.3 · WDC-PMKSY 2.0 watershed development#

PrelimsEnvironment

Centrally Sponsored Scheme of the Department of Land Resources (Rural Development): IWMP 2009-10, part of PMKSY 2015-16, WDC-PMKSY 2.0 for 2021-26.

  • Uses GIS and remote sensing, convergence, springshed rejuvenation; Watershed Yatra and Festival.
  • Progress: about 1.24 lakh water-harvesting structures; 3.08 lakh ha protective irrigation; 1.40 lakh ha shifted from single to two crops; 28.50 lakh farmers; 2.85 crore person-days; afforestation over 1.45 lakh ha. States asked to evaluate by outcomes, not expenditure.

6.8.4 · Jal Sanchay Jan Bhagidari#

PrelimsEnvironment

Community-led groundwater recharge initiative launched 6 September 2024: low-cost recharge, storage structures, revival of defunct borewells.

  • Whole-of-Government, Whole-of-Society; 3Cs: Community, CSR, Cost. Over 27 lakh structures under JSJB 1.0; total above 1.58 crore recharge structures by September 2026. Integrated with Jal Shakti Abhiyan: Catch the Rain in June 2026.

6.8.5 · e-FAST India: PACT and Zero-Emission Truck Marketplace#

PrelimsEnvironment

Launched at the 5th e-FAST India Summit 2026; e-FAST is a NITI Aayog-led platform for zero-emission freight.

  • PACT (Platform for Aggregating Clean Transport) pools demand from shippers and logistics providers along freight corridors; the Truck Marketplace connects manufacturers, LSPs, financiers, charging operators.

70th International Whaling Commission meeting, Hobart.

  • IWC under the International Convention for the Regulation of Whaling 1946; 89 members; India a member since 1981. Cetaceans get highest protection under WPA 1972; Marine Protected Areas; Project Dolphin.

6.8.7 · World Circular Economy Forum 2026#

PrelimsEnvironment

10th WCEF in Gandhinagar, 15-18 September; first in South Asia; theme "Circular Economy: Transition for People and Prosperity".

  • WCEF launched 2017 by Finnish Innovation Fund Sitra; organised by CPCB, MoEFCC, Sitra, Finland, GPCB; 2,000+ stakeholders from 65+ countries. 10YFP Board met on the sidelines with India as Co-Chair (10YFP adopted at Rio+20, 2012, extended to 2030).

6.8.8 · India Electric Mobility Index 2025#

PrelimsEnvironment

NITI Aayog with WRI India; second edition; scores States/UTs out of 100 on 16 indicators across Transport Electrification Progress, Charging Infrastructure Readiness, EV Research and Innovation.

  • Delhi first with 84, then Maharashtra and Karnataka; Chandigarh and Goa among leaders. 29 of 36 States/UTs have EV policies; EVs rose from 0.5% of sales (2018) to 8.25% (2025-26).

6.10a · Species in news: rhino, striped hyena, tilcayo, hornbill#

PrelimsEnvironment

Fauna items from September 2026.

  • Greater one-horned rhino (Rhinoceros unicornis): Project Rhino announced on World Rhino Day 2026; India and Nepal; Assam holds about 80% in India (Kaziranga, Pobitora, Orang, Manas; also Jaldapara, Gorumara, Dudhwa); about 4,075 globally, 3,323 in India; IUCN Vulnerable, CITES I, WPA Schedule I; 2019 National Conservation Strategy.
  • Striped hyena (Hyaena hyaena): India's only hyena; scavenger role; arid and semi-arid areas; IUCN Near Threatened; WPA Schedule I.
  • Tilcayo (Leopardus tilcayo): new cat species described in over a century; about 46 cm and 1.4 kg; Yungas between Andes and Amazon; diverged about 1.4 million years ago.
  • Rufous-necked hornbill (Aceros nipalensis): NE India and north Bengal; 600-2,200 m evergreen hill forest; seed disperser; IUCN Vulnerable, CITES I, WPA Schedule I; documentary The Hornbill Promise (Latpanchar).

6.10b · Species in news: new discoveries and trafficking seizure#

PrelimsEnvironment

New species and a seizure.

  • Gegeneophis chandgadi: new legless caecilian (Grandisoniidae) from Kolhapur, northern Western Ghats; named after the Chandgadi dialect.
  • Duttaphrynus phawngpui: new toad from Phawngpui National Park, Mizoram (named after the state's highest peak). Ingerana aizawl: small frog (22-26 mm) from Aizawl.
  • Ariosoma cmfriense: new eel from about 300 m off Kollam, Kerala, found under the Deep Ocean Mission.
  • Common Wanderer butterfly (Pareronia hippia): philomela form first recorded in Poothurai, Villupuram, Tamil Nadu; a colour form, not a new species.
  • Tokay gecko: DRI seized 86 in Mariani, Assam; NE India and South/Southeast Asia; blue-grey with orange spots; illegal pet and traditional-medicine trade.

6.11 · Places in news#

PrelimsEnvironment

Protected areas in September 2026.

  • Periyar Tiger Reserve: southern Western Ghats, Kerala (Idukki, Pathanamthitta, Kottayam); Project Tiger since 1978, notified 2007; about 925 sq km; Periyar River and Lake, Pamba and Kakki reservoirs; new records include one bird, one butterfly and four odonates.
  • Dibru-Saikhowa National Park (Assam): SC allowed Oil India to file a fresh plea after rejection of its Extended Reach Drilling proposal; 340 sq km national park inside 765 sq km biosphere reserve; ESZ up to 8.7 km; Brahmaputra floodplain; hoolock gibbon, Gangetic dolphin, feral horses; Bengal florican and hog deer affected by invasives and floods.
  • Pichavaram mangrove (Cuddalore, Tamil Nadu): Total Economic Value estimated at Rs 2,485.38 crore.
  • Sathyamangalam Tiger Reserve (Erode, TN): junction of Eastern and Western Ghats, Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve; contiguous with Mudumalai, Bandipur, BRT; Bhavani and Moyar rivers; Irula and Kurumba tribes.
  • Papikonda National Park (Andhra Pradesh): about 1,068 sq km, Godavari gorges, called "Bison Hills"; gaur rescued from a wire snare; Konda Reddis and Koyas.
  • Shri Guru Gorakhnath Community Reserve, Kajalheri (Fatehabad, Haryana): 150-200 turtles incl. Indian softshell, red-crowned roofed and Indian peacock turtles; pollution threat.
