1996PYQ 1(Prelims 1996)

Consider the following statements: The Non-cooperation movement led to the 1. Congress becoming a mass movement for the first time. 2. Growth of Hindu-Muslim unity. 3. Removal of fear of the British might from the minds of the people 4. British Government's willingness to grant political concessions to Indians. Of these statements

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Statements 1 and 3 are correct: the NCM made the Congress a MASS movement for the first time, and ordinary people shed their fear of British might — the movement's lasting psychological gain. Statement 2 fails the test of results: the Khilafat-built Hindu-Muslim unity proved temporary and collapsed after the movement. Statement 4 fails outright: Britain offered no political concessions — it answered the NCM with repression.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Overcounts: statements 2 and 4 do not survive — the unity was transient and no concessions came; only the mass-movement gain (1) and the loss of fear (3) endured.

(B) ✗ — Keeps the failed unity claim (2) while discarding the movement's central achievement — the Congress becoming a mass movement for the first time (1).

(D) ✗ — Statement 4 sinks it: the British government conceded nothing — the NCM ended in suppression and Gandhi's arrest, not in political concessions. (Note: It aimed to resist British rule in India through non-violent means. The ideas of Ahimsa and non-violence and Gandhiji's ability to rally hundreds of thousands of common citizens towards the cause of Independence.)

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Both true, and R is exactly why: at Chauri Chaura (5 February 1922) the mob burned the police station, killing 22 policemen — Gandhi, reading it as proof that mass non-violent discipline had failed, withdrew the mass movement and suspended the planned Bardoli CDM.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — R is not incidental — it IS the explanation: the record ties the withdrawal directly to the Chauri Chaura violence breaking the movement's non-violent foundation.

(C) ✗ — R is true: the Chauri Chaura killings of February 1922 are precisely what as the trigger.

(D) ✗ — A is true: Gandhi did stop the mass movement in February 1922 — only institutional non-cooperation continued. (Note: The Chauri Chaura incident occurred at Chauri Chaura in the Gorakhpur district on 5th February, 1922 when a large group of protesters was participating in the Non-Cooperation Movement clashed with police.)

FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words The three Pilot Projects of 1917-18 — Champaran, Ahmedabad, & Kheda — converted South African Satyagraha into a working Indian method. Examine.

Demand of the question: "Examine" + "converted" demands a process-oriented answer. The body must show what each Pilot Project added to the method that South Africa hadn't already developed.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915 with Satyagraha as a personal method; the three Pilot Projects of 1917-18 tested whether the Indian context could carry the same architecture. Body guidance — 4 themes:

1. Champaran (1917) — 'Champaran Satyagraha, 1917 — The First Civil Disobedience'. First Civil Disobedience on Indian soil. Tested whether Indian peasants — under the Tinkathia indigo system in Bihar — would follow a non-violent, local-issue-led campaign. Result: yes. Rajendra Prasad joined here. Champaran proved Indian rural communities could be mobilised without violence.

2. Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918) — 'Ahmedabad Mill Strike, 1918 — The First Hunger Strike'. First Hunger Strike (fast unto death). Tested the labour-capital arbitration model — Gandhi's Trusteeship doctrine in live action. Anasuya Sarabhai linked Gandhi to the millworkers.

Outcome: 35% wage increase via moral negotiation, not class war.

3. Kheda Satyagraha (1918) — 'Kheda Satyagraha, 1918 — The First Non-Cooperation Movement'. First Non-Cooperation movement. Tested whether peasants would refuse revenue payment to the colonial state. Famine + plague + revenue-collection-as-usual = unjust burden. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel emerged here as a Gandhi lieutenant.

4. The composite outcome. Each Pilot Project proved one repertoire-element worked on Indian soil. Champaran = civil disobedience; Ahmedabad = fast; Kheda = non-cooperation. By 1919, all three were available simultaneously — & Gandhi deployed them at full scale in the Rowlatt Satyagraha.

Closing verdict: The Pilot Projects were not just early campaigns — they were the laboratory in which South African Satyagraha was indigenised. Without the three successful tests of 1917-18, the Rowlatt Satyagraha of 1919 could not have been organised at the all-India scale it took.

Revise: 'The Three Pilot Projects (1917–1918)'-4 (Three Pilot Projects + each individual case).

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words Examine the reasons and consequences of Mahatma Gandhi's withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922.

Demand of the question: "Examine" + "reasons and consequences" = both halves required. Body must explain (a) why Gandhi withdrew despite the movement's momentum, & (b) what it cost — both within the INC and in broader nationalist politics.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Gandhi's withdrawal of the NCM after Chauri Chaura (5 February 1922) was the moment that fixed non-violence as the absolute condition of his political method — & created a permanent rift with sections of the INC. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Events of the NCM (1920–22)'):

1. The Reason — non-violence as absolute (see 'Events of the NCM (1920–22)'). Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur) saw 22 policemen burnt to death by a mob during the NCM. Gandhi held that violence — even by Indians, even by anti-colonial protesters — voided the moral basis of the movement. Means matter as much as ends; Gandhi's two defining principles allowed no compromise.

2. The Internal Cost (see 'Results of the NCM'). Tilak's wing and C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru, & others held the withdrawal as a strategic blunder — the movement was at peak momentum; pausing for one incident in one district threw away national-scale leverage. C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru went on to form the Swaraj Party (December 1922) — Council Entry strategy = Gandhi's withdrawal forced a tactical split within Congress.

3. The Larger Consequence (see 'Results of the NCM'). The Passive Phase (1922-27) followed — no mass movement against the British. But Constructive Work (see 'The Importance of Constructive Work During the Passive Phase') used these years to build Khadi, national schools, & panchayats — when the next mass round came (Civil Disobedience 1930), the INC had a far stronger institutional machine.

Closing verdict: The Chauri Chaura withdrawal cost the immediate momentum but established non-violence as the structural — not tactical — anchor of Indian nationalism. It also forced the Swaraj Party experiment, which itself produced the Council-Entry skill-set the INM would need in 1937 & after.

Revise: 'Chauri Chaura' + 'Results of NCM' + 'The Passive Phase (1922–27)'-13 (Passive Phase + Constructive Work).
