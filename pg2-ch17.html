1 · Federalism IN India · Features · Debate · Evolution · Forms OF Federalism#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Centre State Relations handout pp. 3-7. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • India is a holding-together federation with a strong Centre: federal in structure, unitary in bias. Wheare called it quasi-federal; Austin stressed cooperative federalism. Its working has passed through five phases and blends cooperative, competitive, bargaining, asymmetric and conflictual forms.

WHAT IS A Federation

Idea

  • Powers constitutionally divided between Centre and regional governments, both drawing authority from the Constitution
  • Regional governments have guaranteed powers the Centre cannot take away at will
BasisComing togetherHolding together
Originindependent units unitelarger entity distributes power to regions
Aimmutual security, common interestsaccommodate diversity while keeping unity
Powersunits strongerCentre stronger
ExamplesUSA, Switzerland, AustraliaIndia, Spain, Belgium

Federal features

  • dual polity
  • written Constitution
  • Seventh Schedule division
  • constitutional supremacy
  • rigid amendment (Art 368): ratification by half the States for federal provisions
  • independent judiciary, Art 131
  • bicameralism: Rajya Sabha (Arts 249, 312)

Unitary features

  • Strong Centre
  • Residuary power Art 248
  • single Constitution, citizenship
  • Art 3: Parliament alters States without consent
  • Art 254 Union law prevails
  • Emergency (Art 352)
  • President's Rule (Art 356)
  • Governor (Art 155; Art 200 reservation)
  • integrated institutions: judiciary, AIS, EC, CAG
  • fiscal centralisation

THE Debate

Scholars

  • K. C. Wheare: quasi-federal (federal with strong unitary bias)
  • Granville Austin: cooperative federalism
  • sui generis: of its own kind

Strong-Centre side

  • constitutional centralisation
  • national party high commands
  • financial dependence
  • Governors, AIS, directions

Federal side

  • independent powers
  • S. R. Bommai 1994: federalism is Basic Structure
  • regional parties and coalitions
  • GST Council, Finance Commission, Inter-State Council

Evolution

  1. 1950-67
    Centralised: one-party dominance, Planning Commission 1950, Art 356 used (Kerala 1959)
  2. 1967-80
    Regionalism: non-Congress States in 1967, DMK, Rajamannar Committee 1969, Emergency 1975-77
  3. 1980s-90s
    Assertive States, judicial correction: Punjab, Assam, AP; Sarkaria 1983; Bommai 1994
  4. 1990s-2014
    Coalitions, economic federalism: 1991 liberalisation; 73rd and 74th Amendments 1992; Inter-State Council 1990
  5. 2014 on
    Cooperative and competitive: NITI Aayog 2015, GST Council 2017 (Art 279A), fiscal tensions (GST compensation, cesses, CSS), coalition dependence after 2024

Forms OF Indian Federalism

FormMeaning and tools
Asymmetricdifferent arrangements for different States: Arts 371-371J, Sixth Schedule
Bargaining (W. H. Morris-Jones)continuous negotiation: political, fiscal, institutional (Planning Commission, NDC, now GST Council); risk of inconsistent policy
CooperativeGST Council, NITI Governing Council, Finance Commission, Inter-State Council, joint schemes (MGNREGA, RTE); limited by centralisation, party differences, weak institutions, conditional grants
Competitiveease of doing business, NITI indices (State Health Index, School Education Quality Index, SDG India Index, India Innovation Index), Vibrant Gujarat, Magnetic Maharashtra; risks: unequal competition, concentration, rivalry
Conflictuallegislative, administrative (Governor, CBI, forces, AIS), financial (devolution, GST compensation, borrowing), inter-State disputes
Way forward / key point

Cooperation, competition, bargaining and conflict coexist; the challenge is to keep disagreement from undermining autonomy and cooperation.

2 · Centre-state Legislative Relations · Lists · Residuary · 42nd Amendment · Doctrines#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Centre State Relations handout pp. 7-9. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Powers are split by territory (Art 245) and subject (Art 246, Seventh Schedule). Parliament holds residuary power (Art 248). The 42nd Amendment moved five subjects to the Concurrent List. Courts reconcile overlaps with pith and substance, colourable legislation and harmonious construction.

Distribution OF Powers

Art 245 (territory)

  • Parliament:
    • Whole or any part of India
    • State: whole or part of State
  • no Parliament law invalid for operating outside India
  • Territorial nexus:
    • State law can reach outside if real and sufficient connection
    • State of Bihar v. Charusila Dasi (1959)
Union ListState ListConcurrent List
Parliament exclusive; national importanceStates ordinarily exclusive; regional/localboth can legislate; uniformity plus flexibility
defence, foreign affairs, banking, currency, atomic energypolice, public order, public health, agriculture, prisonseducation, forests, criminal law, marriage, electricity

Position

  • Union List prevails when fields overlap and cannot be reconciled
  • Parliament can legislate on State List for Union Territories (Art 246(4))
  • Concurrent conflicts: Art 254
Note

Residuary (Art 248): subjects in no list, including residuary taxation, belong exclusively to Parliament. Unlike the USA, where undelegated powers stay with States or people.

42nd Amendment Transfer (1976)

Five subjects moved State to Concurrent

  • Education
  • Forests
  • Weights and measures
  • Protection of wild animals and birds
  • Administration of justice (courts other than SC and HC)
Memory aid · EFWWA

Education · Forests · Weights & measures · Wild animals · Administration of justice. (own mnemonic)

Rationale

  • national uniformity
  • cross-border forests and wildlife
  • cooperative legislation

Concerns

  • erosion of State autonomy
  • Union supremacy via Art 254
  • less local responsiveness
  • done during the Emergency without consultation
Value addition

Sarkaria and Punchhi: consult States before legislating on Concurrent List subjects.

Doctrines OF Interpretation

DoctrineMeaningCase
Pith and substancelook at the true nature and purpose; incidental encroachment allowedState of Bombay v. F. N. Balsara (1951)
Colourable legislationwhat cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly; substance over formK. C. Gajapati Narayan Deo v. Orissa (1953)
Harmonious constructionread entries together so each has meaningCalcutta Gas Co. v. West Bengal (1962)
Federal supremacyif entries truly conflict, Union List prevails (Art 246); Concurrent via Art 254
Ancillary powerspower on a subject includes what is needed to make it effective (e.g. tax: assessment, collection)
Limits on delegationrule-making can be delegated, but essential legislative policy cannot
Memory aid · PCHFAD

Pith · Colourable · Harmonious · Federal supremacy · Ancillary · Delegation limits. (own mnemonic)

3 · Parliament ON State LIST · Repugnancy (Art 254) · Model LAWS · FARM LAWS · Assent TO Bills#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Centre State Relations handout pp. 9-13. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Parliament can enter the State List through Arts 249-253, in a Union Territory, and in President's Rule. Art 254(2) lets a presidentially assented State law prevail. Governors' delay in assent led to the Tamil Nadu case (April 2025) and a Presidential Reference opinion (Nov 2025) that rejected court timelines and deemed assent.

Parliament ON State LIST Subjects

ArticleProvision
249Rajya Sabha resolution by two-thirds present and voting in national interest; valid one year, renewable yearly; law ceases 6 months after; also for GST (Art 246A)
250during National Emergency; ceases 6 months after it ends
251State keeps its power; if conflict, Union law prevails while it lasts
252two or more States request; resolutions of State Legislatures (both Houses if bicameral); other States may adopt; only Parliament can amend/repeal; e.g. Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974
253to implement treaties, conventions, decisions of international bodies, even on State List (Punchhi: involve States in treaties)
Others246(4) Union Territories; Art 356 President's Rule
Memory aid · 249-250-252-253

249 national interest · 250 Emergency · 252 States request · 253 treaties. (own mnemonic)

Repugnancy (article 254)

Rules

  • 254(1): State law void to the extent of conflict with a Central law, whether earlier or later
  • 254(2):
    • State law on Concurrent List prevails in that State if reserved for the President and assented
    • But Parliament can later amend, vary or repeal it

Courts ask

  • directly conflicting provisions?
  • would obeying one break the other?
  • did Parliament intend to occupy the whole field?
  • different wording alone is not repugnancy

Model LAWS AND FARM LAWS

Model laws

  • draft laws by the Union that States adopt voluntarily
  • Model Agricultural Land Leasing Act 2016
  • APLM Act 2017
  • Model Tenancy Act 2021
  • Model Prisons and Correctional Services Act 2023
  • pros:
    • Uniformity with autonomy
    • Cons: uneven adoption, funding incentives, "soft centralisation"

Farm Laws 2020

  • State List Entry 14 (agriculture) and 28 (markets and fairs)
  • Union relied on Concurrent List Entry 33 (trade in foodstuffs)
  • States objected:
    • Encroachment, loss of APMC fees, MSP fears, no consultation
    • Punjab and others passed modifying laws (Art 254)
  • Repealed 2021
  • Supreme Court never finally decided legislative competence
  • lesson: consult States

Assent TO State Bills (articles 200, 201)

Provisions

  • Art 200:
    • Governor may assent, withhold (return a non-Money Bill), or reserve for the President
    • Must reserve a Bill that would endanger the High Court's position
  • Art 201: President may assent, withhold or direct reconsideration (non-Money Bill)
  • delays can obstruct elected legislatures
  1. April 2025
    Tamil Nadu Governor case (two judges): no pocket or absolute veto; fixed timelines for Governor and President; used Art 142 to declare 10 Bills deemed assented
  2. Nov 2025
    16th Presidential Reference (five judges, Art 143): Governor has three options with discretion; no judicial timelines; no deemed assent via Art 142; limited mandamus to act within reasonable time for indefinite inaction; decisions not challengeable on merits before Bill becomes law
Note

The Nov 2025 opinion did not undo the 10 Tamil Nadu Bills' validity, but its reasoning on timelines and deemed assent replaced the April 2025 reasoning as precedent. (current affairs; verify)

Commissions AND WAY Forward

Sarkaria on legislation

  • strong Centre but no over-centralisation
  • legislate on Concurrent List only where uniformity is needed, after consulting States
  • Keep residuary taxation with the Union
  • Other residuary matters to Concurrent List
  • keep Art 249 as it is
  • Centre to decide on reserved Bills within four months
  • Liberal Art 254(2)
  • Framework laws
  • Pre-legislative consultation via Inter-State Council

Way forward

  • avoid unnecessary entry into State List
  • consultation on Concurrent List and treaties
  • transparent assent rules
  • strengthen Rajya Sabha for State interests
  • prefer model laws
  • use ISC and GST Council

4 · Centre-state Administrative Relations · Directions · AIS · Councils · BSF · Commissions#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Centre State Relations handout pp. 13-18. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Executive power follows legislative power, but the Union can direct States (Arts 256, 257, 365). All-India Services, the Inter-State Council (Art 263), Zonal Councils and NEC coordinate. Friction: broad directions, AIS control, BSF jurisdiction (2021), Union ministries on State subjects.

Union Control AND Delegation

ArticleProvision
256States must ensure compliance with Parliament's laws; Union may direct
257States must not impede Union executive power; directions on communications of national/military importance and railway protection; Union compensates costs
339(2)directions on ST welfare schemes
347President can direct a State to recognise a language
350Amother-tongue primary instruction; President may direct
365failure to follow valid directions: President may find constitutional machinery has failed (does not automatically justify Art 356)
258President with State consent entrusts Union functions to States; Union compensates
258AGovernor with Union consent entrusts State functions to Union

Emergencies

  • Art 352: Union directions even on State matters
  • Art 356: President assumes State executive functions
  • Art 360: financial propriety, salary cuts, reserve financial Bills

All-india Services

AIS

  • Art 312:
    • Rajya Sabha two-thirds resolution to create
    • Existing: IAS, IPS, IFoS
  • benefits: administrative unity, national integration, expertise, policy coordination
  • issues: dual control, central deputation, political transfers, cadre shortages, State autonomy
  • reform: consultation, cadre planning, independence, transparent transfers

Coordination Institutions

Inter-State Council (Art 263)

  • set up 1990 on Sarkaria's advice
  • PM chair
  • CMs of States, CMs of UTs with legislatures, Administrators of other UTs, specified Union ministers
  • inquire into State disputes, discuss common interests, recommend coordination
  • Advisory
  • Infrequent meetings, weak follow-up, overlap with GST Council and NITI. Sarkaria: permanent ISC
  • Punchhi: strengthen it

Zonal Councils

  • five, under the States Reorganisation Act 1956
  • Union Home Minister chairs
  • CMs are members and rotating vice-chairs
  • Two other ministers per State
  • UT representatives
  • aims: curb acute State consciousness and regionalism, habit of cooperation, solve inter-State problems, regional development

North Eastern Council

  • NEC Act 1971
  • Eight North-Eastern States
  • Regional planning, infrastructure

Others

  • NITI Governing Council
  • GST Council (Art 279A)
  • Empowered Committee of State Finance Ministers (VAT, GST transition)
  • National Integration Council
  • Chief Secretaries' and DGP conferences

Issues

Directions (Arts 256, 257)

  • vague and broad, reduce autonomy, political misuse, Art 365 threat
  • Punchhi: treat as last-resort safeguards, after consultation and warning

BSF jurisdiction (2021)

  • BSF Act 1968, Section 139 notifications
  • Punjab, West Bengal, Assam:
    • 15 km to 50 km
    • Gujarat: 80 to 50 km
    • Rajasthan: 50 km unchanged
    • Entire specified North-East States, J&K, Ladakh
  • Only specified statutory powers
  • Not general police or public order
  • Union: infiltration, smuggling, trafficking, uniformity, quick response. States: police is State List, no consultation (Punjab, West Bengal), overlap, civil liberties

Union ministries on State subjects

  • for: national standards, pandemics and pollution, CSS funds, expertise (COVID-19)
  • against: encroachment, one-size-fits-all, fiscal dependence, centralisation (e.g. Ministry of Cooperation)
  • NITI Sub-Group of CMs on CSS: rationalise schemes, flexibility
CommissionAdministrative recommendations
Sarkaria (1983-88)restrained use of Arts 256-257; permanent ISC under Art 263; prior consultation; Art 356 last resort
Punchhi (2007-10)directions as safety valves; stronger ISC with dispute-resolution role; regular Zonal Councils; Forum of CMs chaired by rotation for sectors (energy, food, education, environment, health); consult on central forces
Way forward / key point

Way forward: revive ISC and Zonal Councils; directions only where necessary; clear CBI consent procedures; reform AIS cadre management; BSF-police protocols; rationalise CSS.
