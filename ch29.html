The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: The Moderates failed, so the Extremists took religion and culture to the masses; Curzon 99-05's laws and the Partition of Bengal gave them a villain and the first mass protest; the Swadeshi toolkit could not reach the poor, the Muslims or the Lower Castes, the INC split at Surat, and the tested toolkit waited for Gandhi.

  1. The petitions that went nowhere. The Moderates have led the INC since 1885, and their method is petition, prayer and press. By the early 1900s it has brought no self-governance, no economic relief and no political power. The Extremists read this as proof that gentle methods do not work against a colonial administration.
    → But a new generation has a different recipe.
  2. Culture becomes a weapon. Lal-Bal-Pal and Sri Aurobindo lead the Extremist phase: Lajpat Rai in Punjab, Tilak in Maharashtra, Pal and Aurobindo in Bengal. Tilak had already turned the Ganapati Festival (1893) and the Shivaji Festival (1896) into political platforms: culture first, then political energy. Most want Swaraj within the Raj; Aurobindo (1907, in Bande Mataram) and Pal want Purna Swaraj.
    → Then a Viceroy hands them a villain.
  3. Curzon 99-05's three orders. In 1899 the Calcutta Municipal Corporation Amendment Act cuts elected members; in 1904 the Indian Universities Act takes the universities under direct control, calling them factories of sedition; and in 1904 the Indian Official Secrets Amendment Act cuts press freedom. Local self-government, education and the press are all hit. The teacher: the stronger the villain, the stronger the hero.
    → And then he cuts a province in two.
  4. The Partition of Bengal. The Risley Papers of 1903 reveal the plan. It is announced in July 1905 and takes effect on 16 October 1905: Bengal (West Bengal, Bihar, Orissa) and East Bengal and Assam, a new Muslim-majority province. The stated reason is good governance; the Indian view is Divide and Rule, since Bengalis become a minority in Bengal and Hindus a minority in EBA. Nawab Salimullah welcomes it and the Muslim League follows in 1906.
    → Bengal answers first with the pen.
  5. The pen, then the boycott. From 1903 to 1905 Surendranath Bannerjea and C.R. Das run a press campaign and Tagore tries Constructive Swadeshi at Shantiniketan; Deuskar's Desher Katha (1904) uses 'Desh' for all India. After about 1.5 years of the Moderates' three Ps the Partition still comes, and on 7 August 1905 the Moderates themselves pass the Boycott Resolution at Calcutta Town Hall.
    → Now the Extremists take the movement over.
  6. The Extremists take over. The Extremists add Swaraj to the goal and Passive Resistance (NC + CD + CW, with violence only if the government uses repression) to the method, and carry the movement out of Bengal: Das and Pal, Tilak, Haidar Raza, Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh, and V.O. Chidambaram Pillai. At Barisal in April 1906 the police stop a procession of Bande Mataram badges, and it becomes a big boost to the movement.
    → They need a toolkit to match.
  7. The toolkit. Boycott and Swadeshi (two sides of one coin), national education with the Bengal National College under Aurobindo, enterprises such as TISCO (1907) and the Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company (1906), picketing, Samitis, Jatras, religious symbols and workers' strikes. Constructive Work aims at Atmashakti, the individual and then the community.
    → But a toolkit is not yet a mass movement.
  8. Why it fell short. Swadeshi goods are costly, so the poor stay out; student volunteers use force and social boycott on Lower Castes and Muslims; institutions are thin; Muslims are put off by Jatras, the Muslim League and the heavy Hindu symbolism; and the Extremists spend energy trying to capture the INC. British imports are not hit.
    → And then the Congress itself breaks.
  9. Calcutta, then Surat. At Calcutta (1906) Naoroji presides and the Extremists pass Swaraj, Boycott, Swadeshi and National Education. Morley's 1906 Budget speech hints at council reforms, and the Moderates move the 1907 session from Poona or Nagpur to Surat. A fight over the presidency between L.L. Rai and Rash Behari Ghosh ends with shoes hurled and the Extremists expelled; in 1908 the Allahabad Convention declares loyalty to the Raj.
    → The Extremists are scattered.
  10. Leaders scattered, guns appear. Tilak is deported to Mandalay (1908-14), Aurobindo retires, Pal retires for a time, and Rai goes abroad (1914-20). Leaderless, the movement gives way to the Revolutionaries (1908-11). The teacher's chain: when the pen failed people came out, when people failed guns, when guns failed MG.
    → Yet the movement leaves something behind.
  11. What was left. Nationalism stays lit, mass politics has begun, the Drain is explained to the poor, Bande Mataram becomes the INM's anthem, and Passive Resistance, Constructive Work, Jatras and Prabhat Pheris are a toolkit Gandhi inherits in 1920. At the Delhi Durbar of December 1911 the Partition is annulled and the capital moves to Delhi; the Muslim League's birth leaves communal politics as the long shadow.

In simple words#

  • The Moderates (petition, prayer, press) got nothing, so the Extremists (Lal-Bal-Pal and Sri Aurobindo) used culture and religion to reach the masses
  • Curzon 99-05 made the villain that made the hero
  • His three laws (Municipal 1899, Universities 1904, Official Secrets 1904) hit local self-government, education and the press
  • Then the Partition of Bengal (announced July 1905, effected 16 October 1905) split the province on Hindu-Muslim lines: Divide and Rule
  • The Moderates began the boycott (7 August 1905)
  • The Extremists led it, adding Swaraj and Passive Resistance (NC + CD + CW, violence only if repression) and spreading it across India
  • Their toolkit had nine parts:
    • Boycott, Swadeshi, national education, enterprises, picketing, Samitis, Jatras, religion and strikes
    • Constructive Work aimed at Atmashakti (self-reliance)
  • It did not become a mass movement: costly goods, force against Lower Castes and Muslims, thin institutions, Hindu symbolism and a fight to capture the INC
  • imports were not hit
  • The Surat Split (1907) was about method, not goal
  • Leaders were scattered (Tilak at Mandalay 1908-14), the Revolutionaries rose, and Gandhi (1920) inherited the tested toolkit
  • 1911 annulled the Partition and moved the capital to Delhi

1 · Drivers of the Extremist rise#

Story link

Because the Moderates' petitions had failed, the Extremists look for a new recipe. Ask: which three causes (collapse, culture, Curzon 99-05) turn a failure into a movement?

1.1 Leaders and goal

  • By 1905 the Moderate phase of the INM ended and the Extremist phase (1905-20) began under Lal-Bal-Pal and Sri Aurobindo: Lala Lajpat Rai (Punjab), Bal Gangadhar Tilak (Maharashtra), Bipin Chandra Pal and Sri Aurobindo Ghosh (Bengal).
  • For most Extremists the goal was Swaraj within the British Raj
  • for a few, Sri Aurobindo and B.C Pal, it was Purna Swaraj (complete independence)
Teacher's board
  • INM is the movie, INC is the hero.
  • The board separates the movement (INM) from the organisation (INC): (1) the INC was dominated by Moderates 1885-1918, ~1920
  • (2) the INM was dominated by Moderates 1857-1905
  • (4) Extremists dominated the INM 1905-20
  • (5) MG dominated the INM [the years are written like "1990-47" on the slide: read as 1920-47
  • Unclear, check your notebook]
  • So the Extremists led the movement but never the organisation, which matches the handout's note that INC control passed to Gandhi at the 1920 Nagpur Session (section 8).
Teacher's board
  • Swaraj is the power of L, E, J (legislative, executive, judicial) split in two: the external part stays with the British and the domestic part comes "significantly" to Indians, from 75% up to 100%, which is called Dominion Status.
  • The Extremists did not believe in the Providence of British rule (that it was a blessing).
  • BUT Swaraj was more practical than Purna Swaraj.
  • That is why most Extremists asked for Swaraj within the British Raj (the first word after "Sym for" is).

1.2 Sri Aurobindo and the first Purna Swaraj demand

  • Sri Aurobindo studied in Cambridge and qualified for the CSE in 1890 but deliberately skipped the horse-riding test.
  • He joined the Baroda Princely State civil service and later served as Vice-Principal of Baroda College.
  • He was the first to publicly demand absolute independence in 1907, through his writings in Bande Mataram (not through an INC resolution).
Teacher's board

On the slide beside "1st formal Purna Swaraj resolution" the teacher adds "in INC" (Lecture 30, slide 58), so the two firsts are different: Aurobindo = first public demand (1907, writings); Hasrat Mohani = first formal resolution in the INC (1921).

Handout note
  • The first formal resolution in the INC for Purna Swaraj was by Hasrat Mohani (with Swami Kumaranand) at the 1921 Ahmedabad Session.
  • It was rejected by the INC, mainly on Mahatma Gandhi's opposition: he argued the demand was premature, irresponsible, and that the masses were not yet disciplined or prepared for a completely independent State.

1.3 The three drivers

Teacher's board
  • "Every phase of the INM was, in a sense, born from the failure of the phase before it." The handout runs the chain: pre-1857 revolts → 1857 Revolt → modern INM → failure of conservative earlier organisations → WEMC-led regional organisations → INC under the Moderates → Moderates' failure → Extremists → Extremists' failure → Revolutionaries → and so on, until Gandhi.
  • Each failure raised the temperature of the next response.
  • Driver 1: The Moderates had failed. The Moderates led the INC since 1885.
  • Their method was petition, prayer and press.
  • By the early 1900s it had yielded nothing concrete: no self-governance, no economic relief, no political power.
  • The Extremists read this as proof that gentle methods do not work against a colonial administration.
  • Driver 2: mass mobilisation through religion and culture.
  • The Moderates worked through newspapers, petitions and annual INC sessions, and their base was the Western Educated Middle Class (WEMC).
  • The Extremists wanted the religious masses, and so spoke in cultural terms.
  • Tilak organised the Ganapati Festival (1893) and the Shivaji Festival (1896) in Maharashtra as platforms for political mobilisation.
Teacher's board
  • Nationalism has three faces: Cultural, Economic (the Drain Theory) and Political (power to the people or nation over the State).
  • The Extremists' recipe is Step 1:
    • Use culture to raise passion and emotion among the masses
    • Step 2: convert that cultural energy into political energy.
  • The masses to be gathered are the non-Western-educated, motivated through Religion and Historical Heroes, then directed into the INM.
  • "Emotion = energizer of Action." Netaji Bose:
    • "Sv = Spiritual father of the INM in Bengal" ("Spiritual" boxed), which makes him a Cultural Nationalist [the slide writes only "Sv", probably Swami Vivekananda, who is named in section 3
    • Check your notebook]
Handout note
  • Handout: this was not a new idea in world history.
  • Across colonised and occupied nations cultural nationalism has often been the first step toward political nationalism, especially when direct political organisation is suppressed.
  • Tilak in the 1890s was doing what many European nationalists did in the 1820s and 1830s.
  • Driver 3: Curzon 99-05 made mass anger unavoidable.
  • Curzon 99-05 governed with an arrogance and authoritarianism that alienated even moderate opinion.
  • His laws concentrated power and told educated Indians their opinions did not matter.
  • Three reactionary laws (Calcutta Municipal Corporation Amendment Act 1899, Indian Universities Act 1904, Indian Official Secrets Amendment Act 1904) each hit a different pillar, local self-government, education and the press, and together showed that the administration would not reform itself.
  • Section 2 gives the laws in detail.
Teacher's board

"Stronger the villain, Stronger the Hero." (Lecture 30, slides 55 and 60.) The handout's version: Curzon 99-05's authoritarianism made even the Moderates of Bengal rethink their methods; he attacked local self-government, education and the press, and deployed DnR @ Bengal.

3 · The Partition of Bengal (announced July 1905, effected 16 October 1905)#

Story link

3.1 Bengal's administrative history before the Partition

  • The British had redrawn Bengal's boundaries many times, each time for their own administrative or political convenience.
YearChange

3.2 The British justification

  • The official rationale was administrative. Undivided Bengal was the most populous province and too large for one administration.
  • The British named three benefits:
    • (1) Assam would grow and, as part of a proper Lieutenant-Governor province, get its own civil services cadre
    • (2) the Assam-Bengal Railways would come under the unified administration of EBA
    • (3) Assam's tea gardens would gain a cheaper maritime outlet via Chittagong port.

3.3 The real reason: what Indians believed

  • Indians saw the Partition as Divide and Rule, meant to weaken the INM in Bengal.
  • If administration were the goal, the logical fix was to reorganise Bengal on linguistic lines, not religious ones.
  • Instead two provinces were divided along the Hindu-Muslim demographic fault line: it was communal engineering.
  • How it weakened the INM: the movement in Bengal was led by the Bengali Bhadralok, upper-caste (UC) Hindu educated professionals.
  • The Partition made Bengalis a minority in West Bengal (diluted by Bihar and Orissa) and Hindus a minority in EBA, so the support base shrank in both provinces at once.
  • The second objective was to appease elite Muslims and counter the rising tide of Hindu cultural nationalism.
  • EBA was designed as a new Muslim Majority Province (MMP).
The Partition: the stated reason against the real one

  • The British were watching Hindu cultural nationalism rise through the 1880s and 1890s.

8 · Impact of the Swadeshi Movement and the Curzonian administration#

Story link

Because the movement failed in its main aim but not in what it taught, its impact is mostly future use. Ask: which impacts are positive and which two are negative?

  • The Swadeshi Movement and Curzon 99-05's administration reshaped the INM in ways that outlasted the movement.
ImpactDetail
8.1 A nationalism that never came downNationalist consciousness, especially in Bengal and Maharashtra, did not subside even after the movement ended and leaders scattered
8.2 The foundation of mass politicsFor the first time the attempt was made to involve not just the WEMC but the Working Class (WC), the masses and ordinary people. Incomplete, but the first attempt: the Working Class movement and Revolutionary activities grew from it
8.3 Economic awareness spreadPeople grasped the Drain of Wealth and the role of British goods in impoverishing Indian industry; Boycott became a major tool of the future INM. To convince the poor to buy costlier Swadeshi goods, Extremists had to explain the Drain in simple language

Who led the Extremist phase and what were their regions?Lal-Bal-Pal and Sri Aurobindo: Lajpat Rai (Punjab), Tilak (Maharashtra), Bipin Chandra Pal and Aurobindo Ghosh (Bengal). "Please Make Bread": Punjab, Maharashtra, Bengal.
What was the Extremists' goal?Swaraj within the British Raj for most; Purna Swaraj for Sri Aurobindo and B.C Pal. The teacher: Swaraj (L, E, J split, 75% to 100% = Dominion Status) was more practical than Purna Swaraj.
Who first demanded absolute independence, and who first moved a formal INC resolution?Aurobindo in 1907 through his writings in Bande Mataram; Hasrat Mohani (with Swami Kumaranand) at the 1921 Ahmedabad Session, rejected on Gandhi's opposition (premature).
What does the teacher mean by "INM = movie, INC = hero"?The Extremists dominated the movement (INM 1905-20) but the organisation (INC) stayed Moderate-dominated 1885-1918, about 1920, until Gandhi.
Why did the Extremists rise (3 drivers)?Triple C: Collapse of the Moderates (petition, prayer, press), Culture and religion to reach the masses (Ganapati 1893, Shivaji 1896), Curzon 99-05 ("stronger the villain, stronger the hero").
What were Curzon 99-05's three reactionary laws?Mighty Unfair Orders: Calcutta Municipal Corporation Amendment Act 1899 (fewer elected members), Indian Universities Act 1904 (direct control; "factories of sedition"), Indian Official Secrets Amendment Act 1904 (press freedom; in force to the 1923 Act).
What did each law attack?Local self-government (1899), education (Universities Act), the press (Official Secrets Act).
What was the Partition of Bengal 1905?Announced July 1905, effected 16 October 1905: Bengal (WB + Bihar + Orissa) and EBA (East Bengal + Assam). Annulled 1911 at the Delhi Durbar (George V); Bengal = WB + EB.
What were the British justifications for the Partition?Administration: Bengal was the most populous province; Assam would get a cadre of officers (LG province); Assam-Bengal Railways unified; cheaper outlet for tea via Chittagong.
What was the real reason (Indian view and teacher)?Divide and Rule: Bengalis a minority in Bengal (weak leaders), Hindus a minority in EBA, co-opt elite Muslims; EBA a Muslim-majority province. INM leaders wanted a language-based partition.
How did the Muslim elite respond?Nawab Salimullah of Dacca welcomed it; ML1906, formed a year later, supported it with the Ulamas, deepening communal lines.
Who passed the Boycott Resolution and when?SNB, K.K Mitra, Anand Mohan Bose and P.C. Ray (Bengal Moderates) at Calcutta Town Hall on 7 August 1905 (Handloom Day).
Who initiated and who led the Swadeshi movement?Initiated by Bengal Moderates (7 Aug 1905); led by the Extremists, who added Swaraj and Passive Resistance and spread it nationally.
What happened at the Barisal Conference (April 1906)?SNB and Aurobindo distributed Bande Mataram badges; police repressed the procession; the teacher: a big boost to the movement.
What is the significance of Desher Katha (1904)?Deuskar's book on economic exploitation (in Bangla, the Drain); "Desh" meant the entire subcontinent: pan-Indian economic consciousness.
Define Passive Resistance (teacher).PR = NC + CD + CW + violence if repression (violence is not Step 1); Active Resistance has violence at Step 1 (Revolutionaries).
Civil Disobedience against Non Cooperation?CD is always illegal (violent or non-violent): civilians breaking unjust laws or orders. NC is always legal, so always non-violent: Boycott + Swadeshi, a coin with two sides.
What does boycott target?Legislature, executive, judiciary (and the State's institutions), titles, education institutions, British goods and services. Swadeshi builds the alternative: national education, panchayats and arbitration courts, enterprises.
Supply side against demand side of the boycott of imports?Supply: importers stop importing, port workers strike, domestic merchants do not circulate (IMP). Demand: shops stop selling, people stop buying, volunteers picket, social boycott.
What is Constructive Work (teacher)?The social domain: NGO-like work; welfare of the poor (vernacular education, skills, health); religion and spirituality for confidence; alcohol de-addiction (SHARE). Goal: individual Atmashakti (Atmanirbharta), then community Atmashakti via H-M, UC-LC unity and women's empowerment.
Name the nine methods.New Citizens Never Stop Protesting; Some Join Rallies Willingly: NC and boycott, CW, National Education, Swadeshi enterprises, Picketing, Samitis, Jatras, Religion, Workers' strikes.
Which national-education bodies were set up?Bengal National College (Aurobindo, principal), National Council of Education (an NCERT equivalent; sciences + humanities) and the Bengal Technical Institute.
Match the Swadeshi enterprises.TISCO: Jamsetji Tata, Dorabji Tata 1907; BCPL: P.C. Ray; BSS: Tilak; Swadeshi Steam Navigation 1906: V.O Chidambaram Pillai, Tuticorin; PNB 1894: Dyal Singh Majithia and Lala Harkishen Lal with L.L Rai; Lakshmi Insurance 1906.
Why were Samitis needed?The INC (a 3-day annual conference; PCCs of 1886 dysfunctional on the ground) had no ground structure; Swadesh Bandhab Samiti (1905, Ashwini Kumar Dutt, Barisal) did CW among Muslim ryots and organised the Barisal Conference.
Why did Swadeshi not become a mass movement?Cheap Foreign Textiles Mocked Congress: Costly goods; Force and social boycott; Thin institutions; low Muslim participation (Jatras, ML1906, Hindu symbols); Capturing the INC. Imports not impacted.
How do you judge any movement (teacher)?Goal achieved or not; ideology of nationalism; level of participation of groups and regions; modern politics criteria: ideology, goals, plan, programme, ORG, strong united leadership, money, voice.
Four Calcutta resolutions of 1906?Swaraj (goal), Boycott, Swadeshi, National Education (methods); the Partition was condemned separately (Prelims 2010: annulment is NOT one of the four). Naoroji as President.
