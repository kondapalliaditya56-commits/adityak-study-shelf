1995PYQ 1(Prelims 1995)

The radical wing of the Congress Party with Jawaharlal Nehru as its one of its main leaders, founded the Independence for India League in opposition to

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — The result was the Nehru Report of August 1928 — the first Indian attempt at a constitutional blueprint.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — A timeline check rules this out: the League was formed in 1928 against the Nehru Report's Dominion Status goal, while the Gandhi-Irwin Pact came only in March 1931. A body founded in 1928 could not be opposing a pact that did not yet exist.

(B) ✗ — The Home Rule Movement belongs to 1916–18 — it had ended a decade before the League was formed in 1928. The radicals' target was the Nehru Report's Dominion Status demand, not Home Rule.

(D) ✗ — The Mont-Ford reforms (GOI Act 1919) were what the Simon Commission had come to review. Nehru and Bose formed the League against the Nehru Report's acceptance of Dominion Status — they wanted complete independence — not against the 1919 reforms. HISTORY (Simon Commission (1927) to Delhi Manifesto (November 1929)) (Note: The Independence of Indian League was formed with Jawahar Lal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose who opposed 1928 Nehru Repot as they wanted complete Independence.)

1998PYQ 2(Prelims 1998)

Simon Commission of 1927 was boycotted because

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Not a single Indian was included among its 7 members. INC opposed the Commission precisely on this ground — that the principle of self-government promised in the Montague Statement of 1917 was being violated, as Indians were not given any say in their own future constitution.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — The Commission had no Muslim League link — all 7 members were British MPs. The boycott ground was the exclusion of Indians as a whole, not a tilt towards any one Indian party.

(C) ✗ — A near-miss option: INC invoked the self-government promise of the Montague

Statement as the reason why exclusion was insulting. The boycott trigger was the all-British composition — entitlement to Swaraj by itself was not the stated reason.

(D) ✗ — There were no differences among the members — the 7 British MPs worked as one team under Sir John Simon. The objection came from the Indian side, over Indian exclusion, not from any split inside the Commission. (Note: Simon Commission was a group of seven British members of Parliament under the chairmanship of Sir John Simon assisted by Clement Atlee. Its objective was to report on India's constitutional progress.)

2010PYQ 3(Prelims 2010)

With reference to Simon Commission's recommendations, which one of the following statements is correct?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Per the ⚠ Alert in Chapter: Prelims 2010 (Simon Commission's key recommendation = abolition of Dyarchy at provincial level). Ending provincial Dyarchy meant replacing it with responsible government in the provinces — exactly what this option states.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — No inter-provincial council under the Home Department appears among the Commission's recorded recommendations; the record links the Simon Commission to one key recommendation — ending provincial Dyarchy.

(C) ✗ — Reversed logic: a bicameral legislature at the Centre was what the Indians' own Nehru Report proposed — a 7-year term for the upper house & a 5-year term for the lower house. The Simon Commission's recorded recommendation concerned provincial Dyarchy, not scrapping bicameralism.

(D) ✗ — The Commission's task was reviewing the constitutional reforms of the GOI Act 1919; police service pay scales were not its subject, and no such discriminatory-pay recommendation is on record. (Note: In March 1944, Mr C Rajagopalachari evolved a formula with the 'Full' approval of Gandhiji for the Congress. The scheme embodying the formula was (i) The league would endorse the demand for independence.)

2011PYQ 4(Prelims 2011)

With reference to the period of Indian freedom struggle, which of the following was/were recommended by the 'Nehru Report'? 1. Complete Independence for India. 2. Joint electorates for reservation of seats for minorities. 3. Provision of Fundamental Rights for the people of India in the Constitution. Select the correct answer using the codes given below

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — The Report asked for Dominion Status — the maximum level of self-government possible without leaving the British Empire — not complete independence, so statement 1 HISTORY (Simon Commission (1927) to Delhi Manifesto (November 1929)) falls. Statements 2 and 3 are both recorded: "No separate electorates" with reservation for Muslims in Muslim minority Provinces & at the Centre (= joint electorates with reserved seats), and "19 Fundamental Rights". Hence 2 and 3 only.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Statement 1 is the one demand the Report did not make: it stopped at Dominion Status, and even Bose's Purna Swaraj resolution against it failed at the Calcutta session.

(C) ✗ — Includes statement 1, which fails because the Report demanded Dominion Status, not complete independence; and it wrongly drops statement 2 — joint electorates with reserved seats for minorities are a recorded communal provision of the Report.

(D) ✗ — Fails on statement 1 alone: complete independence became INC's goal only later as Purna Swaraj; the Nehru Report itself asked for Dominion Status under the British Crown. (Note: The Nehru Report in August, 1928 was a memorandum outlining a proposed new dominion status Constitution for India. It was prepared by a committee of the all parties conference chaired by Motilal Nehru.)

2013PYQ 5(Prelims 2013)

The people of India agitated against the arrival of Simon Commission because

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Not a single Indian was included among its 7 members. INC opposed the Commission precisely on this ground — that the principle of self-government promised in the Montague Statement of 1917 was being violated.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — The opposite of the recorded position: the 10-year review was built into the GOI Act 1919 itself, as the path to the gradual Swaraj promise. Indians wanted the review — what they rejected was a review body with no Indian in it.

(B) ✗ — Two-step elimination: ending provincial Dyarchy meant moving toward responsible government — a gain for Indians, so no reason to agitate against it; and the agitation greeted the Commission's arrival, before it had recommended anything.

(D) ✗ — The Commission's object was reviewing the constitutional reforms of the GOI Act 1919; no partition scheme is recorded anywhere — even Jinnah's 14 Points of 1929 contained every divisive demand — except the demand for Pakistan, which came later. (Note: The Indian Statutory Commission also known as the Simon Commission was sent to India in 1927 to study the Constitutional reforms in India, mainly to study the working of the Government of India Act, 1919.)

2017PYQ 6(Prelims 2017)

The object of the Butler Committee of 1927 was to

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — The Butler Committee was appointed in 1927 to examine & clarify the relationship between the British Crown & the Princely States. Clarifying — and thereby improving — the government's relationship with the Indian States was exactly its object.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Centre–Province division of powers was the battlefield of the Nehru Report and ML1906's demands (residuary powers), not the Butler Committee's brief, which ran between the Crown and the Princely States.

(B) ✗ — Defining the Secretary of State's powers was no part of the Committee's recorded mandate; its subject was Paramountcy over the Princely States.

(C) ✗ — Nothing in the Committee's mandate or its 1929 Report touches the press: its two findings were the re-affirmation of British Paramountcy and the consent condition for Princely States joining a Union of India. (Note: A three-member committee headed by Harcourt Butler, appointed on 16th December, 1927 to examine the relationship between the native states and the paramount power.) FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words The Nehru Report (1928) represented the high-water mark of Hindu-Muslim constitutional consensus before partition. Examine why it ultimately failed.

Demand of the question: "Examine why it ultimately failed" demands a structural-causes analysis. Body must trace from initial unanimity to collapse.

Suggested intro (~25 words): The Nehru Report (August 1928) was the first all-Indian draft constitution. By March 1929 — when Jinnah issued his 14 Points — the consensus had collapsed entirely. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Nehru Report (August 1928)'):

1. The Report's promise (see 'Nehru Report (August 1928)'). Drafted by Motilal Nehru with Sapru, Shuaib Qureshi, Ali Imam — included a Bill of Fundamental Rights (first ever in Indian constitutional thought), Dominion Status, joint electorates with reserved seats for minorities. Initial All Parties Conference welcomed it.

2. The Calcutta breakdown (see 'Three Calcutta Amendments (December 1928) by ML1906'). Muslim League's Three Calcutta Amendments (December 1928) demanded: weighted Muslim representation in Punjab & Bengal, 1/3rd Muslim seats in the Central Legislature, residuary HISTORY (Simon Commission (1927) to Delhi Manifesto (November 1929)) powers to provinces. INC rejected these as exceeding what joint electorates could accommodate. The unanimity broke at this December 1928 moment.

3. Jinnah's 14 Points (see 'Jinnah's 14 Points Demand (March 1929)') — the formal rupture. March 1929: Jinnah codified the ML position into 14 demands — every one of them constitutional safeguards that the Nehru Report's framework could not accommodate. From this point, INC & ML moved on separate tracks.

Closing verdict: The Nehru Report failed because it tried to resolve the communal question through one constitutional formula — joint electorates with reserved seats — which the ML viewed as numerically inadequate protection. The unresolved arithmetic of representation drove the 1930s-40s Two-Nation theory & the eventual Pakistan demand.

Revise: 'Nehru Report' + 'Three Calcutta Amendments' + 'INC Calcutta Session' + 'Jinnah's 14 Points'.

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words Examine why the Diwali Declaration (October 1929) — Irwin's promise of Dominion Status as India's natural goal — failed to satisfy the INC despite its apparent generosity.

Demand of the question: "Examine why it failed despite generosity" demands tracing the British-side reception & the consequent Indian re-evaluation.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Irwin's October 1929 Diwali Declaration appeared to deliver what the INC had asked for — Dominion Status & a Round Table Conference. By December 1929, the INC had moved past it to Purna Swaraj. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Diwali Declaration / Irwin Statement (31 October 1929)'):

1. The Declaration's content (see 'Diwali Declaration / Irwin Statement (31 October 1929)'). Viceroy Irwin announced that Dominion Status was the "natural issue" of India's constitutional progress & called a Round Table Conference. Form: a generous Crown commitment.

2. The London reaction (see 'Diwali Declaration / Irwin Statement (31 October 1929)'). The Conservative opposition in London & sections of the British press reacted hostilely. Irwin was forced to clarify that "natural issue" was not "immediate goal" — & that no timeline was being committed. The substance was diluted in days.

3. The Delhi Manifesto + INC re-evaluation (see 'Delhi Manifesto (November 1929)'). INC's Delhi Manifesto conditionally welcomed the Declaration: prisoners released + RTC to draft Dominion Status constitution. Both conditions unmet. The INC concluded that Britain was unable to deliver Dominion Status even when willing — which is why Lahore Session (December 1929) shifted the goal to Purna Swaraj. The Diwali Declaration's failure was the immediate trigger.

Closing verdict: The Diwali Declaration showed that even a sincere British offer was constrained by British domestic politics. The INC's response — moving from Dominion Status to Purna Swaraj — was the rational conclusion that no British government could deliver what they could promise.

Revise: 'Diwali Declaration' + 'Delhi Manifesto'.
