1995PYQ 1(Prelims 1995)

The radical wing of the Congress Party with Jawaharlal Nehru as its one of its main leaders, founded the Independence for India League in opposition to

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — The League was founded (1928) by Nehru and Bose against the Nehru Report of August 1928, the first Indian constitutional blueprint, because it settled for Dominion Status when the radicals wanted complete independence.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Timeline check: the League dates from 1928, the Gandhi-Irwin Pact only from March 1931; a body cannot oppose a pact that does not yet exist.

(B) ✗ — The Home Rule Movement was 1916-18, a decade before the League; the target was the Report's Dominion Status goal.

(D) ✗ — The Mont-Ford reforms (GOI Act 1919) were what the Simon Commission came to review; the League opposed the Nehru Report's acceptance of Dominion Status.

1998PYQ 2(Prelims 1998)

Simon Commission of 1927 was boycotted because

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Not a single Indian was among its 7 members. INC opposed it on exactly this ground: the self-government principle of the Montague Statement of 1917 was violated by giving Indians no say in their own future constitution.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — All 7 members were British MPs; no Muslim League link. The ground was exclusion of Indians as a whole.

(C) ✗ — Near-miss: INC cited the 1917 self-government promise to show why exclusion was an insult, but the boycott trigger was the all-British composition.

(D) ✗ — The 7 British MPs worked as one team under Sir John Simon; the objection came from the Indian side.

2010PYQ 3(Prelims 2010)

With reference to Simon Commission's recommendations, which one of the following statements is correct?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — The Commission's key recorded recommendation was abolition of Dyarchy at the provincial level. Ending provincial Dyarchy meant replacing it with responsible government in the provinces, exactly what (a) says.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — No inter-provincial council under the Home Department is among its recorded recommendations.

(C) ✗ — Reversed: a bicameral Centre (7-year upper house, 5-year lower house) was proposed by the Indians' own Nehru Report.

(D) ✗ — Police pay scales were not its subject; no such discriminatory-pay recommendation is on record.

2011PYQ 4(Prelims 2011)

With reference to the period of Indian freedom struggle, which of the following was/were recommended by the 'Nehru Report'?
1. Complete Independence for India.
2. Joint electorates for reservation of seats for minorities.
3. Provision of Fundamental Rights for the people of India in the Constitution.
Select the correct answer using the codes given below

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — The Report asked for Dominion Status, the maximum self-government short of leaving the Empire, not complete independence, so 1 falls. 2 and 3 are recorded: no separate electorates, with reservation for Muslims in Muslim-minority provinces and at the Centre (joint electorates with reserved seats), and 19 Fundamental Rights.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Statement 1 is the one demand the Report did not make; Bose's Purna Swaraj resolution against it failed at the Calcutta session.

(C) ✗ — Includes statement 1 (Dominion Status, not independence) and wrongly drops statement 2.

(D) ✗ — Fails on statement 1 alone: Purna Swaraj became INC's goal only later (Lahore, December 1929).

2013PYQ 5(Prelims 2013)

The people of India agitated against the arrival of Simon Commission because

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Not a single Indian was among its 7 members. INC opposed it on precisely this ground, since the self-government principle of the Montague Statement of 1917 was being violated.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Opposite of the record: the 10-year review was built into the GOI Act 1919 itself. Indians wanted the review; they rejected a review body with no Indian in it.

(B) ✗ — Ending provincial Dyarchy meant moving toward responsible government, a gain; and the agitation greeted the Commission's arrival, before it had recommended anything.

(D) ✗ — No partition scheme is recorded; even Jinnah's 14 Points (1929) had every divisive demand except Pakistan, which came later.

2017PYQ 6(Prelims 2017)

The object of the Butler Committee of 1927 was to

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — A three-member committee under Harcourt Butler, appointed 16 December 1927, examined and clarified the relationship between the British Crown and the Princely States. Clarifying, and thereby improving, the government's relationship with the Indian States was its object.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Centre-Province division of powers (residuary powers) was the battlefield of the Nehru Report and ML1906's demands, not the Butler brief.

(B) ✗ — Defining the Secretary of State's powers was no part of its mandate; its subject was Paramountcy over the Princely States.

(C) ✗ — Nothing in its mandate or 1929 Report touches the press; its two findings were Paramountcy re-affirmed and the consent condition for States joining a Union of India.

FYQ 1

The Indian Statutory Commission, appointed in 1927, was headed by whom?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Sir John Simon chaired the 7-member all-British Commission; hence 'Simon Commission'.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Birkenhead was Secretary of State for India, not the Commission's head.

(C) ✗ — Attlee was one of the 7 British MPs on it, not its head; he later became PM (1945-51).

FYQ 2

Who challenged Indians in the British Parliament to draft their own constitution, a taunt that ultimately triggered the Nehru Report exercise?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Birkenhead, Secretary of State for India, challenged Indians in the British Parliament to draft a constitution, believing they could not overcome their internal differences. The result was the Nehru Report.

Wrong options:

(D) ✗ — The challenge came from the Secretary of State for India, not the PM.

Memory hook: Birkenhead taunts - Motilal drafts.

FYQ 3

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — The APC, formally set up in Delhi in 1928, was headed by M.A. Ansari. The drafting committee under it (May 1928) was headed by Motilal Nehru.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Motilal Nehru headed the drafting committee under the APC (May 1928), not the APC itself.

(B) ✗ — Jawaharlal Nehru did not head the APC.

(D) ✗ — Sapru was a prominent liberal leader but did not head the APC in 1928.

Memory hook: APC = Ansari Presides over Conference.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — O'Dwyer was Lt. Governor of Punjab during Jallianwala Bagh (1919), unrelated.

FYQ 6

Viceroy Irwin's Diwali Declaration, announcing the British intent to grant Dominion Status at a future date and proposing a Round Table Conference in London, was issued on which date?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — Irwin 26-31 issued it on 31 October 1929: Dominion Status implicit in the Montague Statement of 1917, intent at a future date, with an RTC in London. An intent only, no decision and no timeline.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — 26 January is associated with the later Purna Swaraj Day (1930) and Republic Day (1950).

(C) ✗ — November 1929 is when the Delhi Manifesto was passed, INC's response to the Declaration.

(D) ✗ — 31 December 1929 was the Lahore Session's Purna Swaraj resolution, not the Irwin Statement.

Memory hook: Diwali 1929 - Irwin lights only an 'intent'.

Chapter-derived Mains practice with the demand, guiding themes and a conclusion.

FYQ 1 The Nehru Report (1928) represented the high-water mark of Hindu-Muslim constitutional consensus before partition. Examine why it ultimately failed. (15 marks, 250 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine why it ultimately failed' demands a structural-causes analysis, tracing the path from initial unanimity to collapse.

Suggested intro (~25 words): The Nehru Report (August 1928) was the first all-Indian draft constitution. By March 1929, when Jinnah issued his 14 Points, the consensus had collapsed entirely.

1. The Report's promise: drafted by a committee under Motilal Nehru (APC head M.A. Ansari); Dominion Status, 19 Fundamental Rights (UAF demanded clearly for the first time), joint electorates with reserved seats for minorities, NWFP and Baluchistan made full provinces, Sindh separated conditionally.

2. The Calcutta breakdown (December 1928): Jinnah's Three Calcutta Amendments (1/3 Muslim seats in the Central Legislature, reservation in MMPs of Punjab and Bengal until UAF, residuary powers to Provinces) were rejected by the APC. Earlier the Four Delhi Proposals (Dec 1927) had been only partly met: separate electorates, 1/3 at Centre and MMP reservation were rejected.

3. Jinnah's 14 Points (March 1929): the formal rupture, codifying every Muslim communal demand (separate electorates, 1/3 at Centre and in cabinets, federalism with residuary powers to Provinces); everything except Pakistan. INC and ML1906 moved on separate tracks, and Birkenhead's taunt stood vindicated.

Closing verdict: the Report tried to settle the communal question with one formula, joint electorates with reserved seats, which ML1906 saw as numerically inadequate protection. The unresolved arithmetic of representation fed the Two-Nation theory and eventually Pakistan.

Revise: Nehru Report + Three Calcutta Amendments + INC Calcutta Session + Jinnah's 14 Points.

Conflict flag: companion says INC rejected the Calcutta amendments; the handout says the APC rejected them (INC's Calcutta session of December 1928 approved the Nehru Report). Handout followed above. The companion's committee names (Sapru, Shuaib Qureshi, Ali Imam) are not in the handout, so they are left out.

FYQ 2 Examine why the Diwali Declaration (October 1929), Irwin's promise of Dominion Status as India's natural goal, failed to satisfy the INC despite its apparent generosity. (10 marks, 150 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine why it failed despite generosity' demands the substance of the offer and INC's consequent re-evaluation.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Irwin 26-31's Diwali Declaration of 31 October 1929 appeared to deliver what INC had asked for, Dominion Status and a Round Table Conference. By December 1929 INC had moved past it to Purna Swaraj.

1. The Declaration's content: Dominion Status said to be implicit in the Montague Statement of 1917, the British intent to grant it at a future date, and an RTC in London.

2. Its hollowness (handout): no decision, only an intent; no timeline, only a vague future date; the wrong forum, an RTC in London rather than a Constituent Assembly in India. Britain offered nothing concrete.

3. The Delhi Manifesto and re-evaluation (November 1929): INC asked for the RTC as a Constituent Assembly implementing Dominion Status, an INC majority of Indian delegates, and amnesty in the Bomb in CLA and Meerut cases. Irwin refused all of it, so the Lahore Session (December 1929) made Purna Swaraj the goal, with 26 January 1930 as Independence Day.

Closing verdict: a vague, untimed offer through a London RTC could not satisfy a party wanting a Constituent Assembly; its failure was the immediate trigger for Purna Swaraj.

Revise: Diwali Declaration + Delhi Manifesto.

Conflict flag: companion says the Manifesto 'conditionally welcomed' the Declaration with two conditions (prisoners released + RTC), that London Conservatives forced Irwin to dilute 'natural issue' to 'not an immediate goal', and that both conditions went unmet. The handout lists three conditions, all refused, and does not mention the Conservative dilution. Handout followed; the dilution claim is dropped.
