In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Part I (Arts. 1 to 4) defines the Union and its territory. Art. 1: 'India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States', not a federation, because States cannot secede. Territory has three parts: States, Union Territories, acquired territories.
  • Art. 2: Parliament can admit or establish new States from outside. Art. 3: Parliament can form new States, change area, boundaries or names of existing States. Art. 4: such laws are not Art. 368 amendments, so a simple majority is enough.
  • Art. 3 needs the President's recommendation and a reference to the State legislature, but its views are not binding. So India is 'an indestructible Union of destructible States'.
  • Berubari (1960):
    • Ceding territory to a foreign country needs an Art. 368 amendment (9th Amendment, 1960)
    • Settling a boundary dispute (1969) does not. The 100th Amendment (2015) exchanged enclaves with Bangladesh: India gave 111, got 51
  • In 1950 there were 29 units (Part A, B, C, D). The Dhar Commission and JVP Committee said no to language
  • After Andhra State (1953) the Fazl Ali Commission led to the States Reorganisation Act, 1956: 14 States and 6 UTs
  • Since then the map kept changing, ending with 28 States and 8 UTs: Telangana (2014) and the J&K Reorganisation Act (2019) are the latest.

1 · Part I: Articles 1 to 4#

  • Part I lays down the political identity and territorial structure of India. Articles 1 to 4 set the legal foundation for territorial integrity and evolution.

Article 1: Union of States

  • "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States." The compound name reconciles the Constituent Assembly's views, some preferring the traditional name and others the contemporary one.
  • "Union of States" was preferred to "Federation of States" (as explained by Ambedkar and the Assembly debates): India is not a compact among sovereign units as in the American model, but a single, indivisible sovereign whole whose divisions exist for administrative convenience.
  • So States have no unilateral right to secede
  • The Union is indestructible

Territory of India: three categories (Art. 1)

CategoryWhat it is
(i)Territories that constitute the States
(ii)Union Territories named in the First Schedule
(iii)Territories that may be acquired by the Government of India

Union of India against territory of India

  • Union of India: The collective of States
  • Territory of India: Broader: States, Union Territories and any future acquisitions
  • At present there are 28 States and 8 Union Territories.
  • The First Schedule is the formal register of States and UTs.
  • States share powers with the Centre
  • UTs and acquired territories are ordinarily run directly by the Centre. Part XXI has special provisions for specified States, and the Fifth and Sixth Schedules create separate regimes for scheduled and tribal areas

Acquiring territory

  • India can acquire territory by modes recognised in international law: cession by treaty (including purchase, gift or plebiscite), occupation of territory not under a recognised sovereign, or other lawful means. Examples integrated since 1950: Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Goa, Daman and Diu, Puducherry and Sikkim.
  • Memory aid (Three kinds of territory): States, Union Territories, Acquired: "Some Unusual Antiques".

Also in the handout

  • The choice of the compound phrase — using both India and Bharat — reflected differing views in the Constituent Assembly
  • Some members preferred the traditional appellation while others favoured the contemporary name, and the adopted wording reconciles both positions
  • Part XXI contains such special arrangements that apply to specified States.
  • Historical incorporations since the commencement of the Constitution provide concrete instances: territories like Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Goa, Daman and Diu, Puducherry, and Sikkim were integrated into the Republic through processes consistent with constitutional and international legal norms
  • Their formal integration and the legal adjustments required are treated elsewhere in the constitutional text and implementing legislation

2 · Articles 2, 3 and 4 at a glance#

Articles related to the Union and its territory

ArticleSubject matter
1Name and territorial extent: "India, that is Bharat"; States and territories per the First Schedule
2Admission or establishment of new States by Parliament; allows India to accept territories from outside
2AAssociation of Sikkim as an "associate state" (added by the 35th Amendment, 1974); repealed after Sikkim became a full State in 1975

Article 2 against Article 3

  • Article 2:
    • Admission or establishment of States from outside the present roster (existing political units or new ones)
    • Terms set by Parliament
  • Article 3: Internal reorganisation among existing States: form, enlarge, reduce, change boundaries or names
  • Memory aid (Articles 1 to 4): Name (1), Admit (2), Reshape (3), Schedules and consequences (4): "Never Ask Rude Students".

3 · Article 3: Parliament's power to reorganise States#

  • Article 3 empowers Parliament to make law for:
  • Forming a new State by separating territory from a State, uniting two or more States or parts, or merging any territory with a part of a State.
  • Increasing a State's area.
  • Reducing a State's area.
  • Changing the boundaries of a State.
  • Changing the name of a State.

The procedure under Article 3

  • Step 1: The Bill needs the President's recommendation before it is introduced in Parliament
  • Step 2: The President refers the proposal to the State legislature for its views within a specified time
  • Views: Not binding on the President or Parliament
  • Amendments: Even if the Bill is amended in Parliament, no fresh consultation is needed
  • UT: For a Union Territory, Parliament can act on its own without referring to any legislature
  • Reading note: The handout says both that the Bill needs the President's recommendation to be introduced and that the President must forward it to the State legislature before granting this recommendation. The point is the same: consultation is mandatory in form, not decisive in substance.

Nature of the Union

Indian Union against the USA

  • India:
    • Parliament holds complete power to redraw the map
    • States have no guaranteed territorial integrity: "An indestructible Union of destructible States"
  • USA:
    • Federal structure protects State boundaries
    • no change without the States' consent: "An indestructible Union of indestructible States"
  • Memory aid (Article 3 verbs): Form, Increase, Reduce, Change boundaries, Change name: "Five Indian Rivers Cross Cities".

Article 4: ordinary law

  • A law under Article 2 or Article 3 is not an amendment under Article 368.
  • It can pass by simple majority and ordinary legislative procedure.
  • Shortcut: PYQ 1994 shortcut. Parliament can increase, diminish, alter boundary and alter name. So the statements "cannot alter the boundary" and "cannot alter the name" are the incorrect ones.

Also in the handout

  • Those views are consultative in character
  • Parliament is not legally bound by the opinion of the State legislature(s) and retains the ultimate authority to legislate on territorial re-adjustments
  • This power is contained in Article 3 under Part-I of the Constitution, which clearly vests the authority in the Parliament to reorganize states in multiple ways.
  • Despite this, popular movements continued to demand linguistic states.

4 · Cession of territory: Berubari and boundary disputes#

Cession and the courts

  • Question: Does Art. 3's power to diminish a State's area allow ceding Indian territory to a foreign State?
  • 1960: Supreme Court: No. Cession is not covered by Art. 3 and needs a constitutional amendment under Art. 368 (case: part of Berubari Union, West Bengal, proposed for Pakistan)
  • Result: 9th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1960 passed to implement the transfer
  • 1969: Supreme Court: settling a boundary dispute with another country does not need an amendment, as it is not a cession of Indian territory

Cession against boundary settlement

  • Cession (1960): Giving away Indian territory: needs Art. 368 amendment
  • Boundary settlement (1969): Resolving a dispute over the line: no amendment needed
  • Memory aid (Court rulings): Cession needs amendment (1960), Boundary settlement does not (1969): "Cede = Change the Constitution, Boundary = Bypass".

Also in the handout

  • The Commission was tasked with re-examining the question of state reorganisation comprehensively.

7 · Language as the basis of reorganisation#

  • In independent India the main basis for reorganising States was language, a break from the colonial pattern of administrative convenience.

The debate

  • For linguistic States:
    • 1920s Congress consensus
    • Gandhiji organised Pradesh Congress Committees on linguistic lines
    • Language is more secular than religion or caste
    • Mother-tongue education
    • Masses participate better
    • Regional languages preserved and enriched
  • Against linguistic States:
    • Nehru, after seeing Partition, feared Lilliputian States
    • Focus should be national identity
    • Language binds strongly so secession demands may follow, as in Europe
    • May raise demands for jobs and subsidies and xenophobia against people from other States

Commissions and committees

  • 1948 June:
    • Linguistic Provinces Commission (S.K. Dhar)
    • Report December 1948 recommends administrative convenience, not language
    • Widespread dissatisfaction
  • 1948 Dec: Congress appoints the JVP Committee: Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, Pattabhi Sitaramayya
  • 1949 Apr: JVP report: language should not be the basis
  • 1953 Oct: Andhra State: first linguistic State of independent India, after the 56-day hunger strike and death of Potti Sriramulu (Telugu areas of Madras State)
  • 1953 Dec: States Reorganisation Commission: Fazl Ali, K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru
  • 1955 Sept:
    • Report: language an important factor, but not strict "one language, one State"
    • Unity of India first
  • 1956: States Reorganisation Act and 7th Amendment
Commissions and committees
  • Memory aid (JVP): Jawaharlal, Vallabhbhai, Pattabhi: "Just Very Peaceful".
  • Memory aid (Fazl Ali's three): Fazl Ali, Panikkar, Kunzru: "Fast Postman Knocks".

Recall sheet#

Cover the right column and answer. Same questions as the flashcards.

What does Article 1 say?
"India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."
Why "Union" and not "Federation"?
India is not a compact among sovereign units; it is one indivisible whole and States cannot secede.
What are the three categories of Indian territory?
Territories of the States; Union Territories (First Schedule); territories that may be acquired.
How many States and UTs now?
28 States and 8 Union Territories.
What does Article 2 do?
Parliament may admit or establish new States on such terms as it thinks fit.
What can Parliament do under Article 3?
Form new States; increase or reduce area; change boundaries; change names.
Procedure under Article 3?
President's recommendation before the Bill; State legislature's views sought but not binding; no fresh consultation after amendment; UTs need no referral.
What does Article 4 say?
Laws under Arts. 2 or 3 are not amendments under Art. 368 and pass by simple majority; they amend the First and Fourth Schedules.
India's description against the USA's?
India: indestructible Union of destructible States. USA: indestructible Union of indestructible States.
What did the Supreme Court hold in 1960 on cession?
Cession of Indian territory needs a constitutional amendment under Art. 368 (Berubari).
What did the Supreme Court hold in 1969?
A boundary settlement with another country needs no amendment.
Which Amendment implemented the Berubari transfer?
The 9th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1960.
What did the 100th Amendment (2015) do?
Gave effect to the Land Boundary Agreement: India gave 111 enclaves, Bangladesh gave 51; settled adverse possession and a 6.1 km stretch; changed the First Schedule for Assam, West Bengal, Meghalaya and Tripura.
Name the awards and agreements before the 100th Amendment.
Radcliffe Award 1947; Bagge Award 1950; Nehru-Noon Agreement 1958; 9th Amendment 1960; 1974 agreement (16 May); 2011 Protocol (6 September).
How many princely States were there and how many joined India?
552; 549 joined; Hyderabad, Junagarh and Kashmir initially refused.
How were Hyderabad, Junagarh and Kashmir integrated?
Police action (Operation Polo); referendum; Instrument of Accession.
Describe the 1950 classification.
Part A 9 provinces; Part B 9 princely States; Part C 10 centrally run; Part D A&N Islands: 29 units.
Which commission rejected linguistic States in 1948?
S.K. Dhar Commission (report December 1948): administrative convenience.
Who made up the JVP Committee and what was its view?
Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, Pattabhi Sitaramayya; report April 1949: language should not be the basis.
Which leader's hunger strike led to Andhra State?
Potti Sriramulu (56 days); Andhra State came in October 1953.
Who made up the Fazl Ali Commission?
Fazl Ali, K.M. Panikkar and H.N. Kunzru (appointed December 1953, report September 1955).
Four factors of the SRC?
Unity and security; linguistic and cultural homogeneity; financial, economic and administrative considerations; welfare and development planning.
What did the SRC recommend and what happened in 1956?
Recommended 16 States and 3 territories; the SRA and 7th Amendment gave 14 States and 6 UTs on 1 November 1956.
Which State is the 15th, 16th, 17th, 18th?
Gujarat (1960), Nagaland (1963), Haryana (1966), Himachal Pradesh (1971).
Which are the 19th to 21st States?
Manipur, Tripura, Meghalaya (1972).
Which is the 22nd State and how did it become one?
Sikkim: associate State in 1974 (35th Amendment, Art. 2A), full State in 1975 (36th Amendment, Art. 371-F).
Which are the 23rd to 25th States?
Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Goa (1987).
Which are the 26th to 29th States?
Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand (2000); Telangana (2014).
What happened in 2019?
Article 370 status revoked; J&K Reorganisation Act made J&K and Ladakh two UTs.
Name the old and new names for Madras, Mysore, Orissa.
Madras to Tamil Nadu (1969); Mysore to Karnataka (1973); Orissa to Odisha (2011).
Why a 2nd SRC?
Many demands for new States, political rather than scientific handling, an evolving process, regional imbalances after 30 years of LPG reforms, and large populations.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Article 2 versus Article 3 Art. 2 deals with admitting or establishing new States that are outside the Union's present States. Art. 3 deals with changes among existing States of the Union. Rule: Art. 2 = new from outside; Art. 3 = internal readjustment.
Trap 2 · Cession versus boundary settlement In 1960 (Berubari) the Supreme Court said ceding territory to a foreign country needs an Art. 368 amendment (9th Amendment, 1960). In 1969 it said settling a boundary dispute needs no amendment. Rule: giving away land = amendment; fixing a disputed border = executive action.
Trap 3 · 28 States or 29 The handout says there are 28 States and 8 UTs, yet its list numbers Telangana (2014) as the 29th State formed. Rule: write 28 States + 8 UTs for today; 29th is only the serial of Telangana's creation (J&K became a UT in 2019).
Trap 4 · First linguistic State Odisha was organised on a linguistic basis in 1936 under British rule. The first linguistic State of independent India is Andhra State (October 1953), after Potti Sriramulu's 56-day fast. Rule: independent India's first = Andhra (1953); Odisha 1936 was before independence.

Book edition: simple words and memory words#

In simple words

  • Part I (Arts. 1 to 4) defines the Union and its territory. Art. 1: 'India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States', not a federation, because States cannot secede. Territory has three parts: States, Union Territories, acquired territories.
  • Art. 2: Parliament can admit or establish new States from outside. Art. 3: Parliament can form new States, change area, boundaries or names of existing States. Art. 4: such laws are not Art. 368 amendments, so a simple majority is enough.
  • Art. 3 needs the President's recommendation and a reference to the State legislature, but its views are not binding. So India is 'an indestructible Union of destructible States'.
  • Berubari (1960): ceding territory to a foreign country needs an Art. 368 amendment (9th Amendment, 1960); settling a boundary dispute (1969) does not. The 100th Amendment (2015) exchanged enclaves with Bangladesh: India gave 111, got 51.
  • In 1950 there were 29 units (Part A, B, C, D). The Dhar Commission and JVP Committee said no to language; after Andhra State (1953) the Fazl Ali Commission led to the States Reorganisation Act, 1956: 14 States and 6 UTs.
  • Since then the map kept changing, ending with 28 States and 8 UTs: Telangana (2014) and the J&K Reorganisation Act (2019) are the latest.
