1 · Recommendations OF THE 2ND Administrative Reforms Commission · Punchhi Commission#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: High. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Local Self-Government module pp. 23-25. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • The 2nd ARC (6th Report, Local Governance, 2007) wanted devolution to be binding ("shall" for "may"), activity mapping and subsidiarity, an independent SEC, a Local Body Ombudsman, no vertical budget approval, no MPs/MLAs in local bodies, rotation after two terms, a directly elected Mayor and a four-tier municipal set-up. The Punchhi Commission added subsidiarity, activity mapping and genuine devolution of the 3Fs.

2ND ARC: 6TH Report, Local Governance (2007)

Key point

The 2nd ARC, in its 6th Report, "Local Governance: An Inspiring Journey into the Future" (2007), recommended reforms to strengthen local self-government.

Recommendations ON Panchayati RAJ Institutions

1. Replacing "May" with "Shall" in Articles 243G and 243W

  • Replace "may", which indicates discretion, with "shall", which makes devolution binding.
  • Significance: States would be required to devolve appropriate powers to Panchayats and Municipalities rather than treating devolution as optional.

2. Legislative Council in All States

  • A Legislative Council in every State, consisting of members elected by local bodies.
  • Objective: strengthen the voice of local bodies at the State level and make the Council an effective institution of representation.

3. Local Legislators Should Not Be Members

  • MPs and MLAs should not become members of PRIs and Municipalities, as their presence may weaken the initiative and leadership of local elected representatives.

4. Appointment of State Election Commissioner

  • By the Governor on the recommendation of a committee comprising:
    • Chief Minister
    • Speaker of the Legislative Assembly
    • Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Assembly

5. Delimitation and Reservation by SEC

  • The task of delimitation of constituencies and reservation of seats should be entrusted to the State Election Commission rather than being controlled by State political authorities.

6. National Platform for SEC-ECI Interaction

  • A national platform where State Election Commissions and the Election Commission of India can interact periodically.
  • Significance: exchange of experiences, resources and best practices would improve election management.

7. Model Law on Devolution

  • The Central Government should prepare a Framework Law on Devolution, based on two principles:
  • A. Activity Mapping: unbundling a broad government function into specific activities and assigning each activity to the appropriate level of government.
  • B. Principle of Subsidiarity: assigning functions to the lowest level of government capable of performing them effectively.
  • The allocation should consider:
    • Homogeneity vs Heterogeneity (uniform implementation or adaptable to local needs)
    • Economies of Scale (more efficient at a higher level?)
    • Externalities (do decisions of one Panchayat adversely affect neighbouring areas?)

8. Assembly Electoral Rolls for Local Elections

  • Use Assembly electoral rolls for local body elections to reduce discrepancies and duplication.

9. Rotation after Two Terms

  • Reserved seats should be rotated after two terms of five years each, instead of frequent rotation after every term.
  • Significance: provides sufficient time for elected representatives to develop lasting ties with their constituencies.

10. SFC Report and Action Taken Report

  • The ATR on SFC recommendations should be placed before the State Legislature within six months of submission of the SFC report.

11. Standing Committee of the Legislative Assembly on Local Bodies

  • A dedicated Standing Committee of the State Legislature should examine:
    • Performance of local bodies
    • Their grievances and institutional problems
    • Financial accountability and audit findings
    • Progress in decentralisation

12. Separate Local Body Ombudsman

  • Similar in purpose to the Lokpal and Lokayukta.
  • It should investigate complaints of corruption and maladministration against elected and appointed functionaries of local bodies.

13. Vertical Approval of Budget to Be Discontinued

  • The budget of local bodies should not be subject to routine approval by the next higher tier or State Government.
  • Rationale: just as the State Government does not require approval of its budget by the Union Government, local bodies should enjoy appropriate budgetary autonomy.

14. Abolish Parastatals and Parallel Programmes

  • Parallel agencies such as DRDA and DUDA (District Urban Development Agency) should be integrated with appropriate elected local bodies.
  • Parallel programmes such as MPLADS and MLALADS, which overlap with local development responsibilities, should be abolished.

15. Restrict State Government's Powers

  • States should not arbitrarily exercise powers to suspend resolutions of local bodies, suspend or remove elected members, or supersede or prematurely dissolve PRIs.
  • Such matters should be dealt with through appropriate independent institutions, including the Local Body Ombudsman, subject to legal safeguards.

16. Local Bodies Should Recruit Their Own Personnel

  • Local bodies should have powers to recruit their own personnel and determine their conditions of service, subject to State laws and standards.

Way forward / key point

2nd ARC: recommended moving towards a Separate System, giving local bodies greater control over their personnel.

Recommendations FOR Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)

1. Set Up the 2nd National Commission on Urbanisation

  • To examine changing patterns of urbanisation and suggest reforms. The first Commission was established in 1985 under Charles Correa.

2. Four-Tier Municipal Set-Up

  • Area Sabha → Area Committee → Ward Committee → Municipal Corporation
  • Area Sabha: a participatory body covering approximately one or two polling booths, analogous to the Gram Sabha.
  • Area Committee: a smaller representative body linked to the Area Sabha.
  • Ward Committee: includes representatives from the constituent neighbourhood-level bodies.
  • Municipal Corporation: performs city-level governance functions.
  • Significance: strengthens citizen participation and accountability at the neighbourhood level.

3. Mayor to Be Elected Directly

  • The Mayor should be directly elected and given real executive powers, functioning as the Chief Executive.
  • A Mayor-Cabinet system should assist the Mayor
  • The Municipal Council should exercise policy, budgetary and oversight responsibilities
  • Objective: reduce the Executive-Deliberative Dichotomy between the elected Mayor and the State-appointed Municipal Commissioner.

4. Representation to Non-Resident Stakeholders

  • People residing in one municipal area but working in another should be given suitable opportunities for representation and consultation, as their interests are also affected by municipal decisions.

5. Separate Ward Committee for Each Ward

  • One Ward Committee for every ward, rather than one committee covering several wards, makes urban participation more local, manageable and accountable.

Punchhi Commission Recommendations

Punchhi Commission on Centre-State Relations (2007-10)

  • 1. Principle of Subsidiarity: functions should be performed by the lowest level of government capable of performing them effectively.
  • 2. Activity Mapping: responsibilities should be clearly distributed among the Union, States and local bodies.
  • 3. Genuine Devolution: local bodies should receive adequate Funds, Functions and Functionaries rather than merely implementing schemes of higher governments.
  • 4. Inter-Governmental Coordination: strengthen institutional consultation and coordination to improve implementation of development programmes.
  • Significance: Local Self-Government should be treated as an important part of democratic decentralisation and cooperative federalism.

2 · Finance Commission Recommendations ON Local Bodies · 13TH TO 16TH FC · Digital Local Governance#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: High. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Local Self-Government module pp. 26-31. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Since the 11th Finance Commission, improving local body finances has been part of the UFC mandate under Article 280(3)(bb) and (c). Grants have grown from Rs 87,519 crore (13th FC) to Rs 2,87,436 crore (14th), Rs 4,36,361 crore (15th) and Rs 7,91,493 crore (16th, 2026-31). Reforms shifted from basic and performance grants to untied and tied splits, entry conditions, property tax floor rates, and OSR-linked performance. Digital tools such as eGramSwaraj and SVAMITVA support transparency.

Union Finance Commission AND Local Bodies

Mandate

  • The UFC recommends measures to improve the finances of local bodies, both urban and rural.
  • Improvement of local body finances has been part of the Finance Commission's mandate since the 11th Finance Commission.
  • Under Article 280(3)(bb) and (c), the UFC recommends measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of States to supplement the resources of Panchayats and Municipalities.
  • Finance Commissions have recommended separate grants for local bodies, over and above the States' share in Central taxes.

Thirteenth Finance Commission

  • Recommended Rs 87,519 crore for local bodies in two parts:
    • 1. Basic Grants (without performance conditionalities)
    • 2. Performance Grants (based on fulfilment of specified conditions, including improvements in accounts and financial management)

State Governments' views (during consultations with the 14th FC)

  • 1. Local Body Grants from Divisible Pool: local bodies should receive a specified percentage of the Centre's divisible pool of taxes. Some States demanded a share of up to 5%.
  • 2. Fewer Conditionalities: conditions for obtaining Performance Grants should be reduced because local bodies often find them difficult to fulfil.

Fourteenth Finance Commission (2015-20)

Grants

  • Recommended grants of Rs 2,87,436 crore for local bodies: Rural Local Bodies Rs 2,00,292 crore (directed to Gram Panchayats) and Urban Local Bodies Rs 87,144 crore.

Use and conditions

  • Basic Grants were intended for water supply, sanitation, sewerage, roads, street lighting and other essential services.
  • Conditions for Performance Grants:
    • 1. Accounts Submission (prescribed audited accounts)
    • 2. Increase in Own Revenue (demonstrate improvement in revenue collection)
    • 3. Service-Level Benchmarks (ULBs report performance against specified public service benchmarks)
  • Trust-based approach: States should not impose additional conditionalities on the release of FC grants beyond those specified.

SFC proposals and 14th FC on SFCs

  • 1. National Platform for Interaction between SFCs: a national coordination platform where SFCs meet regularly to exchange views, ideas and experiences.
  • 2. Simplification of Account Formats:
    • Accounting is technical in nature
    • Difficult formats lead to delays, so formats should be simplified
  • 14th FC on strengthening SFCs:
    • Timely constitution of SFCs
    • Proper administrative support
    • Adequate resources
    • Timely submission of SFC reports and ATRs to State Legislatures

Accounts and audits

  • Importance:
    • Financial Accountability (prevents misuse of public resources)
    • Realistic Financial Assessment (reliable information on revenue and expenditure)
  • 1. Continue Technical Guidance of CAG: the 14th FC recommended continuing the technical guidance and supervision of the CAG in local body accounting and audits, as suggested by the 13th FC.
  • 2. Comprehensive Preparation of Accounts: covering all revenue and expenditure.

Xivth FC ON Local BODY Taxes

Property Tax

  • If effectively implemented, Property Tax can become the mainstay of local body taxes and revenue.
  • Issues:
    • 1. Inadequate Devolution
    • 2. Absence of Rules
    • 3. Irregular Revision
    • 4. Excessive Exemptions
    • 5. Corruption
    • 6. Inadequate Property Database
    • 7. Lack of Political Will (Elite Capture)
    • 8. Annual Rental Value (ARV) basis is difficult to assess and may be manipulated
  • Suggestions by the 14th FC:
    • 1. Fast-Track Property Tax Reforms
    • 2. Minimal Exemptions
    • 3. Regular Revision
    • 4. Plinth Area basis
    • 5. Vacant Land Tax
    • 6. Betterment Tax (tax on the increase in asset value when public improvements raise the value of private property)
    • 7. Advertisement Tax (hoardings, walls, public transport)
    • 8. Entertainment Tax (its present application must account for changes following GST)

Tax on Professions: Article 276

  • Article 276 provides for a maximum Profession Tax of Rs 2,500 per person annually.
  • The 14th FC recommended increasing the ceiling to Rs 12,000 annually and enabling subsequent revisions through Parliamentary legislation, without requiring repeated constitutional amendments.
  • Important: Rs 12,000 was a recommendation, not the current constitutional ceiling.

Non-tax Sources

Four sources

  • 1. Common Productive Assets:
    • Ponds, orchards and other community property could be developed to generate regular revenue
    • Leased on payment of annual fees, with fees revised periodically
  • 2. Share of Cess or Royalty on Minor Minerals:
    • Mining puts a disproportionate burden on local infrastructure and the environment
    • States should share appropriate cess or royalty with local bodies in whose jurisdiction mining takes place
  • 3. Service Charges on Government Property:
    • Articles 285 and 289 provide constitutional tax exemptions for Union property and State property or income respectively, yet government properties use municipal services
    • Union and State Governments should arrange appropriate service charges
  • 4. Municipal Bonds:
    • A source of additional funds for urban infrastructure
    • Access was largely confined to major Municipal Corporations
    • Financing should not remain confined to Tier-I Municipalities but be encouraged for eligible Municipal Councils and Nagar Panchayats
  • Additional 2nd ARC recommendation: large Municipalities may establish Municipal Public Sector Enterprises, such as municipal bus services. The 2nd ARC also recommended higher fines for civic offences such as spitting and littering.

Fifteenth Finance Commission (2021-26)

Overview

  • Chaired by N. K. Singh
  • Recommended Rs 4,36,361 crore for local governments during 2021-26
  • Consultations:
    • Ministry of Panchayati Raj (higher grants for PRIs, Panchayat office buildings)
    • Housing and Urban Affairs (higher grants for ULBs)
    • Environment (air quality grants for Million Plus Cities)
    • Finance (timely SFC reports as a condition)
    • Women and Child Development (gender budgeting, Mahila Sabhas)
    • Tribal Affairs (excluded tribal and Scheduled Areas)
    • State Governments (grants for all three tiers, predictable share)
    • Defence (Cantonment Boards)

1. Rural Local Bodies (Rs 2,36,805 crore)

  • Covered all three tiers:
    • Gram Panchayats 70-85%
    • Block Panchayats 10-25%
    • Zila Panchayats 5-15%
  • Grant conditions:
    • Online availability of provisional accounts of the previous year
    • Online availability of audited accounts of the year before the previous year
    • Compliance with applicable SFC-related requirements
  • Division of rural grants:
    • 40% untied
    • 30% for drinking water, rainwater harvesting and water recycling
    • 30% for sanitation and maintenance of Open Defecation Free (ODF) status

2. Urban Local Bodies (Rs 1,21,055 crore)

  • Category I:
    • Million Plus Cities: Million Plus Cities Challenge Fund Rs 38,196 crore
    • One-third for improving ambient air quality
    • Remaining for drinking water, sanitation and solid waste management
  • Category II:
    • Other Cities and Towns: Rs 82,859 crore
    • 40% untied and 60% tied for specified basic services
  • Conditions for urban grants:
    • Online accounts (provisional and audited)
    • States should notify minimum floor rates for property tax
    • Municipalities should improve property tax collection in accordance with prescribed growth-related conditions
  • Cantonment Boards should receive an appropriate share of grants for non-Million Plus urban areas on a population basis.

3. Timely release of grants

  • The Union Government should release grants in two instalments annually. States should transfer funds to local bodies within 10 days. Delays should attract interest payable by the State Government.

4. Health, new cities, shared services, profession tax

  • Grants for Health:
    • Rs 70,051 crore for local health infrastructure
    • Health is covered under both the Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules
  • New Cities Grants: Rs 8,000 crore performance-based challenge fund for incubation of eight new cities in eight different States.
  • Municipal Shared Service Centres:
    • Rs 450 crore
    • Smaller Municipalities could share trained staff and professional services to address shortages of personnel
  • Profession Tax: the 15th FC also recommended revising the Rs 2,500 ceiling.

Sixteenth Finance Commission (2026-31)

Overview

  • Chaired by Dr Arvind Panagariya
  • Recommended Rs 7,91,493 crore for local bodies during 2026-31: Rural Local Bodies Rs 4,35,236 crore and Urban Local Bodies Rs 3,56,257 crore. (verify)
  • 1. Basic and Performance Grants: ordinary local body grants are divided into 80% Basic and 20% Performance.
  • 2. Tied and Untied Grants:
    • 50% of Basic Grants are tied to sanitation, solid waste management and water management
    • The remaining 50% are untied
  • 3. Performance Grants: linked to improvement in Own Source Revenue (OSR) and State-level fiscal support to local bodies.
  • 4. Mandatory Entry Criteria: local bodies must fulfil conditions relating to their constitutional constitution, public disclosure of accounts and timely constitution of SFCs.
  • 5. Special Infrastructure Grants: Rs 56,100 crore for specified urban wastewater infrastructure.
  • 6. Urbanisation Premium: Rs 10,000 crore to support eligible rural-to-urban transitions.
  • Significance: emphasises financial transparency, stronger SFCs, OSR generation and performance-based grants.
