1 · Introduction and global perspective#

The Central Information Commission (CIC) is a statutory body established under the Right to Information Act, 2005. The right to information promotes transparency, accountability and participatory democracy. The judiciary has treated it, through judicial pronouncements, as a fundamental right under Article 19(1)(a).

  • The CIC is the final appellate authority in RTI matters, but its decision can be challenged by a writ petition before any High Court.
  • By ensuring time-bound disclosure and penalising delays or denials, it strengthens participatory democracy and protects the "Right to Know", a derivative of Article 19(1)(a).

Handout alert

The handout says the 2019 Amendment left no fixed tenure (the Centre prescribes it), and also quotes the DoPT notification (October 2019) of 3 years or age 65. Read them together: the Act no longer fixes tenure; the Centre's current rule is 3 years or 65. For the SIC the handout says "as prescribed by the Central Government or until age 65" and gives no fixed figure.

Grounds for removal (Section 14)

  • Insolvency.
  • Conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude (in the President's opinion).
  • Paid employment outside official duties.
  • Mental or physical infirmity (in the President's opinion).
  • Financial or other interests prejudicially affecting official duties.
  • Proved misbehaviour or incapacity, after inquiry by the Supreme Court and its recommendation for removal.

3 · Powers and functions of the CIC#

The CIC is the final appellate authority for matters concerning Central Public Authorities under the RTI Act. It makes sure the right is not curtailed by delay, denial or excessive fees.

Six roles
AICASQ

Adjudicatory, Investigative, Compliance and enforcement, Advisory and reporting, Supervisory and monitoring, Quasi-judicial: "A Informed Citizen Asks, Sees, Questions."

1. Adjudicatory powers

  • Second appeals against orders of the First Appellate Authority, under Section 19(3).
  • Complaints under Section 18 from any person who: was denied access to information; could not submit an RTI request because no PIO was appointed; was charged unreasonable fees; got incomplete, false or misleading information.

2. Investigative and inquiry powers

  • Can inquire suo motu or on a complaint when there are reasonable grounds.
  • Civil court powers under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908: summon and compel attendance and evidence on oath; discovery and inspection of documents; evidence on affidavits; requisition any public record from any court or office; issue summons for examination of witnesses or documents; inspect records in camera if required to protect sensitive information.
  • Section 18(3): no public authority may withhold any record from the Commission during the inquiry.

3. Compliance and enforcement powers

What the CIC can order
PowerDetail
DisclosureDirect disclosure in a specified form or language
PIOOrder appointment of a PIO where none exists
Proactive publicationMandate publication under Section 4
Record managementRecommend digitisation, indexing, preservation systems
CompensationOrder compensation to applicants for loss or detriment from denial or delay
PenaltyOn errant PIOs: Rs 250 per day, up to Rs 25,000; recommend disciplinary action for persistent violation
Penalty shortcut

250 a day, 25,000 cap: 100 days of delay reach the cap.

4. Advisory and reporting functions

  • Annual report to the Central Government on RTI implementation, laid before both Houses of Parliament.
  • Recommendations to public authorities for conformity with the Act.
  • Awareness and training: workshops, guides, circulars.
  • Policy consultation: assists DoPT and ministries in revising RTI rules, digital disclosure and proactive publication norms.

5. Supervisory and monitoring role

  • Watchdog across all Central Public Authorities, PSUs and UT administrations.
  • Reviews periodic reports of departments under Section 25.
  • Monitors compliance and recommends reforms where delay, secrecy or opacity appear.
  • Works with State Information Commissions for uniform interpretation and best practices.

6. Quasi-judicial nature

  • Proceedings are deemed judicial proceedings under Sections 193 and 228 of the IPC.
  • Orders are binding unless challenged before the High Court under writ jurisdiction (Article 226).
  • Keeps case law repositories for consistency and transparency.

Section 18 complaint

  • By any person: denied access, no PIO, unreasonable fees, incomplete or false information
  • CIC may inquire and use civil court powers

Section 19(3) second appeal

  • Against the First Appellate Authority's order
  • CIC decides on merits as final appellate authority

4 · Recent reforms, challenges and way forward (CIC)#

Key reforms and developments

Four developments
DevelopmentWhat the handout says
RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019Removed the fixed five-year tenure and pay parity with ECs; the Central Government now decides tenure, salary and service conditions. Government: flexibility and administrative efficiency. Critics: independence compromised by financial and administrative dependence on the executive
DigitalisationRTI Online Portal expanded to all UTs and major Central PSUs (e-filing, appeals, fee payment); CIC Dashboard 2.0 (2022); video-conferencing hearings institutionalised during COVID-19
AI-based file tracking (2024)DoPT and CIC piloted an AI-driven system to predict pendency, track appeals and flag delays
Backlog clearance drivesSpecial benches (2021-23); pendency crossed 32,000 by late 2023; summary disposal of repetitive cases, digitisation, link with CPGRAMS

Emerging challenges

  • Institutional independence: the 2019 amendment blurs autonomous oversight and executive control; lack of CIC and EC parity weakens stature.
  • Pendency and capacity: pending appeals consistently above 30,000, average wait over 10 months; vacancies, especially of the Chief Information Commissioner, delay decisions.
  • Transparency fatigue: misuse of Section 8(1) exemptions on vague "national interest" grounds; post-COVID opacity and digital red tape.

Role in transparency (2019 to 2025)

  • PSUs: rulings in ONGC vs RTI Applicant (2022) and the BSNL case (2023): PSUs performing public functions cannot refuse information under commercial secrecy.
  • Public health: in 2021 the CIC directed the Health Ministry to publish vaccine procurement and pricing data suo motu.
  • Environment: in CIC v. Ministry of Environment (2022), proactive disclosure of EIA clearances and expert committee minutes.
  • Whistleblowers: pressed for anonymity and procedural safeguards for RTI activists; urged DoPT to hasten amendments to the Whistleblower Protection Act, 2014.

5 · State Information Commission: status, composition and tenure#

The State Information Commission (SIC) is an independent statutory body under the RTI Act, 2005 for transparency in state public authorities. It decides complaints and second appeals for state departments, PSUs, local bodies and institutions under the State Government.

  • Statutory, not constitutional.
  • Constituted by notification of the State Government under Section 15 of the RTI Act, 2005.
  • Independent; not subordinate to the CIC.

6 · Powers and functions of the SIC#

The SIC is the final appellate authority under the RTI Act at the state level, a quasi-judicial body deciding second appeals and complaints.

1. Adjudicatory powers

  • Section 19(3) appeals: from people unhappy with the departmental appellate authority's decision or inaction; can direct disclosure, modification or rejection on merits.
  • Section 18 complaints from any person who: could not submit an RTI application because no PIO was appointed; was refused information; got no response in the prescribed time; considers the fee excessive; finds information incomplete, misleading or false; or faces any other difficulty in accessing information.

2. Investigative and suo motu powers

  • Can start an inquiry on its own motion if it believes information was unjustly denied or concealed.
  • Civil court powers: summon and compel attendance; production, discovery and inspection of documents; evidence on affidavit; requisition public records from courts or offices; issue summons for witnesses or documents; spot inspections or verification of records.

3. Enforcement and compliance powers

  • Granting access: disclosure in a specific format or mode.
  • Appointment of PIO: direct the authority to designate an officer where none exists.
  • Record management: reforms in record maintenance, cataloguing and disclosure.
  • Compensation for loss or detriment from delay or denial.
  • Penalty: Rs 250 per day of delay up to Rs 25,000; may recommend disciplinary action under service rules.

4. Supervisory and advisory powers

  • Annual report to the State Government on RTI implementation, tabled before the State Legislature.
  • Recommendations on record-keeping, digital disclosure and officer training.
  • Awareness and training: advise the government on public awareness campaigns and sensitisation.
Mirror shortcut

The SIC does what the CIC does, one level down: same Section 18 and 19(3), same civil court powers, same Rs 250 and Rs 25,000, and the report goes to the State Legislature.

SIC powers
AIES

Adjudicate, Investigate (suo motu), Enforce, Supervise and advise: "All Information Ends Secrecy."

7 · RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019: key changes#

Before and after (the handout's table, rebuilt)
AspectBefore (RTI Act, 2005)After (RTI Amendment Act, 2019)
1. Tenure of officeFixed 5-year term or until age 65, whichever earlierNo fixed tenure; term prescribed by the Central Government (for both CIC and SIC)
2. Salary and service conditionsCIC equal to Chief Election Commissioner; ICs equal to Election Commissioners; SCIC equal to EC; SICs equal to Chief Secretary of the StateSalaries, allowances and conditions of CIC, ICs, SCIC and SICs prescribed by the Central Government through executive rules
3. ReappointmentNot eligible after completing tenureRemains the same: no reappointment
4. Deduction of previous pension benefitsDeductions were made from salary for pension or retirement benefits from previous government serviceSuch deductions removed; salaries paid in full regardless of earlier government service
5. Authority determining conditionsFixed by Parliament through the RTI Act, 2005Delegated to the Central Government via executive rule-making power
6. ApplicabilitySeparate provisions for Central and State Commissions under Sections 12-15Uniform control by the Central Government over both levels
Six changes
TSRDAU

Tenure, Salary, Reappointment (same), Deduction of pension (removed), Authority (Centre), Uniform control: "Ten Students Read Documents And Understand."

Amendment shortcut

Five things moved to the Centre (tenure, pay, conditions, rule-making, uniform control), one stayed (no reappointment), one was removed (pension deduction).

The critique

The handout says the Government claimed flexibility and efficiency, while transparency advocates and former commissioners said making the Commissions dependent on the executive compromised their independence.
