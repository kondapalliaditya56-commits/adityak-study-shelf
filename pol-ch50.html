In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The Disaster Management Act, 2005 created a three-tier system: NDMA (national), SDMA (state) and DDMA (district). It moved India from relief-centric to risk management.
  • The NDMA is the apex body, chaired by the Prime Minister, with a Vice-Chairperson (Cabinet Minister status) and up to nine members (Minister of State rank). It works under the Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • Its job: lay down policies, approve the National Disaster Management Plan, guide SDMAs, supervise the NDRF, set minimum relief standards and allow emergency procurement without tenders.
  • The SDMA (Sec 14) is chaired by the Chief Minister
  • The DDMA (Sec 25) by the District Collector with an elected representative as Co-Chairperson
  • Recent steps (2020-25): NDMP 2023 aligned to the Sendai Framework, Aapda Mitra, early-warning and flash-flood systems, and rescue missions abroad: Sagar Bandhu, Dost, Karuna.

1 · Why the Act and what NDMA is#

  • Disasters, natural or human-made, threaten life, livelihood and infrastructure in India because of its vast geography and climatic diversity. Growing cyclones, floods, landslides and industrial accidents showed the need for a permanent institutional mechanism to manage disasters in a scientific and coordinated way.
  • The Act: The Disaster Management Act, 2005 gave, for the first time, a statutory framework for disaster preparedness, mitigation, response and recovery. It marked a paradigm shift from a relief-centric approach to a comprehensive disaster-risk-management system.

Statutory chain created by the Act

  • NDMA: Apex policy-making body that directs and coordinates disaster management across the country
  • SDMAs: State Disaster Management Authorities
  • DDMAs: District Disaster Management Authorities
Statutory chain created by the Act
  • The NDMA is chaired by the Prime Minister and functions under the administrative control of the Ministry of Home Affairs, through the integrated structure of SDMAs and DDMAs.
  • Vision: "To build a safer and disaster-resilient India through a holistic, proactive, technology-driven, and sustainable development strategy". The NDMA today links disaster risk reduction with sustainable development and climate resilience.

4 · Functions and responsibilities of NDMA#

  • The handout groups the work into six heads.

1. Policy formulation and planning

  • Lays down national policies on disaster management: the strategic direction for prevention, mitigation, preparedness and rehabilitation.
  • Approves the National Disaster Management Plan (NDMP), a blueprint integrating sectoral and inter-state coordination mechanisms.
  • Approves plans of Central Ministries and Departments prepared in line with the NDMP.
  • Guides SDMAs through uniform standards and policy alignment.

2. Integration and coordination

  • Ensures DRR measures are integrated into all Central and State development programmes.
  • Coordinates implementation of national policies and plans through the NEC and concerned ministries.
  • Facilitates inter-ministerial and inter-state coordination during major disasters for resource mobilisation.
  • Provides technical and financial guidance to state and district authorities on preparedness.

3. Mitigation and preparedness measures

  • Lays down guidelines for prevention and mitigation with scientific and research institutions.
  • Promotes capacity building and training at all administrative levels, including NIDM and state training institutes.
  • Encourages early warning systems, forecasting tools and hazard mapping using modern technology.
  • Mainstreams climate resilience and eco-friendly practices into mitigation to minimise long-term risks.

4. Emergency response and relief management

  • Exercises general supervision and control over the NDRF for prompt, professional response.
  • Recommends minimum standards of relief (food, shelter, medical aid).
  • Recommends relief in loan repayments or concessional loans in large-scale disasters.
  • Authorises emergency procurement of essential materials for rescue and relief by waiving normal tender procedures.

5. International and inter-governmental support

  • Coordinates India's humanitarian assistance to foreign countries hit by major disasters.
  • Facilitates cooperation with UN bodies, ASEAN, SAARC and BIMSTEC.
  • Represents India on global platforms like the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction (2015-2030).

6. Oversight, reporting and evaluation

  • Prepares an annual report and submits it to the Central Government for presentation before Parliament.
  • Periodically reviews disaster-management plans and performance of SDMAs, DDMAs and NDRF.
  • Evaluates implementation of guidelines under the Act, ensuring accountability of ministries and states.
  • Memory aid (Six function heads): Policy, Integration, Mitigation, Emergency response, International support, Oversight: "Please Inspect My Emergency Inventory Often."
  • Shortcut: Emergency powers in one line. NDRF control + relief standards + loan relief + procurement without tenders. Four verbs, all under head 4.

6 · State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA)#

  • Legal basis: The SDMA is established under Section 14 of the DM Act, 2005. It is the apex disaster-management body at state level for policy-making, coordination and monitoring of preparedness, mitigation and response. Every state and union territory with a legislature must set up its own SDMA.

Functions

  • The SDMA formulates state-specific strategies in line with national guidelines.
  • Policy and planning:
    • Formulate the State Disaster Management Policy consistent with the National Policy
    • Approve the State Disaster Management Plan (SDMP) aligned with the National Plan
    • Approve Departmental Disaster Management Plans
    • Integrate DRR into all state development programmes
  • Coordination and implementation:
    • Coordinate implementation of the SDMP and inter-departmental response
    • Recommend funds for mitigation and preparedness
    • Oversee the State Executive Committee for timely execution
  • Monitoring and evaluation:
    • Review departmental and district-level plans for consistency with state and national priorities
    • Monitor capacity-building and preparedness across districts
    • Issue guidelines to departments for integrating prevention and mitigation

Reporting and importance

  • The SDMA prepares an Annual Report, submitted to the State Government, which lays it before the State Legislature: democratic accountability and transparency.
  • SDMAs are the link between NDMA and DDMAs.
  • They contextualise national policy to the state's geography, vulnerability profile and resource base (for example coastal, hilly, drought-prone states).
  • By mainstreaming DRR into state planning they turn response from reactive to preventive and capacity-oriented.
  • Memory aid (SDMA plans): Policy, then SDMP, then Departmental plans: "Plans Down the line": State policy, State plan, Departmental plans.

7 · District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA)#

  • Legal basis: The DDMA is constituted under Section 25 of the DM Act, 2005. It is the district-level apex body for planning, coordinating and implementing disaster-management measures in line with NDMA and SDMA guidelines. Every district in the country must have a DDMA, for decentralised preparedness and rapid local response.

Composition of the DDMA

PositionRole or designation
ChairpersonDistrict Collector / District Magistrate / Deputy Commissioner (ex-officio)
Co-ChairpersonElected representative of the local authority; in tribal areas, the Chief Executive Member of the District Council (Sixth Schedule areas)
In districts with Zilla ParishadThe Chairperson of the Zilla Parishad acts as the Co-Chairperson
Members (ex-officio)Chief Executive Officer of DDMA, Superintendent of Police, Chief Medical Officer of the district
Other membersUp to two district-level officers nominated by the State Government
Chief Executive Officer (CEO)Appointed by the State Government; executive head of the DDMA

Functions of the DDMA

  • Planning and preparedness:
    • Prepare the District Disaster Management Plan (DDMP) including a detailed District Response Plan (DRP)
    • Identify disaster-prone areas and vulnerable groups
    • Ensure risk mapping and resource inventory
    • Promote community-based preparedness through schools, NGOs and local bodies
  • Coordination and implementation:
    • Coordinate National, State and District Plans at district level
    • Ensure departments and local authorities follow NDMA and SDMA guidelines
    • Oversee Emergency Operation Centres (EOCs) and liaise with state level
    • Facilitate training for officers, employees and voluntary rescue workers
  • Mitigation and response:
    • Set up early-warning systems and quick alerts
    • Coordinate rescue, relief and rehabilitation
    • Manage relief centres and temporary shelters (sanitation, water, food)
    • Ensure medical preparedness, epidemic control and post-disaster psychosocial care
  • Monitoring and review:
    • Review mitigation and capacity-building by departments and local bodies
    • Evaluate district plans and recommend improvements
    • Keep updated data on resources, personnel and logistics

Accountability and significance

  • The DDMA submits periodic reports to the SDMA and the State Government
  • The State Government may assign additional responsibilities or direct special measures
  • This gives vertical coordination across NDMA to SDMA to DDMA.
  • It embodies localised disaster governance (last-mile preparedness), operationalises "community-centric disaster resilience" by integrating panchayats, municipalities and civil-society actors, and turns disaster management from top-down to a grass-roots participatory framework.
  • Memory aid (DDMA people): Collector chairs, Co-chair is the local elected head, Members include SP and CMO, More: two nominated officers: "Collectors Count Many Men".

9 · Recent initiatives (2020-2025): frameworks and warning systems#

  • The architecture has moved to a multi-hazard, technology-driven, climate-resilient approach, and from reactive relief to anticipatory risk governance, integrating NDMA, SDMA and DDMA efforts.

1. Strengthening institutional frameworks

  • Revamped NDMP (2023):
    • Aligned with the Sendai Framework (2015-2030) and SDG 13 (Climate Action)
    • A "whole-of-government and whole-of-society" approach with multi-stakeholder participation and gender-sensitive response
    • Focus areas are pandemic management, cyber disasters, CBRN hazards (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear) and climate-induced migration
  • Climate change missions:
    • NDMA and the Ministry of Environment developed a Climate Risk Atlas (2022) mapping district-level vulnerability to floods, heatwaves and cyclones
    • It guides adaptive planning for SDMAs and DDMAs under the updated NAPCC 2.0

2. Early warning and response

  • Pan-India Early Warning Dissemination System (EWDS):
    • Expanded from coastal to inland flood-prone districts (2021-2024) with ISRO and IMD
    • Real-time alerts through the Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) linked to DoT's emergency messaging system
  • Landslide Atlas (2022): prepared with IMD, IIT Roorkee and NRSC, covering 147 districts.
  • Flash Flood Guidance System (FFGS): operational across all river basins, issuing 6-12 hour alerts.

10 · Recent initiatives: people, cities, technology, pandemic, world#

3. Capacity building and community resilience

  • Aapda Mitra Scheme (Phase II, 2022-27):
    • Flagship NDMA-MHA initiative to train 1 lakh community volunteers across 350 districts in first response, crowd control and basic search-and-rescue
    • It institutionalises local preparedness at DDMA level
  • National School Safety Programme (NSSP 2.0):
    • With UNICEF and NCERT, over 25,000 schools in disaster-prone regions
    • Mock drills, disaster curriculum, retrofitting of unsafe infrastructure

4. Urban and technological initiatives

  • Urban Flood Mitigation Project (2023):
    • 98 high-risk cities, with the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs
    • Integrated stormwater management, micro-drainage mapping, green-infrastructure planning
  • Emerging technologies:
    • AI and drones for rapid damage assessment during cyclones (example Cyclone Biparjoy, 2023)
    • A Disaster Management Information System (DMIS) with ISRO and NIC, integrating real-time satellite imagery and crowd-sourced reports

5. Pandemic and biological disaster preparedness

  • After COVID-19, NDMA institutionalised Health Emergency Management Cells at national and state levels.
  • A new National Institute for Disaster Resilience and Health Security (NIDRHS) is being set up for biosecurity preparedness.
  • SDMAs were tasked with district-level pandemic management plans after 2021 for inter-sectoral coordination.

6. International collaboration and humanitarian diplomacy

  • NDMA has positioned India as a "first responder" nation in South Asia under the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI) and the BIMSTEC Disaster Management Framework (2022).
  • Operations: Operation Sagar Bandhu (Sri Lanka, 2025), Operation Dost (Turkiye-Syria, 2023), Operation Karuna (Myanmar, 2023).
  • India co-chairs the G20 Disaster Risk Reduction Working Group (2023-2024), focusing on resilient infrastructure and finance for DRR.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Who chairs which tier Prime Minister chairs the NDMA, the Chief Minister chairs the SDMA, and the District Collector (Magistrate or Deputy Commissioner) chairs the DDMA. Rule: P-C-C = Prime Minister, Chief Minister, Collector.
Trap 2 · Section numbers The handout places the NDMA chair in Section 3(2), the NEC secretariat in Section 8, the SDMA in Section 14 and the DDMA in Section 25 of the Disaster Management Act, 2005. Rule: SDMA = 14, DDMA = 25; do not mix them.
Trap 3 · Who is Co-Chairperson of the DDMA The Co-Chairperson is an elected representative of the local authority. In Sixth Schedule tribal areas it is the Chief Executive Member of the District Council; where a Zilla Parishad exists its Chairperson is the Co-Chairperson. Rule: Collector = Chair; elected representative = Co-Chair, never the Collector.
Trap 4 · NDMA versus NEC The NDMA is policy-making and chaired by the PM; the National Executive Committee under the Home Secretary implements its plans. Rule: NDMA decides, NEC executes, NDRF responds, NIDM trains.

Book edition: simple words and memory words#

In simple words

  • The Disaster Management Act, 2005 created a three-tier system: NDMA (national), SDMA (state) and DDMA (district). It moved India from relief-centric to risk management.
  • The NDMA is the apex body, chaired by the Prime Minister, with a Vice-Chairperson (Cabinet Minister status) and up to nine members (Minister of State rank). It works under the Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • Its job: lay down policies, approve the National Disaster Management Plan, guide SDMAs, supervise the NDRF, set minimum relief standards and allow emergency procurement without tenders.
  • The SDMA (Sec 14) is chaired by the Chief Minister; the DDMA (Sec 25) by the District Collector with an elected representative as Co-Chairperson.
  • Recent steps (2020-25): NDMP 2023 aligned to the Sendai Framework, Aapda Mitra, early-warning and flash-flood systems, and rescue missions abroad: Sagar Bandhu, Dost, Karuna.
