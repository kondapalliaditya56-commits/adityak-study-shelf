In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The corruption framework: laws such as Sec 203 and 316 BNS, DSPE Act 1946, PCA 1988, PMLA 2003, RTI 2005, Lokpal Act 2013, Whistleblower Act 2014 and bodies such as CBI, CVC, CAG, ED, Lokayuktas and Lokpal.
  • CBI: Special Police Establishment (1941), DSPE Act (1946), named CBI (1963). Divisions: anti-corruption, special crimes, economic offences, banking and securities fraud.
  • CBI problems: the 'caged parrot' image, about one-third posts vacant, a 67% conviction rate (but low in high-profile cases), sanction delays and no suo-moto jurisdiction.
  • Vineet Narain (1997): CVC supervises CBI in corruption cases, Director gets a minimum tenure (2 years), a committee picks and transfers the Director, the Single Directive is struck down and DoPT sets only overall policy.
  • CVC: executive body in 1964 on the Santhanam Committee (1962), statutory since 2003. One Commissioner plus up to two Vigilance Commissioners, chosen by the PM, Home Minister and Leader of Opposition, for 4 years or age 65, with UPSC-level pay.
  • CVC issues: depends on the CBI, has no prosecution power, sanctions are delayed and engagement with civil society is poor. Suggested fixes: independent investigation, time-bound sanction (3-6 months), binding recommendations, more staff and a wider selection panel.

Key terms

DSPE Act, 1946
Law that set up the CBI for anti-corruption investigation

Remark by a former SC judge that the CBI acts like a parrot in a cage, controlled by the ruling party

Issues in functioning

  • Politicisation: a former SC judge called the CBI a 'caged parrot'. Every ruling party at the Centre has used it against opponents, so it is not impartial or autonomous.
  • Shortage of staff: around one-third of posts are vacant.
  • Conviction rate: CBI's is 67%, relatively high against 30-40% for other agencies, but conviction in high-profile cases is low.
  • Sanction for prosecution: too many delays in disposal, partly due to staff shortage. Prosecution follows investigation, but laws like Section 19 of PCA and Section 197 of CrPC protect civil servants and sanction to prosecute has to be provided by the state government. Delayed sanction means delayed prosecution.
  • Jurisdiction:
    • CBI has no suo-moto jurisdiction
    • It deals with cases transferred to it
  • Memory aid (CBI problems): Politicisation, Staff shortage, Conviction in high-profile cases, Sanction delays, Jurisdiction (no suo-moto): "Parrots Seldom Catch Slippery Jackals".

Also in the handout

  • Every ruling party at the Centre has used such organizations to target their political opponents and hence doesn't function in an impartial and autonomous manner.

3 · Vineet Narain Case, 1997#

  • The Supreme Court gave directions on the functioning of the CBI.

The directions

DirectionDetail
CVC supervises CBICVC deals largely with anti-corruption cases and violations of civil services discipline. Through the CVC Act it exercises general superintendence over CBI for work related to cases under the Prevention of Corruption Act
Minimum tenureGuaranteed minimum tenure for the Director and other important CBI positions. Under the DSPE Act the minimum tenure of the Director is 2 years. An ordinance extended this up to 5 years, after the two years in tranches of 1 year each

4 · Central Vigilance Commission: status, selection, term#

How the CVC evolved

  • 1962: Santhanam Committee on prevention of corruption appointed (headed by K Santhanam)
  • 1964: CVC set up by the central government as an executive body to advise and guide central government agencies on vigilance
  • 2003: Made a statutory body by the CVC Act, 2003
How the CVC evolved
  • The CVC is conceived as the apex vigilance institution free from control of any executive authority, monitoring all vigilance activity under the Central Government and advising authorities in planning, reviewing and reforming vigilance work.

Composition

  • A Central Vigilance Commissioner as chairperson and not more than two Vigilance Commissioners as members.

Selection

  • Drawn from two categories: those who are or have been in All India Services or Central Services, and officers of Central Public Sector Undertakings.
  • A selection committee of the PM, Home Minister and Leader of Opposition (or leader of the largest opposition party). Two government representatives means the government has the final say.
CVC selection committee
MOP
  • MMinister of Home Affairs (Home Minister)
  • OOpposition Leader (or leader of the largest opposition party)
  • PPrime Minister

MOP up corruption: the three who pick the CVC; the government holds two of the three seats. Order is rearranged for memory. (Memory aid, not from the handout.)

PJ Thomas Case, 2011

  • Problem: Government's majority in the committee made the Leader of Opposition's membership pointless
  • Ruling: Selection must be on the principle of consensus of all members, not majority
  • Limit: The judgement applies only to this appointment, not to all other committee appointments
Handout alert

Handout alert: The handout says "from both the categories, not more than two people can be selected, thus ensuring at least one from each category". The simple reading: the CVC and two VCs come from the two categories, so each category gets at least one. Use the rule "1 CVC plus up to 2 VCs".

Terms

  • Memory aid (CVC terms): Four years or 65, Salary like UPSC, Proven misbehaviour via SC, Elevation allowed but no reappointment: "Four Silver Pens Elected".
  • Shortcut: CVC versus CBI Director. CVC: 4 years or 65, committee of PM, HM, LoP. CBI Director: minimum 2 years (up to 5 by ordinance), committee of PM, LoP, CJI.

5 · Functions and powers of the CVC#

  • Superintend the CBI in corruption cases under the PCA, 1988, as directed in Vineet Narain (1997).
  • Give directions to the CBI in those cases.
  • Inquire (or cause an inquiry or investigation) on a reference by the Central Government.
  • Inquire into complaints against officials specified in sub-section 2 of Section 8 of the CVC Act, 2003: Group A officials, All India Service officials and equivalent officials in Central PSUs.
  • Review progress of investigations by the DSPE into PCA offences.
  • Review pending sanctions for prosecution under the PCA.
  • Tender advice to the Central Government and organisations on matters referred.
  • Superintend vigilance administrations of Central Government ministries, departments and organisations.
  • Has the powers of a civil court when inquiring.
  • Mandatory consultation with the Commission before rules or regulations on vigilance or disciplinary matters for persons in public services of the Union and members of All India Services.
  • Enforcement Directorate:
    • The CVC chairs (with VCs as members) the committee whose recommendation leads the Centre to appoint the Director of Enforcement
    • The same committee recommends, after consulting the Director, officers of Deputy Director and above
  • CBI officers: the same committee recommends, after consulting the CBI Director, appointment of officers of SP level and above, except the Director, and their extension and curtailment.
  • Annual report to the President, who places it before both Houses of Parliament.
  • Memory aid (Powers): Superintend CBI, Give directions, Inquire, Review, Tender advice, Civil court powers: "Smart Guards Inspect Records, Track Crooks".

Also in the handout

  • CVC should superintend or supervise CBI.

6 · Issues in functioning and how to strengthen the CVC#

  • Overwhelming dependence on the CBI for investigation, which is already politicised and under government control.
  • No independent prosecution power
  • Sanction needs government permission, often delayed
  • Non-cooperative attitude of the government.
  • Poor engagement with civil society, citizens and NGOs working in the field.
Issues in the functioning of the CVC

Seven suggestions to strengthen the CVC

Reform suggestions

SuggestionDetail and handout example
Independent investigative and prosecution powersDirect investigative powers, like the Lokpal's model. The 2nd ARC (Ethics in Governance) recommended functional autonomy for vigilance agencies
Statutory timelines for sanctionTime-bound framework of 3-6 months. Vineet Narain (1997) directed fixed timelines but delays persist. TI India: in 2023, over 70% of sanctions pending beyond 6 months
Binding oversight in disciplinary and vigilance mattersTurn the advisory role into binding oversight across ministries and PSUs. The Sarkaria Commission stressed robust independent oversight of central services
Manpower and technologyMore sanctioned staff, a dedicated Vigilance Cadre, digital real-time monitoring. CVC Annual Report 2023: less than 50% of sanctioned staff, overseeing over 1500 organisations
Civil society participation and citizen feedbackPeriodic consultations with civil society, whistleblowers, NGOs; a public grievance dashboard. 2nd ARC recommended multi-stakeholder engagement. South Korea's ACRC is the best-practice model
Binding recommendationsAmend the CVC Act so recommendations bind ministries in vigilance-lapse cases. The Punchhi Commission stressed statutory backing for vigilance bodies
Transparent selectionAdd judiciary members or eminent anti-corruption experts to the selection panel. The PJ Thomas Case (2011) showed the flaws and urged consensus

From Lakshmikanth · Indian Polity, 8th ed. Ch 64-65, CVC and CBI
  • CBI roots: the Special Police Establishment (1941) investigated bribery in transactions with the War and Supply Department during World War II. The DSPE Act (1946) moved it to the Home Department. The CBI was set up by a Home Ministry resolution in 1963 and DSPE became one of its divisions.
  • Lakshmikanth differs: it says the CBI is not a statutory body and draws its powers from the DSPE Act, 1946. The handout says the DSPE Act establishes it. Use the handout in class.
  • CBI motto: Industry, Impartiality and Integrity. It is administered under DoPT, and acts as the National Central Bureau of Interpol in India.
  • The NIA (formed after the 2008 Mumbai attack) handles terror cases, whereas the CBI handles corruption, economic offences and serious organised crime other than terrorism.
  • CBI cannot work in a State (except railway areas) without that State's consent. It takes up conventional crimes like murder only on a State's reference or when the Supreme Court or a High Court directs.
  • Lakshmikanth differs:
    • Director's tenure. It says the DSPE (Amendment) Act, 2021 allows extension of the fixed 2-year tenure to a maximum of 5 years, one year at a time (three extensions), for reasons recorded in writing
    • The handout speaks of an ordinance. Use the handout, remember the 2021 Act
  • Lokpal Act 2013 changes for the CBI:
    • Director of Prosecution recommended by the CVC
    • Officers of SP rank and above chosen by a committee of the CVC (chair), Vigilance Commissioners, Home Secretary and DoPT Secretary. DSPE (Amendment) Act 2014 lets the leader of the single largest opposition party sit on the Director's committee if there is no recognised LoP
  • CBI and state police:
    • DSPE takes cases mainly about Central affairs, employees and Central PSUs
    • State police take cases mainly about state affairs, even if some Central staff are involved
  • CVC organisation: its own Secretariat, the Chief Technical Examiners' Wing (technical audit of construction works) and Commissioners for Departmental Inquiries (oral inquiries in departmental proceedings). It has civil court powers, sits at New Delhi and governments must record reasons in writing if they reject its advice.
  • CVC jurisdiction also covers Scale V and above in public sector banks, Grade D and above in RBI, NABARD and SIDBI, and senior officers in PSUs, insurance companies and LIC.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Who controls the CBI The CVC superintends the CBI only for cases under the Prevention of Corruption Act. DoPT may set only the overall policy and not day-to-day work or investigations. Rule: CVC = corruption cases; DoPT = broad policy only.
Trap 2 · Two different tenures CVC and Vigilance Commissioners serve 4 years or until 65. The CBI Director has a minimum of 2 years, which the handout says an ordinance allows to extend up to 5 years in 1-year steps. Rule: CVC 4 years; CBI Director minimum 2 years.
Trap 3 · PJ Thomas ruling is narrow The consensus-not-majority principle was laid down for that appointment only, not for every selection committee. Rule: consensus rule applied to the CVC appointment in the PJ Thomas case alone.
Trap 4 · Date mix-ups Santhanam Committee 1962, CVC set up by executive resolution in 1964, statutory in 2003. CBI: SPE 1941, Act 1946, CBI name 1963. Rule: CVC was executive first (1964), statutory later (2003).
