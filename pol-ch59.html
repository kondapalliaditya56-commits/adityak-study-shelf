2 · Central Bureau of Investigation#

How the CBI grew

1941
Set up as the Special Police Establishment
1946
Statutory backing through the Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) Act
1963
Acquired the popular name CBI

Principal divisions

Four divisions
DivisionCovers
Anti-Corruption DivisionCorruption cases
Special Crimes DivisionSerious, sensational, organised crimes
Economic Offences DivisionCheating, Ponzi schemes, counterfeit currency and other economic offences
Banking and Security Frauds DivisionEconomic offences related to banking and the securities market
Four divisions
ASEB

Anti-corruption, Special crimes, Economic offences, Banking and securities: "All Sleuths Expose Bribes".

Issues in functioning

  • Politicisation: a former SC judge called the CBI a 'caged parrot'. Every ruling party at the Centre has used it against opponents, so it is not impartial or autonomous.
  • Shortage of staff: around one-third of posts are vacant.
  • Conviction rate: CBI's is 67%, relatively high against 30-40% for other agencies, but conviction in high-profile cases is low.
  • Sanction for prosecution: too many delays in disposal, partly due to staff shortage. Prosecution follows investigation, but laws like Section 19 of PCA and Section 197 of CrPC protect civil servants and sanction to prosecute has to be provided by the state government. Delayed sanction means delayed prosecution.
  • Jurisdiction: CBI has no suo-moto jurisdiction; it deals with cases transferred to it.
CBI problems
PSCSJ

Politicisation, Staff shortage, Conviction in high-profile cases, Sanction delays, Jurisdiction (no suo-moto): "Parrots Seldom Catch Slippery Jackals".

3 · Vineet Narain Case, 1997#

The Supreme Court gave directions on the functioning of the CBI.

The directions
DirectionDetail
CVC supervises CBICVC deals largely with anti-corruption cases and violations of civil services discipline. Through the CVC Act it exercises general superintendence over CBI for work related to cases under the Prevention of Corruption Act
Minimum tenureGuaranteed minimum tenure for the Director and other important CBI positions. Under the DSPE Act the minimum tenure of the Director is 2 years. An ordinance extended this up to 5 years, after the two years in tranches of 1 year each
Committee appointmentCBI Director's appointment cannot be left to the government's sole discretion. Committee: PM, Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha (or leader of the largest opposition party if there is no recognised LoP), and the CJI
Single Directive struck downThe rule requiring sanction for action against joint secretary rank and above
DoPT controlCBI is under the broad direction and control of the Department of Personnel and Training, meaning only the overall policy framework, not day-to-day workings and investigations
TransferTransfer of the CBI Director must be done by the same committee as for appointment
Six directions
CTCSDT

CVC supervision, Tenure, Committee, Single Directive, DoPT limits, Transfer: "Court Told CBI Stand Different Today".

Reality check

Even after this judgement the CBI is still marred by allegations of politicisation and the 'caged parrot' label.

4 · Central Vigilance Commission: status, selection, term#

How the CVC evolved

1962
Santhanam Committee on prevention of corruption appointed (headed by K Santhanam)
1964
CVC set up by the central government as an executive body to advise and guide central government agencies on vigilance
2003
Made a statutory body by the CVC Act, 2003

The CVC is conceived as the apex vigilance institution free from control of any executive authority, monitoring all vigilance activity under the Central Government and advising authorities in planning, reviewing and reforming vigilance work.

Composition

A Central Vigilance Commissioner as chairperson and not more than two Vigilance Commissioners as members.

Selection

  • Drawn from two categories: those who are or have been in All India Services or Central Services, and officers of Central Public Sector Undertakings.
  • A selection committee of the PM, Home Minister and Leader of Opposition (or leader of the largest opposition party). Two government representatives means the government has the final say.

PJ Thomas Case, 2011

Problem
Government's majority in the committee made the Leader of Opposition's membership pointless
Ruling
Selection must be on the principle of consensus of all members, not majority
Limit
The judgement applies only to this appointment, not to all other committee appointments
Handout alert

The handout says "from both the categories, not more than two people can be selected, thus ensuring at least one from each category". The simple reading: the CVC and two VCs come from the two categories, so each category gets at least one. Use the rule "1 CVC plus up to 2 VCs".

Term, conditions, removal

Terms
PointRule
Term4 years for both CVC and VC; retirement age 65 years
ReappointmentNone at the same post. Elevation allowed (a VC can become CVC) if total stay in the Commission does not exceed 4 years
Bars after serviceNot eligible to be administrators of UTs, diplomatic assignments or any appointment made under the warrant and seal of the President; cannot hold an office of profit under Centre or states
SalaryCVC equals UPSC Chairperson; VCs equal UPSC members. Cannot be altered to disadvantage after appointment
Source of payConsolidated Fund of India
RemovalOn proven misbehaviour or incapacity through a reference to the Supreme Court
Other groundsInsolvency, moral turpitude, private employment, infirmity of body and mind, prejudicial financial interests
CVC terms
FSPE

Four years or 65, Salary like UPSC, Proven misbehaviour via SC, Elevation allowed but no reappointment: "Four Silver Pens Elected".

CVC versus CBI Director

CVC: 4 years or 65, committee of PM, HM, LoP. CBI Director: minimum 2 years (up to 5 by ordinance), committee of PM, LoP, CJI.

5 · Functions and powers of the CVC#

  • Superintend the CBI in corruption cases under the PCA, 1988, as directed in Vineet Narain (1997).
  • Give directions to the CBI in those cases.
  • Inquire (or cause an inquiry or investigation) on a reference by the Central Government.
  • Inquire into complaints against officials specified in sub-section 2 of Section 8 of the CVC Act, 2003: Group A officials, All India Service officials and equivalent officials in Central PSUs.
  • Review progress of investigations by the DSPE into PCA offences.
  • Review pending sanctions for prosecution under the PCA.
  • Tender advice to the Central Government and organisations on matters referred.
  • Superintend vigilance administrations of Central Government ministries, departments and organisations.
  • Has the powers of a civil court when inquiring.
  • Mandatory consultation with the Commission before rules or regulations on vigilance or disciplinary matters for persons in public services of the Union and members of All India Services.
  • Enforcement Directorate: the CVC chairs (with VCs as members) the committee whose recommendation leads the Centre to appoint the Director of Enforcement; the same committee recommends, after consulting the Director, officers of Deputy Director and above.
  • CBI officers: the same committee recommends, after consulting the CBI Director, appointment of officers of SP level and above, except the Director, and their extension and curtailment.
  • Annual report to the President, who places it before both Houses of Parliament.
Powers
SGIRTC

Superintend CBI, Give directions, Inquire, Review, Tender advice, Civil court powers: "Smart Guards Inspect Records, Track Crooks".

6 · Issues in functioning and how to strengthen the CVC#

Issues

  • Overwhelming dependence on the CBI for investigation, which is already politicised and under government control.
  • No independent prosecution power; sanction needs government permission, often delayed.
  • Non-cooperative attitude of the government.
  • Poor engagement with civil society, citizens and NGOs working in the field.

Seven suggestions to strengthen the CVC

Reform suggestions
SuggestionDetail and handout example
Independent investigative and prosecution powersDirect investigative powers, like the Lokpal's model. The 2nd ARC (Ethics in Governance) recommended functional autonomy for vigilance agencies
Statutory timelines for sanctionTime-bound framework of 3-6 months. Vineet Narain (1997) directed fixed timelines but delays persist. TI India: in 2023, over 70% of sanctions pending beyond 6 months
Binding oversight in disciplinary and vigilance mattersTurn the advisory role into binding oversight across ministries and PSUs. The Sarkaria Commission stressed robust independent oversight of central services
Manpower and technologyMore sanctioned staff, a dedicated Vigilance Cadre, digital real-time monitoring. CVC Annual Report 2023: less than 50% of sanctioned staff, overseeing over 1500 organisations
Civil society participation and citizen feedbackPeriodic consultations with civil society, whistleblowers, NGOs; a public grievance dashboard. 2nd ARC recommended multi-stakeholder engagement. South Korea's ACRC is the best-practice model
Binding recommendationsAmend the CVC Act so recommendations bind ministries in vigilance-lapse cases. The Punchhi Commission stressed statutory backing for vigilance bodies
Transparent selectionAdd judiciary members or eminent anti-corruption experts to the selection panel. The PJ Thomas Case (2011) showed the flaws and urged consensus
Handout alert

The handout numbers these suggestions 26 to 32 (list numbers carried over from a longer list). There are seven suggestions; the stray numbers are dropped. Also, the handout lists Sarkaria and Punchhi Commissions (on Centre-State relations) as examples for vigilance oversight; quote them as the handout does.

Seven reforms
ISBMCBT

Independent powers, Sanction timelines, Binding oversight, Manpower, Civil society, Binding recommendations, Transparent selection: "If State Bodies Meet Citizens, Bribes Take Time".
