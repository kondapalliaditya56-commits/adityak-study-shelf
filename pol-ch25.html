Closing idea: Voters choose on both passing issues and lasting loyalties, and Indian democracy keeps free elections while struggling with money, identity and real choice.

In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • A voter's choice comes from individual and group choices (religious group, social group and so on). The handout splits the influences into short-term and long-term.
  • Short-term influences work in one election only: the economy (inflation, unemployment), local conditions, leaders, specific events (the Emergency hurt Indira Gandhi) and media coverage.
  • Long-term influences last: social (age, gender, inhabitation, caste, religion), economic (income groups versus the poor) and political (ideology, family background, party identification).
  • Trends: voters choose the party or leader over the candidate, independents do poorly, people vote against the ruling party, and national and State choices differ.
  • The handout lists paradoxes of Indian democracy: muscle and money, identity politics (language, then caste after Mandal, then religion after the Ram Janmabhoomi Movement), poverty, no real choice, and Psephocracy.
  • The Democracy Index (0-10, 60 indicators, 5 categories, 4 regime types) called India a Flawed democracy in 2020 (6.61). International Democracy Day is 15 September.

1 · What decides a vote#

  • Individual voting is the function of individual choices and group choices (religious group, social group and so on). The determinants of a voter's choice split into short-term influence and long-term influence.

Short-term versus long-term

  • Short-term:
    • Immediate influence on specific elections only
    • They do not decide voters' long-term behaviour
  • Long-term: Lasting factors: social, economic and political
  • Memory aid (Long-term trio): Social, Economic, Political: "Some Electors Ponder."

2 · Short-term influences#

  • State of the economy at election time: inflation, unemployment and similar parameters shape voter behaviour.
  • Local conditions of life: water supply, power supply, road conditions.
  • Personality or public standing of leaders.
  • Specific events before the election: the imposition of the National Emergency affected election results for Indira Gandhi.
  • Pattern of media coverage of issues.
  • Memory aid (Five short-term factors): Economy, Local conditions, Personality of leaders, Specific events, Media: "Every Leader Prefers Smooth Messages."

  • SStanding of leaders: personality or public standing
  • EEvents before the election, such as the Emergency

3 · Long-term factors#

Five social factors

GenderGender inequality can give one gender greater participation and awareness than the other
InhabitationIn the initial decades after independence urban voters were considered politically more aware and active than rural voters; now rural people are also politically conscious and aware
CasteEspecially in rural areas caste loyalties run very deep; caste-based mobilisation of OBC and Dalit communities increased its influence further
ReligionReligion is used by exploiting the apprehension of minority and majority. In the initial decades the minority's apprehension was exploited; in recent times the majority's apprehension is seen. These apprehensions can be real or imaginary

Economic factors

Income groups and voting

  • Higher, middle and lower-income groups:
    • More concerned with the problems of society
    • Politically more aware
    • Conscious of the effects of government policies on their long-term interests
  • Poor sections:
    • More concerned with their personal economic problems
    • Only sense of belonging is to caste and religion
    • Vote as their employer says, more so in rural India

Political factors

  • When a voter's ideology matches a party's ideology, the voter tends to vote for that party.
  • Political family background and political socialisation also affect voting.
  • Party identification: the psychological attachment with a political party.

Trends in voting

  • Party or leader over candidate: Voters vote for the party or party leaders rather than the individual candidate
  • Leader image: The party depends on the image of the leader, not its programme or ideology
  • Independents: Independents tend to perform poorly
  • Vote against: People vote against the ruling party rather than for another party
  • Two levels: There is a distinction between national and State choices of electors
Trends in voting
  • Shortcut: Anti-incumbency rule of thumb. The handout's trend is "vote against, not for": the ruling party carries the burden, whoever gains.

Also in the handout

  • Thus the party becomes dependent on the image of the leader rather than their program or ideology.

4 · Paradoxes in our democracy#

The paradoxes (handout order)

ParadoxOne sideOther side
1. Participation versus muscle and moneyParticipation has increased; peaceful transition of power; gun culture stamped outMuscle and money power in elections is very much prevalent
2. Bureaucracy and the StateBureaucracy works within a democratic framework and political controlThe State has become more repressive through draconian laws; bureaucracy often behaves anti-people
3. Issue-based versus identity politicsDemocratic politics should be issue-basedIn most countries politics of identity prevails on a large scale
4. Democracy and abject povertyVoting rights, regular elections and peaceful transfer should let the poor remove parties that ignore themThat is not the case
5. Too many parties, no real choiceMany partiesMost stand for the same type of economic ideologies and policies
6. Tolerance without liberal ethicTolerance is propagated as a virtueWithout a strong liberal ethic it cannot be practised, so mob lynching, vigilantism are justified
7. Multi-party system and personality cultMulti-party system existsPersonality cult prevails; democracy should not rest on a leader's personality
8. PsephocracyDemocratic practices honoured during free and fair elections and peaceful transferNot between two elections: leads to electoral dictatorships
9. Contradictions in the party systemSee below
  • Memory aid (Nine paradoxes): Participation vs muscle, Bureaucracy vs repression, Identity vs issues, Democracy vs poverty, Too many parties, Tolerance without liberalism, Multi-party vs cult, Psephocracy, Contradictions: "Poor Boys Ignore Dull Talk, Think More, Plan Carefully."

Politics of identity: three phases

  • Phase 1: Language-based identity played an important role
  • Phase 2: After the Mandal Commission period, caste-based identity politics took centre stage
  • Phase 3: After the Ram Janmabhoomi Movement, religion-based identity politics began to dominate
Phases of identity politics
  • With excessive focus on identities, identities get frozen, so negotiation and reconciliation become impossible.
  • Memory aid (Identity phases): Language, Caste (post-Mandal), Religion (post-Ram Janmabhoomi): "Look, Cast Right."
Three phases of identity politics
LCR

Lines Create Rifts

  • LLanguage-based identity first
  • CCaste-based identity after the Mandal Commission period
  • RReligion-based identity after the Ram Janmabhoomi Movement

Identity lines create rifts, and when identities freeze, negotiation and reconciliation become impossible. (Memory aid, not from the handout.)

Democracy and poverty in detail

  • Economic inequality is not as much a scandal as social inequality. The reservation (quota) system is an example of the effort to address social inequalities.
  • But these efforts did not convert into material or economic benefits for the poor.
  • Indian democracy has been preoccupied with transforming the pre-existing imbalance of social power.
  • The idea was to bring new political elites from Backward Classes. Backward-class people did not get substantial benefit from this
  • The material status of the poor remained unchanged

Psephocracy

  • Psephocracy: A democracy in which democratic practices are honoured during the election (free and fair) and at peaceful transfer of power, but not in the period between two consecutive elections. This leads to electoral dictatorships.

Contradictions in the party system

  • Memory aid (Seven contradictions): Personality cult at the Centre, High command parties, Increasing regional parties, Real choice absent, Fluid ideology, Consensus difficult, plus rising Independents: "Parties Hold Interests, Rarely Following Conscience."
  • The personality cult re-emerged at the central level.
  • High command-oriented, centralised parties.
  • Increasing influence of regional parties.
  • Multi-party system but absence of real choice.
  • Increasing number of independent candidates.
  • Fluid ideology of members: candidates now change parties and ideology frequently.
  • The multi-party system has made the system more representative, yet consensus-building has become difficult.
Handout alert

Handout alert: The handout says independents tend to perform poorly (Trends) and also lists increasing numbers of independent candidates (party-system contradictions). They answer different questions: numbers rise, success stays low. Also the paradox list has nine headings only if "contradictions in the party system" is counted as the last one; the handout does not number them.

Also in the handout

  • Without liberal ethics, tolerance cannot be realized into practice.
  • This would lead to electoral dictatorships.

5 · Democracy Index and International Democracy Day#

  • Scores countries on a 0 to 10 scale using 60 individual indicators in five core categories: electoral process and pluralism, functioning of government, political participation, political culture, civil liberties.
  • Each country is placed in one of four regime types: Full democracy, Flawed democracy, Hybrid regime, Authoritarian regime.
  • 2024 edition:
    • Global democracy is under strain
    • The average global score dropped to its lowest since the index began in 2006
  • 2020 Index: the EIU ranked India a "Flawed democracy". India's score fell from an earlier peak to 6.61 in 2020, which the EIU described as democratic backsliding.
  • Memory aid (Five categories): Electoral process, Functioning of government, Participation, Political culture, Civil liberties: "Every Fair Poll Protects Citizens."
  • Shortcut: Index ladder. Full > Flawed > Hybrid > Authoritarian. India (2020): second step, Flawed, score 6.61.

International Democracy Day

  • 15 September each year. On this date in 2007 the Universal Declaration on Democracy was adopted by the Inter-Parliamentary Union.
From Lakshmikanth · Indian Polity, 8th ed. Ch 84, Voting Behaviour
  • Voting behaviour is also called electoral behaviour and is a form of political behaviour. Its scientific study is psephology, from the Greek 'psephos', the piece of pottery on which votes about banishing dangerous people were inscribed.
  • Why study it (five points): it explains political socialisation, shows how far democracy is valued by elite and masses, shows the real impact of the ballot box, tells whether electoral politics continues or breaks with the past, and shows whether it is modern or primordial.
  • N.G.S. Kini sees voting behaviour as a mode of legitimising democratic rule, an instance of participation and integration into the political community, an act of decision-making, a role-action in a political culture, and a direct relation of citizens to government.
  • Caste:
    • Rajni Kothari said 'Indian politics is casteist, and caste is politicised'. Paul Brass: in the countryside, caste solidarity is by far the most important factor
    • Large castes back a respected member of their caste or a party their caste identifies with
  • Language and region: language rose with the 1956 reorganisation of States, and the rise of the DMK and TDP is linked to it. Regional sub-nationalism feeds regional parties, and secessionist parties sometimes call for an election boycott.
  • Personality:
    • The images of Nehru, Indira Gandhi, Rajiv Gandhi, Jayaprakash Narayan, Vajpayee and Narendra Modi swayed voters
    • Regional leaders do the same in States
  • Money: votes are freely exchanged for notes, liquor or goods despite expenditure limits, but money works only in normal times, not in a wave election (Paul Brass: a clear tendency in one direction for a national party or leader, based on issues that go beyond local calculations).
  • Performance of the ruling party is judged on its manifesto
  • The Congress defeat in 1977 and the Janata defeat in 1980 show anti-incumbency, meaning dissatisfaction with the ruling party's performance
  • Other factors in Lakshmikanth: events before the poll (war, murder of a leader, corruption scandals), economic conditions (inflation, food shortage, unemployment), factionalism, candidate orientation, the election campaign, political family background and the media.
  • Lakshmikanth differs:
    • Classification. It splits the determinants into socio-economic and political factors and lists ten (caste, religion, language, region, personality, money, ruling party's performance, party identification, ideology, others). The handout instead uses short-term and long-term influences and adds age, gender and inhabitation. Use the handout's frame in class
    • LK's list is useful extra content

Traps#

Trap 1 · Independents: more of them, less success The handout says independents tend to perform poorly, yet also lists increasing numbers of independent candidates as a party-system contradiction. Rule: numbers rise, success stays low.
Trap 2 · Whose apprehension is exploited Religion is used by exploiting apprehension: the minority's in the early decades after independence, the majority's in recent times. Either can be real or imaginary. Rule: early = minority, recent = majority.
Trap 3 · Order and trigger of identity politics Phase 1 is language; phase 2 is caste after the Mandal Commission period; phase 3 is religion after the Ram Janmabhoomi Movement. Rule: language, then caste (Mandal), then religion (Ram Janmabhoomi).
Trap 4 · Democracy Index numbers The Index has 60 indicators in 5 categories and 4 regime types. India was a Flawed democracy (second step, not Hybrid) with 6.61 in 2020. International Democracy Day (15 September) marks the 2007 Universal Declaration on Democracy by the Inter-Parliamentary Union. Rule: 0-10, 60 indicators, 5 categories, 4 types; Full > Flawed > Hybrid > Authoritarian.
