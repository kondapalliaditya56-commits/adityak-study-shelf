Closing idea: Voters choose on both passing issues and lasting loyalties, and Indian democracy keeps free elections while struggling with money, identity and real choice.

1 · What decides a vote#

Individual voting is the function of individual choices and group choices (religious group, social group and so on). The determinants of a voter's choice split into short-term influence and long-term influence.

Short-term

  • Immediate influence on specific elections only
  • they do not decide voters' long-term behaviour

Long-term

  • Lasting factors: social, economic and political
Long-term trio
SEP

Social, Economic, Political: "Some Electors Ponder."

2 · Short-term influences#

  • State of the economy at election time: inflation, unemployment and similar parameters shape voter behaviour.
  • Local conditions of life: water supply, power supply, road conditions.
  • Personality or public standing of leaders.
  • Specific events before the election: the imposition of the National Emergency affected election results for Indira Gandhi.
  • Pattern of media coverage of issues.
Five short-term factors
ELPSM

Economy, Local conditions, Personality of leaders, Specific events, Media: "Every Leader Prefers Smooth Messages."

3 · Long-term factors#

Social factors

Five social factors
FactorHow it shapes voting
AgeThe age of the voter affects behaviour
GenderGender inequality can give one gender greater participation and awareness than the other
InhabitationIn the initial decades after independence urban voters were considered politically more aware and active than rural voters; now rural people are also politically conscious and aware
CasteEspecially in rural areas caste loyalties run very deep; caste-based mobilisation of OBC and Dalit communities increased its influence further
ReligionReligion is used by exploiting the apprehension of minority and majority. In the initial decades the minority's apprehension was exploited; in recent times the majority's apprehension is seen. These apprehensions can be real or imaginary
Social factors
AGICR

Age, Gender, Inhabitation, Caste, Religion: "A Good Idea: Count Rightly."

Economic factors

Higher, middle and lower-income groups

  • More concerned with the problems of society
  • politically more aware
  • conscious of the effects of government policies on their long-term interests

Poor sections

  • More concerned with their personal economic problems
  • only sense of belonging is to caste and religion
  • vote as their employer says, more so in rural India

Political factors

  • When a voter's ideology matches a party's ideology, the voter tends to vote for that party.
  • Political family background and political socialisation also affect voting.
  • Party identification: the psychological attachment with a political party.

Trends

Trends in voting

Party or leader over candidate
Voters vote for the party or party leaders rather than the individual candidate
Leader image
The party depends on the image of the leader, not its programme or ideology
Independents
Independents tend to perform poorly
Vote against
People vote against the ruling party rather than for another party
Two levels
There is a distinction between national and State choices of electors
Anti-incumbency rule of thumb

The handout's trend is "vote against, not for": the ruling party carries the burden, whoever gains.

4 · Paradoxes in our democracy#

The paradoxes (handout order)
ParadoxOne sideOther side
1. Participation versus muscle and moneyParticipation has increased; peaceful transition of power; gun culture stamped outMuscle and money power in elections is very much prevalent
2. Bureaucracy and the StateBureaucracy works within a democratic framework and political controlThe State has become more repressive through draconian laws; bureaucracy often behaves anti-people
3. Issue-based versus identity politicsDemocratic politics should be issue-basedIn most countries politics of identity prevails on a large scale
4. Democracy and abject povertyVoting rights, regular elections and peaceful transfer should let the poor remove parties that ignore themThat is not the case
5. Too many parties, no real choiceMany partiesMost stand for the same type of economic ideologies and policies
6. Tolerance without liberal ethicTolerance is propagated as a virtueWithout a strong liberal ethic it cannot be practised, so mob lynching, vigilantism are justified
7. Multi-party system and personality cultMulti-party system existsPersonality cult prevails; democracy should not rest on a leader's personality
8. PsephocracyDemocratic practices honoured during free and fair elections and peaceful transferNot between two elections: leads to electoral dictatorships
9. Contradictions in the party systemSee below
Nine paradoxes
PBIDTTMPC

Participation vs muscle, Bureaucracy vs repression, Identity vs issues, Democracy vs poverty, Too many parties, Tolerance without liberalism, Multi-party vs cult, Psephocracy, Contradictions: "Poor Boys Ignore Dull Talk, Think More, Plan Carefully."

Politics of identity: three phases

Phases of identity politics

Phase 1
Language-based identity played an important role
Phase 2
After the Mandal Commission period, caste-based identity politics took centre stage
Phase 3
After the Ram Janmabhoomi Movement, religion-based identity politics began to dominate
  • With excessive focus on identities, identities get frozen, so negotiation and reconciliation become impossible.
Identity phases
LCR

Language, Caste (post-Mandal), Religion (post-Ram Janmabhoomi): "Look, Cast Right."

Democracy and poverty in detail

  • Economic inequality is not as much a scandal as social inequality. The reservation (quota) system is an example of the effort to address social inequalities.
  • But these efforts did not convert into material or economic benefits for the poor.
  • Indian democracy has been preoccupied with transforming the pre-existing imbalance of social power.
  • The idea was to bring new political elites from Backward Classes. Backward-class people did not get substantial benefit from this; the material status of the poor remained unchanged.

Psephocracy

Psephocracy

A democracy in which democratic practices are honoured during the election (free and fair) and at peaceful transfer of power, but not in the period between two consecutive elections. This leads to electoral dictatorships.

Contradictions in the party system

Seven contradictions
PHIRFC

Personality cult at the Centre, High command parties, Increasing regional parties, Real choice absent, Fluid ideology, Consensus difficult, plus rising Independents: "Parties Hold Interests, Rarely Following Conscience."

  • The personality cult re-emerged at the central level.
  • High command-oriented, centralised parties.
  • Increasing influence of regional parties.
  • Multi-party system but absence of real choice.
  • Increasing number of independent candidates.
  • Fluid ideology of members: candidates now change parties and ideology frequently.
  • The multi-party system has made the system more representative, yet consensus-building has become difficult.
Handout alert

The handout says independents tend to perform poorly (Trends) and also lists increasing numbers of independent candidates (party-system contradictions). They answer different questions: numbers rise, success stays low. Also the paradox list has nine headings only if "contradictions in the party system" is counted as the last one; the handout does not number them.

5 · Democracy Index and International Democracy Day#

The Democracy Index

  • Scores countries on a 0 to 10 scale using 60 individual indicators in five core categories: electoral process and pluralism, functioning of government, political participation, political culture, civil liberties.
  • Each country is placed in one of four regime types: Full democracy, Flawed democracy, Hybrid regime, Authoritarian regime.
  • 2024 edition: global democracy is under strain; the average global score dropped to its lowest since the index began in 2006.
  • 2020 Index: the EIU ranked India a "Flawed democracy". India's score fell from an earlier peak to 6.61 in 2020, which the EIU described as democratic backsliding.
Five categories
EFPPC

Electoral process, Functioning of government, Participation, Political culture, Civil liberties: "Every Fair Poll Protects Citizens."

Index ladder

Full > Flawed > Hybrid > Authoritarian. India (2020): second step, Flawed, score 6.61.

International Democracy Day

15 September each year. On this date in 2007 the Universal Declaration on Democracy was adopted by the Inter-Parliamentary Union.
