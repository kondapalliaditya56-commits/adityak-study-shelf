2000PYQ 1(Prelims 2000) — Match List I–II: Four Land Revenue Systems

(Match List) Match List I with List II and select the correct answer using the codes given below the lists: List I (Description) List II (System) A. Land allotted to big feudal landlords 1. Jagirdari System B. Land allotted to revenue farmers / rent 2. Ryotwari System collectors C. Land allotted to each peasant with the 3. Mahalwari System right to sublet, mortgage, transfer, gift or sell D. Revenue settlements made at village level 4. Zamindari System

Answer: (B)

(b) ✓ — Per and Pillar 1 (Experiment 3: Permanent Settlement Act, 1793),, and: A (big feudal landlords) = Zamindari (List II #4); B (revenue farmers / rent collectors) = Jagirdari, the Mughal apex revenue collector (List II #1); C (each peasant with sub-let / mortgage / transfer rights) = Ryotwari (List II #2); D (settlements made at village level) = Mahalwari, post R.M. Bird 1833 (List II #3). The matched code = A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3.

Why others wrong: (a) A-1 would mean feudal landlords = Jagirdari, but Jagirdari is a Mughal revenue-assignment system, not feudal landlordism; the Zamindari label fits big feudal landlords. (c) A-3 wrongly maps feudal landlords to Mahalwari; Mahalwari was a village-community settlement, not a landlord system. (d) A-2 wrongly maps feudal landlords to Ryotwari; Ryotwari placed legal title in the ryot, the opposite of feudal landlordism.

Memory hook: Big feudal = Zamindari, peasant-with-title = Ryotwari, village mahal = Mahalwari, Mughal jagir = Jagirdari.

2001PYQ 2(Prelims 2001) — Why Zamindars Did NOT Issue Pattas Under PS1793

Under the Permanent Settlement, 1793, the Zamindars are required to issue pattas to the farmer which were not issued by many of the Zamindars. The reason was:

Answer: (B)

(b) ✓ — Per, the Permanent Settlement made Zamindars deposit only 10/11 of revenue to the State; everything they collected below that threshold sat outside State supervision. With no audit trail, issuing a patta (which legally recorded the ryot's rent) would only constrain the Zamindar. So Zamindars simply did not issue pattas — there was no official check upon them. Confirmed by 's ⚠ Alert (Prelims 2001 + 2011 + 2024 trap cluster).

Why others wrong: (a) records that the ryot became a "tenant at will" and patta system was ignored — relationship was coercive, not trust-based. (c) Pillar 2 places revenue responsibility on the Zamindar; patta issuance was the Zamindar's duty, not the British Government's. (d) shows ryots faced rising rents and eviction — they had every reason to want pattas as a legal safeguard, not the opposite.

Memory hook: No State check below 10/11 — so Zamindar pocketed extra and skipped the patta.

2011PYQ 3(Prelims 2011) — Litigation Surge After Cornwallis86-93's 1793 Settlement

The tendency for increased litigation was visible after the introduction of the land settlement system of Lord Cornwallis in 1793. The reason for this is normally traced to which of the following provisions?

Answer: (D)

(d) ✓ — None of (a), (b) and (c). The tendency for increased litigation after 1793 traces to the Cornwallis Code of 1793, whose reorganisation of the judicial system in practice made it inefficient: the District Collector was stripped of all judicial & magisterial powers, so revenue disputes once settled swiftly & informally on the spot now had to be formally filed in the Diwani Adalats; court fees were abolished to make justice "accessible," which flooded the courts with genuine & frivolous suits alike (fees reimposed in 1795); & the formal recognition of lawyers (Vakils) with a rigid hierarchy of appeals let cases be deliberately prolonged & appealed. None of the three listed provisions is that reason.

Why others wrong: (a) While true that Zamindars became absolute owners, the impoverished ryots did not have the means to drag these powerful landlords to court. This power imbalance led to exploitation, not a surge in formal litigation. (b) East India Company did not become the overlord of the Zamindars in 1793. The EIC became the sovereign overlord decades earlier, in 1765, when it acquired the Diwani rights of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa. The auctions by the Sunset Clause created grievances and disputes over land titles, but the tendency for higher litigation was due to the Cornwallis Code (1793). (c) Cornwallis's courts were anything but efficient — slow, costly, procedure-heavy.

Memory hook: Sunset Clause auctioned titles — every default became a court case.

2012PYQ 4(Prelims 2012) — Ryotwari: All Three Statements Correct

With reference to Ryotwari Settlement, consider the following statements: 1. The rent was paid directly by the peasants to the government. 2. The government gave Pattas to the Ryots. 3. The lands were surveyed and assessed before being taxed. Which of the statement(s) given above is/are correct?

Answer: (C)

(c) ✓ — Per: Statement 1 is correct — settlement was done directly with the ryot, so rent reached the government directly from the peasant. Statement 2 is correct, the State recognised the ryot's legal title and the Patta system (which the Permanent Settlement Act, 1793 had killed in Bengal) was preserved here. Statement 3 is correct — assessment was based on detailed field surveys (soil productivity, irrigation, acreage, crops, past trends) before tax was fixed. All three correct → option (c). Confirmed by 's ⚠ Alert (Prelims 2012 + 2017 trap cluster).

Why others wrong: (a) Only 1 — leaves out two correct statements (Pattas + survey). (b) 1 and 2 — leaves out the survey/assessment statement, which explicitly affirms. (d) None of these — directly contradicts.

Memory hook: Ryotwari = ryot pays State direct, gets a patta, after the survey.

2017PYQ 5(Prelims 2017) — Ryotwari Architects: Read and Munro, NOT Cornwallis

Who among the following was/were associated with the introduction of Ryotwari Settlement in India during the British rule? 1. Lord Cornwallis 2. Alexander Read 3. Thomas Munro

Answer: (C)

(c) ✓ — Per: Statement 1 (Cornwallis) is wrong — Cornwallis was architect of the Permanent Settlement Act, 1793 and even tried to extend it to Madras using the Poligars; Ryotwari was set up against his template. Why others wrong: (a) Only 1 — Cornwallis pushed Permanent Settlement, not Ryotwari. (b) 1 and 3 — wrongly retains Cornwallis. (d) 1, 2 and 3 — wrongly retains Cornwallis.

Memory hook: Read started it in 1792, Munro fixed it by 1820 — Cornwallis was on the other side.

2021PYQ 6(Prelims 2021) — Economic Result of British Rule = Commercialisation of

Agriculture Prelims 2021 · Single-correct Economically, one of the results of the British rule in India in the 19th century was the:

Answer: (C)

(c) ✓ — Per: the British demanded land revenue in cash, not in kind. To raise cash, ryots were pushed off food crops (rice, wheat, millets) into cash crops — indigo, cotton, opium, jute, sugarcane — destined for British factories and global commodity markets. This structural shift is precisely "commercialisation of Indian agriculture" and is recorded as one of the most important 19th-century economic legacies of British rule. Confirmed by 's ⚠ Alert (Prelims 2021 trap).

Why others wrong: (a) and the Alert mark this as wrong — handicrafts suffered under deindustrialisation, not export growth. (b) The Alert marks growth of Indian-owned factories as without conclusive evidence in the 19th century. (d) The Alert marks rapid urbanisation as without conclusive evidence in this period.

Memory hook: Cash revenue demand → cash crops → food security collapse → late-19th-century famines.

Memory hook: Cornwallis86-93 = Permanent only; Sunset Clause = Zamindar lost land on default. Ryotwari = Munro/Read, NOT Cornwallis.

2011PYQ 9(Prelims 2011) — What was the reason for Mahatma Gandhi to organise a

The administration did not suspend the land revenue collection in spite of a drought. 2. The administration proposed to introduce permanent settlement in Gujarat. Which of the statement(s) given above is/are correct?

Answer: (A)

(a) ✓ — Per in: under the Ryotwari settlement, Kheda's ryots were legally entitled to a remission of Land Revenue if crop yield fell below one-quarter of normal output. Statement 1 captures this exactly — revenue collection was not suspended despite the failed harvest. Statement 2 is wrong — Gujarat / Kheda was under Ryotwari (a Bombay Presidency area), and there was no proposal to introduce Permanent Settlement there. Hence only 1.

Why others wrong: (b) Only 2 — disproves the Permanent Settlement proposal claim. (c) Both 1 and 2 — wrongly retains Statement 2. (d) Neither 1 nor 2 — wrongly drops the legally entitled remission that affirms.

Memory hook: Crop failed, plague struck, law said remit — government refused, so MG launched Kheda.

Chapter-derived Mains practice with the demand, guiding themes and a conclusion.

FYQ 1 Compare the Permanent Settlement and the Ryotwari Settlement in terms of their impact on the cultivating class.Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words

Demand: Show how the choice of landowner shaped ryot outcomes; cite specific corrective Acts.

Guiding themes:

1. PS1793 — Zamindar as legal owner; ryot became "tenant at will"; Patta system collapsed; abwabs continued; absentee landlordism emerged (see 'Bengal — Three Experiments').

2. Corrective: Rent Act 1859 + Bengal Tenancy Act 1885 restored occupancy rights (see 'Bengal — Three Experiments').

3. Ryotwari — ryot as legal owner; direct State settlement; but over-assessment → indebtedness → Mirasidars became new big landowners (see 'Ryotwari Settlement').

4. Conclusion: both systems ruined the cultivating class through different mechanisms — neither delivered the security of the Mughal Patta system.

FYQ 2 Explain how the British policy of cash-revenue demand led to the commercialisation of Indian agriculture and its consequences.Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words

Demand: Trace the policy-to-outcome chain — cash demand → cash-crop shift → food security collapse → late-19th-century famines.

Guiding themes:

1. Mughal system allowed payment in kind; the British demanded cash (see 'Commercialization of Agriculture').

2. Ryots compelled — or induced by moneylenders and traders — to shift to cash crops: indigo, cotton, opium, jute, sugarcane.

3. Land that had historically grown rice, wheat and millets now given over to commodity crops destined for British factories or global markets.

4. Food security declined — less food grain in reserve when droughts/floods hit.

5. Directly fed the famines of the late 19th century (link to Drain Theory chapter).

6. Prelims 2021 anchor: commercialisation as the documented economic result of British rule (see 'Commercialization of Agriculture').
