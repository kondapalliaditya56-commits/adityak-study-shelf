CIC salaryRs 2,50,000 per month
IC salaryRs 2,25,000 per month

  • Salary and service conditions are prescribed via rules and delinked from CEC/EC pay parity.

Before

  • Tenure and conditions codified in the RTI Act
  • pay linked to the CEC and ECs (as the handout contrasts)

After (2019)

  • Tenure, salary, allowances and service conditions determined by executive rules
  • salary de-linked from Election Commission norms
  • CIC remains a statutory body (not constitutional)
Numbers

3 yrs or 65, no reappointment, 5 yrs combined. CIC 2.5 lakh, IC 2.25 lakh.

Handout alert

Ch 56 says the 2019 Amendment removed the five-year term; this chapter gives the notified tenure as 3 years or age 65 (whichever is earlier) and a 5-year ceiling on combined IC and CIC tenure. Use this chapter for the figures; Ch 56 for the principle that the Centre decides.

3 · Removal process#

Removal of CIC or IC by the President

Ground 1
Proved misbehaviour or incapacity: after a Supreme Court inquiry (if referred by the President)
Safeguard
The requirement of the Supreme Court's recommendation for misbehaviour or incapacity is retained
Ground 2
Without an SC inquiry, the President may remove on any of five grounds below
Grounds without SC inquiry
No.Ground
1Declared insolvent
2Convicted of an offence involving moral turpitude
3Engaged in paid employment during tenure
4Physically or mentally unfit to continue
5Holds any financial or other interest likely to affect duties prejudicially
Five grounds
ICPUF

Insolvent, Convicted (moral turpitude), Paid employment, Unfit (physical or mental), Financial interest: "I Can't Pay Us Fully."

Two tracks

Misbehaviour or incapacity = SC inquiry. The five other grounds = no SC inquiry.

5 · Issues and challenges#

RTI is a means to access to information. There is a gap between the right to information as a means and access to information as an end.

Attitude and political will

  • Bureaucratic mindset: RTI is an accountability tool, so the bureaucracy resists embracing it fully.
  • Absence of political will: the bill was well drafted and appreciated across the globe, but hesitancy in bureaucracy and then politicians weakened political will; many scams being exposed harmed the existing government.
  • Waning interest: media, social activists and civil society must keep generating awareness; of late interest in media and activists is waning.

Money, awareness and law

  • No specific budgetary allocation: no separate funds for RTI implementation; the 2nd ARC suggested 1 percent of flagship scheme funds for upgrading RTI infrastructure.
  • Limited awareness: a PwC study found only 12 percent of women, 26 percent of men, 13 percent of rural and 33 percent of the urban populace aware of the RTI law.
  • Vague terms and phrases in the law lead to implementation issues.

Commissions

  • IC dominated by retired bureaucrats affects impartiality.
  • Penal provisions rarely applied: Satark Nagrik Sangathan found a penalty was not imposed in 95 percent of the cases where it should have been; alleged soft corner of retired bureaucrats for the government machinery.
  • No organic relationship between CIC and SIC; ideally a national platform for collaboration, but none exists.
  • Orders not implemented: no contempt powers with the ICs.
  • Annual reports delayed: every IC must submit an annual report to the legislature; 21 out of 29 ICs did not submit a report in 2019-20; the SICs of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana have not published reports since 2017 and Odisha since 2015-16.

PIOs and records

  • Extra burden on PIO without incentives: a person with existing principal duties takes on the work, causing lack of motivation.
  • Juniors appointed as PIO to interpret the law; officers with sufficient seniority should be appointed.
  • Poor record keeping: a key reason for delay; a specialized record keeping agency to advise and train has been suggested.
  • Abuse of Schedule II: many bodies included against the spirit of the law.

Pendency

  • An estimated 40 to 60 lakh RTI requests are filed annually.
  • Pendency in 26 ICs is 2.55 lakh cases; vacancies in IC posts add to the delay.
  • Estimated average time to dispose of a request is 1 year 11 months; COVID worsened this.
  • Delay violates the spirit of the law since RTI requests are filed for contemporary needs.

6 · Impact of RTI#

  • Beyond transparency it has become a tool for grievance redressal; many citizens benefited.
  • It has helped expose corruption, wasteful expenditure, scams, junkets, false narratives, recruitment results, marks etc.
  • Rural population has gained access largely because of NGO help.
  • It became a national movement and aided other rights-based movements like the Right to Food and right to education.
  • RTI has been more successful for personally relevant information; where controversial information of large public interest is involved, the movement has fallen short.

Strengths

  • Transparency tool
  • grievance redressal
  • exposes corruption and waste
  • national movement aiding Right to Food and education

Gaps

  • Rural access depends on NGOs
  • falls short on controversial information of large public interest

Classification of confidential documents

  • The norms for classification are laid down by the Ministry of Home and are not in the public domain.
  • The Public Records Act, 1993 mandates a 5-year review of top secret, secret and confidential information for declassification or downgrading.
  • Declassification procedure and norms are also not public, so the discretion of the government has to be checked.
