1 · Articles 25-26 · Conversion · Essential Religious Practices · Temples#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Fundamental Rights 2 handout pp. 26-30. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Art 25 protects conscience and the right to profess, practise and propagate, but propagation is not a right to convert (Stainislaus). Art 26 protects denominations. Courts use the Essential Religious Practices test, with Santhara, Sabarimala and temple control as live examples.

FOUR Articles AT A Glance

ArtProtects
25freedom of conscience; profess, practise, propagate religion
26freedom to manage religious affairs
27no tax for promoting a particular religion
28religious instruction and worship in educational institutions

Article 25 AND Conversion

Art 25

  • subject to public order, morality, health and other Fundamental Rights
  • State may regulate economic, financial, political and other secular activity linked to religion and make welfare and reform laws
Note

Rev. Stainislaus v. State of Madhya Pradesh (1977): right to propagate does not include a Fundamental Right to convert another person; upheld laws against conversion by force, fraud or inducement.

Criticisms

  • propagation becomes a hollow right
  • religion is a matter of conscience
  • allurement is hard to define
  • burden of proof on voluntariness

State laws

  • Several States ban conversion by force, fraud, coercion, some inducement
  • Some need notice or declaration
  • Gujarat Freedom of Religion (Amendment) Act, 2021:
    • Linked conversion with interfaith marriage
    • Reporting and investigation
    • Gujarat HC stayed parts on interfaith marriage without proof of force, fraud or allurement
  • Issue: State may stop forced conversion but not intrude on conscience, privacy, choice

Article 26 AND Essential Religious Practices

Art 26

  • denominations may establish and maintain institutions, manage religious affairs, and own and administer property as per law (26(c), (d))

ERP doctrine (Shirur Mutt, 1954)

  • is it essential or integral to the religion? within protected religious affairs? subject to public order, morality, health, other rights? religious or really a secular activity the State can regulate?
Memory aid · EPSR

ERP test: Essential part? · Protected sphere? · Subject to limits? · Religious or secular? (own mnemonic)

Issues with ERP

  • courts lack theological competence
  • may privilege some practices
  • black-and-white answers on diverse beliefs
  • Reform vs autonomy
  • Balance religious freedom, equality, constitutional morality, reform

LIVE Examples

Santhara (Nikhil Soni, 2015)

  • Rajasthan HC declared the Jain fast-to-death unlawful, not an ERP
  • Supporters: spiritual discipline, not suicide, autonomy
  • SC stayed the judgment in 2015, so the HC ban is not operative
  1. 2016
    Haji Ali Dargah: Bombay HC upheld women's right to enter the inner sanctum
  2. 2016
    Shani Shingnapur: Bombay HC stressed women's access; women entered
  3. 2018
    Sabarimala: SC 4:1 held exclusion of women of menstruating age unconstitutional
  4. 2019
    review: larger questions on religious freedom and ERP referred to a larger Bench
  5. 2026
    nine-judge bench heard the reference; reserved judgment May 2026 (verify)

Four competing dimensions

  • individual vs State
  • individual vs individual
  • group vs State
  • State regulation in public interest

Loudspeakers and Azaan (Allahabad HC, 2020)

  • Azaan is religiously significant but loudspeakers are not essential
  • Azaan by human voice continues
  • Loudspeakers can be regulated for noise pollution

Religious Property AND Temple Management

Shirur Mutt principles

  • religious autonomy in protected affairs
  • State may regulate economic, financial, administrative activity
  • donations, accounts, property can be regulated
  • but no excessive interference (judicial review)
For regulationAgainst excess regulation
financial transparency and accountabilityweakens religious autonomy
stops misuse of donations and propertybureaucratisation, political interference
protects devotees, promotes reformexcessive State control

Models and cases

  • SGPC: historic Gurdwaras under the Sikh Gurdwaras Act, 1925, with elected community participation
  • Char Dham Devasthanam Act, 2019 (Uttarakhand):
    • Board over major shrines
    • Repealed 2021 after opposition
  • Sree Padmanabhaswamy (2020): SC upheld Travancore Royal Family's traditional management with court-set arrangement
  • temples with different arrangements: Tirupati, Jagannath, Vaishno Devi, Badrinath, Kedarnath

Property

  • 26(c), (d) own and administer property per law
  • Art 300A: no deprivation of property except by authority of law
  • religious property is not immune from lawful acquisition for public purposes, but must respect religious character

2 · Articles 27-28 · Secularism · Communalism#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Fundamental Rights 2 handout pp. 30-33. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Art 27 bars taxes that promote a religion; Art 28 bars religious instruction in wholly State-funded schools. India follows principled distance, not strict separation. Communalism, narrow secularism and weak institutions threaten it; the cure is acceptance, scientific temper, rule of law and inclusive development.

Articles 27 AND 28

Art 27

  • no one compelled to pay taxes whose proceeds go to promote a particular religion
  • a regulatory fee for administering religious institutions is not automatically barred
Institution (Art 28)Religious instruction
wholly State-fundedprohibited, subject to 28(2)
State-administered but set up under a religious endowment or trustpermitted where the trust requires it
State-aided or recognisedpermitted, but no compulsion without prescribed consent (28(3))

Education vs instruction

  • Aruna Roy (2002): religious education (about religions, philosophy, ethics) is not prohibited religious instruction (promoting adherence to one religion)
  • D. A. V. College (1971): teaching the life and teachings of Guru Nanak is not automatically prohibited instruction

Against compulsory prayers

  • freedom of conscience
  • Art 28
  • scientific temper

For

  • ethical values
  • Indian culture
  • value-based education
Way forward / key point

Way forward: inclusive, voluntary, value-based practice without imposing one community's beliefs.

Secularism

Meaning

  • George Jacob Holyoake popularised the term
  • State not controlled by religion
  • Freedom to follow, change or not follow a religion
  • No discrimination on religion

Indian model

  • "Secular" added to the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment, 1976
  • Spirit present from the start
  • not strict separation:
    • Principled Distance (Rajeev Bhargava): neither hostile to nor merged with religion
    • Engages for welfare, equality, reform
  • features:
    • No theocracy
    • Religious freedom
    • Equality of religions
    • Principled intervention
    • Protection of diversity
  • basis: Preamble, Arts 14-16, 25-28, 29-30, 51A

Cases

  • S. R. Bommai (1994): secularism is part of the Basic Structure
  • Balram Singh (2024): rejected challenges to "Socialist" and "Secular" in the Preamble
CountryModel
Indiaprincipled distance, equal freedom, intervention for reform
USAconstitutional separation of Church and State
Francelaïcité: secular public institutions, limits on public religious display (response to Church power)
UKestablished Church with broad religious freedom

Why secularism matters

  • J. S. Mill: harm principle, individual freedom
  • John Rawls: veil of ignorance, fairness
  • peace and harmony
  • scientific temper and reform
  • democracy: equal participation
Memory aid · IFPMD

Importance: Individual freedom (Mill) · Fairness (Rawls) · Peace · Modernity · Democracy. (own mnemonic)

Communalism

Meaning and types

  • Using religious identity to create divisions and competing political interests
  • Treats communities as homogeneous and conflicting
  • Majority communalism (may pose as nationalism) and minority communalism (separatism, polarisation)
  • Both threaten secularism and integration

Challenges to secularism

  • narrow conception: State-centric, tolerance not acceptance, ignores intra-religious (caste, sect) divides
  • extreme religiosity and polarisation:
    • Ritual and costume differences
    • Mobilisation on religious identity
    • Demonisation, mob violence
  • failure of institutions: family, education, political parties
  • weak law: no single Central law on communal violence, prevention, rehabilitation, compensation
  • developmental imbalances: insecurity from unequal opportunity
Memory aid · NEFLD

Challenges: Narrow concept · Extreme religiosity · Failing institutions · Law gaps · Development gaps. (own mnemonic)

Note

Sachar Committee (2006): documented Muslims' disadvantages in education, employment, credit, public-sector representation; urged equitable development. Amartya Sen: people have multiple identities; a singular identity breeds hostility and blocks cooperation.

Measures

  • acceptance over tolerance
  • scientific temper and constitutional morality
  • rule of law against violence and discrimination
  • inclusive development
  • constructive social work like Gandhi's

3 · Articles 29-30 · Minority Education · RTE · AMU#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Fundamental Rights 2 handout pp. 34-38. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • Art 29(1) protects any section's culture (not only minorities); 29(2) bars discrimination in admission. Art 30 lets minorities run their institutions but not maladminister. Minority status is State-wise (TMA Pai). Pramati exempted minority schools from RTE; AMU (2024) overruled Azeez Basha.

Articles 29 AND 30

Safeguards for minorities

  • common domain: Preamble, Arts 14-16, 25-28, 46, Fundamental Duties
  • special domain:
    • Arts 29, 30
    • 15(5) excludes Art 30(1) minority institutions from its special admission rules
ClauseContent
29(1)any section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture may conserve it; not only minorities
29(2)no denial of admission to State-maintained or aided institutions on religion, race, caste, language only
30(1)religious and linguistic minorities may establish and administer educational institutions of choice
30(1A)protects the right on compulsory acquisition of property
30(2)no discrimination in State aid because of minority management
Value addition

Right to administer is not right to maladminister. State may regulate standards, qualifications, transparency.

WHO IS A Minority

Note

T. M. A. Pai Foundation (2002): for Art 30, minority status (religious and linguistic) is determined State-wise, not nationally. E.g. Hindus are a religious minority in Punjab, Nagaland, Mizoram. The Centre's notified list is statutory, for schemes and institutions.

Six notified religious minorities

  • Muslims
  • Christians
  • Sikhs
  • Buddhists
  • Zoroastrians (Parsis)
  • Jains (notified 2014)
Memory aid · MCSBZJ

Six minorities: Muslims · Christians · Sikhs · Buddhists · Zoroastrians · Jains. (own mnemonic)

Linguistic minorities

  • Art 347: President may direct recognition of a language on substantial demand
  • Art 350A: mother-tongue instruction at primary stage
  • Art 350B:
    • Special Officer appointed by the President
    • Report laid before Parliament and sent to States

Institutions

  • NCM: statutory, National Commission for Minorities Act, 1992
  • NCMEI: NCMEI Act, 2004, on minority status and educational rights

Regulation AND RTE

29(2) vs 30(1)

  • Aided minority institutions may give reasonable preference to their community but cannot exclude all others
  • State may regulate admission for merit and transparency
  • Unaided institutions have more flexibility with fair, non-exploitative admission
Note

Sk. Md. Rafique (2020): West Bengal Madrasah Service Commission Act: teacher selection through a statutory commission is not automatically unconstitutional for minority institutions; regulation must not extinguish Art 30 rights.

Note

Pramati Educational and Cultural Trust (2014): RTE s.12(1)(c) (25% entry seats for weaker sections) cannot be applied to minority institutions in a way that impairs Art 30(1); aided and unaided minority institutions exempted. Critics: RTE aims at social inclusion; exemption reduces opportunity. Suggestion: admit weaker children from their own community.

Note

Anjuman Ishaat-e-Taleem Trust (Sept 2025) (verify): SC doubted the blanket exemption; read Arts 21A and 30 harmoniously; RTE need not destroy minority character; called for larger Bench. Pramati stays binding until altered.

Aligarh Muslim University

  1. 1877
    Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College (Sir Syed Ahmad Khan); AMU Act, 1920 makes it a university
  2. 1967
    S. Azeez Basha: not a minority institution because established by statute
  3. 1981
    AMU Amendment Act recognises the Muslim community's role
  4. 2005-06
    Allahabad HC rejected the minority-based reservation
  5. 2019
    SC refers issue to seven judges
  6. 8 Nov 2024
    AMU v. Naresh Agarwal: 4:3; statutory incorporation does not remove minority status; test = origin, purpose, community role; pre-1950 institutions can claim Art 30; national importance does not exclude. Azeez Basha overruled but AMU's status left to a regular Bench

Minority Welfare Schemes AND Significance

Against minority-specific schemes

  • Art 15(1) religion-only basis
  • welfare should follow deprivation
  • other protections exist

For

  • socio-economic disadvantage
  • Art 14 reasonable classification
  • Art 46
  • national integration
Way forward / key point

Minority schemes are not automatically unconstitutional; validity depends on legal basis, objective, eligibility, equality. Significance of 29-30: identity, equality, minority autonomy, inclusive education, integration.

4 · WAQF (amendment) ACT, 2025 · Nazul LAND#

Handout only · Saarthi IAS
Exam lens

Priority: Low. Mains PYQs quoted: None quoted in the handout · Fundamental Rights 2 handout pp. 38-39. Prelims PYQs quoted: none quoted in the material.

In simple words
  • The 2025 amendment changes Waqf registration, boards and dispute handling. The SC in September 2025 refused a full stay but paused the five-year Muslim requirement, executive decisions on government-land disputes, and capped non-Muslim members. The final ruling on validity is pending. Nazul land is government land under lease.

Background AND KEY Changes

Waqf

  • permanent dedication of property for religious, charitable or other purposes under Muslim law: mosques, dargahs, graveyards, schools
  • Waqf Act, 1995 was the main framework
  • The Waqf (Amendment) Act, 2025 (also linked to the UMEED framework) changes registration, management and oversight
ProvisionChange
Non-Muslim representationnon-Muslim members in the Central Waqf Council and State Waqf Boards
Waqf by userrestricts future recognition based only on long religious use; protects existing registered Waqfs
Five-year requirementperson creating a Waqf must have professed Islam for at least five years
Government oversightgovernment-designated officer in disputes over government property claimed as Waqf

For

  • transparency and accountability
  • property protection against fraudulent claims
  • modernisation of records
  • better supervision

Against

  • Art 26 autonomy hit by non-Muslim members
  • waqf by user hurts undocumented religious properties
  • five-year rule intrudes on religious freedom
  • separation of powers: executive deciding property rights

Constitutional questions

  • Art 14 equality
  • Art 25 (five-year rule)
  • Art 26 (denominational administration)
  • Separation of powers

Supreme Court Interim Order, September 2025

Note

SC refused to stay the whole Act but suspended parts (verify against the order):

Three directions

  • five-year practising Muslim rule: stayed until States set a mechanism to decide who qualifies
  • government property disputes:
    • Stayed executive power to change Waqf status and revenue records before adjudication
    • Disputes go to the Waqf Tribunal and courts
  • non-Muslim members: not suspended but capped at four in the Central Waqf Council and three in State Waqf Boards
Memory aid · FGN

Interim order: Five-year rule paused · Government-land disputes to tribunal · Non-Muslim members capped (4 and 3). (own mnemonic)

Value addition

Interim relief is not a final ruling on validity; keep the two apart in answers.

Nazul LAND

What it is

  • Government-owned land leasable to persons or institutions
  • Many came under government ownership in the colonial period
  • Government keeps ownership, others hold leasehold
  • Uses: housing, schools, hospitals
  • leasehold:
    • Right to use for a period
    • Freehold: ownership

Link to Waqf

  • dispute: can long religious use create Waqf status over land claimed by government? The interim order stops executives from altering disputed status before proper adjudication
  • Core issue: balance protection of religious endowments, property rights, transparency and lawful oversight
