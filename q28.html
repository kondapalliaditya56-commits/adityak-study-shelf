FYQ 1

Who was the Secretary of State for India when the Indian Councils Act of 1892 was enacted?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(d) ✓ — The record explicitly names Secretary of State: Lord Cross | Viceroy: Lansdowne88-94 (1888–1894) and the Act is also called the Lord Cross Act.

Why others wrong: (a) Lansdowne was the Viceroy (1888–94), not the SoS; (b) Salisbury is not the SoS named for ICA 1892; (c) Morley is linked to ICA 1909, not 1892.

Memory hook: Cross at the centre — the SoS, not the Viceroy.

FYQ 2

Which Viceroy's tenure (1888–1894) saw the passing of the Indian Councils Act of 1892?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(c) ✓ — The record fixes the Viceroy as "Lansdowne88-94 (1888–1894)", and reiterates "During Lansdowne88-94's tenure".

Why others wrong: (a) Ripon's tenure ended before 1892; (b) Dufferin preceded Lansdowne;

(d) Curzon came much later (1899–1905).

Memory hook: Lansdowne 88–94 — the Cross Act sits in the middle.

FYQ 3

Consider the following statements about the membership mechanism under the Indian Councils Act of 1892: 1. Non-official members were chosen by direct election from the general public. 2. The legal text of the Act explicitly used the word "election". 3. The mechanism was described as "nomination on the basis of recommendation". Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — The record states the word "election" was "deliberately omitted from the text of the Act" and the mechanism was strictly defined as 'nomination on the basis of recommendation'. No direct election existed.

Why others wrong: (a) The Act introduced no direct election by the public, so "1 only" is wrong; (c) Statement 2 is wrong — the word "election" was specifically avoided; (d)

Statement 1 is wrong — no direct election.

Memory hook: Nominated, never elected — the word the Act refused to print.

FYQ 4

Under the Indian Councils Act of 1892, which body directly recommended non-official members to the Imperial Legislative Council (ILC)?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(c) ✓ — The record specifies that for the ILC, the Viceroy nominated non-officials based on recommendations from the non-official members of the PLCs & the Bengal Chamber of Commerce — not directly from local bodies.

Why others wrong: (a) Municipalities and District Boards fed into PLCs, not ILC; (b) Universities and Trade Associations fed into PLCs; (d) Zamindars fed into PLCs, not directly into ILC.

Memory hook: Two tiers: local bodies feed PLCs; PLCs feed ILC.

FYQ 5

Which of the following was/were rights granted to non-official members of the Imperial Legislative Council under the Indian Councils Act of 1892? 1. The right to ask questions of the executive 2. The right to ask supplementary questions 3. The right to discuss the Budget 4. The right to vote on the Budget

Answer: (A)

(A)

(a) ✓ — The record grants questions (with 6 days' notice, no supplementary questions allowed) and Budget discussion (no vote, no amendment). Supplementaries and voting on the Budget were specifically denied.

Why others wrong: (b) Supplementary questions were "not allowed"; (c) Voting on the Budget was denied; (d) Both supplementaries and Budget vote were denied.

Memory hook: Ask once, talk once — never follow up, never vote.

FYQ 6

With reference to the size of the Imperial Legislative Council after the Indian Councils Act of 1892, what was the total number of additional members?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(d) ✓ — The record states: the total number of additional members was now set at 10 to 16 — and of these, at least half could be non-officials. It also notes the upgrade from 6–12 under ICA 1861.

Why others wrong: (a) 6–12 was the ICA 1861 figure, replaced by 1892; (b) 16–60 was the ICA 1909 (Morley–Minto) range; (c) No such figure appears for ICA 1892.

Memory hook: 10–16 in '92 — up from 6–12 of '61.

FYQ 7

The Indian Councils Act of 1892 has been seen as a catalyst for the rise of Extremism in Indian politics. Which of the following best explains this consequence?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — The record states: If 'prayers & petitions' could produce only this, then the method itself was bankrupt. The disillusionment seeded by ICA 1892 was the soil in which Extremism would take root.

Why others wrong: (a) No elected majority was granted; the council had an official majority;

(c) The Viceroy's veto was not abolished; (d) Separate electorates came with ICA 1909, not

1892.

Memory hook: Hollow Act, cracked faith — Extremism's seed-bed.

FYQ 8

Consider the following statements regarding the Viceroy's Executive Council (VEC) under the Indian Councils Act of 1892: 1. The VEC was reconstituted with an Indian majority. 2. The number of Ordinary Members was increased to ten. 3. The composition of the VEC remained unchanged from the position in 1874. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(c) ✓ — The record is explicit: The VEC under the Indian Councils Act of 1892 did not change at all, and summarises "VEC: Unchanged from 1874."

Why others wrong: (a) No Indian majority was introduced; (b) Ordinary Members remained six — Home, Revenue/Agriculture, Law, Military, Finance, Public Works — with no increase; (d) Both 1 and 2 are independently false.

Memory hook: VEC frozen since '74 — '92 didn't touch the inner cabinet.

FYQ 9

Which one of the following changes was made by the Indian Councils Act of 1892 with respect to Centre–Province relations?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(d) ✓ — The record states: the Governor no longer needed the Viceroy's approval to nominate Non-Officers to the PLC … This was a small but genuine decentralisation. All other aspects of Viceregal control over provinces remained as under ICA 1861.

Why others wrong: (a) No vote on Budget was given (even at the Centre, only discussion was allowed); (b) "a single unified budget with no separate provincial budget" remained; (c) These powers remained "exactly as they were under ICA 1861."

Memory hook: One small step — Governor freed from Viceregal pre-clearance.

FYQ 10

Which of the following civil service changes are correctly attributed to the Aitchison Commission's recommendations implemented during Lansdowne's tenure? 1. Covenanted Civil Services were renamed Imperial Civil Services 2. Uncovenanted Civil Services were renamed Provincial Civil Services 3. The Statutory Civil Services were abolished 4. These changes were enacted as provisions of the Indian Councils Act of 1892

Answer: (A)

(A)

(a) ✓ — The record lists CCS→ICS, UCS→PCS, and abolition of Statutory CS (created 1878) as administrative orders of Lansdowne's era on Aitchison Commission's recommendations. Crucially, they were administrative orders of the era, not provisions of the Indian Councils Act of 1892.

Why others wrong: (b), (c), (d) Statement 4 is the classic trap — these were administrative measures, not provisions of ICA 1892.

Memory hook: Same era, separate file — Aitchison sits outside the Act.

FYQ 11

Consider the following statements regarding the powers of non-official members in the Imperial Legislative Council under the Indian Councils Act of 1892: 1. They could move resolutions on policy matters. 2. They could demand a vote on a Government resolution. 3. They could discuss the annual Budget. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — The record states opposition members could not bring any resolution before the council, nor could they demand a vote on a government resolution, while "The Budget could be discussed for the first time" without vote or amendment.

Why others wrong: (a) Resolutions could not be brought by non-officials, so 1 is wrong; (c) No vote could be demanded on Government resolutions, so 2 is wrong; (d) Both 1 and 2 are independently false.

Memory hook: Talk to the Budget, but not the resolutions — speech only, no motion.

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — The record specifies the two tiers: District Boards, Municipalities, Universities, Trade Associations, and Zamindars fed PLCs; Bengal Chamber of Commerce and non-official members of PLCs fed the ILC.

FYQ 13

Which of the following demands of the Moderates was at least partially conceded by the Indian Councils Act of 1892?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(c) ✓ — The Moderates' scorecard lists "Expansion of ILC & PLCs: ✓ Yes — but dissatisfactory" as the only partially met demand. All other items are marked "✗ No."

Why others wrong: (a) Indianisation: ✗ No; (b) Non-official majority: ✗ No; (d)

Constitutionalism with Preamble: ✗ No.

FYQ 14

Consider the following conditions attached to asking questions in the Imperial Legislative Council under the Indian Councils Act of 1892: 1. Six days' prior notice was required 2. Discussion of the answers given was permitted 3. Supplementary questions were allowed. Which of the conditions given above is/are correct?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(a) ✓ — The record states: 6 days' prior notice was required, there could be no discussion of the answers given, and no supplementary questions were allowed. Only Statement 1 is correct.

Why others wrong: (b) Discussion of answers was barred; (c) Both 2 and 3 are false; (d)

Statements 2 and 3 are both false.

Memory hook: Six days' notice — no follow-up, no chat after.

FYQ 15

With reference to the Indian Councils Act of 1892, which one of the following statements best describes the legislative role of non-official Indian members in the Imperial Legislative Council?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — The record states: Their presence was advisory, not decisive — the legislative function was recommendatory, not mandatory. The council was recommendatory due to the official majority and the Viceroy's absolute veto.

Why others wrong: (a) Non-officials were outvoted by the official majority on every bill; (c) No suspensive veto was given; (d) No transferred subjects existed — that idea belongs to later reforms (GoI 1919).

Memory hook: Advisory voice, official vote — recommendatory, not mandatory.

FYQ 16

Disappointment with the Indian Councils Act of 1892 led the Moderates to formally raise their ceiling of political demand. The new goal — self-government on the lines of Australia and Canada — was officially adopted by the Indian National Congress at which session?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(c) ✓ — The record states: This officially became the goal of the Indian National Congress at the 1906 Calcutta session, named Swaraj — Self-Government within the Empire, also called Home Rule.

Why others wrong: (a) Madras 1887 predates the demand-shift; (b) Bombay 1904 — no such adoption per Chapter; (d) Surat 1907 is the split session, not the Swaraj-adoption session.

Memory hook: Calcutta 1906 — Moderates raise the bar to Swaraj.

FYQ 17

Why did the official majority in the Imperial Legislative Council under the Indian Councils Act of 1892 effectively neutralise the Indian non-official members?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(d) ✓ — The record states: the Viceroy's Executive Council plus the official additional members always comfortably outnumbered the non-official Indians. The official majority was not just a political preference — it was mathematically guaranteed by the structure of the Act.

Why others wrong: (a) Non-officials could vote; they were merely outvoted; (b) No such probation existed; (c) Dissolution power as described is not the basis of the official majority.

Memory hook: VEC + official additionals — the maths always wins.

FYQ 18

Consider the following statements about the Indian Councils Act of 1892: 1. It introduced an elected majority in the Imperial Legislative Council. 2. It permitted discussion of the Budget but not voting on it. 3. It made the Provincial Legislative Councils financially independent of the Centre. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(a) ✓ — Only Statement 2 is correct. The record confirms Budget discussion was allowed but no vote or amendment was permitted.

Why others wrong: (b) Statement 1 is wrong — no elected majority; the word "election" was avoided and an official majority prevailed; (c) Statement 3 is wrong — a single unified budget with no separate provincial budget remained; (d) Both 1 and 3 are independently false.

Memory hook: Talk the Budget, can't touch it — and provinces still on the Centre's purse.

FYQ 20

The Indian Councils Act of 1892 has been described as "simultaneously a result of the Moderates' struggle and proof of its failure." Which of the following best captures the reasoning behind this characterisation?

Answer: (D)

(D)

(d) ✓ — The record states the Act was an Act that gave just enough to show the British had listened, and little enough to show they had no intention of actually conceding power.

Why others wrong: (a) Most Moderate demands were rejected (six demands, one partially met); (b) The Moderates did secure council expansion, so they were not wholly ignored; (c) The Moderate–Extremist split (Surat) came in 1907, not at the moment of enactment.

Memory hook: Just enough to listen, little enough to refuse — the polite British No. Chapter – The Extremists and the Swadeshi Movement (1905–11)
