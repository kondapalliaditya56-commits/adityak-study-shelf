1 · The 'wicked problem' of corruption#

Despite many laws (PCA, Lokpal, CVC Act), corruption persists. A proliferation of laws often signals systemic inefficiency. Corruption is termed a 'wicked problem': it can only be controlled, not eliminated.

Five toolkits
SCARI

Stick, Carrot, Administrative and technical, Radical, International: "Some Cats Always Run Inside".

2 · Stick measures#

Stick measures
MeasureContent
Legal system and prosecutionHigher conviction rates deter (e.g. Botswana). In India delayed trials and lenient punishments reduce deterrence. Harsh penalties like China are unsuitable here because of rule-of-law gaps and risk of victimisation
Sting operations and entrapmentAdmissibility of sting evidence is ambiguous. Natural stings (citizen recordings) should be admissible. Legal clarity is needed to avoid misuse, as in Sweden's restrictive stance
Tightening anti-corruption lawsThe 2018 amendments diluted the PCA. Political will is lacking to plug loopholes in whistleblower laws, the CVC Act, the Lokpal Act etc.
Declaration of assetsMonitor disproportionate lifestyle indicators and conduct rigorous audits
Effective anti-corruption agenciesIndia's fragmented structure (CVC, CBI, Lokpal, ED) lacks focus. Singapore (CPIB) and Hong Kong (ICAC) succeed with a single dedicated agency
Public shaming and blacklistingNaming corrupt officials (as in South Africa) and blacklisting firms from public contracts are soft deterrents
Penalties targeting financial securityForfeiture of pensions and future financial benefits creates fear of long-term loss
Seven sticks
LSTAEPP

Legal prosecution, Sting, Tighter laws, Assets, Effective agency, Public shaming, Pension forfeiture: "Lions Snapped Their Angry Enemies, Pouncing Promptly".

India

  • Fragmented: CVC, CBI, Lokpal, ED lack focus

Success models

  • Singapore CPIB and Hong Kong ICAC: one dedicated agency

3 · Carrot measures#

  • Substantial pay increase: higher salaries, paired with strict enforcement, can improve integrity at entry level.
  • Citizen-centric anti-corruption helplines: Delhi's anti-corruption hotline lets citizens report bribe demands without fear. Effective whistleblower and witness protection laws are essential to replicate it.
Handout alert

The earlier chapter on the Problem of Corruption says simply raising salaries may not make a significant dent, while this chapter backs pay rises if paired with strict enforcement. Use both: pay helps only with enforcement.

4 · Administrative and technical measures#

Administrative measures
MeasureContent
Job rotationFrequent transfers in sensitive positions disrupt entrenched networks; Kautilya's Arthashastra supports this
Strengthen auditingCAG's limited coverage (25% of auditable units yearly) hampers oversight. Need manpower, IT tools and random audits
Procurement norms and transparencyInternal bid norms and a comprehensive Public Procurement Law needed. Public procurement is 30% of GDP and corruption-prone. The 2012 bill lapsed and urgent revival is needed
Digitisation of servicesOnline delivery reduces physical interface and curbs petty corruption
CCTV in high-risk zonesFunctional CCTV in police stations, borders and vulnerable departments; prioritise high-risk areas first
Five admin tools
JAPDC

Job rotation, Audit, Procurement, Digitisation, CCTV: "Just Ask People: Dial Complaints".

5 · Radical approaches#

Radical approaches
ApproachExample and note
Departmental disbanding (Georgia Model)Non-reformable departments (e.g. Traffic Police in Georgia, 2004) disbanded, fresh recruitment under stricter norms
Gender balance in bureaucracy (Peru)Greater women representation showed reduced corruption tendencies; can be piloted in India
Monetising bribes (Romania Model)Allowing official payments to avoid petty bribes (as in Romania's conscription policy); controversial for equity concerns
General amnestyAmnesty to disclose black money (with 50% surrender) is debated; stricter enforcement is essential afterwards
Radical four
DGMA

Disband (Georgia), Gender (Peru), Monetise (Romania), Amnesty: "Do Good, Make Amends".

7 · Critique of the global anti-corruption industry and alternatives#

  • Western countries ignored corruption until the 1990s. After globalisation, anti-corruption became a geopolitical tool.
  • The 'anti-corruption industry' with vested NGO interests often prioritises data production over ground impact. Cultural imperialism concerns persist.

Alternative approaches

  • Spiritual education: ethics and meditation in curricula to foster internal integrity.
  • Integrity Recognition Certificates (IRC): officially recognise honest officers to create peer-pressure networks against corruption; must be insulated from politicisation.

8 · Social audit: meaning and features#

What it is

In evaluating schemes, if the local population is involved through public hearings or Jan Sunwai, funds, resources, beneficiaries and records are laid bare. People give input and pick loopholes, which checks corruption, increases transparency, fixes accountability and strengthens democracy. The handout traces its origin to CSR in private companies. Details of implementation, resources, financial and non-financial, are shared with beneficiaries on a public platform (Jan Sunwai, public hearing). The Gram Sabha can be called so people can hold the local bureaucracy and Panchayat leaders accountable and evaluate real impact.

Key condition

Social audit works only with a sound access to information regime: public agencies must fully reveal all details of the scheme, or the process is a lost cause.

Features

Four features
FeatureMeaning
Multi-perspectiveBenefits beneficiaries, local leaders and civil servants; they can present their side if loopholes are found; bureaucracy, leaders and people all give perspectives
RegularPeriodic, depending on the scheme, so issues are re-evaluated and addressed continuously
ComparativeTime-based comparison and comparison across villages, showing weaknesses against what other villages did well
Strengthens demand sidePressure on local bureaucracy to implement honestly is weak; the process strengthens demand side weaknesses and indirectly targets supply side ones
Features
MRCD

Multi-perspective, Regular, Comparative, Demand side: "My Report Cards Deliver".

Significance

  • Improves people's participation in administration.
  • Benefits reach targeted persons.
  • Builds social capital (community building, people coming together for change).
  • Identifies human resource and training needs of civil servants.
  • Highlights development concerns.
  • Facilitates transparency and checks corruption.
  • Helps design improved programmes in future.

Issues

  • Not part of mainstream policy discourse.
  • A statutory requirement under very few programmes; NREGA is one where it must be held.
  • Lack of trained social auditors.
  • Opposition from vested interests.
  • Symptom-cause relationship: dealing only with surface symptoms (e.g. awareness drives for plague outbreaks) without root causes (poor sanitation, congested housing) gives minimal impact. A scheme's failure at a remote symptom level should not justify its discontinuation unless all causal layers are examined. Audits must examine multiple cause-effect layers, not in isolation, so trained auditors are essential.

9 · Strengthening social audit#

Strengthening plan
HeadingSteps
Legal and institutional frameworkModel Social Audit Law (inspired by Meghalaya's 2017 Act) for all public service schemes. Independent audit units such as Andhra Pradesh's SSAAT. Support the CAG and build PRI technical and human capacity
Empower stakeholdersPublic awareness and training of Gram Sabha members and social auditors (standards, ethics). NGO and media partnerships. Recognition schemes for active Gram Sabha participants
Standardise and digitiseOnline transparency portals for real-time access to audit reports, expenditure and PRI records. Proactive disclosure of project data. Unified audit guidelines and checklists across sectors
Best practices to scaleChhattisgarh Model: link social audits with grievance redressal and use wall writing for community transparency. Karnataka Model: rotate audit teams after every three audit cycles to prevent familiarity and collusion

The handout numbers the Chhattisgarh and Karnataka models as 26 and 27 (stray list numbers from a longer list); they are just two models. Numbers dropped.

Strengthening
LESA

Legal and institutional, Empower stakeholders, Standardise and digitise, Adopt best practices: "Learn, Educate, Standardise, Adopt".

10 · Success stories of social audit in India#

Five stories
StoryWhat happened
MGNREGA social audits in Andhra PradeshAutonomous Society for Social Audit, Accountability and Transparency (SSAAT). Trained village-level resource persons audited, leading to recovery of crores misappropriated in wage payments. The World Bank lauded it as a global best practice
Jharkhand Social Audit Unit (SAU)Covers MGNREGA, PDS and pensions. Exposed ghost beneficiaries, wage payment delays and ration diversion in PDS. SC in the Swaraj Abhiyan Case (2016) appreciated the role of social audits in MGNREGA. Built local capacity and empowered Gram Sabhas
Rajasthan MKSSMazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan jan sunwais in the 1990s revealed massive leakages. The movement led to the Right to Information Act, 2005
Citizen Report Cards, BengaluruA civil society organisation pioneered CRCs to evaluate public services. Surveys on water supply, public hospitals and transport led to measurable improvement: bribery rates fell and satisfaction rose. Endorsed in a 2003 study and now globally recognised
Benchmarking in municipal servicesLocal governments compared service delivery costs (e.g. cost per km of road construction). Public disclosure of the comparisons built citizen pressure on inefficient bodies and reduced corruption in urban local governance
Story to state

AP = SSAAT, Jharkhand = SAU, Rajasthan = MKSS, Bengaluru = CRC. MKSS leads to RTI 2005; Swaraj Abhiyan is 2016.

Four places
AJRB

Andhra, Jharkhand, Rajasthan, Bengaluru: "All Just Right Beautiful".
