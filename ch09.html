1 · How did a trading company become a State?#

  • Companies exist to make profit for their shareholders. The EIC was no different.
  • But what the EIC became in India went far beyond a trading enterprise. It became a Company State that, by 1857, controlled 63% of India's territory and 78% of its population.
  • This chapter deals with Empire Building by the EIC.
Teacher's board

On the slide the teacher struck out "controlled" and wrote "RULED": by 1857 the EIC ruled 63% of the territory and 78% of the population. His fork explains the numbers: India = British India (63% of territory, 78% of population) + 565 Princely States (37% of territory, 22% of population).

1.2 Two kinds of wars: for trade and for territory

  • The wars the EIC fought in India fall into two broad categories.
  • Wars for trade: fought to protect or expand trade.
  • Wars for territory: fought for empire building. These were different in nature and in motivation.
Teacher's board

The board's tree. The EIC was not formed for territory (T). Its goal was trade with the East Indies (not just India). So its wars were of two kinds. (1) For trade: either defensive, to protect present trade (the Carnatic Wars), or offensive, to expand trade (the Battle of Suvali 1612, from Chapter 8). (2) For territory: only in two situations, given below. Note the point for this chapter: the Carnatic Wars were wars for trade, even though they ended up laying the base for territory.

Wars for territory happened under two specific conditions.

  1. When the Governor or Governor-General in India was personally ambitious: an empire builder by temperament. Britain was far away. Communication was slow. These men had enough operational freedom to pursue wars that London might not have formally approved.
  2. At times the EIC Army put pressure for wars. Any army has a structural interest in war: war brings a bigger defence budget, prestige, promotions and political relevance to the military. The EIC Army was no different.

2 · Wars for trade: the Anglo-Mughal War (1686-90)#

  • The Anglo-Mughal War of 1686-90 was fought in Bengal and the Arabian Sea. It was the first significant military clash between the EIC and an Indian power.
  • It is also called Child's War, after two men:
  • Josiah Child: Chairman of the Court of Directors of the EIC in London.
  • John Child: Governor of Bombay and the "General" of all EIC forces in India.
  • The reasons were two-fold:
  • Aurangzeb 58-07's constant warfare was disrupting EIC trade (problems in sourcing goods from the hinterland).
  • Ongoing disputes with the Nawab of Bengal over bribes and heavy customs duties levied on the EIC.

Child's War, 1686 to 1691

The EIC seizes Hugli
It escalates: attacks Mughal ships in the Arabian Sea
Aurangzeb 58-07's military crushes the EIC in both Bombay and Bengal
By 1690 the Company surrenders
February 1690: Aurangzeb 58-07 pardons the EIC in return for Rs 1.5 lakh compensation
1691 Farman: old privileges restored and duty-free trade in Bengal for Rs 3,000 a year
Teacher's board

The starred box on the slide: "1651: the 1st EIC factory in Bengal, in Hughli." That is why Hugli is the place the EIC seizes in this war.

2.1 Why did Aurangzeb 58-07 not abolish the Company and expel the British?

Because the EIC was economically useful.

  • Its trade brought forex/gold into India, and Aurangzeb 58-07 needed them for war.
  • With Aurangzeb 58-07's own constant warfare disrupting domestic trade, the EIC's export business was, ironically, even more valuable to the Indian economy.
  • Indian bankers earned interest by lending money to the Company for the purchase of goods.
  • Indian merchants, artisans and ryots all participated in and benefited from this trade ecosystem.

2.2 The 1691 Farman

  • The 1691 Farman was technically a Parwana, because it was issued by the Subahdar of Bengal, not an imperial farman. (For Farman, Parwana and Nishan see Chapter 8.)
  • It was issued under Aurangzeb 58-07's approval.
  • It restored the EIC's old privileges and gave it the right to duty-free trade in Bengal in exchange for Rs 3,000 per annum.
  • The Company had been defeated militarily, yet it got this privilege, because of the benefits of European trade with India.
Teacher's board

Why give duty-free trade to a defeated company? The board's flow answers it. Ryots and artisans sell to the Indian merchant; the Indian merchant sells to the EIC; Indian bankers give loans to the EIC. Everyone in the chain earns. The money the EIC saves on duty will be used to buy more Indian goods, which means more exports. The starred aside: "TODAY we don't tax exports" (the same logic: a State gains more from exports than from taxing them).

3 · The Carnatic Wars (1746-48, 49-54, 56-63): why the French, and why three wars#

  • To understand why conquest by the EIC began in Bengal, one must first understand what was happening in South India, and why the French were there.
  • Joseph Francois Dupleix 42-54 was the Governor-General of the French EIC from 1742-54.
  • Dupleix 42-54 had personal ambitions of empire building and of dominating the international trade of South India. He would become the template for everything that followed.
  • By 1740, French EIC trade was roughly half the value of British EIC trade.
Teacher's board

The board's frame for all three wars. The reason was the international trade of India: "who will dominate the purchase of goods." The main players were the British EIC versus the French EIC. The 1st and 3rd wars were a result or a part of some international war (Britain versus France). The 2nd was caused by factionalism in Hyderabad and Carnatic: "R vs R" (rival against rival), with the French behind one rival and the British behind the other. Royal armies (British and French) took part in the 3rd Carnatic War 1756-63 ONLY. Result: 3 divided by 2 = 1.5 each: the French won the 1st war and the 1st phase of the 2nd; the British won the 2nd phase of the 2nd and the 3rd. After 1756-63 the French were limited to Chandernagore and Pondicherry (the full list of five posts is in section 6.4).

3.1 Reasons for the Carnatic Wars

The EIC-French contest in India had two overlapping reasons.

  1. European rivalry. Two global wars spilled into India. As the two nations fought, their respective companies also entered into hostilities in India.
  2. Succession disputes in Carnatic and Hyderabad contributed to the 2nd Carnatic War (1749-54). The strategy was simple: put your chosen candidates in power to gain strategic influence.

4 · The 1st Carnatic War (1746-48) and the Battle of Adyar/St Thome (1746)#

4.1 Reason

  • The 1st Carnatic War (1746-48) was the Indian chapter of the War of the Austrian Succession (1740-48).
  • In that war Britain and France, along with their respective allies, fought each other across Europe, America and India, each wanting its preferred candidate on the Austrian throne.

4.2 Events during the war

From Madras to Adyar, 1746

**Dupleix 42-54 captures the EIC's Fort St George in Madras
** the EIC is defeated
The Nawab of Carnatic, Anwaruddin 44-49, intervenes: he expected the French to hand over the fort to the Nawab as promised
Dupleix 42-54 refuses
Anwaruddin 44-49 sends an army of 10,000 cavalry
Battle of Adyar/St Thome (1746): about 700 French-trained Indian sepoys and 300 French soldiers completely destroy the Nawab's 10,000-strong traditional cavalry
  • The battle was fought on the north bank of the River Adyar.
  • The French force was roughly 700 French-trained Indian sepoys and 300 French soldiers. It completely destroyed the Nawab's 10,000-strong traditional cavalry.
  • What happened here changed the nature of power in India.

Note

The handout's Diagram 1 (section 7) adds one name the text does not give: "La Bourdonnais 35-46 takes Fort St George, Madras." The handout text and the slide credit the capture to Dupleix 42-54 (the Governor-General). La Bourdonnais 35-46 and Madras appear in Chapter 8.

4.3 Why the French won: three advantages of European warfare

The French won due to three critical advantages that would define European military superiority in India across all the wars to come.

AdvantageWhy it mattered
1. Superior artilleryLight, mobile European cannon that could be repositioned rapidly on the battlefield, against which massed cavalry had no answer (link: like the small bronze cannons of Babur 26-30, spelt Babar 26-30 in this handout)
2. Tactical disciplineEuropean-trained infantry fought in coordinated formations, including infantry squares that could hold ground against cavalry charges from any direction
3. Regular cash salariesUnlike Indian armies, where soldiers were often not paid regularly, European-trained sepoys received regular wages. This created a professional, reliable fighting force with no incentive to desert between campaigns
Teacher's board

On the three-advantages slide (Lecture 12, slide 69) the teacher underlined "light, mobile European cannon", "Babur 26-30", "formations", "infantry squares" and "small, disciplined, European-trained infantry with modern artillery." He labelled the photograph "Infantry Square" and traced its corner: the men face outward on every side, so a cavalry charge from any direction meets a wall of muskets. Remember

Takeaway (Adyar 1746). This was not just a military victory. For the first time it was proven conclusively that a small, disciplined, European-trained infantry with modern artillery could crush a vastly larger traditional Indian army. The psychological impact was enormous: it gave Europeans the confidence to start interfering directly in Indian political disputes, which is exactly what Dupleix 42-54 proceeded to do. The Indian royal elites also wanted to use the military service of the Europeans in their internal and external disputes.

Teacher's board

The Wagner Group analogy (handout). Have you heard of the Wagner Group? It is a company which sells military service to the Russian State. In a way the French were now the Wagner Group of India: a company whose army could be hired by Indian rulers. The teacher develops this on the board in section 5.1.

4.4 Consequence: the Treaty of Aix-La-Chapelle (1748)

  • However, in North America, which was more strategically important to France, Britain had won.
  • So in the Treaty of Aix-La-Chapelle (1748), which ended the War of the Austrian Succession, both sides agreed to return each other's possessions in America and India.
  • Madras went back to the EIC.
  • For Dupleix 42-54 this was deeply frustrating: his victories in India were traded away at a negotiating table in Europe.
  • But he was not done. His next opportunity came almost immediately, in the form of succession disputes: one in Hyderabad, one in Carnatic.
Teacher's board

The board's reasoning for the treaty. "The French won in India BUT the British won in the Americas." The two countries valued different places: Britain's main interest = India; France's main interest = the Americas, especially North and Central America. So France gave back Madras to recover what it had lost in America. Three results he lists: (1) no change in territory; (2) European infantry is far greater than Indian cavalry (the lesson of Adyar stays even though Madras is returned); (3) "Dupleix 42-54 KAT gaya" (Hindi: he was cut out, left empty-handed).

5 · The 2nd Carnatic War (1749-54): how Dupleix 42-54 invented the blueprint for empire in India#

5.0 The succession disputes that triggered the war

  • In Carnatic: Anwaruddin 44-49 had been appointed Nawab of Carnatic by the Nizam of Hyderabad in 1744. (The slide adds: he then became autonomous of the Nizam.) On his death a succession dispute began between:
  • his son Md Ali Wallajah (Md Ali 52-95), and
  • Chanda Sahib 49-52, the son-in-law of the earlier Nawab Dost Ali Khan 32-40 (1732-40).
  • In Hyderabad: Nizam-ul-Mulk 24-48 died in 1748. The rivals for the throne were:
  • his son Nasir Jung 48-50, and
  • his grandson Muzaffar Jung 50-51 (the slide adds: grandson from a daughter).
  • Dupleix 42-54 backed Muzaffar Jung 50-51 in Hyderabad and Chanda Sahib 49-52 in Carnatic. He wanted his chosen candidates in power for economic/trade and political influence.
  • An anxious EIC backed the opposing sides: Nasir Jung 48-50 in Hyderabad and Md Ali 52-95 in Carnatic.

5.2 Phase 2: the EIC bounces back

  • The EIC decided to fight back by supporting Md Ali 52-95 (Carnatic), who was holding out in Trichinopoly.

  • Clive 57-67 became a celebrated figure back in Britain.

  • By 1754 the French had lost the 2nd Carnatic War.

The board's theory of treaties (marked "imp factors"). War is expensive for both, the losing party and the victorious party, so both want an end to the war. The victor will agree to end the war if it has achieved its war aims, that is, the core goal of the war.   6. 1st war: Dupleix 42-54 takes Fort St George, Madras; refuses to hand it to Anwaruddin 44-49; Adyar/St Thome 1746 (north bank of the Adyar): 700 French-trained sepoys + 300 French beat 10,000 cavalry. Three advantages: light mobile artillery, tactical discipline (infantry squares), regular cash salaries. Aix-La-Chapelle 1748: Madras returned.
  7. 2nd war backers. French: Chanda Sahib 49-52 (Carnatic), Muzaffar Jung 50-51 (Hyderabad). EIC: Md Ali 52-95, Nasir Jung 48-50. Mnemonics: MuzaFFrance; Chanda (moon) and Paris = romance; Nasir Hussain, England captain.
  8. Phase 1: Ambur 1749 (Anwaruddin 44-49 killed); Dec 1750 Nasir Jung 48-50 killed; Feb 1751 Muzaffar Jung 50-51 killed at Lakkireddipalli Pass by the Nawab of Kurnool; Bussy installs Salabat Jung 51-62.
  9. Gains: Bussy resident at the Nizam's court; French army paid by Hyderabad through the Northern Sarkars; 2,00,000 pounds; Bussy's jagirs at Masulipatnam and Yanam ("boochi"); Dupleix 42-54 Nawab from the Krishna to Cape Comorin with Chanda Sahib 49-52 as Deputy Nawab of Arcot; Villianallur jagir Rs 1 lakh a year.
  10. Dupleix 42-54's model: candidate in power, army at his expense, territory as payment, Resident as the real power. Wagner Group analogy. Prelims 1996 answer: Dupleix 42-54.
  11. Phase 2: Dupleix 42-54 leaves Arcot unguarded for Trichinopoly; Clive 57-67 takes Arcot (1751) with 200 Europeans and 300 sepoys, 53-day defence; Paris refuses help; lost by 1754. Treaty of Pondicherry 1754: Dupleix 42-54 recalled (Godeheu); French keep Hyderabad, Bussy, Northern Sarkars and factories; Md Ali 52-95 Nawab; Chanda Sahib 49-52 beheaded by Pratap Singh 39-63 of Thanjavur.
  12. 3rd war: Lally (French State) with Bussy versus Eyre Coote (British State) with Clive 57-67. Wandiwash 1760 decisive; Pondicherry (HQ since 1701, Fort Louis) lost. France lost: Bussy left the Northern Sarkars (Clive 57-67 took them 1759); Lally's rude conduct; unpaid soldiers; British navy.
  13. EIC won all three: France starved Dupleix 42-54; leadership; navy; finances (private joint-stock firm versus State PSU; Plassey gains); French tactical mistakes (Arcot, Northern Sarkars).
  14. Treaty of Paris 1763: France back to pre-1749; five posts PM KYC (Pondicherry, Mahe, Karaikal, Yanam, Chandernagore); no strong army or forts. 1769 French EIC abolished. EIC monopoly of India's international trade (Portuguese 1612, Dutch 1650s, French 1763). Later: Treaty of Hyderabad 1766 (Northern Sarkars, Rs 7 lakh); Subsidiary Alliance Hyderabad 1798, Mysore 1799, Travancore 1805; Carnatic annexed 1801. Prelims 1995: Wandiwash = French vs EIC.
