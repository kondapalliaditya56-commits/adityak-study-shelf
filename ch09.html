Mains hooks

  • The teacher's board question (marked FYQ-M, year not written): 'Model of empire building (in India) by trading companies was founded by the French and implemented by the British. Analyse.' Founded = the 2nd Carnatic War (Dupleix 42-54's four steps); implemented = future topics (Plassey, Buxar, Subsidiary Alliance)
  • How did a trading company become a State? (wars for trade versus wars for territory; the two conditions: an ambitious Governor and EIC Army pressure)
  • Why did France lose and the EIC win the Carnatic Wars? (leadership, navy, finances, tactical mistakes; the five factors)
  • How did the Carnatic Wars lead to Plassey 1757 and to British domination of South India?

Dupleix 42-54 invented the model (back a claimant, station your army at his cost, take territory as payment, sit in his court as Resident); the British EIC, with deeper pockets and a stronger navy, implemented it.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: A profit-making company discovers that Indian quarrels can buy territory: Dupleix 42-54 proves it at Adyar 1746 and in the 2nd Carnatic War (back a claimant, station an army, take land, sit in court). The EIC reacts at Arcot, wins the 3rd war with royal armies, a navy and deeper pockets, and the Treaty of Paris 1763 leaves it the trade and the road to Plassey.

  1. A shop that became a State. Companies exist for profit, and the EIC was no different, yet by 1857 it ruled 63% of India's territory and 78% of its population. The 1661 Charter had given it forts, troops, war and peace and courts, and 1676 its own mint. Its wars were for trade (defensive, like the Carnatic Wars) or for territory, when a Governor was personally ambitious or the EIC Army pushed for war.
    → But first the Company met an Indian power, and lost.
  2. Child's War (1686-90). Aurangzeb 58-07's wars and quarrels over bribes and customs in Bengal pushed the EIC to seize Hugli and attack Mughal ships. Aurangzeb 58-07 crushed it in Bombay and Bengal, the Company surrendered by 1690, and in February 1690 he pardoned it for Rs 1.5 lakh. The 1691 Farman, technically a Parwana, gave duty-free trade in Bengal for Rs 3,000 a year, because the EIC brought forex and gold and fed bankers, merchants, artisans and ryots.
    → So the Company learns patience, and a rival arrives from France.
  3. Enter Dupleix. Joseph Francois Dupleix 42-54 is Governor-General of the French EIC from 1742 to 1754, with personal ambitions of empire and of dominating South India's trade; by 1740 French trade is about half of the British. The three Carnatic Wars are 1746-48, 1749-54 and 1756-63: the 1st and 3rd are global wars spilling into India, and the 2nd is factionalism, R versus R.
    → The first war begins in Europe, but Dupleix fights it in India.
  4. Adyar, 1746. The War of the Austrian Succession (1740-48) reaches India, and Dupleix 42-54 captures the EIC's Fort St George at Madras. Anwaruddin 44-49, Nawab of Carnatic, expected the fort and, when Dupleix 42-54 refused, sent 10,000 cavalry; at Adyar about 700 French-trained sepoys and 300 French soldiers destroyed them with light cannon, infantry squares and regular pay. It was a turning point: Indian kings now wanted European help to modernise their armies.
    → But Madras is traded away in Europe, and Dupleix 42-54 'aches'.
  5. Aix-La-Chapelle, 1748. The treaty ended the Austrian war and returned possessions in America and India, so Madras went back to the EIC. The French had won in India but the British in the Americas, and Britain's main interest was India while France's was the Americas. Nothing changed in territory, European infantry stayed far greater than Indian cavalry, and Dupleix 42-54 was left empty-handed.
    → But India offers new quarrels at once.
  6. Two quarrels, four claimants. In Carnatic, Md Ali 52-95 (son of Anwaruddin 44-49) faces Chanda Sahib 49-52 (son-in-law of Dost Ali Khan 32-40). In Hyderabad, after Nizam-ul-Mulk 24-48 dies in 1748, his son Nasir Jung 48-50 faces his grandson Muzaffar Jung 50-51. The French move first for Chanda Sahib 49-52 and Muzaffar Jung 50-51, and the anxious EIC reacts for Md Ali 52-95 and Nasir Jung 48-50, fearing lost trade.
    → The French strike first, and win.
  7. Phase 1: the French kingmaker. At Ambur in 1749 Anwaruddin 44-49 dies and Chanda Sahib 49-52 is made Nawab; by December 1750 Nasir Jung 48-50 is dead and Muzaffar Jung 50-51 is Nizam. Muzaffar Jung 50-51 is killed in February 1751 at Lakkireddipalli Pass, so Bussy installs Salabat Jung 51-62. The French gain a resident and an army at Hyderabad, paid for with the Northern Sarkars, 2,00,000 pounds and jagirs, and Dupleix 42-54 styles himself Nawab from the Krishna to Cape Comorin. His four steps are a model: a succession dispute, an army at the State's cost, territory as payment, a Resident.
    → But a model that works once can be matched by a rival.
  8. Phase 2: Arcot, 1751. Dupleix 42-54 fixes on Trichinopoly, where Md Ali 52-95 holds out, and leaves Arcot unguarded, so Clive 57-67 takes it from Madras with about 200 Europeans and 300 sepoys and holds it for 53 days. Paris, wanting to honour the 1748 treaty and keep America, refuses to help. By the Treaty of Pondicherry (1754) Dupleix 42-54 is recalled, Godeheu replaces him, the French keep Hyderabad influence and Md Ali 52-95 becomes Nawab of Carnatic; Chanda Sahib 49-52 is beheaded at Tanjore by Pratap Singh 39-63.
    → Now Europe's wars bring royal armies to India.
  9. The 3rd war and Wandiwash. The Seven Years' Global War (1756-63) brings royal armies for the first time: Count de Lally with Bussy for France, Eyre Coote with Clive 57-67 for Britain. Bussy leaves the Northern Sarkars to help Lally, so Clive 57-67 takes them in 1759, and Lally's rude conduct and unpaid soldiers weaken the French, who also face a superior British navy. Eyre Coote wins the decisive battle of Wandiwash on 22 January 1760 and Pondicherry falls in 1761.
    → The treaty table closes the book.
  10. Paris, 1763. France loses all gains since 1749, keeps five posts (Pondicherry, Mahe, Karaikal, Yanam, Chandernagore) and may not keep a strong army or fortify its bases; in 1769 the French EIC is abolished. Having countered the Portuguese by 1612 and the Dutch by the 1650s, the British EIC now has a monopoly over India's international trade. It won all three wars because France starved Dupleix 42-54, had weaker leadership and a weaker navy, and could not match a private joint-stock firm with deep capital.
    → But the real result is bigger than trade.
  11. From Carnatic to Plassey. The 3rd war brought large royal military forces to India, and that strength plus Clive 57-67's shrewd diplomacy made Plassey 1757 possible. In 1766 Hyderabad ceded the Northern Sarkars to the EIC for Rs 7 lakh a year; Hyderabad signed the first Subsidiary Alliance in 1798, Mysore in 1799, Travancore in 1805, and Carnatic was annexed in 1801. The French founded the model of empire building and the British implemented it.

In simple words#

  • The EIC was a profit-making company, yet by 1857 it ruled 63% of India's territory and 78% of its population. The 1661 Charter gave it forts, troops, war and peace and courts
  • 1676 gave it a mint
  • Its wars were of two kinds: for trade (defensive: the Carnatic Wars; offensive: Suvali 1612) and for territory (an ambitious Governor, or EIC Army pressure).
  • The Anglo-Mughal War (1686-90), or Child's War, was the first clash with an Indian power. The EIC lost, paid Rs 1.5 lakh and still won the 1691 Farman, because it was economically useful.
  • Dupleix 42-54 of the French EIC made the Carnatic Wars the first empire-building model. At Adyar 1746 1,000 French-trained men beat 10,000 cavalry
  • In the 2nd war he backed a claimant, stationed an army at his cost, took territory as payment and placed a Resident
  • The British reacted:
    • Clive 57-67 took Arcot (1751)
    • In the 3rd war (royal armies, Seven Years' Global War) Wandiwash 1760 broke the French and the Treaty of Paris 1763 left them five posts
  • The EIC won on leadership, navy, money and French mistakes, and the 3rd war's royal troops made Plassey 1757 possible.

1 · How did a trading company become a State?#

Story link

Chapter 1 of the story: a profit-making firm with State-like powers. Because the 1661 Charter gave forts, troops and courts, the Company could wage war. Ask: under which two conditions did wars for territory happen?

  • Companies exist to make profit for their shareholders. The EIC was no different.
  • But what the EIC became in India went far beyond a trading enterprise.
  • It became a Company State that, by 1857, controlled 63% of India's territory and 78% of its population.
  • This chapter deals with Empire Building by the EIC.
Teacher's board
  • On the slide the teacher struck out "controlled" and wrote "RULED": by 1857 the EIC ruled 63% of the territory and 78% of the population.
  • His fork explains the numbers: India = British India (63% of territory, 78% of population) + 565 Princely States (37% of territory, 22% of population).

1.2 Two kinds of wars: for trade and for territory

  • The wars the EIC fought in India fall into two broad categories. Wars for trade: fought to protect or expand trade. Wars for territory: fought for empire building. These were different in nature and in motivation.
Teacher's board
  • The board's tree. The EIC was not formed for territory (T).
  • Its goal was trade with the East Indies (not just India).
  • So its wars were of two kinds.
  • (1) For trade: either defensive, to protect present trade (the Carnatic Wars), or offensive, to expand trade (the Battle of Suvali 1612, from Chapter 8).
  • (2) For territory: only in two situations, given below.
  • Note the point for this chapter: the Carnatic Wars were wars for trade, even though they ended up laying the base for territory.

Wars for territory happened under two specific conditions.

  • 1. When the Governor or Governor-General in India was personally ambitious: an empire builder by temperament. Britain was far away. Communication was slow.
  • These men had enough operational freedom to pursue wars that London might not have formally approved.
  • 2. At times the EIC Army put pressure for wars.
  • Any army has a structural interest in war: war brings a bigger defence budget, prestige, promotions and political relevance to the military.
  • The EIC Army was no different.

The EIC Army's self-reinforcing war cycle (condition 2)

  • Overhype threats:

    • War brings budget, prestige, promotions

2 · Wars for trade: the Anglo-Mughal War (1686-90)#

Story link

Because Aurangzeb 58-07's wars and Bengal's customs disputes hurt trade, the Company picked a fight and lost. Ask: why did Aurangzeb 58-07 not expel it?

  • The Anglo-Mughal War of 1686-90 was fought in Bengal and the Arabian Sea.
  • It was the first significant military clash between the EIC and an Indian power.
  • It is also called Child's War, after two men: Josiah Child: Chairman of the Court of Directors of the EIC in London. John Child: Governor of Bombay and the "General" of all EIC forces in India.
  • The reasons were two-fold: Aurangzeb 58-07's constant warfare was disrupting EIC trade (problems in sourcing goods from the hinterland). Ongoing disputes with the Nawab of Bengal over bribes and heavy customs duties levied on the EIC.

Child's War, 1686 to 1691

  • The EIC seizes Hugli
  • It escalates: attacks Mughal ships in the Arabian Sea
  • Aurangzeb 58-07's military crushes the EIC in both Bombay and Bengal
  • By 1690 the Company surrenders
  • February 1690: Aurangzeb 58-07 pardons the EIC in return for Rs 1.5 lakh compensation
  • 1691 Farman: old privileges restored and duty-free trade in Bengal for Rs 3,000 a year
Teacher's board

The starred box on the slide: "1651: the 1st EIC factory in Bengal, in Hughli." That is why Hugli is the place the EIC seizes in this war.

2.1 Why did Aurangzeb 58-07 not abolish the Company and expel the British?

Because the EIC was economically useful.

  • Its trade brought forex/gold into India, and Aurangzeb 58-07 needed them for war.
  • With Aurangzeb 58-07's own constant warfare disrupting domestic trade, the EIC's export business was, ironically, even more valuable to the Indian economy.
  • Indian bankers earned interest by lending money to the Company for the purchase of goods.
  • Indian merchants, artisans and ryots all participated in and benefited from this trade ecosystem.

2.2 The 1691 Farman

  • The 1691 Farman was technically a Parwana, because it was issued by the Subahdar of Bengal, not an imperial farman.
  • (For Farman, Parwana and Nishan see Chapter 8.)
  • It was issued under Aurangzeb 58-07's approval.
  • It restored the EIC's old privileges and gave it the right to duty-free trade in Bengal in exchange for Rs 3,000 per annum.
  • The Company had been defeated militarily, yet it got this privilege, because of the benefits of European trade with India.
Teacher's board
  • Why give duty-free trade to a defeated company? The board's flow answers it.
  • Ryots and artisans sell to the Indian merchant
  • The Indian merchant sells to the EIC
  • Indian bankers give loans to the EIC. Everyone in the chain earns
  • The money the EIC saves on duty will be used to buy more Indian goods, which means more exports.
  • The starred aside: "TODAY we don't tax exports" (the same logic: a State gains more from exports than from taxing them).

3 · The Carnatic Wars (1746-48, 49-54, 56-63): why the French, and why three wars#

Story link

Because the rival in India was now France, three wars followed. Ask: which two wars were global spillovers, and which one was factionalism?

  • To understand why conquest by the EIC began in Bengal, one must first understand what was happening in South India, and why the French were there.
  • Joseph Francois Dupleix 42-54 was the Governor-General of the French EIC from 1742-54.
  • Dupleix 42-54 had personal ambitions of empire building and of dominating the international trade of South India.
  • He would become the template for everything that followed.
  • By 1740, French EIC trade was roughly half the value of British EIC trade.
Teacher's board
  • The board's frame for all three wars.
  • The reason was the international trade of India: "who will dominate the purchase of goods." The main players were the British EIC versus the French EIC.
  • The 1st and 3rd wars were a result or a part of some international war (Britain versus France).
  • The 2nd was caused by factionalism in Hyderabad and Carnatic: "R vs R" (rival against rival), with the French behind one rival and the British behind the other.
  • Royal armies (British and French) took part in the 3rd Carnatic War 1756-63 ONLY.
  • Result:
    • 3 divided by 2 = 1.5 each: the French won the 1st war and the 1st phase of the 2nd
    • The British won the 2nd phase of the 2nd and the 3rd.
  • After 1756-63 the French were limited to Chandernagore and Pondicherry (the full list of five posts is in section 6.4).

3.1 Reasons for the Carnatic Wars

  • The EIC-French contest in India had two overlapping reasons.
  • 1. European rivalry. Two global wars spilled into India.
  • As the two nations fought, their respective companies also entered into hostilities in India.
  • 2. Succession disputes in Carnatic and Hyderabad contributed to the 2nd Carnatic War (1749-54).
  • The strategy was simple: put your chosen candidates in power to gain strategic influence.

4 · The 1st Carnatic War (1746-48) and the Battle of Adyar/St Thome (1746)#

Story link

Because the Austrian war reached India, Dupleix 42-54 took Madras and met the Nawab's cavalry. Ask: who sent the 10,000 cavalry, and what did Adyar prove?

4.1 Reason

  • The 1st Carnatic War (1746-48) was the Indian chapter of the War of the Austrian Succession (1740-48).
  • In that war Britain and France, along with their respective allies, fought each other across Europe, America and India, each wanting its preferred candidate on the Austrian throne.

4.2 Events during the war

From Madras to Adyar, 1746

6.5 Link with Plassey, and what came later

Key takeaway
  • Takeaway: link with the Battle of Plassey (1757).
  • The 3rd Carnatic War brought large numbers of British Royal military troops to India, and it was this military strength, and Clive 57-67's shrewd diplomacy, that made Plassey possible.
  • Without Carnatic there may not have been a Plassey, which led to Empire Building by the EIC.

From a global war to Plassey (teacher's flow)

  • Seven Years' Global War (1756-63)
  • 3rd Carnatic War
  • British Royal military in India
  • Confidence of Clive 57-67 for accepting Jafar's offer: Plassey 1757

Later:

  • In the Treaty of Hyderabad (1766), Hyderabad formally ceded the Northern Sarkars to the EIC in exchange for Rs 7 lakh annually and protection against the Marathas and Mysore.
  • Hyderabad became the 1st Indian state to sign a Subsidiary Alliance with the EIC, in 1798.
  • Mysore signed in 1799 and Travancore in 1805, while Carnatic was annexed in 1801.
  • Thus the Carnatic Wars, though fought for the international trade of India, laid the foundation of British domination of South India.

Recall sheet#

Read the question, answer in your head, then tap to check.

What did the EIC become in India by 1857?A Company State ruling 63% of India's territory and 78% of its population.
What four rights did the 1661 Charter give the EIC?To build forts, command troops, make war and peace, and exercise civil and criminal jurisdiction over its settlements in India. (The right to mint its own money came in 1676.)
Under which two conditions did wars for territory happen?A personally ambitious Governor or Governor-General (an empire builder), and pressure from the EIC Army.
Name the four major empire builders.Robert Clive 57-67 (1757-60 and 1765-67), Richard Wellesley 98-05, Francis Hastings 13-23, Dalhousie 48-56.
What is the EIC Army's self-reinforcing war cycle?Overhyped threats lead to pressure to expand; expansion creates new borders; new borders bring new threats, fictional or real.
Why is the Anglo-Mughal War (1686-90) called Child's War?After Josiah Child (Chairman, Court of Directors) and John Child (Governor of Bombay and "General" of EIC forces in India).
How did the Anglo-Mughal War end, and what did the 1691 Farman grant?Aurangzeb 58-07 crushed the EIC in Bombay and Bengal and pardoned it in February 1690 for Rs 1.5 lakh. The 1691 Farman (technically a Parwana, by the Subahdar of Bengal) restored old privileges and gave duty-free trade in Bengal for Rs 3,000 a year.
Why did Aurangzeb 58-07 not expel the EIC?It was economically useful: forex/gold for his wars, interest for Indian bankers, and gains for merchants, artisans and ryots.
Who was Dupleix 42-54?Governor-General of the French EIC, 1742-54, with ambitions of empire building and of dominating South India's international trade.
What caused each of the three Carnatic Wars?1st: the War of the Austrian Succession (1740-48). 2nd: succession disputes (factionalism) in Carnatic and Hyderabad. 3rd: the Seven Years' Global War (1756-63).
What happened at Adyar/St Thome in 1746?About 700 French-trained sepoys and 300 French soldiers destroyed Anwaruddin 44-49's 10,000 cavalry on the north bank of the Adyar.
Three European military advantages shown at Adyar?Superior light mobile artillery, tactical discipline (infantry squares), and regular cash salaries.
Why is Adyar 1746 a turning point?It proved a small disciplined European-trained infantry could crush a far larger Indian army; Europeans began interfering in Indian disputes and Indian rulers wanted their military service.
What is the Wagner Group analogy?A company that sells military service; the French EIC's army was hired by Indian claimants in the same way.
Treaty of Aix-La-Chapelle (1748)?Ended the Austrian War; possessions returned in America and India; Madras went back to the EIC.
Who backed whom in the 2nd Carnatic War?Carnatic: Chanda Sahib 49-52 (French) versus Md Ali 52-95 (EIC). Hyderabad: Muzaffar Jung 50-51 (French) versus Nasir Jung 48-50 (EIC).
What happened at Ambur (1749) and at Lakkireddipalli Pass (February 1751)?Ambur: Nawab Anwaruddin 44-49 was killed and Chanda Sahib 49-52 was declared Nawab at Arcot. Lakkireddipalli Pass: Muzaffar Jung 50-51 was killed by the Afghan Nawab of Kurnool, who felt cheated of promised territory.
What did the French gain in Hyderabad?Bussy as resident and agent, a French army paid by Hyderabad through the Northern Sarkars, 2,00,000 pounds, and Bussy's jagirs in Masulipatnam and Yanam.
What did Dupleix 42-54 gain in Carnatic?He declared himself Nawab of all lands between the Krishna and Cape Comorin, with Chanda Sahib 49-52 as Deputy Nawab of Arcot, plus a Rs 1 lakh jagir at Villianallur.
What is "boochi"?A word in Andhra folklore meaning ghost or the feared, derived from Bussy's name, preserving his terror in the Northern Sarkars.
State Dupleix 42-54's model of empire building.Put your candidate in power, station your army at his expense, extract territory as payment, and sit in the court as the real power.
What was Dupleix 42-54's critical mistake in Phase 2?He left the capital Arcot unguarded to focus on Trichinopoly; Clive 57-67 took Arcot and held it for 53 days.
Terms of the Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)?Dupleix 42-54 recalled (Godeheu replaced him); French kept Hyderabad influence, the Northern Sarkars and their factories; Md Ali 52-95 became Nawab of Carnatic.
What happened to Chanda Sahib 49-52?He escaped to Tanjore and was beheaded by Pratap Singh 39-63, the Maratha Raja of Thanjavur.
Who led each side in the 3rd Carnatic War?Count de Lally (French State) with Bussy; Eyre Coote (British State) with Robert Clive 57-67.
Four reasons France lost the 3rd Carnatic War?Bussy abandoned the Northern Sarkars; Lally's poor leadership; financial weakness (unpaid soldiers); the superior British navy.
Terms of the Treaty of Paris (1763), and what followed in 1769?France lost all gains since 1749, kept five posts (Pondicherry, Mahe, Karaikal, Yanam, Chandernagore) and could not keep a strong army or fortify. In 1769 France abolished the French EIC.
How are the Carnatic Wars linked to Plassey?The 3rd Carnatic War brought British Royal troops to India; that strength and Clive 57-67's diplomacy made Plassey possible.
Treaty of Hyderabad (1766)?Hyderabad ceded the Northern Sarkars to the EIC for Rs 7 lakh a year and protection against the Marathas and Mysore.
Order of Subsidiary Alliances in the south?Hyderabad 1798 (the first), Mysore 1799, Travancore 1805; Carnatic was annexed in 1801.
