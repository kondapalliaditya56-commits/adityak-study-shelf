Closing idea: Article 244 gives tribal India two tracks of protection: guardianship through the Governor and Tribes Advisory Council in the Fifth, self-rule through elected councils in the Sixth.

In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Art. 244 (Part X) sets a special system for scheduled areas and tribal areas. The Fifth Schedule covers scheduled areas and tribes in any State except Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram
  • The Sixth Schedule covers tribal areas of those four North-East States
  • Fifth Schedule: the President declares or changes Scheduled Areas after consulting the Governor. The Governor reports to the President and can adapt laws and make regulations (with the President's assent). A Tribes Advisory Council (TAC) of 20 members advises.
  • Sixth Schedule: tribal areas become Autonomous Districts (and Autonomous Regions), each with a 30-member council (26 elected, 4 nominated) that can make laws, run courts and collect land revenue and taxes.
  • Laws of Parliament and the State do not automatically apply in autonomous areas
  • They apply only with changes or not at all. The Governor can order an inquiry and even dissolve a council
  • Reviews: Dhebar Commission (appointed 1960, report 1961) and Bhuria Commission (2002, report 2004).
  • PESA Act, 1996 (in force 24 December 1996) extends Panchayati Raj to Fifth Schedule areas and makes the Gram Sabha the nodal authority, with ownership of minor forest produce.

Key terms

Scheduled Area
Tribal-dominated area declared by the President under the Fifth Schedule (outside the four NE States)
Tribal Area
Area in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura or Mizoram governed by the Sixth Schedule
Tribes Advisory Council
20-member body in each State with Scheduled Areas; three-fourths are ST members of the Assembly
Autonomous District
Tribal area of the four NE States given self-governing powers, still under the State's executive authority
Autonomous Region
Sub-division of a district when many tribes live there, with its own Regional Council
District Council

1 · Article 244: two tracks#

  • Article 244, in Part X, creates a distinct administrative regime for "scheduled areas" and "tribal areas" because of their unique social, cultural and historical circumstances.

The two tracks

  • Fifth Schedule: Administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in any State except Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram
  • Sixth Schedule: Autonomous arrangements for the tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram
  • Shortcut: Track shortcut. Fifth = outside the north-east, Governor and TAC. Sixth = inside the four north-east States, elected councils.

Also in the handout

  • Its provisions apply to scheduled areas and tribal populations outside the specified northeastern region and provide the central statutory basis for administration in those parts of the country.

2 · Fifth Schedule: Scheduled Areas#

  • Scheduled Areas are largely inhabited by tribal communities who have faced social and economic marginalisation. The Constitution limits the direct application of the ordinary State administration and gives the Union a greater supervisory role.

Identification and modification

  • The President holds exclusive authority to designate any area as a Scheduled Area, in consultation with the Governor.
  • This includes increasing or reducing the area, modifying boundaries, and cancelling or issuing fresh notifications.

Executive powers

  • The State's executive power continues within Scheduled Areas.
  • The Governor carries a special responsibility: periodic reports to the President on administration, annually or whenever directed.
  • The Union may issue directions to the State for good governance and welfare.

Fifth Schedule in four moves

  • 1 President: Declares, increases, reduces and cancels Scheduled Areas
  • 2 Governor: Reports to the President annually or when asked
  • 3 Tribes Advisory Council: Each State with Scheduled Areas must create one
  • 4 Governor regulates:
    • Excludes or modifies laws
    • Frames regulations on land and money-lending
Fifth Schedule in four moves
  • Memory aid (Four moves): President declares, Governor reports, TAC, Regulations: "Pretty Girls Take Rides".

Handout alert: This chapter says "total 20 members". The Schedules chapter (Ch 4) says "up to 20" and adds that, if STs have little representation, the Governor nominates and not less than half must be ST persons. Use "up to 20, about three-fourths ST MLAs" for answers.

Governor's legislative and regulatory power

  • The Governor may exclude or modify the application of Acts of Parliament or the State law in Scheduled Areas.
  • The Governor may frame regulations to: prevent or regulate land transfer among Scheduled Tribe members, control land allotment, and restrict exploitative money-lending.
  • Regulations need the President's assent before they come into force.
  • The Governor may even repeal or amend existing laws in Scheduled Areas, subject to constitutional approval.
  • Memory aid (Regulation topics): Transfer of land, Allotment of land, Money-lending: "Tribal Areas Matter".

5 · Sixth Schedule: tribal areas of the north-east#

  • It covers Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram, where tribal groups kept their indigenous cultural identity, customs and traditional institutions. The Constitution gives them substantial autonomy and self-governing powers.

Structure

  • The tribal areas are Autonomous Districts, still within the State Government's executive authority.
  • The Governor can restructure them: alter boundaries, size, names, and create or abolish districts.
  • If several tribes live in one district, the Governor may form Autonomous Regions with separate Regional Councils.

  • Memory aid (Sixth States): Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram: "All My Tribal Mates".

Also in the handout

  • Although granted autonomy, they remain within the overall executive authority of the respective State Government.
  • Creation of Autonomous Regions within Districts If multiple tribes inhabit a single district, the Governor may further subdivide the area into Autonomous Regions to ensure adequate representation for each tribal group.

6 · Sixth Schedule: powers of the councils#

Law-making

  • District and Regional Councils make laws on: land use and ownership, forests (excluding reserved forests), water resources like canals, village administration, inheritance, customary marriage and divorce, local social customs. These laws come into force only with the Governor's assent.
  • Memory aid (Seven subjects): Land, Forests (not reserved), Water, Village administration, Inheritance, Marriage and divorce, Customs: "Little Frogs Wade Very Indifferently Most Congested".
Current affairs · July 2026AHDC versus Sixth Schedule ADCAHDCs are statutory (LAHDC Act 1995, Leh 1995, Kargil 2003, 30 members each) and cannot make laws, Sixth Schedule ADCs under Art 244(2) can, and have village courts.

Justice

  • Councils may set up village councils or courts for civil and tribal cases.
  • Appeals go to the District or Regional Council.
  • The Governor decides the extent of High Court jurisdiction over these courts.

Administration and welfare

  • District Councils can manage primary schools, markets, dispensaries, roads, ferries and fisheries.
  • They may regulate money-lending and trade by non-tribals, subject to the Governor's assent.
  • Memory aid (Council works): Primary schools, Markets, Dispensaries, Roads, Ferries and fisheries: "Pay My Dear Rickshaw Fare".

Finance

  • Assess and collect land revenue and impose certain local taxes: an independent financial base.

General laws and oversight

  • Acts of Parliament or the State Legislature do not automatically apply: they apply with modifications or are excluded.
  • The Governor may set up a Commission of Inquiry
  • On its recommendation the Governor can modify arrangements or even dissolve a District or Regional Council

Also in the handout

  • Unlike tribal populations in many other parts of India who have assimilated into mainstream society to a significant extent, these northeastern tribes continue to live by their own cultural and social value systems.
  • Judicial Functions through Local Courts To uphold customary justice systems, the Councils may: - Establish village councils or courts - Handle civil disputes and tribal cases Appeals may be taken to the District or Regional Council.
  • Administrative Powers and Community Welfare Responsibilities District Councils can manage and develop public service and commerce infrastructure: - Primary schools - Markets - Dispensaries - Roads - Ferries and fisheries They may also regulate money-lending and trade by non-tribals, subject to Governor’s assent, to prevent exploitation of tribal communities.

8 · PESA Act, 1996: background#

  • The Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 extended the 73rd Amendment (1992) to Fifth Schedule areas, which are mostly inhabited by Scheduled Tribes.

How PESA came about

  • 1992: 73rd Amendment constitutionalises Panchayati Raj but does not automatically apply to Scheduled Areas
  • Art. 243M(4)(b): Parliament may, by law, extend Part IX to Scheduled Areas with suitable modifications and exceptions
  • 1995: Dilip Singh Bhuria Committee (an MP) examines how to adapt Panchayati Raj to tribal traditions
  • 24 December 1996: PESA Act comes into force

Gram Sabha powers under PESA

  • Planning and welfare:
    • Approve development plans and programmes
    • Identify beneficiaries of poverty alleviation and welfare programmes
  • Resources and rights:
    • Own MFP (tendu leaves, honey, bamboo, etc.) with full rights to collect, process and sell
    • Be consulted to prevent land alienation and restore unlawfully alienated land
  • Markets and money:
    • Control haats (weekly markets)
    • Regulate money lending
  • Village resources and justice:
    • Manage minor water bodies and local natural resources sustainably
    • Resolve minor disputes by traditional customary methods
  • Memory aid (Gram Sabha's seven): Development plans, Beneficiaries, MFP, Land alienation, Haats and money, Water and resources, Resolving disputes: "Don't Break My Lovely Hot Water Rack".
  • PYQ trap: PESA objectives: self-governance, recognising traditional rights, freeing tribals from exploitation. It does not create autonomous regions (2013).

Traps#

Trap 1 · Fifth versus Sixth Schedule Both come under Art. 244. The Fifth applies to scheduled areas in every State except four; the Sixth only to tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram. Rule: Sixth = four NE States only; everywhere else = Fifth.
Trap 2 · President or Governor In the Fifth Schedule the President declares Scheduled Areas (after consulting the Governor). In the Sixth the Governor reorganises districts, and council laws need the Governor's assent. Rule: declare Scheduled Area = President; reshape Autonomous Districts = Governor.
Trap 3 · 20 or 30 The Tribes Advisory Council has 20 members (three-fourths ST MLAs). A District Council has 30 (26 elected plus 4 nominated). Rule: Advisory Council 20; District Council 30.
Trap 4 · Two Bhurias and Dhebar Dhebar Commission: 1960, report 1961. Bhuria Commission on Scheduled Areas: 2002, report 2004. A separate Bhuria Committee (1995) shaped PESA. Rule: Dhebar first (1961); Bhuria commission 2004; Bhuria committee 1995 = PESA.

Book edition: simple words and memory words#

In simple words

  • Art. 244 (Part X) sets a special system for scheduled areas and tribal areas. The Fifth Schedule covers scheduled areas and tribes in any State except Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram; the Sixth Schedule covers tribal areas of those four North-East States.
  • Fifth Schedule: the President declares or changes Scheduled Areas after consulting the Governor. The Governor reports to the President and can adapt laws and make regulations (with the President's assent). A Tribes Advisory Council (TAC) of 20 members advises.
  • Sixth Schedule: tribal areas become Autonomous Districts (and Autonomous Regions), each with a 30-member council (26 elected, 4 nominated) that can make laws, run courts and collect land revenue and taxes.
  • Laws of Parliament and the State do not automatically apply in autonomous areas; they apply only with changes or not at all. The Governor can order an inquiry and even dissolve a council.
  • Reviews: Dhebar Commission (appointed 1960, report 1961) and Bhuria Commission (2002, report 2004).
  • PESA Act, 1996 (in force 24 December 1996) extends Panchayati Raj to Fifth Schedule areas and makes the Gram Sabha the nodal authority, with ownership of minor forest produce.

Key terms

Scheduled Area
Tribal-dominated area declared by the President under the Fifth Schedule (outside the four NE States)
Tribal Area
Area in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura or Mizoram governed by the Sixth Schedule
Tribes Advisory Council
20-member body in each State with Scheduled Areas; three-fourths are ST members of the Assembly
Autonomous District
Tribal area of the four NE States given self-governing powers, still under the State's executive authority
Autonomous Region
Sub-division of a district when many tribes live there, with its own Regional Council
District Council
30 members: 26 elected for five years, 4 nominated by the Governor (during pleasure)
PESA Act, 1996
Law extending Part IX (Panchayats) to Fifth Schedule areas; based on the Bhuria Committee (1995)
Minor Forest Produce (MFP)
Items such as tendu leaves, honey and bamboo; the Gram Sabha owns and manages them under PESA
