Exam lens#

Prelims hooks

  • Cabinet Mission 1946 is the anchor: members, three sections A/B/C, three union subjects, 10-year lock, Constituent Assembly elected by PLAs, Direct Action Day 16 Aug 1946. Over 10 PYQs.
  • Wavell Plan 1945: Shimla Conference, 50:50 Hindus and Muslims in the VEC, all Indians except Governor General and C-in-C, collapse over ML1906's monopoly on Muslim seats.
  • Rajaji Formula 1944: plebiscite, confederation, Gandhi-Jinnah talks, pamphlet 'The Way Out' (Prelims 2010).
  • Interim Government 1946: Nehru head, Liaqat Ali Khan got Finance, Patel Home, Baldev Singh Defence.
  • Plan Balkan (Mountbatten, April 1947), 3rd June Plan, Radcliffe Line (17 Aug 1947), Indian Independence Act provisions.
  • Princely States: 565 = 552 to India + 13 to Pakistan; 549 by accession + J&K, Junagadh, Hyderabad.
  • People facts: Kripalani INC President 1947, Gandhi not a Congress member at independence, Ambedkar elected from West Bengal, Sachchidananda Sinha provisional President of CA.

Mains hooks

  • Role of British imperial power in complicating the transfer of power in the 1940s (teacher's board PYQ): veto to ML1906, balkanisation moves, passive mediator, late Radcliffe Line.
  • Why partition: British divide and rule, WEMC Muslims, Hindu revivalism, INC's own concessions (Bipin Chandra's two failures).
  • Cabinet Mission as a Two-Nation, One-State plan: grouping compulsory or voluntary.
  • Compare Rajaji Formula, Desai-Liaqat, Wavell, CMP, 3rd June plan: what each offered ML1906 and how each raised Jinnah's floor.
  • Geopolitics: Cold War, Pakistan's value to the West, post-WWII partitions.

From 1944 every failed settlement raised ML1906's floor, until the 3rd June Plan made partition the only exit.

The story in one go#

Read it like a film first. Then go to the notes: every fact below is expanded there.

Story spine: In 1944 Rajaji offered Jinnah a plebiscite and a weak common centre, and Jinnah took the measure of how far INC would bend. Each later try, the Desai-Liaqat Pact and the Wavell Plan, failed on ML1906's claim to speak for all Muslims, and that claim got stronger after the December 1945 elections. Britain, shaken by QIM, INA agitations and the Naval Mutiny, offered a united India through the Cabinet Mission, but the grouping dispute produced Direct Action Day and a broken Interim Government. By 1947 Atlee fixed a deadline, Mountbatten dropped the idea of unity, INC accepted the 3rd June Plan, and the Indian Independence Act turned partition into law.

  1. Rajaji meets Jinnah halfway. Rajagopalachari proposed that ML1906 back Purna Swaraj and join a coalition at the Centre. After the war a plebiscite in Muslim-majority regions would decide on partition, and a yes meant a confederation with a weak centre. INC wanted a Lucknow-Pact-like deal because the British had said no progress without ML1906's consent.
    → But Jinnah wanted far more than a confederation.
  2. Jinnah says no and learns the floor. Jinnah wanted only Muslims to vote, a fully separate Pakistan (Two Nations, Two States) and all of it before the British left. The talks failed, but Jinnah now knew INC was ready for a Two-Nation, One-State solution. Readiness was INC's compulsion, not its wish.
    → So INC tried again, this time in the Central Assembly.
  3. Desai-Liaqat Pact: parity on paper. Bhulabhai Desai and Liaqat Ali Khan agreed on a VEC of Indians with 50% INC and 50% ML1906, and 20% of legislature seats for minorities. Gandhi knew of it. Jinnah refused to acknowledge any knowledge of it, testing how far INC could bend.
    → Now the British borrowed this very formula.
  4. Wavell Plan at Shimla. After the QIM leaders were released, Wavell4347 held the Shimla Conference in June 1945 and proposed a VEC of Indians with 50% Hindus and 50% Muslims. Churchill wanted visible progress before British elections. ML1906 insisted all Muslim ministers be its own; INC said it was not a Hindu party.
    → But the failure itself handed ML1906 a veto and a prize.
  5. December 1945 elections. Wavell4347 declared failure because ML1906 could not be convinced, an implicit veto at the top. ML1906's prestige soared and it swept the seats reserved for Muslims, winning Bengal and Sindh. INC won elsewhere, including NWFP and Assam, and led a coalition in Punjab.
    → Meanwhile Britain itself was cracking.
  6. Britain exhausted: Cabinet Mission arrives. Atlee became PM in July 1945, Japan surrendered, the INA trials united Indians, and the Naval Mutiny broke out on the day the Cabinet Mission arrived. Britain no longer trusted the army and was drained by two world wars and the Depression. In March 1946 Atlee said a minority would not be allowed a veto over the majority.
    → So the Mission offered a united India, but with a catch.
  7. Cabinet Mission Plan and the grouping fight. A weak centre for Defence, Communications and Foreign Affairs, three sections A, B and C, and a Constituent Assembly of PLA members plus princely nominees. INC said grouping was voluntary; ML1906 said it was compulsory, and the British agreed. That made the plan a Pakistan within India.
    → Then INC's July 1946 stand handed Jinnah his trigger.
  8. Direct Action and a broken government. INC declared NWFP and Assam would not join Groups B and C, and Nehru said only the Constituent Assembly was binding. ML1906 rejected the plan and called Direct Action Day for 16 August 1946; 5,000 died in Calcutta in a week. ML1906 later joined the Interim Government out of fear, took Finance and made it unworkable. The Constituent Assembly met on 9 December 1946 without ML1906.
    → So by early 1947 the British changed course.
  9. Atlee's deadline and Plan Balkan. In February 1947 Atlee fixed 30 June 1948 for the British exit, with or without a constitution, and Paramountcy was not to pass to any successor. Mountbatten replaced Wavell4347 and came up with Plan Balkan in April 1947, which Nehru killed. Mountbatten concluded that CMP1946 and unity were dead horses, entirely due to Jinnah.
    → So only partition was left on the table.
  10. 3rd June Plan and the Radcliffe Line. INC accepted partition and dominion status in return for a small Pakistan, a strong centre and exit by 15 August 1947. Punjab and Bengal PLAs decided on partition; NWFP, Sylhet, Sindh and Baluchistan voted to join Pakistan. Radcliffe drew the line alone and announced it on 17 August, two days after independence.
    → Then the law and the princes had to be settled.
  11. Independence Act and 565 princes. The Indian Independence Act of July 1947 gave legal form to the plan and ended Paramountcy over all 565 states. Patel and V.P. Menon brought 552 into India: 549 by accession and J&K, Junagadh and Hyderabad later. The other 13 went to Pakistan.

In simple words#

  • 1944 to 1947 is one story: every attempt at a joint INC-ML1906 settlement failed, and every failure made ML1906 stronger.
  • The Rajaji Formula offered a plebiscite and a confederation; Jinnah wanted Two Nations, Two States and learned how far INC would bend.
  • Desai-Liaqat and the Wavell Plan both used 50:50 parity; Jinnah denied the first and the second collapsed over ML1906's claim to be the only Muslim voice.
  • The Cabinet Mission was the only plan offering a united India and Purna Swaraj; it broke on whether grouping was compulsory.
  • Direct Action Day (16 Aug 1946) and ML1906's use of Finance in the Interim Government showed unity was gone.
  • Atlee's deadline and Plan Balkan pushed toward fragments; the 3rd June Plan gave INC a small Pakistan, a strong centre and a quick exit.
  • 565 princely states: 552 to India, 13 to Pakistan. The Act ended Paramountcy but handed it to nobody.
  • Partition was a long process, not one failure: British divide and rule, elite Muslims, Hindu revivalism, and INC's own concessions.

1 · Proposals#

Story link

Because Britain had said that no constitutional progress would come without ML1906's consent, INC tried to buy that consent. Ask: what did Rajaji offer, and what did INC give up?

1.1 The three planks

  • The formula had three main planks.
  • 1.1 (Swaraj): ML1906 should support the goal of Purna Swaraj.
  • 1.2 (Transfer of Power): INC and ML1906 should be ready to form a coalition government at the Centre.
  • 1.3 (Unity): after the war a plebiscite would be held in Muslim-majority regions on the question of partition. If the vote favoured it, the result would be a confederation: a weak common centre with strong provinces.
  • Link forward to the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946 and Syed Ahmed Khan's Two-Nation, One-State theory. The idea of confederation versus a strong unitary centre is the core fault-line in every negotiation from here to 1947.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: INC aimed for a pact like the Lucknow Pact 1916 (LP1916) because the British had made it clear, in the August Offer 1940 and the Cripps Mission 1942, that there would be no future progress without the consent of ML1906. That gave ML1906 a veto.
  • Teacher flags the plebiscite, the Muslim-majority regions, the question of partition, and Two-Nation One-State as the lines to hold.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board on the confederation: the Central list is Defence, Communications and Foreign Affairs. For the outside world we act as one state: fight, talk, trade. All other subjects sit on the Provincial list, and inside there are two ways of life and two constitutional systems, i.e. the 2N1S solution, a Pakistan within India.
  • The board's one-liner: 'From outside we are one, but when you open the door, you find two: India and Pakistan.'

  • Slide PYQ note (not in handout text): Rajaji evolved the formula in March 1944 with the full approval of Gandhiji; point (i) was that the League would endorse the demand for independence.
Key takeaway
  • Rajaji Formula = Purna Swaraj + coalition at Centre + post-war plebiscite in Muslim-majority regions leading to a confederation (weak centre, strong provinces).

2 · Result: Talks Collapse#

Story link

Because Rajaji had offered something short of Pakistan, Jinnah's reply measured the gap. Ask: how many of Jinnah's three conditions broke the formula?

  • Gandhi and Jinnah met to discuss the formula, but the talks failed. Jinnah's demands were very different from what Rajagopalachari proposed.

3 · Desai-Liaqat Pact, 1945#

Story link

Because the Rajaji route had failed, the leaders of INC and ML1906 in the Central Assembly tried their own pact. Ask: what split did they agree, and why did Jinnah deny it?

  • The pact was negotiated and finalised by Bhulabhai Desai and Liaqat Ali Khan, leaders of INC and ML1906 respectively in the Central Legislative Assembly (CLA).
  • The goal was the same as the Rajaji Formula: build INC-ML unity and present joint demands to the British (like LP1916) for a smoother transfer of power.
  • Proposal 1, VEC: all ministers Indians except the Governor General and the Commander-in-Chief; of these Indian ministers 50% from INC and 50% from ML1906.
  • Proposal 2, Legislature: 20% of Central Legislature seats reserved for minorities, especially Dalits and Sikhs; the remaining seats split equally between INC and ML1906. Seats filled first by nomination, with direct or indirect elections to follow later.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: VEC is all Indians except the GG and C-in-C; the rest of the members are 50% INC and 50% ML1906. In the legislature, 20% goes to other minorities such as Sikhs and SCs, and the rest seats are 50% INC and 50% ML1906.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: the 14 Points demanded 33% at the Centre; now 50% is rejected by Jinnah. Reasoning: even parity, far beyond one-third, did not satisfy him because the aim had moved on.
  • Why it failed: Gandhi was aware of the pact. It failed because Jinnah refused to acknowledge any knowledge of it. He was testing how far INC could bend. Now he had a minimum base, and any future negotiation would need to offer ML1906 something even more.
  • Notice: each concession was not building goodwill, it was raising Jinnah's floor.

4 · Wavell Plan, 1945#

Story link

Because INC and ML1906 had already agreed on parity in principle, the British borrowed it before their own elections. Ask: where exactly did a nearly agreed plan snap?

Why it was tabled

  • The Wavell Plan was discussed at the Shimla Conference in June 1945, held after the release of the INC leaders imprisoned during the Quit India Movement (1942-45). The release also formally ended the QIM.
  • Timing came from British domestic politics: Winston Churchill wanted visible progress on the India problem before elections in Britain.
  • The plan was based closely on the Desai-Liaqat Pact, because INC and ML1906 had already agreed on it in principle, making rapid acceptance more likely. Also any change to the VEC could be made swiftly at the behest of the Governor General.

4.1 Proposals of Wavell4347

  • i) In the VEC all ministers Indians except the Governor General and the C-in-C; 50% Hindus and 50% Muslims among Indian ministers.
  • ii) The Governor General/Viceroy rules on the aid and advice of ministers but retains veto power.
  • iii) The portfolio of Foreign Affairs to be offered to Indians (recall: in the Cripps Mission 1942 foreign policy was already offered).
  • iv) Wavell4347 wanted a joint list of names for ministership from the parties; if impossible, each party would send a separate list.
Teacher's board
  • Teacher's board: in the VEC all are Indians except GG and C-in-C, and the rest members are 50% Hindu and 50% Muslim.
  • Why failed? (1) Jinnah: all Muslims to be only from ML1906, because ML is the only true representative of Muslims. (2) INC: we are not a Hindu party, but an Indian party.
  • The decision rested with Wavell4347. He declared the plan failed because Jinnah/ML1906 could not be convinced. This equals an implicit veto at the top.

4.2 Why it failed

  • The plan collapsed on a single point: ML1906 insisted that Muslim ministers in the VEC should come only from ML1906, not from INC, which had its own Muslim leaders. ML1906 rejected the plan when Wavell4347 refused to guarantee this.
  • INC refused the demand, arguing it was not a Hindu organisation and that INC's Muslim leaders also had the right to be ministers.
  • Wavell4347 announced failure, saying ML1906 could not be convinced. In effect that gave ML1906 an implicit veto on the transfer of power.
  • The announcement boosted ML1906's prestige enormously, and it did very well in the December 1945 elections in seats reserved for Muslims.
Handout alert
  • Slide vs handout: the handout says 50% Hindus and 50% Muslims for Wavell4347, and 50% INC and 50% ML1906 for Desai-Liaqat. Keep the wording separate: Hindu/Muslim for the Wavell Plan, INC/ML1906 for the pact.
  • Handout PYQ alert lists Prelims 1996x2, 1998x2, 2000 and 2007 for the Wavell Plan. The stem on the slides is Prelims 2007: A true (equal Hindu and Muslim members), R false (Churchill's election timing, not a partition-avoiding design).
  • Slide PYQ note: Wavell Plan was proposed at the Simla conference 1945; Indians would draft the Constitution.
Key takeaway
  • Wavell Plan: Shimla June 1945, VEC with 50% Hindus and 50% Muslims, GG retains veto, Foreign Affairs to Indians. It broke on ML1906's monopoly demand, which gave ML1906 an implicit veto.

5 · Cabinet Mission Plan (CMP1946)#

Story link

Because the QIM, the INA agitations and the Naval Mutiny shook British confidence, Britain finally offered a united India. Ask: what in the plan allowed Jinnah to read a Pakistan into it?

5.1 Background

  • July 1945: Clement Atlee became British PM after Churchill lost the elections. 15 August 1945: Japan surrendered.
  • INA trials: the Red Fort trial of Prem Sehgal, Shah Nawaz Khan and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon drew massive attention and produced remarkable communal unity in the INA agitations of 1945-46.

8 · 3rd June Plan / Mountbatten Plan (1947)#

Story link

Because Plan Balkan was dead and Jinnah had killed unity, Mountbatten shifted to a partition INC could live with. Ask: what did INC get for accepting partition?

8.1 Context

  • MTB's brief: a deadline of October 1947 to explore options for unity and partition. Within two months he concluded that CMP1946 and the idea of a united India were dead horses, entirely due to Jinnah.
  • British national interest: better two countries friendly to Britain than risk an unfriendly Pakistan from direct action or civil war. Atlee later said: 'Britain wanted a united India, tried but failed.'
  • Three choices: 1st, India as a colony; 2nd, a friendly united India; 3rd, now the only realistic one, a friendly India and a friendly Pakistan.
  • The harder point: partition was avoidable if the British had acted against communal violence rather than playing the passive mediator between INC and ML1906. They chose passivity because it served British national interest.
  • Dominion status to both India and Pakistan kept both in the British Commonwealth, even temporarily, a network of colonies with a special economic relationship, giving trade and business benefits to British firms. It was a political victory for the British government among its own people.

8.2 INC's demands accepted

  • Since INC agreed to partition and dominion status, its other demands were accepted:
    • i) Pakistan as small as possible, with an informal promise that the 565 princely states would not be given independence.
    • ii) A strong, unitary centre for India, like the Nehru Report 1928 and unlike the Rajaji Formula 1944.
    • iii) British exit in 72 days from 3rd June, i.e. 15 August 1947, a year ahead of the Atlee deadline.

8.3 The mechanics of partition

  • In Punjab and Bengal a simple majority of either the Hindu or the Muslim members of the PLAs in favour would lead to partition. So the decision rested with the two big Muslim-majority provinces.
  • Which areas join Pakistan: i) plebiscite in Sindh; ii) referendum in NWFP and the Sylhet district of Assam; iii) voting by political leaders in Baluchistan.
  • INC had won the December 1945 elections in NWFP and Assam, so a referendum was chosen there for a clear, direct answer. A plebiscite seeks only a non-binding opinion; a referendum is binding.
  • If the vote favoured partition, the CA set up by the Cabinet Mission would split into two CAs for two dominions, and power would pass to two centres by 15 August 1947, the appointed day. This is why India became an 'indestructible union of destructible states': power passed only to the Union at the Centre, and provinces did not separately decide to form a union as in the USA.
  • INC and ML1906 both accepted the Mountbatten Plan. Votes favoured partition in Punjab and Bengal; NWFP, Sindh, Baluchistan and Sylhet voted to join Pakistan.
  • Frontier Gandhi, Khan Abdul Gaffar Khan, boycotted the referendum and passed the Bannu Resolution (June 1947) demanding an independent Pakhtonistan for Pashtuns. NWFP's vote therefore went to Pakistan as his followers did not vote.
  • Pakistan banned the Khudai Khidmatgars in 1948. 'Pakhto' means the Pakhtun code of life. Some Pashtuns wanted to join Afghanistan. NWFP is today Khyber Pakhtunwa.

8.4 Boundary Commission

  • Two boundary commissions were set up for the west (Punjab) and the east (Bengal), both headed by Sir Cyril Radcliffe, a British jurist who had never been to India.
  • Each had four judges, two nominated by INC and two by ML1906, who were deadlocked on partisan lines. The lines were therefore Radcliffe's unilateral decision.
  • The Radcliffe Line was announced on 17 August 1947, two days after independence, ensuring maximum chaos in the transfer of power.

10 · Indian Independence Act (July 1947)#

Story link

Because INC and ML1906 had accepted the Mountbatten Plan, Parliament gave it legal form. Ask: what exactly ended on the appointed day, and what carried on?

  • The Act gave legal status to the Mountbatten Plan. Its key provisions:
    • 10.1 Partition of British India into two independent dominions, independent of each other, on the appointed day, 15 August 1947.
    • 10.2 Governor General: each dominion headed by a GG appointed by the King. The same GG served both until their CAs passed a law for a separate GG. Pakistan decided Jinnah would be its GG.
    • 10.3 End of dominion status: the CAs of the two dominions could pass constitutions ending dominion status (India's CA did so on 26 January 1950).
    • 10.4 Jurisdiction and Paramountcy: British Parliament's jurisdiction over British India ended on the appointed day, and Paramountcy over the 565 states ended, not transferred to any government in the two dominions. Legally each princely state had a right to independence.
    • 10.5 Abolition of the title 'Emperor of India' and of the office of Secretary of State for India (created in 1858).
    • 10.6 Until a new constitution, the Government of India Act 1935 would apply.
    • 10.7 Territories of India and Pakistan to be decided 'before or after' the appointed day by a boundary commission appointed by the GG.
  • India was a dominion of Britain from 15 August 1947 until 26 January 1950, when the Constitution came into effect.

11 · Why Partition?#

Story link

Because the 3rd June Plan looked sudden but was not, we must trace the long process. Ask: which of the four causes belongs to the British, and which to Indians?

  • The acceptance of partition in the 3rd June Plan was not sudden; it was the culmination of a long process.
  • Historian Bipin Chandra: INC failed on two counts: it failed to bring Muslims into the INM as a mass constituency, and it failed to curb Muslim communal forces, especially from 1937.
  • The proof: ML1906 won 90% of Muslim seats in the 1945 PLA elections.
  • By June 1947 INC had given up on a united India, defeated by communal violence, the failure of the Interim Government and ML1906's refusal to join the CA (August 1946). Even the Bengal government of ML1906 promoted communal riots. The 3rd June Plan was a compulsion, not a choice.

11.1 British Divide and Rule (DnR)

  • A sustained structural effort across decades:
    • i) Hunter's Indian Musalmans (1871) argued that British neglect of Muslims had contributed to the Faraisi movement (1830-70s), planting the idea that Muslims were a separate, neglected political category.
    • ii) Census 1881 gave a sense of homogeneous religious identity to internally heterogeneous communities; religion-based statistics brought religious identity into public policy.
    • iii) 1897 reservation in government jobs for Muslims.
    • iv) Bengal Partition (1905) created a Muslim-majority province and rewarded elite Muslims.
    • v) Positive response of Minto05-10 to the Simla Deputation (1906).
    • vi) Formation of ML1906.
    • vii) Separate electorates for Muslims under ICA 1909.
    • viii) In the August Offer (1940) and the Wavell Plan (1945), ML1906 was given a veto on constitutional progress.

11.2 Role of Western-Educated Middle-Class Muslims (WEMC Muslims)

  • Muslim organisations led by Syed Ahmed Khan gave the Two-Nation Theory, labelled INC a Hindu organisation, demanded reservation and special status for the Muslim Quam, and supported the British in return.
  • The four Delhi Proposals (December 1927) and three Calcutta Amendments (December 1928) failed. Jinnah then issued the 14 Points (March 1929), the blueprint for all future ML1906 politics.
  • ML1906 also opposed the CDM (1930-34) and QIM (1942-45), reducing Muslim participation in the INM.

11.3 Hindu Revivalism (1880s-1900s)

  • i) The Shuddhi and cow protection movements of the Arya Samaj turned aggressive after Swami Dayanand Saraswati died in 1883.
  • ii) Cow-killing riots (1893).
  • iii) The Hindi-Urdu controversy (1880s-1900) in the North-West Provinces and Awadh (set up in 1877): Hindi identified with Hindus, Urdu with Muslims, a cultural fault-line.
  • INC did not condemn the 1893 riots, because of its 1886 rule not to take up social issues. Muslim participation in INC declined and INC did little to reclaim Muslim leadership.
  • The Swadeshi Movement (1905-11) also hurt Muslim participation: heavy Hindu religious symbolism, Muslims and the British shown as partners in Jatras, and a social boycott that hurt poorer communities. ML1906 and the Ulamas discouraged participation.

11.4 Role of INC

  • INC's own actions compounded the problem:
    • i) Cripps Mission (1942): INC accepted provincial autonomy for Muslim-majority provinces (like Art. 370).
    • ii) Rajaji Formula (1944): in the MG-Jinnah talks INC agreed to a plebiscite for 'partition without independence' (like Art. 370 for MMPs), partly conceding self-determination.
    • iii) CMP1946: INC opposed only compulsory grouping, not grouping itself (CMP was in essence Two-Nation, One-State).
    • iv) March 1947: INC first explicitly mentioned partition, saying Punjab and Bengal would have to be partitioned if the country was divided, months before the 3rd June Plan.
  • Two false beliefs proved costly:
    • v) That partition would be temporary. Gandhi told INC 'don't accept partition in your hearts', but hearts do not make borders.
    • vi) That appeasement would reduce the fear-of-majority complex. Instead each concession raised ML1906's prestige: Lucknow Pact (1916), Rajaji Formula (1944), Desai-Liaqat Pact (1945) each raised its floor.

Gandhi, alone

  • Gandhi could not start a movement against partition because communalism had spread too far. He said Hindus and Sikhs also desired partition, which made Muslims hate him, and it made Hindus and Sikhs abandon him. By mid-1947 he was alone.
  • Mountbatten called him a 'one-man boundary force' against communal violence.
  • Verdict: acceptance of partition was the culmination of a long process, not a single act of failure.

Read the question, answer in your head, then tap to check.

What are the three planks of the Rajaji Formula?ML1906 supports Purna Swaraj; INC and ML1906 form a coalition at the Centre; after the war a plebiscite in Muslim-majority regions, a yes leading to a confederation.
What is a confederation in the Rajaji Formula?A weak common centre with strong provinces.
Why did INC try for a Lucknow-Pact-like pact?The British made clear in the August Offer 1940 and Cripps 1942 that there would be no progress without ML1906's consent.
What were Jinnah's three demands against the Rajaji Formula?Separate electorates (only Muslims vote); Two Nations, Two States; implementation before the British left.
What did Jinnah learn from the 1944 talks?How far INC would bend: it was ready for a 2N1S solution.
Who negotiated the Desai-Liaqat Pact?Bhulabhai Desai (INC) and Liaqat Ali Khan (ML1906) in the Central Legislative Assembly.
What did the Desai-Liaqat Pact propose?VEC all Indians except GG and C-in-C with 50% INC and 50% ML1906; 20% of legislature seats for minorities, the rest split equally.
Why did the Desai-Liaqat Pact fail?Jinnah refused to acknowledge any knowledge of it, even though Gandhi knew.
Why was the Wavell Plan built on Desai-Liaqat?Both parties had agreed in principle, and Churchill wanted progress before British elections.
What were the Wavell Plan proposals?All Indians in VEC except GG and C-in-C; 50% Hindus, 50% Muslims; GG retains veto; Foreign Affairs to Indians; a joint list of names.
On what single point did the Wavell Plan collapse?ML1906's demand that all Muslim ministers be from ML1906 alone; INC refused.
What was the effect of Wavell4347's announcement of failure?An implicit veto for ML1906 and a boost in its prestige before the December 1945 elections.
What happened in the December 1945 PLA elections?INC won everywhere except Bengal, Punjab and Sindh; ML1906 swept Muslim seats and got Bengal and Sindh; INC led a coalition in Punjab and won NWFP and Assam.
Who were the members of the Cabinet Mission?Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander.
Why did Britain offer a united India in 1946?QIM, INA agitations and the Naval Mutiny, loss of trust in the army, an exhausted economy, and the Cold War interest in a friendly India.
What were the three union subjects under CMP1946?Defence, Communications and Foreign Affairs.
What were the three sections?A: Hindu-majority provinces; B: NWFP, Punjab, Sindh; C: Bengal and Assam (placed there because of Sylhet).
How many constitutions minimum and maximum under CMP?Minimum 4 (3 group + 1 union); maximum 569 (565 states + 3 + 1).
What was the fault-line in the CMP debate?INC said grouping was voluntary; ML1906 said compulsory, and the British agreed.
When was Direct Action Day and what happened?16 August 1946; communal riots in Calcutta killed 5,000 in a week.
Why did ML1906 join the Interim Government and what did it take?Out of fear of INC holding power alone; it took Finance and used it to obstruct.
Who was provisional and who permanent President of the CA?Sachchidananda Sinha (9 Dec 1946); Rajendra Prasad (11 Dec 1946).
What did the Atlee Statement of February 1947 announce?British exit by 30 June 1948, with or without a constitution; Paramountcy lapsing for all 565 states; Mountbatten to replace Wavell4347.
What was Plan Balkan?Mountbatten's April 1947 voluntary-union plan giving power to 11 provinces separately; Nehru killed it as balkanization.
What did INC get in return for accepting the 3rd June Plan?A small Pakistan, an informal promise on the princely states, a strong centre, exit in 72 days by 15 August 1947.
How was partition decided in Punjab and Bengal?A simple majority of either the Hindu or the Muslim members of the PLAs in favour of partition.
Why a referendum in NWFP and Sylhet?INC had won there in December 1945; a referendum is binding, a plebiscite is only an opinion.
Who drew the Radcliffe Line and when was it announced?Sir Cyril Radcliffe, 17 August 1947, two days after independence.
How do the 565 princely states split?552 to India (549 by accession, plus J&K, Junagadh, Hyderabad) and 13 to Pakistan.
What did the Indian Independence Act do to Paramountcy?Ended it over all 565 states without transferring it, so each was legally free to be independent.
What are the four causes of partition in the handout?British divide and rule; WEMC Muslims; Hindu revivalism; INC's role.
