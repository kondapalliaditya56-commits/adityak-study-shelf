Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — The correct pairing is A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1: the Indian Association held the first National Conference at Calcutta in 1883; the Muslim League was founded at Dacca in 1906; the All India State People's Conference was formed in 1927; and the Communal Award was announced from Whitehall in August 1932. (All four events are.)

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — All four pairings fail: it gives 1883 to the Muslim League (founded 1906) and 1906 to the National Conference (held 1883), and also swaps the Communal Award (1932, not 1927) with the State People's Conference (1927, not 1932).

(D) ✗ — C-2 and D-1 are right, but the first two are swapped: the 1883 event is the National Conference at Calcutta, not the founding of the Muslim League — the League came at Dacca only in 1906. 1999PYQ 2(Prelims 1999)

Which one of the following Indian leaders was dismissed by the British from the Indian Civil Service?

Answer: (B)

(B) ✓ — Surendranath Banerji is the one officer among the four whom the British REMOVED: he entered the ICS in 1869 and was dismissed in 1874 over a minor procedural lapse — an injustice that became political fuel; he went on to found the Indian Association (1876).

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Satyendranath Tagore was the FIRST Indian to clear the civil services examination (1864) and served out his career — he was never dismissed.

(C) ✗ — R.C. Dutt was "a former ICS officer turned economic historian" — he retired from the service; he was not dismissed.

(D) ✗ — S.C. Bose left the ICS by resignation (1921) to join the national movement — a voluntary exit on principle, the opposite of a dismissal. (Note: In 1869 Surendranath Banerjee became the second Indian to succeed in the Indian Civil Service competitive examination. Later in 1874 he was dismissed for a minor and apparently inadvertent procedural lapse.)

2003PYQ 4(Prelims 2003)

With reference to colonial rule India, what was sought by the Ilbert Bill in 1883?

Answer: (A)

(A) ✓ — The British were not going to treat Indians as equals — not in courts, not in civil services, not in political life.

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — Restrictions on the native press describe the Vernacular Press Act 1878 under Lytton76-80 — not the Ilbert Bill, which dealt with the criminal jurisdiction of Indian judges.

(C) ✗ — Holding the ICS examination in India was a separate, rejected demand: Lytton cut the age limit to 19 in 1876 and the exam stayed London-only; a simultaneous India exam came only under GOI Act 1919.. The Ilbert Bill said nothing about civil service examinations.

(D) ✗ — Arms licensing was the Arms Act 1878 (Indians required a licence; Europeans and Eurasians exempt). The Ilbert Bill proposed no change to arms law; it was a judicial-jurisdiction measure. (Note: The main aim of Ilbert Bill introduced in 1883, during the viceroyalty of Lord Ripon, was to bring Indians and Europeans on par as far as the criminal jurisdiction of courts was concerned.)

The first Factory Act was passed in 1881. This Act prohibited the employment of children below 7 years of age and the employment of children under 12 years of age for more than 9 hours. Other chief events of his period were: end of Second Anglo-Afghan War (1878–80).

Wrong options:

(B) ✗ — Bentinck28-35's tenure ended in 1835, nearly half a century before the 1881 Act; no factory legislation existed in his era.

(D) ✗ — Canning56-62 governed during the Revolt and the transfer to the Crown (1856–62) — two decades before the first Factories Act of 1881, which the record places squarely in Ripon80-84's tenure.

2013PYQ 6(Prelims 2013)

The Ilbert Bill controversy was related to the

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — The bill proposed to extend the jurisdiction of Indian district judges to try cases involving Britons in the mofussil (rural) areas. This would end an existing privilege by which a Briton in rural India could not be tried before an Indian judge — i.e., removal of the disqualification on Indian magistrates trying Europeans.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Restrictions on carrying arms came from the Arms Act 1878 under Lytton76-80 — a different controversy, five years before the Ilbert Bill.

(B) ✗ — Restrictions on Indian-language newspapers came from the Vernacular Press Act 1878 — not from the Ilbert Bill, which was a judicial measure.

(D) ✗ — The Ilbert Bill contained nothing about customs duties on cotton; it dealt solely with which judges could try Britons. (Note: The Ilbert Bill was introduced in 1883, by Viceroy Ripon to amend the existing laws to allow the Indian judges to try British offenders in criminal cases at the district level. It was named after Sir Courtenay Ilbert.)
