FYQ 1

Which of the following were grievances of peasants in Awadh that fuelled the Kisan Sabha Movement around 1918–20? 1. Illegal abwabs and nazrana 2. Bedakhli i.e. eviction at the will of landlords 3. Forced labour (hari and begar) 4. Permanent Settlement of Bengal

Answer: (A)

(A)

(a) ✓ — Awadh peasants' specific grievances were illegal abwabs and nazrana (extra cesses beyond legal rent), bedakhli (eviction at the landlord's will), and forced labour i.e. hari and begar. Permanent Settlement applied to Bengal, not Awadh, and finds no place in the Awadh peasants' charter — see.

Why others wrong: (b) wrongly includes Permanent Settlement and omits illegal abwabs/nazrana; (c) wrongly includes Permanent Settlement and omits bedakhli; (d) wrongly includes Permanent Settlement, which was a Bengal-specific land-revenue arrangement, not an Awadh grievance.

Memory hook: Awadh's three A-B-H pains — Abwabs, Bedakhli, Hari-begar. Permanent Settlement stayed back in Bengal.

FYQ 2

With reference to the UP Kisan Sabha (UPKS) set up in February 1918, who among the following was NOT among its co-founders?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — UPKS February 1918 was co-founded by Gauri Shankar Mishra, Indra Narayan Dwivedi and Madan Mohan Malaviya. Jawaharlal Nehru was NOT associated with UPKS 1918; he engaged with the Kisan Sabha movement only from June 1920 onwards, when he visited Pratapgarh with Baba Ramchandra — see.

Why others wrong: (a) Mishra was an operational organiser and co-founder; (c) Dwivedi was the other operational organiser and co-founder; (d) Malaviya was the senior political patron lending his weight as co-founder.

Memory hook: UPKS 1918 = Mishra-Dwivedi-Malaviya trio. Nehru joined only in 1920 — two years late to the party.

FYQ 3

Which one of the following events is identified as the immediate trigger that ignited the Akali Movement (1920–25) for wresting control of Gurudwaras from the Mahants?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(c) ✓ — The trigger for the Akali Movement was the Mahants honouring General Dyer after the Jallianwala Bagh massacre of 1919. When the Mahants felicitated the very man who had ordered the massacre of Sikhs at Amritsar, ordinary devotees decided they could no longer tolerate Mahant control over their religious institutions — see.

Why others wrong: (a) the 1849 annexation only worsened the British–Mahant collusion but was not the immediate trigger; (b) the Singh Sabha Movement of the 1880s laid the long-term reformist foundation, not the immediate trigger; (d) the SGPC's formation in 1920 was a consequence of the movement having already begun, not its trigger.

Memory hook: Mahants garlanded Dyer — Sikhs replied with the Akali Movement.

FYQ 5

Answer: (A)

(A)

(a) ✓ — After the truce reached at Bardoli, an independent tribunal was appointed and it revised the land revenue increase down to 6.03% — a far cry from the original 30%. This is offered as a classic example of Gandhian arbitration revealing economic truth through a middle path based on facts — see.

Why others wrong: (b) 12.5% finds no place's outcome of Bardoli; (c) 15% finds no place's outcome of Bardoli; (d) the 30% increase was NOT retained — the very point of the truce was to bring it down sharply to 6.03%.

Memory hook: 30% balloon → 6.03% pin-prick. Bardoli tribunal cut land revenue to one-fifth.

FYQ 6

Apart from zamindars, peasants resented the additional tyranny of thikedars (rent collectors). 3. It strictly adhered to the Gandhian principle of non-violence. Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(b) ✓ — Statement 1 is correct: Madari Pasi, a leader from the lower castes, emerged at the head of the Eka Movement. Memory hook: Eka = Madari Pasi + thikedars rage. Non-violence? Not signed up for it.

The revolt later took a communal colour only after INC leaders were arrested and organised political guidance collapsed — see read with.

(d) Christian missionary estate-holders find no place in the Mapilla grievance described by the record.

Memory hook: Mapilla tenants Muslim, Jenmies Hindu — agrarian fight at the start, communal turn only after leaders were jailed.

FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words

"The peasant movements of the 1920s were nationalist in their political vocabulary but agrarian in their motivating grievances." Critically examine.

Demand of the question: "Critically examine" + dual claim demands a balanced analysis. The body must (a) accept the dual character — political vocabulary vs material grievance — & (b) trace how each pulled the movements in different directions.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Each peasant movement of the 1920s adopted INM political language (Swaraj, Satyagraha) but was triggered by specific agrarian distress — high revenue, eviction, indebtedness — which is what actually brought peasants to the streets. Body guidance — 4 themes:

1. Political vocabulary, agrarian core (see 'How did Peasant Movements connect with INM?' and 'Kisan Sabha Movement (1918) and Awadh Kisan Sabha (1920)' and 'Eka Movement (1921) — Awadh' and 'Bardoli Satyagraha (1928) — Surat, Gujarat'). Bardoli was called a "Satyagraha"; Eka Movement adopted oath-bound discipline like NCM; Awadh Kisan Sabha borrowed nationalist vocabulary via Nehru. But the trigger in each case was a specific revenue or rent grievance — not a constitutional question.

2. The INC's tactical relationship (see 'How did Peasant Movements connect with INM?' and 'A

Note on Peasant Movements and INC'). INC absorbed peasant energy when convenient (Bardoli, Champaran earlier) but withdrew from peasant struggles when they threatened the multi-class coalition — especially when peasants attacked landlords (Moplah 1921) or threatened zamindari interests (Awadh radicalism 1921-22).

3. The class-coalition problem (see 'A Note on Peasant Movements and INC'). Peasant movements forced the INC to choose between deepening rural mobilisation (which alienated zamindars in the INC's coalition) & containing it (which limited mass-base growth). The 1920s show INC choosing containment — Gandhi suspended NCM partly fearing peasant violence.

4. The deep legacy (see 'A Note on Peasant Movements and INC'). Despite the INC's caution, the peasant movements built infrastructure — Kisan Sabhas in UP, Bihar, Bengal — that would re-emerge in the 1930s All-India Kisan Sabha (1936) & beyond. Tebhaga (1946) & Telangana (1946-51) grew on this base.

Closing verdict: The 1920s peasant movements were not subordinated to the INM nor wholly autonomous from it. They were a partial overlap zone — where nationalist political language met agrarian material struggle. The unresolved tension was the INC's central post-1947 problem.

Revise: 'Peasant Movements + INM' + 'Moplah class-coalition issue' + 'Bardoli' + 'Synthesis'.

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words What made the Bardoli Satyagraha (1928) the most successful peasant movement of the 1920s? Examine its method & outcome.

Demand of the question: "What made it the most successful" + "examine method & outcome" — body must identify the structural reasons for Bardoli's success that other peasant movements lacked.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Bardoli succeeded where Mapilla, Eka & Awadh Kisan Sabha had failed — it married disciplined Gandhian Satyagraha to a focused, technical revenue grievance under uncompromised leadership. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Bardoli Satyagraha (1928) — Surat, Gujarat'):

1. The focused grievance. A 22% revenue increase in a single taluka was a specific, technical issue — not a generalised anti-feudal demand. Easy to document; easy to defend; easy to negotiate. By contrast, Moplah carried communal & landlord-tenant complications; Eka faced fragmented opposition.

2. The disciplined leadership. Sardar Patel had organisational experience from Kheda 1918. Bardoli's tax-refusal was prepared with meticulous record-keeping — every peasant's compliance with non-payment was tracked. The British faced a unified front, not a fragmented agitation.

3. The outcome — the technical win. The Maxwell-Broomfield Inquiry Committee reduced the increase from 30% to 6.03% — a clear quantitative victory that legitimised both Patel's leadership & the Satyagraha method. The women of Bardoli conferred the title "Sardar" on Patel. K.M. Munshi & Lalji Nayak resigned from the Bombay Legislative Council in solidarity.

Closing verdict: Bardoli succeeded because the grievance was technically clean, the leadership was disciplined, the demand was specific, & the negotiation channel stayed open. The pattern became a template — same model used in the 1930-32 Civil Disobedience peasant mobilisations.

Revise: 'Bardoli Satyagraha'.
