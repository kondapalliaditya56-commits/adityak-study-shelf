In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Local self-government means villages and towns run their own affairs through elected bodies. It was only a Directive Principle (Article 40) and a State List subject (entry 5) until 1992.
  • Several committees (Balwant Rai Mehta 1957 first) argued for democratic decentralisation
  • Rajasthan started Panchayati Raj on 2 Oct 1959 at Nagaur, then Andhra Pradesh
  • The 73rd Amendment (1992) made village bodies constitutional: Part IX (Arts 243 to 243O) and the Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects). It gives a three-tier system, 5-year terms, one-third women seats, a State Election Commission and a State Finance Commission.
  • The 74th Amendment (1992) did the same for towns: Part IX-A (Arts 243P to 243ZG) and the Twelfth Schedule (18 subjects), with Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council and Municipal Corporation.
  • Problems remain: weak devolution (3Fs and the 4th F, Functionality), tied funds, heavy dependence on grants, inactive Gram Sabhas. The 15th Finance Commission gave a record Rs 4.36 lakh crore and linked grants to reforms.

Key terms

Gram Sabha
All registered voters of a village; the base of the democratic pyramid (Art 243A)
Intermediate level
The level between village and district, notified by the Governor; states with population up to 20 lakh may skip it
Eleventh Schedule
List of 29 subjects that Panchayats can handle (Art 243G)
Twelfth Schedule
List of 18 functional items for municipalities (Art 243W)
State Finance Commission (SFC)
Set up by the Governor every 5 years to review finances of Panchayats and municipalities
State Election Commission (SEC)
Controls elections to Panchayats and Municipalities; head appointed by the Governor, removed like a High Court judge
PESA Act, 1996
Extends Panchayat self-rule to Scheduled Areas where Part IX does not apply
District Planning Committee
Joins plans of panchayats and municipalities into one district plan; four-fifths of members elected (Art 243ZD)

1 · Local government and the original Constitution#

  • Panchayati Raj is India's framework of rural local self-government: elected bodies at village (Gram Panchayat), intermediate/block (Panchayat Samiti) and district (Zila Parishad) levels plan, implement and monitor rural development. It brings decisions closer to people and keeps governance accountable and inclusive.
  • Gandhi's idea: Gram Swaraj: self-reliant, self-governing villages as the backbone of democracy. Gandhi believed "India lives in her villages", so empowerment must begin at the village.

What local government means

  • Administration of a small locality by a body representing local inhabitants.
  • A fairly large level of autonomy.
  • Raises at least part of its revenue locally.
  • Spends it on local services: basic amenities, sewage, sanitation, water supply, street lighting, roads, bridges, upkeep of the area.

The original Constitution

Only two provisions at the start

ProvisionContent
Article 40 (DPSP, Part IV)State shall organise village panchayats and give them the powers and authority needed to function as units of self-government
State List, entry 5Local self-government is a State subject
  • The original Constitution gave local bodies constitutional importance but not constitutional status
  • Status came in 1992. Art. 40 was only directive, so implementation was left to each State
  • Shortcut: Importance versus status. Importance = Art. 40 + State List entry 5 (1950). Status = Part IX through the 73rd Amendment (1992).

Also in the handout

  • In 1992, the government of India came up with the 73 rd and the 7 4th amendment act which gave constitutional status to the urban and rural local bodies.

2 · Evolution of Panchayati Raj#

  • PRIs are set up by Acts of State legislatures
  • The 73rd Amendment Act, 1992 constitutionalised them

From Mehta to the first panchayats

  • Community Development Programme (1952) and National Extension Service (1953): Needed a check on how well they worked
  • Balwant Rai G. Mehta Committee (1957):
    • Appointed by the Government of India
    • Proposed "democratic decentralization"
  • NDC (January 1958): Accepted the recommendations but let States adopt their own models, provided core principles stayed uniform
  • 2 October 1959:
    • Rajasthan first, in Nagaur district
    • Andhra Pradesh followed the same year

4 · The road to the 73rd Amendment#

  • Before 1992, PRIs were weak and irregular because they depended on State discretion and had no constitutional backing.

Three governments, three attempts

  • Rajiv Gandhi Government (1989):
    • 64th Amendment Bill: three tiers, regular elections, reservation for SCs, STs and women, State Finance and Election Commissions, protection from arbitrary dissolution. Lok Sabha passed it in August 1989
    • Rajya Sabha rejected it (critics feared centralisation)
  • V.P. Singh Government (1990):
    • June 1990 two-day conference of Chief Ministers
    • Fresh Bill in the Lok Sabha in September 1990
    • Government fell so the Bill lapsed. Built consensus and cooperative federalism
  • P.V. Narasimha Rao Government (1991-92):
    • Redrafted, removed centralising clauses
    • Bill in the Lok Sabha September 1991
    • Passed December 1992
  • 73rd Amendment Act, 1992:
    • Presidential assent 20 April 1993
    • In force 24 April 1993
    • Added Part IX (Arts. 243-243O) and the Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects)

Failed versus successful

  • 64th Bill (1989):
    • Rajiv Gandhi
    • Passed Lok Sabha
    • Defeated in Rajya Sabha
    • Seen as over-centralising
  • 73rd Act (1992):
    • Narasimha Rao
    • Consultation and redrafting
    • Stressed State autonomy
    • Became law
  • Memory aid (Leaders): Rajiv (64th, 1989), V.P. Singh (1990 Bill, lapsed), Narasimha Rao (73rd, 1992): "Road Via New-delhi".

Also in the handout

  • However, the discussions and consensus built during this period laid the groundwork for the eventual success of the next attempt.
  • The government redrafted the earlier proposals, removing contentious clauses that were perceived as centralizing.

5 · 73rd Amendment: Part IX, Articles 243 to 243E#

  • The Act made PRIs part of the justiciable Constitution. It operationalised Art. 40, obliged States to set up a uniform three-tier system, hold regular elections and devolve powers, and marks India's shift from representative to participatory democracy.

Gram Sabha in more detail

  • Direct-democracy platform of rural governance.
  • Powers:
    • Approves the Gram Panchayat's annual plans and budgets
    • Reviews development and welfare schemes
    • Conducts social audits (e.g., MGNREGA Sec. 17)
    • Identifies beneficiaries for housing, pensions, subsidies
  • Backing: State laws (e.g., MP Gram Swaraj Adhiniyam, Kerala People's Plan Campaign) and flagship programmes like MGNREGA and the PESA Act 1996.
  • Eg: Kerala and Rajasthan empower Gram Sabhas to approve annual plans and social audits.
  • Memory aid (Gram Sabha powers): Approves plans and budgets, Reviews schemes, Social audits, Identifies beneficiaries: "All Residents See Improvements".
  • PESA Act 1996:
    • Extends Gram Sabha powers to Scheduled Areas (Fifth Schedule)
    • Lets it approve projects, manage minor water bodies, and safeguard tribal culture and identity
  • Examples in the handout: Himachal Pradesh and Sikkim: functional three-tier. Goa, being smaller, runs a two-tier system. Bihar and Rajasthan have expanded women's reservation to 50%.

7 · Compulsory and voluntary provisions#

Mandatory versus voluntary (73rd Amendment)

  • Mandatory (binding on all States):

  • PESA Act 1996 (President's assent 24 December 1996):
    • Ten states have Fifth Schedule areas. Gram Sabha approves plans and identifies beneficiaries
    • STs get not less than one-half of seats and all chairperson posts
    • The state may nominate unrepresented STs up to one-tenth of members
    • Gram Sabha owns minor forest produce and is consulted before land acquisition
  • Panchayat finances (2nd ARC):
    • Sources are Central Finance Commission grants (Art 280), State Finance Commission devolution (Art 243-I), state loans and grants, Centrally Sponsored Schemes and own revenue. Only Gram Panchayats have a real tax domain
    • The intermediate and district tiers rely on tolls, fees and non-tax revenue
  • Urban history:
    • Charter for the Madras Corporation issued 30 December 1687, corporation began 29 September 1688. Lord Ripon is called the father of local self-government in India
    • Hobhouse chaired the 1907 Royal Commission. The 65th Bill was introduced in August 1989 and defeated in the Rajya Sabha in October 1989
  • Area type under the 74th Act depends on population, density, revenue, share of non-agricultural jobs and economic importance. An industrial township run by a company need not have a municipality. Wards committees are for municipalities with population of three lakh or more.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Who is directly elected? All Panchayat members at village, intermediate and district level are directly elected. Chairpersons at intermediate and district levels are elected indirectly by and from the elected members. The village chairperson is elected as the State law says. Rule: members = direct everywhere; chairperson = indirect for the top two tiers, State law for the village tier.
Trap 2 · 73rd and 74th numbers mixed up 73rd gave Part IX (243 to 243O), Eleventh Schedule with 29 items, in force 24 April 1993. 74th gave Part IX-A (243P to 243ZG), Twelfth Schedule with 18 items, in force 1 June 1993. The failed bills were also different: the 64th Bill (1989) was for Panchayats and the 65th Bill (Nagarpalika Bill, 1989) was for municipalities. Rule: 73 goes with the older rural body and the bigger list (29); 74 goes with towns and 18.
Trap 3 · Committee mix-ups Balwant Rai Mehta proposed three tiers; Ashok Mehta proposed two tiers (Zila Parishad and Mandal Panchayat of 15,000 to 20,000 people) but was not implemented because the Janata Government fell; L.M. Singhvi wanted a new Part and constitutional recognition; Thungon's report was the base of the 64th Bill. Rule: tie each committee to one idea: Mehta = three tiers, Ashok Mehta = two tiers, Singhvi = new Part.
Trap 4 · Mayor and committees: which is ornamental? In a Municipal Corporation the Mayor is largely ceremonial and the Municipal Commissioner is the chief executive. In a Municipality the Chairperson holds real executive powers. A Notified Area Committee is entirely nominated. Also: DPC has four-fifths elected members, MPC has two-thirds. Rule: Corporation = Commissioner rules (Bombay pattern); Municipality = Chairperson rules; Notified Area = nominated.

Book edition: simple words and memory words#

In simple words

  • Local self-government means villages and towns run their own affairs through elected bodies. It was only a Directive Principle (Article 40) and a State List subject (entry 5) until 1992.
  • Several committees (Balwant Rai Mehta 1957 first) argued for democratic decentralisation; Rajasthan started Panchayati Raj on 2 Oct 1959 at Nagaur, then Andhra Pradesh.
  • The 73rd Amendment (1992) made village bodies constitutional: Part IX (Arts 243 to 243O) and the Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects). It gives a three-tier system, 5-year terms, one-third women seats, a State Election Commission and a State Finance Commission.
  • The 74th Amendment (1992) did the same for towns: Part IX-A (Arts 243P to 243ZG) and the Twelfth Schedule (18 subjects), with Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council and Municipal Corporation.
  • Problems remain: weak devolution (3Fs and the 4th F, Functionality), tied funds, heavy dependence on grants, inactive Gram Sabhas. The 15th Finance Commission gave a record Rs 4.36 lakh crore and linked grants to reforms.

Key terms

Gram Sabha
All registered voters of a village; the base of the democratic pyramid (Art 243A)
Intermediate level
The level between village and district, notified by the Governor; states with population up to 20 lakh may skip it
Eleventh Schedule
List of 29 subjects that Panchayats can handle (Art 243G)
Twelfth Schedule
List of 18 functional items for municipalities (Art 243W)
State Finance Commission (SFC)
Set up by the Governor every 5 years to review finances of Panchayats and municipalities
State Election Commission (SEC)
Controls elections to Panchayats and Municipalities; head appointed by the Governor, removed like a High Court judge
PESA Act, 1996
Extends Panchayat self-rule to Scheduled Areas where Part IX does not apply
District Planning Committee
Joins plans of panchayats and municipalities into one district plan; four-fifths of members elected (Art 243ZD)
