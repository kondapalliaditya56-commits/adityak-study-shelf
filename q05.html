2010PYQ 1Ahmad Shah Abdali's immediate reason for Panipat 1761

What was the immediate reason for Ahmad Shah Abdali to invade India and fight the 3rd Battle of Panipat?

Answer: (A)

Why (a) is correct: In March 1758, Raghunath Rao crossed into Punjab and drove away Timur Shah (Abdali's son and his Viceroy in Lahore — also Governor of Punjab and Kashmir). The Marathas then appointed Adina Beg Khan as Governor of Punjab on an annual tribute of ₹75 lakh, and on his death Sabaji Scindia took charge. This Maratha conquest of Punjab was a direct, personal challenge Abdali could not ignore — the immediate trigger for his invasion. You will find this in 'How did Rohilkhand end?' (inside the ⚠ Alert block).

Why the other options are wrong:

  • (b) Maratha expansion was a long-term concern, not the immediate trigger — the specific trigger was the 1758 expulsion of Timur Shah from Lahore.
  • (c) The Mughal Emperor at this time was a puppet figure with no tribute leverage on Abdali.
  • (d) There was no separate Sultan of Punjab — Punjab was the very province Abdali had appointed Timur Shah to govern.

2017PYQ 1 Fragmented polity in mid-18th centuryMains 2017 · 10 marks · 150 words

"Clarify how mid-eighteenth-century India was beset with the spectre of a fragmented polity."

Demand of the question: Show how India was a fragmented polity by the mid-eighteenth century. The verb " clarify " means make the case clearly with evidence — give multiple regional examples. The phrase " spectre of a fragmented polity " tells you the question accepts the premise as a recognised condition; your job is to show how, not to debate whether it was fragmented.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Mid-eighteenth-century India was indeed beset by the spectre of a fragmented polity — Mughal authority had broken apart into many separate regional powers across the subcontinent.

Guiding themes (pick 4–5 for a 150-word answer):

1. Successor States — Bengal, Awadh, Hyderabad (see Chapter 3, Successor States).

2. Maratha Confederacy (see Chapter 4, Rise of Marathas).

3. Jats and Afghans — three rebel states:

  • Bharatpur.
  • Farrukhabad.
  • Rohilkhand.

4. Sikh State — Banda Bahadur08-15 → Misl Confederacy → Ranjit Singh01-39 (see Chapter 6 — 'How did Banda Bahadur08-15 establish the first Sikh State — and why did it collapse?' + 'Synthesis — three rebel states, same pattern' + 'How did Ranjit Singh01-39 unify the Misls and build a powerful Sikh State?' + 'What were the terms of the 1809 Treaty of Amritsar with the EIC?').

5. Mysore + Travancore + Rajputs (see Chapter 7 — 'Akbar56-05 and Rajputs' through 'What made Tipu82-99 a different kind of ruler — and why did he still lose?').

6. Synthesis — three routes to rebel-state formation (agrarian-base-upward / Mughal-court-downward / ground-up-mercenary), one outcome: the fragmentation the question describes.

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): The fragmented polity that haunted mid-eighteenth-century India was already a settled reality — the EIC's later expansion only used this break-up; it did not cause it.

FYQ 1 Why Did Surajmal56-63’s Jat Kingdom Withdraw Militarily Before Panipat 1761? 150 words · 10 marks "Surajmal56-63 of the Jat Kingdom withdrew militarily before the Third Battle of Panipat 1761 even though he had been an ally of the Marathas. Examine the reasons for the withdrawal and its impact on the battle outcome."

Demand of the question: Explain why Surajmal56-63 withdrew + show the impact of that withdrawal on the Maratha defeat.

Suggested intro (~25 words): "Surajmal56-63 of Bharatpur and Sadashivrao Bhau of the Marathas clashed over war strategy before Panipat 1761. Surajmal56-63 withdrew his army but still helped with supplies."

Guiding themes:

1. Strategy clash — Surajmal56-63 wanted a decentralised guerrilla approach (Jat home terrain near Agra). Sadashivrao Bhau wanted a pitched battle.

2. Court protocol clash — Bhau treated Surajmal56-63 as a junior. The Jat ruler took offence.

3. Withdrawal scope — Surajmal56-63 pulled his army out of the joint force but still helped with essential supplies (food, water). The withdrawal was military, not total.

4. Impact on Panipat 1761 — Maratha numbers were down; guerrilla option was lost; Abdali’s horse-mounted guns broke a fixed Maratha line. (See Chapter 4: 'Wars, Treaties & Territories — through to the 3rd Battle of Panipat 1761 & beyond' Why Abdali won)

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): "Surajmal56-63’s withdrawal was about strategy and respect, not betrayal. But it removed the Maratha option to fight on Jat terms."

Revise: 'Surajmal56-63’s reign' + Ch 4 'Third Panipat' + 'Why Abdali won'.

FYQ 2 Why Did the Afghan Kingdoms of Farrukhabad and Rohilkhand Never Become All-India Powers? 150 words · 10 marks "In the mid-18th century, Afghan kingdoms of Farrukhabad and Rohilkhand rose alongside the Marathas, Jats and Successor States. But they never became all-India powers. Examine why."

Demand of the question: Show the structural limits that kept Afghan kingdoms regional.

Suggested intro (~25 words): "Farrukhabad and Rohilkhand were Pathan-warlord kingdoms set up in the Mughal Doab. They grew quickly but stayed regional — they never built the base for all-India rule."

Guiding themes:

1. Mercenary origin, not popular base — founders (Md Khan Bangash14-43; Ali Mohammad Khan) were Pathan mercenaries hired by Mughals, then turned independent. No mass support among local peasants.

2. Geography — hemmed in — Farrukhabad sat between Awadh and Marathas; Rohilkhand between Awadh and Punjab Sikhs. No room to expand.

3. Lost key wars — Farrukhabad annexed by Awadh in 1749. Rohilkhand crushed by Awadh + EIC in the First Rohilla War 1773-74.

4. No strategic alliance — even Abdali’s 1761 Panipat win did not translate into Afghan rule of India; Abdali went home, leaving Farrukhabad and Rohilkhand alone against the rising EIC.

Suggested conclusion (~20 words): "Afghan kingdoms were warlord kingdoms — strong in the saddle, weak in the soil. Regional limits and lost wars kept them off the all-India stage."

Revise: 'Farrukhabad (est. 1714)' + 'Pathan warlord origin' + '1749 Awadh annexation' + 'Rohilkhand (est. 1737–40)'.
