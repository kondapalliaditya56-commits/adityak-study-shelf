Left Wing Extremism (LWE)High priority

Handout + slidesHandout p.9-27 · class slides 11-18PYQ: 2013, 2015, 2018, 2020, 2022, 2025

LWE began at Naxalbari (1967) and thrived where land, forest and governance promises failed.

1 · Start here: simple words and key terms#

In simple words
  • LWE began at Naxalbari (1967) and thrived where land, forest and governance promises failed.
  • The state answered with force + development + rights. Police and public order stay with States.
  • Violence is down 88-90% since 2010; handout says zero affected districts by Mar-Apr 2026.
Key termIn plain words
Political legitimacy deficitGovernance deficit → Naxals gain legitimacy → vicious cycle. Use as the core cause in any ‘why’ answer.
Circular governance trapForest security measures cut local connect, weakening governance, which keeps security risk alive.
Indicative legislation (PESA)Lays guidelines; works only after State amendments.
Clear, hold, developForce wins an area, then development wins the tribal population.
Legacy & Thrust districtNo longer affected; support continues to stop relapse.
Adivasi paradoxTribals trapped between Maoist threats and police raids.
Structural disintegrationTeacher: from tactical containment to breaking leadership, cadre and support.

3 · Overview#

What is LWE?

  • Radical political ideology: abolish capitalism, build a socialist or communist order, dismantle existing power structures; uses bombings, assassinations, armed insurrection.
  • Teacher's keywords: ✎ write this in every intro fed by disaffection, disinformation, displacement → aims at a protracted people's war, radical political-economic-social change, overthrow of government.
QuoteBy
“Naxalism is not merely a law and order issue… directly related to underdevelopment… tribal areas are the main battleground.”Dr. Manmohan Singh
“The Constitution intends that the land always should remain with the Tribals.”Samatha judgement
“Naxalism didn't spread because of poverty; poverty spread because of Naxalism.”Home Minister (slide 18)
“Movement is on its last legs.”K. Vijay, MHA Security Advisor
Current affairs

Naxal Mukt Bharat: target 31 Mar 2026 (Centre-State strategy of 24 Aug 2024); declared fulfilled on the date. Handout: no district LWE-affected; 2026 so far: 33 incidents, 11 deaths (to 29 Jul).

4 · Data: peak to present#

PYQs

PYQ 2018 · downward trend

  • Districts: 126 (2014) → 0 (Mar-Apr 2026). 38 Legacy & Thrust districts (13 in Chhattisgarh) kept under support to prevent relapse.
  • Violent incidents: 1,936 (2010) → 234 (2025), −88%; 870 in 2014.
  • Deaths (civilians + SF): 1,005 (2010) → 100 (2025), −90%; 310 in 2014.
  • Police stations reporting violence: 465 (2010) → 119 (2025).
  • 2025: 364 neutralised, 1,022 arrested, 2,337 surrendered. 3,927 surrendered 2024-Mar 2026.
  • Shift: from expanding “Red Corridor” to consolidating LWE-free areas and relapse prevention.

Evolution: four phases

Four phases
PhaseKey points
1st 1967-72Naxalbari (WB) 1967, also Bihar, Srikakulam (AP) → CPI (ML) 1969, Charu Majumdar. Crushed by 1972 (United Front govt + Indira Gandhi's govt).
2nd 1975-2004Factionalised in Emergency. Four groups: MCC, PWG, PU, CPI-ML (Liberation) (Liberation contested elections). 1998: PU + PWG → CPI-ML (People's War). Bihar caste war: Dalits (MCC) vs Ranvir Sena, Sunlight Sena.
3rd 2004-122004: PU + MCC + PWG → CPI-Maoist, unified command. Salwa Judum 2005-09; Operation Green Hunt 2009-12. 2010 Dantewada: 76 killed (75 CRPF), Green Hunt stalled.
4th 2013-Violence below 1990s average. Reasons: Integrated Action Plan, roads, police stations, NREGA; Maoist tactical retreat.

5 · Drivers of Naxalism (Expert Group, Planning Commission and ARC II)#

PYQs

PYQ 2020 · determinants · PYQ 2022 · social-economic-developmental

Memory aid · Drivers (7 groups)
PLEDGES
Unkept state PLEDGES (land, forests, governance) gave Maoists their opening.
PPolice and state repressionLLand and agrarianEEconomic and socialDDisplacementGGovernanceEEmpowerment (psychological)SSocial exclusion, alienation
GroupKey points
Social-economicLand rights, no jobs, discrimination; cash-credit shortage → moneylenders; Dalit-tribal exploitation, forced labour, untouchability. Livelihood: PDS corruption, lost traditional occupations and common-property rights, Minimum Wages Act not applied (Naxals enforce wages).
Land and agrarianLand reforms failed (ceilings evaded, special tenures exempt). Encroached government/community land (even water bodies); no title for landless; tribal-to-non-tribal transfer in Fifth Schedule areas; LARR 2013 not implemented. Bihar: Naxals helped Musahars take land.
Displacement PYQ 2015FCA 1980 kept forest dwellers near eviction. 40% of displaced are tribal evictees; irrigation and power projects without rehabilitation; acquisition for ‘public purpose’ without compensation.
Governance73rd Amendment unfulfilled; absent staff, poor health and schools; police misuse; circular governance trap (forest security → less local connect → more insecurity). No rural dispute resolution → Naxal People's Courts.
Police and state repressionArbitrary police action, forest-staff harassment, brutal suppression; TADA/POTA on tribals leave land fallow, Naxals encroach.
Social exclusion and cultural alienationDenial of dignity; atrocities, civil-rights and bonded-labour laws poorly enforced (Karimnagar 1980 forced-labour violence); alienation from land and culture.
Psychological empowermentYouth gain power and identity by challenging traditional power holders.
Teacher stressed (slide 12)

Governance deficit → Naxals gain legitimacy → vicious cycle. Keywords: Xaxa (40% tribal victims), land to the tiller, CFR lacking, SC/ST Act not enforced, judicial alienation.

7 · Surrender policy#

PYQs

PYQ 2015 · mainstream

  • Part of multi-pronged strategy; paired with firm police action. Aim: gainful employment so cadres do not return.
  • Benefits: ₹5 lakh (higher cadres), ₹2.5 lakh (others), weapon incentives, ₹10,000/month for 3 years training. Data: 2,337 (2025); 3,927 (2024-Mar 2026).

8 · Government approach and ARC II strategy#

PYQs

PYQ 2018 · approach · PYQ 2020 · GoI, administration, forces · PYQ 2022 · multilayered strategy

  • Seventh Schedule: Police and Public order = State subjects; Centre supplements. National Policy and Action Plan: security + development + rights and entitlements.
  • Security front: CAPF battalions, training, State police modernisation, intelligence, fortified police stations. Development front: roads, telecom, skill, financial inclusion.
  • Three-pronged: (1) necessary force, (2) address grievances via infrastructure, (3) political demands with fixed timelines. Low-key (North-East) over Sri Lanka's ‘hammer and tongs’ vs LTTE. Clear, hold and develop.
ARC II capacity buildingKey points
Security forcesTemporary development role where agencies cannot work (WB: police kept schools running); SOPs within constitutional bounds; special task forces like Greyhounds.
AdministrationStrengthen field formations; Oversight Committee for FRA 2006 (tribals, conservationists, committed individuals).
PersonnelTrain at LBS National Academy; reimburse States for incentives to serve in difficult areas; revive inspections.
Local bodiesImplement PESA 1996 to close governance deficit.
Civil societyBridge between extremists and government; educate on futility of violence; ventilate grievances legally.
Cut financesBRO or government agencies build roads directly; anti-extortion and economic offences wing.

9 · Case study: Salwa Judum (2005, Chhattisgarh)#

  • ‘Peace March / Purification Hunt’ (Gondi); state-backed vigilantes, successor to Jan Jagran Abhiyan (1991); mining ties (Tata, Essar). SPOs / Koya Commandos by 2008; many ex-Maoists.
  • Impact: 600+ villages burned, ~300,000 displaced, 98 SPOs and up to 800 killed; Darbha Ghat massacre.
  • Nandini Sundar v. State of Chhattisgarh: (1) excessive delegation in Chhattisgarh Police Act 2007; (2) SPOs in combat roles with 25 h law + 12 h Constitution training; (3) honorarium only → Article 14; endangered lives → Article 21.

10 · Fifth Schedule: non-implementation#

PYQs

PYQ 2013 · Art. 244, Fifth Schedule

  • PESA is ‘indicative’ (ARC II): works only if States amend Panchayati Raj Acts; implementation unsatisfactory.
DimensionProvision
Administration of Scheduled AreasPresident declares area after consulting Governor; Governor regulates land transfer, land allotment, money-lending for STs.
Tribes Advisory CouncilUp to 20 members, three-fourths ST; advises on welfare.
Special provisionsGovernor can bar or modify any Act in Scheduled Areas; repeal or amend laws.
Reports to the PresidentGovernor reports annually; Union ensures implementation.
Memory aid · Fifth Schedule dimensions
STAR
The guiding STAR of tribal governance.
SSpecial provisionsTTribes Advisory CouncilAAdministration of Scheduled AreasRReports to the President
  • Failures: some tribal-majority areas lack Fifth Schedule status; laws extended in routine, community governance lost legitimacy; Governor's office lacks capacity.
  • Land alienation: MP Land Regulation rules 1959 allow transfer to non-tribals; Chhattisgarh permits acquisition on small loan default; Rajasthan: district administration can overturn Gram Sabha.
  • Reports to President: casual and irregular (Mungekar); AP and MP sent none from 1999-2000, Maharashtra and Orissa from 2000-01 (Bhuria, 2002-04). TAC: tokenistic; no TAC discussed land alienation 2005-2011.
  • Mungekar: ‘particularisation, not generalisation; discretion, not rule by rote’. Samatha: Schedules exist to stop exploitation; land stays with tribals. Failure creates a governance vacuum the Maoists fill.
Current affairs

PESA status (Mar 2026): Fifth Schedule areas in 10 States; 9 have framed PESA Rules, Odisha only draft; MoPR has 10 PESA indicators; Jharkhand PESA Rules 2025 listed.

Teacher stressed (slide 15)

Quote Mungekar and Xaxa reports (boxed in red). Governor: lack of capacity; TAC: tokenistic; laws: routine application; reports: casual.

11 · Women in the Red Corridor#

  • Appeal: large share of cadres, all-women squads (KAMS) fighting forced marriage, abduction, bigamy, domestic violence, police brutality. NCRB 2019: 84,938 crimes in AP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, MP, Odisha. SC/ST women bear gender-caste-class burden.
  • Problems: 35-40% of members but 1 of 20 in central committee; no menstrual leave or hygiene; anaemia; forced vasectomy and abortions; sexual exploitation.
  • Way forward: gender-sensitive alternatives to cadre life; rework hard-power responses; diminish motivations, don't demean them.

12 · Forest policy and the Forest Rights Act#

PYQs

PYQ 2015 · displacement of tribals

Memory aid · Four FRA rights
TURF
Who holds the TURF.
TTitle (max 4 ha)UUse (MFP, grazing)RRelief and developmentFForest management
  • Teacher: historical injustice (British 1865-1894, IFA 1927, FCA 1980) → course correction (BK Roy Burman, FRA).
  • FRA 2006 (FDST + OTFD): 4 rights: title (max 4 ha, land already cultivated), use (MFP, grazing, routes), relief and development, forest management. OTFD: 3 generations (75 yrs) before 13 Dec 2005. Gram Sabha initiates; Gram Sabha → SDM Committee → District Committee. Free informed consent for National Parks and Sanctuaries.
FRA issuesDetail
Policy gapsVague terms (‘primarily resides’, ‘bonafide livelihood’); PVTG position; FCR 2022 need no Gram Sabha consent at preliminary approval; 2,400 sq km diverted under FCA.
ImplementationForest Dept resists CFR (Sarguja: 27 CFR titles not recognised); only 40% claims accepted (MoTA, teacher).
RejectionsWrong OTFD reading, no GPS proof, disputed ‘orange areas’ (Chhattisgarh); Xaxa: not communicated, no appeal explained, documents demanded despite oral evidence.
ViolationsEviction before verification (Thane); flawed Forest Rights Committees; women named in <10% documents (UN Women 2012).
  • Teacher's Gadchiroli triangle: infrastructure development vs recognition of CFR vs rehabilitation of surrendered Naxals.

13 · 2026 framework, emerging issues and value addition#

PYQs

PYQ 2022 · emerging issues · PYQ 2025 · eliminate by 2026

  • Two-tier policy: Legacy & Thrust (insurgency ended, consolidate) and District of Concern (network dismantled, targeted work).
  • Emerging challenges: Adivasi Paradox (between Maoists and police raids); Urban Naxalism; dissent vs terrorism (UAPA vs Jal-Jangal-Zameen activists); development trap in mineral lands; weak grassroots institutions.
  • Way forward (slide): conflict transformation (PMRDF, fast-track undertrials); Gram Sabha controls MFP via Van Dhan Vikas Kendras; Odisha SETU model (Malkangiri) PYQ 2015 · Malkangiri; local police replace CAPF (like Greyhounds).
  • Keywords: ✎ slide 18 soft-power approach; Maoism survives in security vacuums; political legitimacy deficit; radicalisation pathways; insurgency as development failure; ideological anachronism (1920s Marxism); penetration-integration gap.
Current affairs

Urban Naxalism and law: PM: ‘vanishing from jungles but taking roots in urban centres’. 4 LWE special courts in Chhattisgarh. Maharashtra Special Public Security Bill 2024: vague ‘unlawful activity’, no legal vetting, no due process on seizure. Bastar: most developed tribal division by Dec 2030 (Kanker, Kondagaon, Bastar, Sukma, Bijapur, Dantewada, Narayanpur).
