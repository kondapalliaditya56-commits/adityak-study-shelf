1 · Overview#

The National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC) is a constitutional body under Article 338. Its objective is to safeguard the rights and interests of the Scheduled Castes and the Anglo-Indian community. It reflects the framers' commitment to social justice, equality and protection against exploitation or discrimination of these historically marginalised communities.

Core idea

NCSC = Art. 338. Its twin for tribes is the NCST under Art. 338A, and for backward classes the NCBC under Art. 338B.

2 · History of the NCSC#

From Special Officer to NCSC

1950: Special Officer
Art. 338 provided a Special Officer (Commissioner for SCs and STs) to investigate safeguards and report to the President
1978: Non-statutory Commission
multi-member Commission for SCs and STs set up by a Government Resolution; worked alongside the Commissioner
1987: Renamed
functions modified; became the National Commission for SCs and STs; still non-constitutional
1990: 65th Amendment Act
replaced the single Special Officer with a high-level multi-member National Commission; constitutional status under Art. 338; replaced both Commissioner and the earlier non-statutory Commission
2003: 89th Amendment Act
split the combined Commission into two constitutional bodies: NCSC (Art. 338) and NCST (Art. 338A)
2004: NCSC comes into existence
Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three other Members, appointed by the President

3 · Composition#

The Commission has one Chairperson, one Vice-Chairperson and three other Members.

  • All are appointed by the President of India (the composition note adds: on the recommendation of the Central Government), for a fixed tenure prescribed by law.
  • The President determines the service conditions and tenure, ensuring independence and stability.
  • Headquarters: New Delhi.
  • A Secretary and staff drawn from the Union public service assist the Commission.

4 · Vision and Mission#

Vision: ensure the holistic development, empowerment and protection of Scheduled Castes, fostering equality, social justice and dignity, in accordance with the Constitution.

Seven mission areas
AreaWhat it means
1. Monitoring and evaluationEvaluate planning, execution and outcomes of schemes; ensure benefits reach the intended beneficiaries
2. Enforcement of safeguardsOversee implementation of constitutional and legal safeguards; ensure compliance by Central and State Governments and Public Sector Undertakings
3. Complaint redressalExamine and investigate complaints of violations, deprivation of rights or discrimination; take action for timely redressal
4. Policy recommendationsGive informed recommendations to Central and State Governments; strengthen policies and schemes
5. Proactive empowermentIdentify gaps in frameworks and suggest measures; promote education, employment and social upliftment
6. Guardianship of justice and rightsVigilant guardian without fear or favour; keep interventions aligned with the Constitution and the best interests of Scheduled Castes
7. Awareness and outreachPromote awareness of rights and schemes; encourage participation in governance and development
Mission
MECPPGA

Monitoring, Enforcement of safeguards, Complaint redressal, Policy recommendations, Proactive empowerment, Guardianship, Awareness: "Many Eager Citizens Push Progress, Guarding All".

The Extension of Mandate line speaks of "SCs and STs" and extends to other backward classes. This reads like text from the older combined commission. 6 · Wings of the NCSC#

The NCSC works through three specialised wings. Each handles a category of complaints, monitors safeguards and checks compliance.

1. Atrocities and Protection of Civil Rights Wing

  • Atrocity cases and violations of legal safeguards
  • enforcement of the Protection of Civil Rights Act, the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act and the Minimum Wages Act

2. Service Safeguards and Reservation Policy Wing

  • Protection of SC employees in Central and State Governments and PSUs
  • adherence to reservation policy and fair service practices

3. Development and Welfare Wing

  • Socio-economic empowerment
  • review of Central and State welfare programmes for equitable access and execution

9 · Powers and Authority#

The Commission has autonomy to regulate its own procedure.

Constitutional clauses
ClauseContent
Art. 338(8)While investigating or inquiring into complaints, the NCSC has powers of a civil court
Art. 338(9)Central and State Governments must consult the Commission on all major policy matters affecting Scheduled Castes

Civil court powers under Art. 338(8)

  • Summoning and enforcing attendance of any person from any part of India and examining on oath.
  • Requiring discovery and production of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavits.
  • Requisitioning public records from any court or office.
  • Issuing summons for examination of witnesses and documents.
  • Exercising any other powers determined by the President.
Civil court powers
SDARSO

Summon and examine on oath, Documents, Affidavits, Records of any court or office, Summons for witnesses and documents, Other powers set by the President: "Six Duties Are Really Serious Ones".
