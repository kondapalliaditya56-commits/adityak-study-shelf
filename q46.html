1996PYQ 1(Prelims 1996)

Which one of the following is not correct about the Cabinet Mission Plan?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Statement (c) is the non-feature: CMP1946 was the only proposal where the British offered a united India & Purna Swaraj — it turned down the demand for a separate Pakistan. At most it carried implicit grouping, never acceptance of Pakistan.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Provincial grouping was a real CMP feature — provinces were divided into Sections A, B and C, each framing a group constitution — so it cannot be the 'not correct' pick.

(B) ✗ — An Interim Cabinet of Indians was also a real feature: Immediate transfer of executive power to an interim government at the Centre. (This was done in September 1946, with J.L. Nehru as Prime Minister.).

(D) ✗ — The constitution-framing right was central to the plan: a Constituent Assembly of elected PLA members plus Princely-State nominees was to frame the constitution — a real feature, not the answer. (Note: The Cabinet mission plan recommended an undivided India. It turned down the Muslim League's demand for a separate Pakistan.)

1996PYQ 2(Prelims 1996)

Assertion (A) The British sovereignty continued to exist in free India. Reason (R) The British sovereign appointed the last Governor General of Free India. In the context of the above two statements, which one of the following is correct?

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — A is false, R is true. Free India was sovereign: Yes, symbolic sovereignty continued to exist for crown and dominion status itself lasted only until 26 January 1950 but in the strict language of constitutional law (and UPSC answer keys), "symbolic sovereignty of the Crown" is not the same as "British sovereignty" which implies actual powers to rule India. Indian Independence Act 1947 explicitly ended the British Parliament of any power to legislate for India from the appointed day (15th Aug 1947). R is true: Each dominion would be headed by a Governor General appointed by the King — Mountbatten.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Treats A as true, but British sovereignty did not continue: the Act ended the British Parliament's jurisdiction on the appointed day — the Crown's residual dominion link was terminable, and terminated, by India's own Constituent Assembly.

(B) ✗ — Also treats A as true; it fails the same way — sovereignty had passed: jurisdiction of the British Parliament ended and Paramountcy lapsed on the appointed day.

(C) ✗ — R is not false: the Governor General of each dominion was "appointed by the King" — the British sovereign did appoint free India's last Governor General. (Note: India became sovereign nation on 15 August, 1947. However Lord Mountabatten as a last Governor General of free India was appointed by British sovereign.)

1998PYQ 8(Prelims 1998)

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — By June 1947, INC had given up on a united India — defeated by a combination of communal violence, the failure of the Interim Government, and ML1906's refusal to join the Constituent Assembly. Direct Action Day alone had killed 5,000 in Calcutta in one week. Avoiding ever-larger communal slaughter was the operative reason.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — INC never accepted the Two-Nation theory: even its Cabinet-Mission objection was only to compulsory grouping, not to a united India, and Gandhi told INC — "don't accept partition in your hearts" (11.4(v)).

(B) ✗ — Not helpless imposition: INC accepted the 3rd June Plan with its own counter-demands met — Pakistan as small as possible, a strong unitary centre for India.

(D) ✗ — Freedom was already coming: the Atlee Statement had fixed a British exit by 30 June 1948 "with or without a constitution". The recorded driver of acceptance was the communal violence. (Note: The period of 1940s were the considered crisis time for Hindu Muslim unity thereafter Indian National Congress agreed in 1947 to the partition of the country because they wanted to avoid large scale civil war.)

Wrong options:

FYQ 1 Mains-FYQ · 15 marks · 250 words The Partition of India in 1947 was the outcome of structural failures of Indian constitutional negotiation between 1944 & 1947, not the inevitable consequence of the Two-Nation Theory. Examine.

Demand of the question: "Examine" + "structural failures vs inevitable" demands a balanced analysis.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Partition was not pre-determined by the 1940 Lahore Resolution. It was the cumulative outcome of failed negotiations between September 1944 (Gandhi-Jinnah) and June 1947 (Mountbatten Plan). Body guidance — 4 themes:

1. Rajaji Formula failure (see 'Proposals'). Gandhi-Jinnah talks Sep 1944. The Formula's sequence (freedom first, then plebiscite) was Gandhi's offer; Jinnah's counter was Two-Nation recognition first. Compromise architecture existed but was rejected.

2. Wavell Plan failure (see 'Wavell Plan, 1945'). Simla 1945. INC + ML structural parity in Executive Council was offered. Jinnah's demand for ML monopoly on Muslim seats — denying Maulana Azad (INC Muslim) any place — was unprecedented & rejected by Wavell. Collapsed.

3. Cabinet Mission Plan + INC ambiguity (see 'Cabinet Mission Plan (CMP1946)'). May-June

1946. The three-tier federation rejected outright Pakistan in favor of grouping. INC initially accepted but later qualified its acceptance on the compulsory grouping interpretation. ML responded with Direct Action Day (16 Aug 1946) → Calcutta Killings → communal violence cascade. INC's interpretive backtracking gave ML the political opening to walk away.

4. Mountbatten Plan as residual outcome (see '3rd June Plan / Mountbatten Plan (1947)'). By June 1947, alternative structures had repeatedly failed. Partition was offered not because it was preferred but because no other arrangement could be made operational. Mountbatten's compressed timeline (15 Aug 1947) made any alternative arrangement impossible.

Closing verdict: Partition reflected institutional inability to design a federal structure both INC & ML could accept. The Two-Nation Theory was ML's framework; INC's competing framework existed too. What failed was the negotiation architecture between them — across Rajaji, Wavell, Cabinet Mission. Partition was not inevitable; it was residual.

Revise: 'Proposals'-2 (Rajaji) + 'Wavell' + 'Cabinet Mission' + 'Mountbatten Plan' + 'Why Partition'.

FYQ 2 Mains-FYQ · 10 marks · 150 words Examine how 565 Princely States were integrated into the Indian Union in 1947-49 through the Patel-Menon approach.

Demand of the question: "Examine how integrated" requires explaining the framework & tools used.

Suggested intro (~25 words): 565 Princely States had to be integrated quickly between June 1947 (Mountbatten Plan) & October 1948 (Hyderabad). Patel's Department of States + V.P. Menon designed the integration architecture. Body guidance — 3 themes (see 'Integration of Princely States'):

1. The Two-Step Architecture. Step 1: Standstill Agreement (continuing existing administrative + economic relations). Step 2: Instrument of Accession (transferring 3 subjects — Defence, External Affairs, Communications — to Indian Union). This was the minimum legal commitment needed.

2. The Stick + Carrot. Patel's stick: implicit threat of public agitation, blockade, military action against holdouts. Carrot: Privy Purses (financial compensation), continued titles & ceremonial dignities, integration into Indian states administrative structure.

3. The Hard Cases. Most States acceded by 15 August 1947. Junagadh (Sept 1947) joined Pakistan but population was Hindu — plebiscite then Indian merger. Hyderabad (Sept 1948) resisted — Operation Polo (police action) forced accession. Kashmir (Oct 1947) — Pakistani tribal raid forced Maharaja Hari Singh's Instrument of Accession in exchange for Indian military intervention.

Closing verdict: The Patel-Menon framework integrated 565 disparate Princely Units within 15 months — an unprecedented administrative achievement. Without the Standstill + Instrument architecture, India would have inherited a fragmented sub-continent.

Revise: 'Integration of Princely States'.
