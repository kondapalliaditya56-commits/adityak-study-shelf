1996PYQ 1(Prelims 1996)

Which one of the following is not correct about the Cabinet Mission Plan?

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Acceptance of Pakistan is the non-feature. CMP1946 was the only proposal where the British offered a united India and Purna Swaraj; it turned down the demand for a separate Pakistan, carrying at most implicit grouping.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Grouping was real: provinces in Sections A, B and C, each framing a group constitution.

(B) ✗ — An Interim Cabinet of Indians was real: executive power transferred at the Centre (September 1946, Nehru as PM).

(D) ✗ — The Constituent Assembly (elected PLA members plus Princely-State nominees) was to frame the constitution; a real feature.

1996PYQ 2(Prelims 1996)

Assertion (A): The British sovereignty continued to exist in free India.
Reason (R): The British sovereign appointed the last Governor General of Free India.
In the context of the above two statements, which one of the following is correct?

Answer: (D)

(D) ✓ — A is false, R is true. The Indian Independence Act 1947 ended the British Parliament's jurisdiction on the appointed day (15 Aug 1947); only a symbolic Crown link remained, ended by India's CA on 26 Jan 1950. R is true: each dominion's Governor General was appointed by the King, i.e. Mountbatten.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — Treats A as true, but the Act ended Parliament's jurisdiction on the appointed day.

(B) ✗ — Also treats A as true; sovereignty had passed and Paramountcy lapsed.

(C) ✗ — R is not false: the King did appoint free India's last Governor General.

FYQ 1 The Partition of India in 1947 was the outcome of structural failures of Indian constitutional negotiation between 1944 & 1947, not the inevitable consequence of the Two-Nation Theory. Examine. (15 marks, 250 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine' + 'structural failures vs inevitable' demands a balanced analysis.

Suggested intro (~25 words): Partition was not pre-determined by the 1940 Lahore Resolution. It was the cumulative outcome of failed negotiations between September 1944 (Gandhi-Jinnah) and June 1947 (Mountbatten Plan).

1. Rajaji Formula failure: Gandhi-Jinnah talks, Sep 1944. Gandhi offered freedom first, then plebiscite; Jinnah's counter was Two-Nation recognition first. A compromise architecture existed but was rejected.

2. Wavell Plan failure: Simla 1945. INC-ML parity in the Executive Council was offered; Jinnah's demand that ML hold all Muslim seats, denying Maulana Azad (INC Muslim) any place, was rejected by Wavell4347 and the talks collapsed.

3. Cabinet Mission Plan and INC ambiguity: May-June 1946. The three-tier federation rejected outright Pakistan in favour of grouping. INC accepted, then qualified its acceptance on compulsory grouping. ML replied with Direct Action Day (16 Aug 1946), the Calcutta killings and a cascade of violence; INC's interpretive backtracking gave ML the opening to walk away.

4. Mountbatten Plan as residual outcome: by June 1947 alternatives had repeatedly failed; partition was offered because no other arrangement could be made operational, and the 15 Aug 1947 timeline made any alternative impossible.

Closing verdict: Partition reflected an institutional inability to design a federal structure both INC and ML could accept. The Two-Nation Theory was ML's framework; INC's competing framework existed too. What failed was the negotiation architecture, across Rajaji, Wavell and Cabinet Mission. Partition was not inevitable; it was residual.

Revise: Rajaji Formula + Wavell Plan + Cabinet Mission + Mountbatten Plan + Why Partition.

FYQ 2 Examine how 565 Princely States were integrated into the Indian Union in 1947-49 through the Patel-Menon approach. (10 marks, 150 words)

Demand of the question: 'Examine how' requires explaining the framework and tools used.

Suggested intro (~25 words): 565 Princely States had to be integrated quickly between June 1947 (Mountbatten Plan) and October 1948 (Hyderabad). Patel's Department of States and V.P. Menon designed the integration architecture.

1. The two-step architecture: Step 1, Standstill Agreement (continuing existing administrative and economic relations). Step 2, Instrument of Accession (transferring 3 subjects, Defence, External Affairs and Communications, to the Indian Union), the minimum legal commitment needed.

2. Stick and carrot: the stick was the implicit threat of public agitation, blockade and military action against holdouts; the carrot was Privy Purses, continued titles and ceremonial dignities, and a place in the Indian states' administrative structure.

3. The hard cases: most states acceded by 15 August 1947. Junagadh (Sept 1947) joined Pakistan but its population was Hindu, so a plebiscite then led to Indian merger (Feb 1948). Hyderabad (Sept 1948) resisted until Operation Polo forced accession. Kashmir (Oct 1947): a Pakistani tribal raid forced Maharaja Hari Singh's Instrument of Accession in exchange for Indian military intervention.

Closing verdict: The Patel-Menon framework integrated 565 disparate units within about 15 months, an unprecedented administrative achievement; without the Standstill plus Instrument architecture India would have inherited a fragmented sub-continent.

FLAG: the companion lists Junagadh as 'Sept 1947' joining Pakistan and a plebiscite after; handout dates the plebiscite and integration to February 1948. Also, by the handout's arithmetic 549 states acceded by or shortly after 15 Aug 1947 and 13 went to Pakistan (565 = 552 + 13).

Revise: Integration of Princely States.

PYQ 1 Mains PYQ (Guiding Theme)Assess the role of British imperial power in complicating the process of transfer of power during the 1940s. (marks/words not given in the companion)

FLAG: the companion gives only the question for this Mains PYQ; the answer below is built from the handout (Secs 6-8) and the finished chapter notes. The teacher's board version: 'How Bx complicated ToP in 1940s'.

Demand of the question: 'Assess' - weigh how far British interests, not only Indian divisions, made the transfer of power harder.

Suggested intro: Britain's interest drove the process; its choices in the 1940s repeatedly raised the League's floor and left a legal vacuum on exit.

1. Veto to ML1906: August Offer 1940, Cripps 1942 and the Wavell Plan 1945 each gave the League an effective (implicit) veto on transfer of power.

2. Atlee Statement, Feb 1947: a pro-balkanisation statement, 565 states outside any successor government, power to provinces not in the CA, 30 June 1948 deadline 'with or without a constitution' (a threat of anarchy); Mountbatten replacing Wavell4347.

3. Plan Balkan (April 1947): a voluntary union of 11 provinces and all states; Nehru opposed it as balkanisation.

4. Passive mediator: partition was avoidable had Britain acted against communal violence rather than stay a neutral broker between INC and ML1906; this served British national interest.

5. Dominion status and Commonwealth: both dominions stayed in the Commonwealth, with trade benefits for British firms.

6. Cold War: a friendly Pakistan on the Arabian Sea and Central Asia flank was geopolitically valuable.

7. Radcliffe Line announced on 17 August 1947, after independence, so Britain would bear no blame for violence and migration.

Balance: Atlee's March 1946 statement that a minority would not be allowed a veto reversed earlier policy, and the Cabinet Mission offered a united India; ML1906 and INC's grouping dispute also share responsibility.

Closing: British national interest shaped the process; Britain 'tried but failed' at unity while leaving the hardest problems (violence, boundaries, states) to the last days.
