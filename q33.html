2013PYQ 3(Prelims 2013)

Annie Besant was 1. responsible for starting the Home Rule Movement. 2. the founder of the Theosophical society. 3. the President of the Indian National Congress. Select the correct answer using the codes given below

Answer: (C)

(C) ✓ — Statements 1 and 3 are true: Besant launched the All India Home Rule League (September 1916) and was made INC President at the 1917 Calcutta Session. Statement 2 fails: she was the HEAD of the Theosophical Society in Madras but not its founder — that was Madame Blavatsky and Colonel Olcott.

Wrong options:

(A) ✗ — "Only 1" undercounts: statement 3 is also true — Besant presided over the INC's 1917 Calcutta Session.

(B) ✗ — Keeps the false statement 2 (Besant led, but did not found, the Theosophical Society) and discards the true statement 1 (she launched the Home Rule Movement).

(D) ✗ — Overcounts: statement 2 is the trap — the Theosophical Society's founders were Blavatsky and Olcott; Besant was its later head. (Note: Even though, Annie Besant was associated with the Theosophical society, she was not its founder. It was founded by Madame Blavatsky and Henry Olcott. Thus, Statement 2 is incorrect. Statement 3 is correct.)

FYQ 1

In which month and year did Bal Gangadhar Tilak set up the Indian Home Rule League?

Answer: (A)

(A)

(A) ✓ — Tilak set up the Indian Home Rule League in April 1916; Annie Besant's All India Home Rule League followed in September 1916. The Indian HRL had its headquarters at Poona with 6 HISTORY (Home Rule League Movement (1916–18) & Lucknow Pact (1916)) branches and operated in Bombay Province/Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Berar, Karnataka and Central Provinces).

Why others wrong: (b) September 1916 is when Besant set up the All India Home Rule League, not Tilak's Indian HRL; (c) December 1916 is the date of the Lucknow Session where Extremists actually re-entered INC, not the founding of Tilak's league; (d) April 1917 is unrelated — Besant's arrest occurred in 1917 but the leagues were already founded by then.

Memory hook: Tilak's Indian league bloomed in April; Besant's All-India league arrived in September.

FYQ 2

Where was the headquarters of Annie Besant's All India Home Rule League located?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — Besant's All India Home Rule League had its headquarters at Madras and operated 200 branches across the rest of British India outside Tilak's zone; Madras was also the seat of Besant's Theosophical Society).

Why others wrong: (a) Poona was the headquarters of Tilak's Indian Home Rule League, not Besant's; (b) Bombay city was excluded even from Tilak's zone and was not the HQ of either league; (d) Calcutta is the venue of the 1917 INC Session where Besant was made President, not the HQ of her league.

Memory hook: Besant the Theosophist anchored both her Society and her League at Madras.

FYQ 3

Annie Besant, the founder of the All India Home Rule League, was inspired by the Home Rule movement of which country?

Answer: (B)

(B)

(B) ✓ — Annie Besant was an Irish woman inspired by the Home Rule movement in Ireland; she joined INC in 1914 and later headed the Theosophical Society based in Madras).

Why others wrong: (a) Scotland had no Home Rule movement that inspired Besant; (c) Wales is not mentioned as the source of Besant's inspiration; (d) Canada was already a Self-Governing Dominion and not a Home Rule model.

Memory hook: Irish Home Rule fired the Theosophist; Indian Home Rule followed her ship.

FYQ 4

Tilak's famous quote on untouchability — "If God tolerates untouchability then I won't recognise him as God" — was delivered at which forum?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — Tilak delivered the specific quote against untouchability at the 1918 All India Depressed Classes Conference. His focus on caste unity went back much earlier — at the Ganpati Festival of 1893, he had made Lord Ganapati, worshipped by both Upper and Lower Castes, the centrepiece).

Why others wrong: (a) The Ganpati Festival of 1893 was where Tilak began his caste-unity outreach using Lord Ganapati as a common deity, but the specific quote came later; (b) The Lucknow Session 1916 saw the Pact and Extremists' re-entry, not this quote; (d) The 1915 Bombay Session permitted Extremists' re-entry but was not the venue for this specific quote.

Memory hook: Ganapati in 1893 opened the temple door; the 1918 Depressed Classes platform delivered the line.

FYQ 6

FYQ 8

Who authored the Commonwealth of India Bill (1925) proposing Dominion Status for India?

Answer: (C)

(C)

(C) ✓ — Annie Besant authored the Commonwealth of India Bill (1925) proposing Dominion Status for India — a direct extension of her lifelong campaign for self-governance within the Empire; she had earlier popularised the idea of a voluntary 'Commonwealth of Nations', a phrase coined by Lord Rosebery in 1884).

Why others wrong: (a) Motilal Nehru authored the Motilal Nehru Report of 1928, which first formally used the phrase "Dominion Status", but not the 1925 Bill; (b) Tilak had died in 1920 and could not have authored a 1925 Bill; (d) Jinnah did not author the Commonwealth of India Bill 1925.

Memory hook: Rosebery coined Commonwealth, Besant bottled it into a 1925 Indian Bill.
