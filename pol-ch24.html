In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • Part XV (Articles 324-329) is the base: the Election Commission (Art. 324) runs elections, there is one roll (325) and adult suffrage at 18 (326), Parliament makes election law first (327) and States fill gaps (328), and courts are barred from delimitation and seat allotment (329).
  • The officer ladder runs CEO (State) to DEO (district) to Returning Officer (constituency), plus the ERO for rolls and the Presiding Officer at one polling station. Observers are General, Expenditure and Police.
  • The poll goes from notification, nomination, scrutiny, withdrawal to campaign, secret-ballot voting and counting. The campaign ends 48 hours before polling closes and the MCC applies from the day the schedule is announced.
  • RPA 1950 is about rolls, seats and delimitation
  • RPA 1951 is about conduct, offences and disputes. The Delimitation Act 2002 redrew boundaries on the 2001 Census
  • Seat numbers stay frozen
  • Reforms came in phases: before 1996 (age 18, EPIC, EVM law), the 1996 package, after 1996 (affidavits, postal and proxy voting) and since 2010 (NRI voting, NOTA, VVPAT). Money power is the unfinished problem and electoral bonds were struck down in February 2024.

Key terms

1 · Part XV: the constitutional base#

  • India's electoral system rests on Part XV (Articles 324-329). These Articles create one independent machinery to run elections to Parliament, State Legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President. Core principles: a single electoral roll per territorial constituency, equal access to the vote, adult suffrage, and legislative competence to frame election laws. The aim is free, fair and uniform elections across the country.
  • The Constitution gives election details only for the President and Vice-President.
  • Elections to Parliament and State Legislatures are governed by law made by Parliament under Article 327.
  • Memory aid (Articles 324 to 329): Commission (324), Single roll (325), Adult suffrage (326), Parliament's law (327), State legislature's law (328), Bar on courts (329): "Chief Says All People Shall Ballot."

Article 327: Parliament's power

  • Parliament may make law on all matters relating to or connected with elections to either House of Parliament or the House(s) of a State Legislature. This includes the preparation of electoral rolls, the delimitation of constituencies and all other matters needed for the due constitution of the House(s).

Two limits on this power

Principles binding Parliament

  • No separate electorate:
    • The Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto reform) had given separate electorates during British rule to divide the country
    • Article 325 says no person is ineligible for inclusion in, or to claim inclusion in, a special electoral roll on grounds of religion, race, caste or sex
  • Universal adult suffrage: Article 326: elections to the House of the People and State Legislative Assemblies are on the basis of adult suffrage

Power of State Legislatures (Article 328)

  • Subject to the Constitution, and in so far as Parliament has not made provision, a State Legislature may make law on all matters relating to elections to its House(s), including preparation of electoral rolls and other matters needed for due constitution of the House(s). So the State can supplement but not supersede parliamentary law: a unified national frame, with States filling residual details.
  • Shortcut: Parliament first, State second. 327 = Parliament leads. 328 = State fills the gap. Remember "328 is 327's junior".

Also in the handout

  • These safeguards aim to preserve impartiality and autonomy of the ECI in administering elections.
  • This sequence ensures uniformity and predictability in all elections across India.

2 · Articles 325, 326 and 329 in detail#

Article 325: one roll, equal franchise

  • One general electoral roll for every territorial constituency.
  • No exclusion or special roll on grounds only of religion, race, caste or sex.
  • This abolished separate electorates and communal representation. Every eligible citizen stands equal before the franchise
  • Voting rights must not be partitioned on communal or identity lines

Article 326: adult suffrage

  • The person must:
  • be a citizen of India;
  • be not less than 18 years of age on the date fixed under a law of the appropriate Legislature (changed from 21 to 18 by the 61st Amendment Act, 1988, in force 28 March 1989);
  • not be disqualified under the Constitution or any law of the appropriate Legislature on the ground of non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, or corrupt or illegal practice (the RPA lists some other disqualifications).
  • Such a person is entitled to be registered as a voter.
Handout alert

Registration is not the right to vote: Article 326 provides only for registration as a voter. It does not itself provide the right to vote or the right to get elected. By Supreme Court judgments the right to vote is a Constitutional Right. In the Rajbala case (2015), on Haryana Panchayat elections, Justice Chelameswar and Justice Abhay Manohar Sapre held that the right to vote and the right to contest a Panchayat election are Constitutional Rights.

Article 329: courts kept out

  • Laws on delimitation of constituencies and allotment of seats, and orders of the Delimitation Commission, cannot be questioned in any court. So the orders are final and elections are not stalled by endless litigation. (Delimitation itself runs under Articles 82 and 170 and Delimitation Acts passed after each Census.)
  • Elections to Parliament or State Legislatures can be questioned only by an election petition presented as the appropriate legislature prescribes (Art. 329(b)).
  • Election petitions have been tried by the High Courts since the mid-1960s, with appeals to the Supreme Court. The RPA 1951 supplies grounds, procedure and time limits.
Handout alert

Handout alert: The handout says delimitation orders cannot be questioned in court (Art. 329) but later says that, per a 2024 Supreme Court verdict, courts can now review an order that is "manifestly arbitrary or violates constitutional values". Write both: the constitutional bar and the 2024 limited review.

  • Tribunals and L. Chandra Kumar (1997): Later amendments allowed specialised tribunals (Articles 323A/323B), including for some election-related matters. In L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997) the Supreme Court held that Art. 323B, to the extent it ousted High Court review (Arts. 226/227) and Supreme Court review (Art. 32), was unconstitutional. Judicial review is a basic feature: tribunals may exist but cannot wholly exclude ordinary courts from constitutional questions.

3 · Election management: who runs the poll#

  • The Election Commission of India (ECI) is a permanent, constitutional, multi-member body (Art. 324). It has superintendence, direction and control of preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of all elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President.
  • Composition: Chief Election Commissioner and such other Election Commissioners as the President may appoint.
  • Independence: the CEC can be removed only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge.

The officer ladder

From the ECI to the booth

  • ECI:
    • Directs and supervises everything
    • Appoints field officers (in consultation with the State/UT where stated)
  • CEO:
    • Chief Electoral Officer for each State and UT
    • Oversees and co-ordinates election work
    • Principal link between ECI and sub-state officials
    • Appointed in consultation with the State/UT administration
  • DEO:
    • District Election Officer: nodal official in a district, a State Government officer nominated by the ECI in consultation with the State
    • Supervises district planning
  • RO: Returning Officer for each parliamentary and assembly constituency: conducts the election from nomination to counting
  • ERO: Electoral Registration Officer: prepares and maintains the roll for a constituency
  • Presiding Officer:
    • Runs one polling station with polling officers
    • Keeps secrecy and order
    • Returns records to DEO/RO

4 · Election procedure step by step#

  • Lok Sabha and State Assemblies normally face elections at five-year intervals. The House may be dissolved earlier if the Government loses the confidence of the Lower House and no alternative majority is possible. The ECI then runs the full machinery for voting, counting and constitution of the new House within the constitutional limits.

From schedule to new House

  • Poll schedule:
    • ECI issues the calendar a few weeks before the formal process
    • MCC becomes operative immediately and notifications are set in motion
  • Notification:
    • Invites electors to choose representatives
    • Candidates may then file nomination papers
  • Scrutiny:
    • Returning Officer examines nominations after a brief window
    • May reject defective ones after a summary hearing
  • Withdrawal: Accepted candidates may withdraw within a short prescribed period
  • Oath or affirmation: Before an officer authorised by the ECI (Returning Officer or Assistant RO), in person at presenting nomination papers or at the latest before the day of scrutiny
  • Final list and symbols:
    • Ballot shows candidate names (in prescribed languages) and allotted symbols
    • Recognised parties get their allotted symbols
  • Campaign:
    • Manifestos, rallies, literature
    • Official campaign runs from finalised list up to 48 hours before polling closes
    • MCC applies
  • Polling:
    • Secret ballot
    • Phases and multiple days for security
  • Counting:
    • EVMs opened under Returning Officers and Observers
    • largest number of valid votes wins (first-past-the-post, single-member constituencies)
  • Notification of members:
    • ECI compiles list and issues notifications
    • Only then can the President or Governor summon the new House
From schedule to new House
  • Memory aid (Pre-poll steps): Notification, Nomination, Scrutiny, Withdrawal: "Never Neglect Small Words."

Polling day details

  • Polling stations are in public institutions (schools, community halls) and kept accessible and manageable in size.
  • On arrival: name checked on the roll, identity verified, indelible ink on the left forefinger, voter slip issued, then the vote is cast (EVM ballot button) under the Presiding Officer's supervision. Stations stay open for a prescribed minimum duration.
  • EVMs replaced paper ballots progressively
  • ECI-listed advantages: no doubtful or invalid ballots, much faster counting, less paper, lower printing cost

Media, opinion polls and exit polls

  • Accredited media may witness polling and counting, subject to secrecy of the ballot.
  • The ECI regulates opinion polls and exit polls:
    • Restrictions on publishing certain opinion-poll results close to and during polling
    • exit polls only after polling ends in the final phase

Election petitions

  • If an elector or candidate believes malpractice affected the result, an election petition can be filed. It is tried by the High Court of the State
  • Success can lead to annulment of the election and a re-poll in that constituency
  • Shortcut:
    • Campaign clock. Official campaign ends 48 hours before polling closes
    • liquor sale is also barred 48 hours before poll closure (1996 reform). Two "48"s: campaign and liquor

6 · Election laws and the Model Code of Conduct#

  • Elections involve over 90 crore voters, thousands of constituencies and hundreds of parties, so laws ensure uniformity, transparency and accountability from rolls to results. The framework has four pillars:
  • Constitutional Provisions (Articles 324-329).
  • Representation of the People Acts, 1950 and 1951.
  • Delimitation Act, 2002.
  • Rules, Orders and other allied laws governing specific procedures.

Authority of the MCC

  • It is a voluntary code: if violated there is no legal backing to enforce punishment.
  • The ECI rejected making it a law: many provisions already sit in laws like the IPC and RPA 1951, and a law would keep cases in court for long, defeating the aim of immediate resolution.
  • Parties respect the punishments the Commission gives for MCC violation.

7 · Representation of the People Act, 1950#

  • Focus: the structure of elections: seats, constituency boundaries and electoral rolls. It makes sure every eligible citizen can vote and constituencies are fairly represented.

Nine key provisions

  • 1. Allocation of seats: Gives effect to Arts. 81 and 170: seats in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies among States and UTs, linked to Census population
  • 2. Delimitation: The President, in consultation with the ECI, delimits constituencies to avoid imbalance from uneven population growth
  • 3. Electoral rolls:
    • Preparation, maintenance and revision for all constituencies
    • Revised periodically and before every election to add those who turned 18 and remove ineligible names
  • 4. Qualification to register:
    • Citizen of India
    • 18 or above on the qualifying date
    • Ordinarily resident in the constituency
  • 5. Disqualification to register:
    • Unsound mind declared by a competent court
    • non-citizens
    • Persons disqualified by law for offences or corrupt practices
  • 6. Election officers:
    • CEO in every State, DEOs in each district, EROs for rolls
    • All under the ECI
  • 7. UTs in Rajya Sabha: How UT members of the Council of States are chosen
  • 8. Legislative Councils: In bicameral States, defines the local authorities (municipal bodies, panchayats, other local self-governing institutions) whose members elect Council representatives
  • 9. Bar on civil courts: Section 30: no Civil Court may decide whether a person is entitled to be registered on a roll, or question the legality of action by an ERO or roll-revision authority
Nine key provisions
  • Shortcut: RPA 1950 in one line. 1950 = Structure and rolls (seats, delimit, register). 1951 = Conduct and disputes. "Fifty builds, Fifty-one runs."
  • Memory aid (Registration test): Citizen, Age 18+, Resident (ordinarily): "Cars Always Run."

8 · Representation of the People Act, 1951#

  • In force from 17 July 1951, it is the cornerstone of the electoral process. It covers the conduct of elections from nominations to results, disputes and disqualification.

Purpose

  • Conduct of elections to Parliament and State Legislatures.
  • Qualifications and disqualifications of candidates.
  • Registration of political parties and election expenses.
  • Corrupt practices and election offences.
  • Resolving election disputes through petitions.

Qualifications and disqualifications of members

Who may sit

  • Qualifications:
    • Citizen of India
    • Age 25 (Lok Sabha and Assembly) or 30 (Rajya Sabha and Council)
    • An elector in any parliamentary constituency (for Parliament) or assembly constituency (for a State Legislature)
  • Disqualifications:
    • Office of profit under the government (unless exempted)
    • Unsound mind or undischarged insolvent
    • Conviction for certain offences or corrupt practices
    • Failure to lodge election expenses
    • Defection under the Tenth Schedule (added later)

Other features

  • Notification: once the President (Parliament) or Governor (State) issues the notification, the ECI starts the process: notification and nominations, scrutiny by Returning Officers, withdrawal, polling and counting under ECI supervision, publication of results in the official gazette.
  • Machinery: Returning Officers, Presiding and Polling Officers, Observers and Micro-Observers, all under ECI control.
  • Registration of political parties (added in 1989):
    • Party submits its constitution, office-bearers' details and membership criteria
    • By performance it may be recognised as a National Party or a State Party
  • Conduct:
    • Nomination of candidates and agents
    • Ballot, EVM and postal voting
    • Polling and counting
    • Declaration of winners
    • filing of expenses. Candidates must declare assets, liabilities and educational qualifications
  • Expenditure and free supply:

9 · Delimitation Act, 2002#

  • Uneven growth (migration, urbanisation, demographic variation) left some constituencies far larger than others. To deliver "one vote, one value", the Constitution provides readjustment of seats and boundaries after every Census. Delimitation is redrawing boundaries so each constituency has a roughly equal population.

Constitutional basis

  • Article 82: Parliament enacts a law to readjust Lok Sabha constituencies after each Census.
  • Article 170: same for State Legislative Assemblies.
  • Articles 330 and 332: reserved representation for SCs and STs, with reserved seats tied to their population share in the latest Census, linking reservation to delimitation.

Purposes of the 2002 Act (based on the 2001 Census)

  • Redefine constituency boundaries for Lok Sabha and Assemblies.
  • Re-fix reserved seats for SCs and STs in proportion to population.
  • Correct inequality from uneven population growth and migration.
  • Balance rural and urban representation.

Delimitation Commission

  • Composition:
    • Chairperson: a person who is or has been a Supreme Court Judge, appointed by the Central Government
    • The Chief Election Commissioner or an Election Commissioner nominated by the CEC, ex officio
    • The State Election Commissioner of the State concerned, ex officio
  • Functions:
    • Fix boundaries and number of constituencies in each State and UT
    • Identify SC and ST reserved constituencies
    • Publish draft proposals for objections and suggestions
    • Finalise orders after feedback
  • Memory aid (Commission members): Judge (chair), CEC or EC, State EC: "Jolly Chiefs Sing."
  • Memory aid (Guiding principles): Equal population, Administrative convenience (district and tehsil boundaries), Geographical contiguity, Community representation (SC/ST concentration): "Every Area Gets Covered."

  • The 42nd Amendment (1976), extended by the 84th Amendment (2001), froze total Lok Sabha and Assembly seats until the first Census after 2026, so States that control population growth are not penalised.

11 · History of electoral reforms and the committees#

  • Electoral reforms have been on the national agenda since the 4th general elections (1967). Regional parties formed after 1967, India became a true multi-party democracy, competition rose and so did unethical practices. Challenges in each decade: money and muscle power, criminalisation of politics, voter apathy, digital information manipulation. Reform is a continuing necessity, not a one-time exercise. Electoral reforms are continuous efforts to improve the legal, administrative and procedural side of elections so they stay free, fair, transparent, inclusive and credible. They include:
  • Updating electoral laws and institutions.
  • New technology such as EVMs and VVPATs.
  • Financial and ethical transparency.
  • Wider participation through NOTA, NRI voting and postal ballots.

Tarkunde Committee (1974, set up by Jayprakash Narayan)

  • Fix the strength of the ECI at 3.
  • Reduce voting age to 18.
  • Voter's councils in every polling booth area to ensure fairness.
  • Committee-based appointment of the ECI: Prime Minister, Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha, and Chief Justice of India. The EC's independence is of prime importance.

16 · Electoral finances and state funding#

Why finance reform is needed

  • Money gives electoral advantage: wider and repeated outreach.
  • Crorepati candidates:
    • 2009 Lok Sabha 16%
    • 2014 27%. Crorepati MPs: 442 in the 16th Lok Sabha
    • 475 in the 17th
  • Bribing voters:
    • Black money
    • Creates incentives to be corrupt once elected
  • Crony capitalism: big corporations donate expecting favours (quid pro quo).
  • Violates equal participation: big donors benefit more from government policy than common people.
  • Large donations are institutionalised corruption: parties shape economic positions to attract funding.

Corporate donations

  • Justice Louis Brandeis: "We can have democracy or we can have great wealth concentrated in a few hands."
  • Economic inequality leads to political inequality. As elections get costlier, parties depend on the super-rich (0.1% of the population).
  • Link between donations and policy: Germany is the only EU country allowing outdoor cigarette advertisements as major parties get donations from tobacco companies. In India, the corporate tax rate has come down significantly.
  • USA: super-rich share of political donations rose from 15% (1980) to 70% (2016), while the bottom 50% saw income and corporate taxes fall significantly.
  • France banned corporate donations in 1995 and capped individual contributions at 6,000 Euro. Chile and Brazil banned corporate donations recently.

State funding of elections

  • The State provides funds to parties on the eve of elections instead of corporates.

Electoral bonds

Life of an electoral bond

  • Issue:
    • SBI issues interest-free bearer instruments for anonymous donations to parties
    • Anyone can buy them
  • Denominations: Rs 1,000, 10,000, 1 lakh, 10 lakh, 1 crore
  • 15 days: The party must encash it in its account within 15 days
  • Unencashed: SBI deposits the amount in the PMRF (Prime Minister's Relief Fund)
  • Verdict: Struck down in February 2024 by the Supreme Court for violating voters' right to information, ending a seven-year era of largely opaque political funding

Issues with the scheme

  • Disclosure gaps:
    • Parties need not disclose money raised through bonds so one cannot know which corporate house gains
    • Donor and amount unknown even to parties, though SBI works under the government so information may be accessed wrongfully, giving the ruling party the upper hand
  • Wider harms:
    • Company shareholders do not know donation details
    • Ruling party at distinct advantage
    • Black money funnelled through shell companies
    • Taxpayers completely in the dark

    • Tankha Committee (2010)
    • J.S. Verma Committee (2013)
    • Law Commission 244th (2014) and 255th (2015)
  • 1996 and later details: insulting the National Flag, the Constitution or preventing the National Anthem (Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971) bars contesting for 6 years. The 1997 President and Vice-President rule raised proposers from 10 to 50 and from 5 to 20, and the deposit from Rs 2,500 to Rs 15,000. In 2009 Lok Sabha deposits rose from Rs 10,000 to Rs 25,000 (SC/ST Rs 5,000 to Rs 12,500).
  • 2003 changes: candidates must give criminal and asset details in an affidavit, and false information is an electoral offence. Parties may take any contribution from a person or company (not a government company) and must report amounts above Rs 20,000. Travel costs of leaders campaigning for a party are not counted in a candidate's expenses.

    • Even if NOTA gets more votes than any candidate, the candidate with the most votes is still declared elected. VVPAT slips stay visible for seven seconds

Recall sheet#

Cover the right column and answer. Same questions as the flashcards.

Which Articles form Part XV on elections?
Articles 324 to 329 (and the repealed 329A).
What does Article 325 provide?
One general electoral roll per territorial constituency; no exclusion on grounds of religion, race, caste or sex.
What does Article 326 provide?
Adult suffrage: citizens aged 18 and above, subject to disqualification for non-residence, unsound mind, crime or corrupt or illegal practice.
Which Amendment lowered the voting age from 21 to 18?
The 61st Amendment Act, 1988; in force 28 March 1989.
What is the difference between Articles 327 and 328?
327: Parliament's power to make election laws. 328: State Legislature's power, only where Parliament has made no provision.
What can challenge an election result?
Only an election petition (Art. 329(b)), tried by the High Court with appeal to the Supreme Court.
Name the five election officers from State to booth.
CEO, DEO, Returning Officer, Electoral Registration Officer, Presiding Officer.
Three types of ECI observers?
General, Expenditure and Police Observers.
When does official campaigning end?
48 hours before polling closes.
Four stages of the MCC's history?
1960 Kerala; 1962 circulated; 1979 amplified; 1991 consolidated and strictly enforced.
Area of MCC enforcement?
Lok Sabha: whole country. Assembly: whole State. By-election: the district of the constituency.
Why did the ECI reject making MCC a law?
Many provisions already exist in IPC and RPA 1951, and court cases would be slow and defeat quick resolution.
What does RPA 1950 do?
Allocates seats, delimits constituencies, prepares electoral rolls, sets officers; Section 30 bars civil courts on roll entitlement.
Age limits to contest under RPA 1951?
25 for Lok Sabha and Assembly; 30 for Rajya Sabha and Council.
List the RPA 1951 corrupt practices.
Bribery and undue influence; religion or caste for votes; false statements about candidates; booth capturing or tampering; exceeding expenditure limits.
Who sits on the Delimitation Commission?
A Supreme Court Judge (chair), the CEC or an EC, and the State Election Commissioner.
Principles of delimitation?
Equal population, administrative convenience, geographical contiguity, community representation.
Which Amendments froze seats and used the 2001 Census?
42nd (1976) and 84th (2001) froze seats; 87th (2003) used 2001 Census for boundaries.
Symbols Order year and a key rule?
1968; recognised parties get reserved symbols, independents get free symbols.
What did Tarkunde Committee (1974) recommend?
ECI of 3; voting age 18; voter's councils; selection committee of PM, Leader of Opposition and CJI.
What did Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990) recommend?
Voter ID; lower contesting ages (25 for RS, 21 for LS); ban transfers; higher deposits; standing committee; 2 years jail for MCC violation.
Law Commission 255th Report on petitions?
Trial in 6 months; judgment in 90 days; appeals to Supreme Court only on law.
What did the 1996 campaign reform change?
Campaign period cut from 20 to 14 days.
Presidential and VP nominations (1997)?
50 proposers for President, 20 for Vice-President; deposit Rs 15,000.
What did NOTA come from?
A 2013 Supreme Court directive (handout also credits PUCL 2003).
What did Subramanian Swamy v. ECI (2013) uphold?
The VVPAT system.
What does the CEC and Other ECs Act, 2023 provide?
Selection panel of PM, Leader of Opposition and a Minister; six-year tenure or age 65; criticised for limiting independence.
What did ADR (2020) direct?
Parties must justify and publish reasons for fielding candidates with criminal cases within 48 hours.
Electoral bond details?
SBI issued; Rs 1,000 to 1 crore; encash within 15 days or amount goes to PMRF; struck down Feb 2024.
What did the Law Commission say on state funding?
Partial state funding in kind is recommended (170th and 255th) but it also set preconditions: de-criminalisation, inner-party democracy, transparency.

Traps#

Trap 1 · Art. 326 gives registration, not the right to vote Art. 326 provides only for registration as a voter and does not itself give the right to vote or to be elected. Supreme Court judgments (for example Rajbala, 2015) call the right to vote a Constitutional Right. Rule: Art. 326 = adult suffrage and registration; the right to vote comes from the courts' reading.
Trap 2 · EVM years The handout gives different EVM years in different places. The reform table says legalised 1989, first tested 1998, fully used in Goa 1999. Rule: for years use 1989 (law), 1998 (test), 1999 (Goa); treat the early 2000s as general background.
Trap 3 · NOTA 2003 or 2013, and the two ADR cases The case table lists PUCL as 2003 but the reform table dates NOTA to 2013. ADR 2002 is about disclosure of candidate details; ADR 2020 is about justifying criminal candidates within 48 hours. Rule: PUCL (2003) case, NOTA reform 2013; ADR 2002 = disclosure, ADR 2020 = 48-hour justification.
Trap 4 · Delimitation orders and the seat freeze Art. 329 bars courts from questioning delimitation orders, yet a 2024 Supreme Court verdict allows review of an order that is manifestly arbitrary. The 42nd Amendment started the seat freeze and the 84th extended it, on the 1971 population. Rule: write both the constitutional bar and the 2024 limited review; freeze = 42nd (1976) then 84th (2001), 1971 population; 87th (2003) uses the 2001 Census for boundaries.
