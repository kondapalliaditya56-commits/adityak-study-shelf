2.1 · Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT): 10 years#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

India's Flexible Inflation Targeting framework completed 10 years.

  • FIT: RBI uses interest rates to keep inflation within a publicly announced range while supporting growth. Legal basis: RBI Act, 1934 amended in 2016.
  • Centre, in consultation with the RBI, sets the target every five years, based on headline CPI. Retained at 4% with a +/-2% band for April 2026 to March 2031. Inflation outside the band for three consecutive quarters is a failure. The MPC sets the repo rate.
  • Results: average CPI inflation fell from 8.1% (2006-16) to 4.6% (2016-26); average GDP growth 6.8% to 7%. Inflation stayed within the band three-fourths of the time in 2016-21 and two-thirds in 2021-26.
  • RBI Discussion Paper (August 2025): headline vs core (food and fuel are over 37% of the consumption basket; only Uganda targets core); 4% still right (advanced economies about 2%, major developing 3-6%); band should be guided by threshold inflation (upper) and the rate below which low inflation discourages production (lower).
  • Concerns: weak transmission to lending rates; flat Phillips curve (2012-2026) so rate hikes may cost jobs with little fall in inflation, raising stagflation risk; about 90% of workforce informal; inflation mostly supply-driven (crop failure, heatwaves, crude).
  • Way forward: Government absorbs supply shocks, RBI prevents second-round effects; extend benchmark-linked rates to NBFCs and microfinance; bring inflation closer to 4%; cold chains, warehousing, APMC reform, rule-based buffer stock release.
Prelims fodder
  • MPC: statutory body under the RBI Act, 2016; 6 members (3 RBI, 3 Government nominees); majority vote; Governor has the casting vote.
  • Headline inflation = total; core inflation excludes food and energy.
  • Phillips curve: inverse relation between inflation and unemployment. Stagflation: high inflation, stagnation and unemployment together.
Mains fodder
  • Urjit Patel Committee (2014): CPI as nominal anchor, 4% target with +/-2% band, committee-based decisions.
  • Keywords: Price stability; Credible nominal anchor; Inflation expectations; Flat Phillips curve; Supply-side inflation

2017 Prelims: MPC decides the benchmark rates; it is not a 12-member body and not chaired by the Finance Minister.

2.2 · Surge in inflows under RBI's forex swap facility#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

Inflows under the RBI special USD-INR swap facility reached $143.596 billion (data to 18 September 2026).

  • RBI introduced special USD-INR swap facilities in June 2026 for fresh FCNR(B) deposits and specified ECBs and OFCBs. FCNR(B) window: fresh deposits with tenor 3 to 5 years; closed in August 2026.
  • Swap is two-legged: spot leg (banks give dollars, RBI gives rupees) and reverse leg at maturity.
  • Other RBI steps: dollar sales and sell-buy swaps; special dollar window for IOCL, BPCL, HPCL; restrictions on forex derivatives (exposure threshold cut from $100 million to $5 million; cancelled contracts cannot be rebooked); Forex Risk Reserve (20% requirement on specified derivatives above $2 million); non-deliverable rupee contracts only for genuine hedging.
  • Why inflows matter: rupee stability, reserves, financing the CAD, import security (crude, fertiliser), lower imported inflation (but excessive appreciation hurts exports).
  • Concerns: oil import dependence; volatile capital flows (US yields); rupee weakened beyond Rs 90 per dollar in early 2026; nearly $133 billion of the $143.596 billion came from FCNR(B), a temporary buffer with repayment obligations; surplus liquidity of Rs 11.16 lakh crore in September 2026.
  • Way forward: diversify energy imports; stable long-term FDI and deeper bond markets; exchange-rate flexibility with targeted intervention; reserve adequacy checks; sterilisation through VRRR, SDF and OMO.
Prelims fodder
  • Reserves: Foreign Currency Assets, Gold, SDRs, Reserve Tranche Position in IMF.
  • FCNR(B) and ECBs operate under FEMA, 1999.
Mains fodder
  • Impossible Trinity: free capital mobility, managed exchange rate and monetary autonomy cannot all be held.
  • 2013 taper tantrum: FCNR(B) and overseas-borrowing swap windows mobilised about $34.3 billion.
  • Keywords: Sterilised intervention; Reserve adequacy; Monetary trilemma; Debt-creating inflows; Rollover risk

2.3 · Global bond sell-off and rising sovereign yields#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

Long-term yields in the US, UK, France and Japan hit multi-decade highs on inflation worries and fiscal pressure.

  • US 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.34%, highest since 2002, with the sharpest quarterly rise since 1994 in Q3 2026. UK 30-year gilt crossed 6%, highest since 1998. France and Japan yields also rose.
  • Bond prices and yields move in opposite directions. Causes: persistent inflation, high interest-rate expectations, fiscal pressure (more bond supply), higher energy prices.
  • Why it matters: costlier government borrowing, less fiscal space, higher corporate and household rates, pressure on equities, risk to banks and pension funds.
  • For India: FPI outflow risk, rupee pressure, imported inflation, higher G-sec yields, tougher balance for RBI.
Prelims fodder
  • 1 basis point = 0.01 percentage point.
  • Real yield = nominal yield adjusted for expected inflation. Yield curve = yields across maturities. Quantitative easing = central bank asset purchases. Fisher effect: nominal yield = real yield + expected inflation.
Mains fodder
  • Structural shift away from the post-2008 era of very low rates and QE; global spillover to emerging economies even without domestic rate hikes.
  • Keywords: Real yield; Yield curve; Imported inflation; Quantitative easing; Fiscal space
PYQ connect

2021 Prelims: Indian G-sec yields are influenced by US Fed actions, RBI actions, and inflation and short-term rates (all three).

2.4 · Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) 2024-25#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

MoSPI released ASI results for FY 2024-25.

  • Conducted by NSO under MoSPI; collection under the Collection of Statistics Act, 2008 (amended 2017). Covers factories under the Factories Act, 1948, bidi and cigar units, non-CEA electricity undertakings, and units with 100+ employees on the Business Register. Factory threshold used: 10+ workers with power or 20+ without. OSH Code 2020 raises the licence threshold to 20 with power and 40 without.
  • Top factories: Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Maharashtra. Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh and Haryana account for about 56% of manufacturing employment. Top GVA states: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh. Top GVA industries: basic metals, motor vehicles, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, food products. Top employers: food products, textiles, basic metals, motor vehicles.
  • Positives: employment growth 7.19% (5.92% last year); GVA 9.59% with input costs (7.47%) slower than output (7.81%); wages 12.08% faster than GVA and profits (7.73%); fixed capital 10.54% (capital deepening).
  • Concerns: regional and sectoral concentration (five industries over 45% of GVA); capital growing faster than output and jobs (capital intensity may limit jobs).
  • Way forward: reward domestic value addition in PLI; industrial corridors and plug-and-play parks in lagging states; support labour-intensive sectors (food processing, textiles, footwear, toys); skilling and MSME technology.
Prelims fodder
  • ASI = annual structure of registered manufacturing; IIP = short-term volume changes; ASUSE = unincorporated non-agricultural enterprises.
Mains fodder
  • Examples: PLI and mobile phones; Tamil Nadu dispersed industrialisation and women in factory jobs. China and Vietnam manufacturing about 25% of GDP.
  • Keywords: Capital deepening; Labour-intensive manufacturing; Regional industrial imbalance; Productivity-wage nexus

2.5 · District Mineral Foundations (DMF) and PMKKKY#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

The CAG flagged irregularities in DMF functioning and PMKKKY implementation in Odisha.

  • DMF: non-profit trust set up by States in every mining-affected district. Legal basis: Section 9B of the MMDR Act, 1957 (inserted by the 2015 Amendment). Funding: 30% of royalty (leases before 12 January 2015) or 10% (auction leases after that date). Two tiers: Governing Council and Managing Committee. Guided by the Fifth and Sixth Schedules, PESA 1996 and FRA 2006.
  • Status: DMFs in 656 districts of 23 States; over Rs 1.30 lakh crore collected; top States Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh.
  • PMKKKY (2015, Ministry of Mines): implemented by DMFs using DMF funds. At least 70% to high-priority sectors (drinking water, pollution control); up to 30% to other priority sectors (infrastructure, irrigation); at least 70% of funds in directly affected areas.
  • CAG concerns: diversion to ineligible items (wage payments under MGNREGS, government vehicles); no perspective or annual plans; endowment funds not created; about 54% fund utilisation; no social audits.
  • Way forward: rule-based allocation; perspective and annual plans; link DMF portals with WAMIS; collect dues with penal interest; mandatory social audits.
Prelims fodder
  • MMDR Act, 1957: Union regulates major minerals; States regulate minor minerals (sand, gravel) by rules. National Mineral Exploration and Development Trust set up under the 2015 Amendment.
Mains fodder
  • Principle: those who bear the costs of mining should share its benefits. Cases: Project SAMARTHYA (Gujarat, youth training), Project Aangan-Chatra (Jharkhand, 40 anganwadis).
  • Keywords: District Mineral Foundation; Royalty; Social audit; Endowment fund

2.6 · Western Dedicated Freight Corridor (WDFC) completed#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

The PM inaugurated the final sections of the WDFC, completing the 2,843-km DFC network (Eastern and Western).

  • India became the first country to run double-stack container trains under overhead electric traction on a dedicated freight corridor.
  • Western DFC 1,506 km: Dadri (UP) to JNPT (Maharashtra), JICA yen loans. Eastern DFC 1,337 km: Ludhiana (Punjab) to Sonnagar (Bihar), partly World Bank financed (initially planned to Dankuni, West Bengal). Implementing agency: DFCCIL (SPV under the Ministry of Railways, established 2006 under the Companies Act).
  • Union Budget 2026-27 proposed an East-West DFC from Dankuni (West Bengal) to Surat (Gujarat); other proposed: East Coast, North-South.
  • Significance: rail freight about Rs 1.90 per tonne-km against up to Rs 3.78 by road; decongests passenger lines; supports DMIC and Amritsar-Kolkata corridor; emissions 22 g vs 62 g CO2 per tonne-km (rail vs road); supports PM GatiShakti.
  • Gaps: terminal congestion and stabling; PPP failure (Sonnagar-Dankuni); average DFC speed 37 km/h in 2024-25 (38 in 2023-24); holding line gaps.
  • Way forward: fast-track future corridors; last-mile multimodal links (MMLPs, ICDs); private freight terminals; IoT tracking and predictive maintenance.
Prelims fodder
  • National Rail Plan Vision 2030: raise rail freight modal share to 45%. Modal share now: rail 18%, road 71% (NITI Aayog, 2021). Services: Trucks on Train, FOIS.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Freight decongestion; Logistics cost; PM GatiShakti; National Logistics Policy

2.7 · Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Amendment Rules, 2026#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

The Department of Consumer Affairs notified amendments to the 2020 E-Commerce Rules; in force from January 2027.

  • Complaints: acknowledged in 48 hours and resolved in a month (continues); consumer gets a copy of the recorded complaint; every e-commerce entity must become a National Consumer Helpline (NCH) convergence partner.
  • Search and listings: no manipulation of search results; main ranking factors explained in order of importance; sponsored listings clearly marked.
  • Price cuts must show the prior price, meaning the lowest price in the preceding 30 days.
  • Dark patterns: follow the 2023 Guidelines; yearly self-audit and a prominently displayed compliance certificate.
  • Disclosures: country of origin, best-before date, return-shipping cost, delivery and payment terms; seller GSTIN or MSME registration. Express consent needed for promotional use of consumer information.
  • Need: online shoppers about 290-300 million; about 29% of NCH complaints in 2025 were e-commerce (5.11 lakh of 17.72 lakh).
  • Issues: self-audit may not be enough; enforcement at scale; overlap with competition, data protection (DPDP Act 2023, Rules 2025) and legal metrology; burden on small businesses.
  • Way forward: CCPA audit standards and continuous compliance; automated and risk-based supervision; coordination among CCPA, CCI, MeitY and Legal Metrology.
Prelims fodder
  • Marketplace model: platform facilitates transactions; inventory model: entity owns and sells the stock.
  • CCPA: Section 10, Consumer Protection Act, 2019; set up 2020, New Delhi.
  • CCI Market Study (2020) flagged platform neutrality, price parity, deep discounts.
  • EU also uses a 30-day lowest-price benchmark.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Dark patterns; Choice architecture; Information asymmetry; Platform accountability
PYQ connect

2022 Prelims: foreign-owned e-commerce firms may not sell own goods as inventory and are limited in owning large sellers.

2.8 · DILRMP 3.0 (2026-31)#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

The Ministry of Rural Development issued Operational Guidelines for DILRMP 3.0 (2026-2031).

  • History: NLRMP (2008-16) from merging CLR and SRA&ULR; DILRMP (2016-26) as a Central Sector Scheme with Bhu-Aadhaar (ULPIN) and NGDRS. DILRMP 3.0: Central Sector Scheme, outlay Rs 565.50 crore over FY 2026-27 to 2030-31, run by the Department of Land Resources; shift from digitisation to an integrated, interoperable GIS-based Digital Public Infrastructure.
  • Achievements: 99.90% Records of Rights digitised, 97% cadastral maps, 99% Sub-Registrar Offices computerised.
  • Pillars: Land Stack (federated, links cadastral maps, RoR, registration and court cases through APIs); Bhu-Aadhaar (14-digit geospatial ID for every parcel); Registration Seva Kendras (75 high-footfall SROs, like Passport Seva Kendras); NAKSHA (urban survey, urban property cards).
  • Need: over 60% of civil litigation is land related and takes about 20 years; fragmented databases; unclear titles delay projects; small farmers lack verifiable titles for loans.
  • Challenges: digitally signed RoRs not legally valid in all States; presumptive titling, not guaranteed title; legacy errors; NAKSHA still at pilot stage.
  • Way forward: legal validity for digital RoRs; conclusive titling (Rajan committee on Financial Sector Reforms; NITI Aayog draft Model Land Titling Act with Land Titling Authority and Appellate Tribunals); verify legacy records; auto-triggered mutation after registration.
Prelims fodder
  • SVAMITVA (2020, Ministry of Panchayati Raj): property cards for village abadi areas. Cadastral map: large-scale map of land parcel boundaries and ownership. Mutation: updating land records after sale, inheritance, partition or court order.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Bhu-Aadhaar (ULPIN); Land Stack; Digital Public Infrastructure; Conclusive titling

2.9.1 · MDR on high-value UPI merchant payments#

PrelimsEconomy

NPCI announced a Merchant Discount Rate on select high-value person-to-merchant (P2M) UPI payments under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. UPI stays free for P2P.

  • MDR: fee a merchant pays its bank for accepting digital payments, as a percentage of value; shared among acquiring bank, issuing bank, UPI app and NPCI.
  • Framework: P2P free (any amount); P2M up to Rs 2,000 free; above Rs 2,000 0.4%; capped at Rs 300 for Rs 75,000 and above; essential sectors (railways, telecom, insurance, fuel, utilities) above Rs 2,000 flat Rs 5.
  • A Small Merchant Fund from part of MDR revenue for Tier 3-6 centres, North-East, J&K, Ladakh, and PM SVANidhi and PM Vishwakarma beneficiaries. Merchants cannot pass MDR to customers.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: MDR; P2M; PSS Act 2007; UPI sustainability

2.9.3 · Double deflation in GDP estimation#

PrelimsEconomy

Q1 FY27 real GDP grew 7.8% and nominal GDP 10.3%, an implicit GDP deflator of about 2.3%.

  • GDP deflator: change in prices of all domestically produced goods and services, from nominal and real GDP.
  • The new 2022-23 base series makes wider use of double deflation: real GVA estimated by deflating output and intermediate inputs separately (GVA = output minus intermediate inputs), more accurate when their prices move differently, especially in manufacturing.

2.9.4 · Mega Ports notified under the Indian Ports Act, 2025#

PrelimsEconomy

Deendayal (Kandla), Jawaharlal Nehru (Nhava Sheva), Paradip and Mundra were notified as mega ports under Section 73 of the Indian Ports Act, 2025.

  • Eligibility: major and non-major ports (State consulted for non-major). Cargo threshold: at least 150 MMT in the previous year or 7.5 million TEUs. Validity: five years.
  • Deendayal: Gulf of Kutch, largest major port by cargo (2025-26), tidal port. Mundra: Gulf of Kutch, private (Adani Ports), non-major. JNPT: Navi Mumbai, most containerised trade. Paradip: Odisha (Mahanadi delta), only mega port on the east coast.
  • India: 14 major ports (12 operating) and over 200 non-major ports. Major ports: Union List, Major Port Authorities Act, 2021. Non-major: Concurrent List, State Maritime Boards. Ports carry about 95% of trade by volume and 70% by value.
Mains fodder
  • Keywords: Mega port; Indian Ports Act 2025; TEU

2.9.5 · Districts as Export Hubs (DEH)#

Prelims + MainsEconomy

The Districts as Export Hubs initiative now covers over 770 districts. India's goods and services exports were US$ 825.25 billion in 2024-25.

  • Aim: turn every district into an export hub by finding export-potential products and services, removing bottlenecks, improving market access. Nodal: Department of Commerce; implemented by DGFT; institutionalised under FTP 2023. A convergence framework using existing Central and State schemes.
  • ODOP vs DEH: ODOP focuses on a distinctive product (branding, promotion); DEH on the whole district export ecosystem (products, services, logistics, standards, market access) through Centre-State-District convergence.
  • Focused implementation phase from June 2026 in 27 States/UTs with 24 DGFT regional authorities; focus on exporter activation, capacity building and export growth.

6.1.1 · UK recognises India's CCTS under UK CBAM#

Prelims + MainsEnvironment

The UK recognised India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme as a qualifying overseas carbon-pricing scheme under its Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism.

  • UK CBAM applies from 1 January 2027 to carbon-intensive imports (iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen); charge based on embedded emissions; carbon price paid in the exporting country can be credited.
  • CCTS: market-based mechanism creating the Indian Carbon Market with tradable Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs); notified June 2023 under the Energy Conservation Act 2001 as amended in 2022; targets emission intensity in energy-intensive industries and India's NDC.
  • Institutions: Central Government; National Steering Committee (NSCICM); BEE as administrator (rules, targets, accreditation, issues CCCs); Grid India (registry); CERC (regulates trading on power exchanges); Accredited Carbon Verification Agencies. Obligated entities meet targets or buy CCCs; offset mechanism lets non-obligated projects earn CCCs.
  • Challenges: relief conditional on proof and verification; only carbon price actually paid counts (not free allowances, rebates); compliance burden on MSMEs; UK can review recognition. Way forward: stronger MRV, credible price signal, MSME support, policy stability.
  • India's first soil carbon payments (September 2026): over Rs 2.9 crore by DBT to 2,550 small farmers in Punjab and Haryana; Aadi Programme (Grow Indigo since 2019, ICAR support); Verra VM0042 methodology.

1.2 · Defining Urban Areas in India#

Prelims + MainsPolity

MoHUA told a parliamentary panel that census-town criteria do not capture the scale of urbanisation. The Registrar General of India will keep the existing framework for Census 2027.

  • Statutory towns: notified under law by the State/UT, irrespective of census thresholds; they have municipalities, corporations or cantonment boards.
  • Census towns must meet all three: population at least 5,000; density at least 400 persons/km2; 75% of the male main working population in non-agricultural work. They may stay under rural local governance but count as urban in Census statistics.
  • Measures differ: national definition 31.2% (2011), UN estimate 35.7% (2025); Agglomeration Index 55.3% (2010) lived in urban-like areas; DEGURBA (UN-endorsed, 1 km2 grids, rural-urban continuum).
  • Limits: male-only occupational test excludes women; large revenue-village boundaries lower density; uniform thresholds miss small hill and north-eastern settlements; no commuting or functional linkage test.
  • Effects: hidden urbanisation; governance mismatch (rural bodies facing drainage, waste, land use needs); planning and funding gaps in peri-urban zones; political economy of reclassification (fear of losing rural scheme benefits, new municipal taxes).
Prelims fodder
  • Peri-urban area: rural-urban transition zone, also called rurban.
  • RGI is an office under the MHA.
  • Agglomeration Index was developed for the World Bank World Development Report.
  • Other countries: Canada (1,000 people and 400/km2), USA (2,000 housing units or 5,000 people).
Mains fodder
  • Way forward: review the male-only test and thresholds; use grids, satellite mapping and commuting data; clear procedure for statutory recognition with staff and finance; transition funding, including the Sixteenth Finance Commission Urbanisation Premium; coordinate municipal and panchayat plans.
  • Line to use: India's cities are growing faster than its definitions; an outdated definition is a governance deficit.
  • Keywords: Hidden urbanisation; Census town; Peri-urban belts; Urbanisation Premium

PrelimsSociety & Social Justice

NSO released district-level PLFS labour indicators for the first time. PLFS design revised in January 2025: districts replaced NSS Regions as sampling basis; survey year moved from July-June to calendar year.
