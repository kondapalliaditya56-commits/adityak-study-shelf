In simple words, key terms and memory words#

In simple words

  • The Election Commission (EC) is a permanent, independent body under Article 324. It holds the superintendence, direction and control of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President.
  • Panchayat and Municipality elections are not under it
  • They belong to separate State Election Commissions (73rd and 74th Amendments)
  • It has a Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) plus other ECs fixed by the President. Since October 1993 it has been a three-member body. Tenure is 6 years or age 65, whichever is earlier
  • Pay equals a Supreme Court Judge
  • Disagreements are settled by majority
  • The CEC can be removed only like an SC Judge. Other ECs and Regional Commissioners can be removed only on the CEC's recommendation.
  • Its work is administrative (rolls, schedule, Model Code of Conduct), advisory (disqualification, President's Rule) and quasi-judicial (party recognition and symbols).
  • On the ground: CEO (state), DEO (district), Returning Officer (constituency), Presiding Officer (booth).

Key terms

Regional Commissioners
Appointed by the President, after consulting the EC, to assist the Commission

1 · Overview: Article 324 and what it covers#

  • The Election Commission is a constitutionally established, permanent and independent authority created to safeguard the integrity of the electoral process.
  • Article 324 vests it with superintendence, direction and control of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President.
  • It is an all-India body, working uniformly across the Union and the States for these elections.

Local bodies are separate

  • Elections to Panchayats and Municipalities do not fall under Article 324.
  • The Constitution provides separate State Election Commissions, constituted by the respective State Governments, to supervise and conduct local body elections.
  • So Article 324 gives a unified national framework for major offices, while local government elections follow a decentralised, state-specific mechanism.

ECI versus State Election Commission

  • Election Commission of India:
    • Article 324
    • Elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President, Vice-President
    • All-India body
  • State Election Commission:
    • Constituted by the State Government
    • Elections to Panchayats and Municipalities
    • Outside Article 324
  • Memory aid (What ECI runs): Parliament, State Legislatures, President, Vice-President: "People Select President and VP."
  • Shortcut: ECI or SEC. Top four offices = ECI. Panchayats and Municipalities = State Election Commission.

2 · Structure and composition#

Constitutional provisions (Article 324)

  • The Commission has a Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and such number of other Election Commissioners (ECs) as the President determines from time to time.
  • The President appoints both the CEC and the other ECs.
  • When other ECs are appointed, the CEC automatically acts as Chairperson.
  • The President may appoint Regional Commissioners, when required, after consulting the Election Commission.
  • The President specifies the service conditions and tenure of ECs and Regional Commissioners.

Evolution of membership

How the Commission grew

  • 1950 to 15 Oct 1989: Single-member body: only the CEC
  • 16 Oct 1989: Two additional ECs appointed to handle more work after the voting age fell from 21 to 18
  • January 1990: Posts of the two ECs abolished: back to a single member
  • October 1993: Two ECs appointed again: a three-member body ever since
How the Commission grew
  • Shortcut: Membership counts. 1 (1950-89), 3 (Oct 1989 to Jan 1990), 1 (1990-93), 3 (since Oct 1993).

One lone CEC, a trio, one lone CEC, then a trio for good. (Memory aid, not from the handout.)

Tenure, status and decisions

  • The CEC and the other ECs:
  • have equal powers in all operational and administrative matters;
  • receive identical salaries, allowances and privileges equal to a Judge of the Supreme Court;
  • serve six years or until age 65, whichever is earlier;
  • may resign at any time or be removed before completing their tenure as per constitutional provisions.
  • When Commissioners differ in opinion, decisions are taken by majority.
  • National Voter's Day: In 2011, 25 January was declared "National Voter's Day", to mark the Election Commission's foundation day.
  • Memory aid (Article 324 composition): CEC plus ECs, President appoints, Regional Commissioners, Service conditions by President: "Commissioners Picked, Regions Served."

3 · Safeguards for the autonomy of the Commission#

  • Article 324 builds protections to insulate the Commission from executive interference and keep public confidence.

Three safeguards

SafeguardWhat it means
1. Security of tenure of the CECCannot be removed except in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court: removal by the President after both Houses of Parliament pass a resolution by a special majority on proven misbehaviour or incapacity. Though appointed by the President, the CEC does not hold office at the President's pleasure
2. Non-diminution of service conditionsThe CEC's pay, allowances or other service benefits cannot be reduced to his or her disadvantage after appointment
3. Removal of other functionariesAn Election Commissioner or Regional Commissioner cannot be removed except on the recommendation of the CEC. An internal check on arbitrary executive action
Safeguards for the Commission's autonomy
SET
  • SSecurity of tenure: the CEC is removed like a Supreme Court Judge
  • EECs and Regional Commissioners removed only on the CEC's recommendation
  • TTerms of service of the CEC cannot be reduced after appointment

These three safeguards SET the Commission free of the executive. The order is rearranged for memory. (Memory aid, not from the handout.)

Removing the CEC

  • Misbehaviour or incapacity proven: Grounds as for an SC Judge
  • Resolution in both Houses: Special majority
  • President orders removal: Only after both Houses act

Limitations (gaps in the design)

  • The Constitution gives no formal qualifications (legal, administrative, educational or judicial) for appointment.
  • It does not fix a definitive term of office for members (beyond the age and tenure norms provided elsewhere).
  • There is no bar on retiring Election Commissioners being given other offices by the government.
  • These gaps are seen as weaknesses against perceptions of executive influence.
  • Memory aid (Safeguards): Tenure like an SC Judge, Non-diminution of service conditions, Removal of ECs only on CEC recommendation: "The Nation Relies on it."
  • Memory aid (Gaps): Qualifications not specified, Term not fixed, Post-retirement jobs not barred: "Quiet Time Passes."
  • Shortcut: CEC versus EC protection. CEC = removed like an SC Judge. EC and Regional Commissioners = removed only on the CEC's recommendation.

Also in the handout

  • This non-diminution clause ensures that once appointed the CEC cannot be penalised by retrospective or subsequent reduction in pay, allowances or other service benefits.
  • These gaps have been identified as potential weaknesses in the statute-based architecture for insulating electoral administration from perceptions of executive influence.
Three gaps in Article 324's design
NOT
  • NNo qualifications specified in the Constitution
  • OOffices after retirement: no bar on later appointments
  • TTerm of office not definitively fixed

4 · Powers, responsibilities and functions#

  • Authority is exercised through three categories: administrative, advisory and quasi-judicial.

Three kinds of function

CategoryWhat the Commission does
AdministrativeSupervises the electoral machinery. Determines the territorial extent of constituencies under the Delimitation Commission Act of Parliament. Prepares and updates electoral rolls. Fixes and publishes the election schedule, scrutinises nomination papers, and cancels polls for serious irregularities such as rigging, booth-capturing or violence. Requisitions staff from the Centre or state (through the President or Governors). Appoints returning and presiding officers at district and booth level. Organises broadcast time for parties on radio and TV. Issues and enforces the Model Code of Conduct
AdvisoryAdvises the President on disqualification of MPs and advises Governors on disqualification of members of state legislatures. Advises whether elections can be held in a State under President's Rule (emergency extension considerations). Recognises political parties and recommends classification as national or state parties based on electoral performance
Quasi-judicialSettles disputes on recognition of parties and allocation of election symbols. Appoints officers and inquiry bodies to investigate irregularities. Enforces election laws and its own orders through due procedure. Manages the India International Institute of Democracy and Election Management (set up in 2011)
Three kinds of EC functions
AAQ

Act Advise Quell

  • AAdministrative: rolls, schedule, Model Code of Conduct
  • AAdvisory: disqualification, President's Rule
  • QQuasi-judicial: party recognition and symbol disputes

The EC acts to run polls, advises the President and Governors, and quells disputes over parties and symbols. No good single word exists for this list, so this is a sentence aid; the order of items is free. (Memory aid, not from the handout.)

Registration, symbols and party status

  • The Commission registers political parties. Based on poll performance, parties get national or state party status.
  • Recognition affects entitlement to reserved symbols, broadcast time and other privileges.
  • It decides election symbols and adjudicates disputes, under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968.
  • National party symbols cannot be used by another party anywhere in India. State party symbols can be used in states other than the one where the party is recognised as a state party.
  • Detailed powers are in the Representation of the People Act (RPA).
  • Plenary power: Gill vs. CEC (1977): The Commission has plenary power: it can use powers not even mentioned in the RPA to ensure free and fair elections. In 2019 the Commission nullified the election of Vellore constituency after reports of large-scale bribing.

National versus state party symbols

  • National party: Symbol cannot be used by another party in any part of India
  • State party: Symbol can be used in other states, not where the party is recognised
  • Memory aid (Three functions): Administrative, Advisory, Quasi-judicial: "All Around Quality."
  • Memory aid (Administrative tasks): Constituencies, Rolls, Schedule, Nomination scrutiny, Cancel polls, Requisition staff and appoint officers, Model Code: "Commissioners Run Sound National Contests, Rightly, Methodically."
  • Shortcut: Advisory in two lines. President on MPs, Governor on state legislators. Also advises on holding elections under President's Rule and recognises parties as national or state.

5 · Organisational support and field structure#

  • The ECI works through a central headquarters in New Delhi and a cadre of officers, with a hierarchical, multi-level machinery.

Field hierarchy

  • ECI headquarters: New Delhi
  • Chief Electoral Officer (CEO): State level, designated in consultation with the state government
  • District Election Officer (DEO): District level, often the District Collector or a senior administrative official
  • Returning Officer (RO): Each parliamentary or assembly constituency, nominated by the ECI
  • Presiding Officers and polling staff: Each polling station or booth, appointed by the RO (via the DEO)
Field hierarchy
  • This layered structure ensures the Commission's policies and procedures are implemented across India.
  • Memory aid (Field ladder): CEO, DEO, RO, Presiding Officer: "Come Down Right Port." Top to bottom: state, district, constituency, booth.

6 · Vision, mission and core principles#

  • Vision: an Institution of Excellence: continuously enhancing citizen engagement and participation, deepening electoral democracy in India and serving as a model globally.
  • Core purpose (mission): preserve independence, integrity and operational autonomy while ensuring elections are accessible, inclusive and ethically conducted, with the highest standards of professionalism for free, fair and transparent elections.

Ten core principles

  • Constitutional values: equality, equity, impartiality, independence and the rule of law in exercising superintendence, direction and control.
  • Credible elections: freeness, fairness, transparency, integrity, accountability, autonomy and professionalism.
  • Inclusive participation: voter-centric, voter-friendly environment that removes barriers to enrolment and voting.
  • Stakeholder engagement: constructive engagement with parties and all stakeholders to promote responsible political conduct.
  • Public awareness and trust: public understanding of electoral processes among voters, parties, election functionaries and candidates.
  • Human resource development: invest in people so electoral services are delivered professionally.
  • Infrastructure quality: build and maintain quality electoral infrastructure.
  • Adoption of technology: appropriate technology for efficiency, accuracy and transparency.
  • Innovation and excellence: innovative practices that raise institutional performance.
  • Reinforcing democratic values: maintain and enhance people's confidence and trust in the electoral system.
  • Memory aid (Ten principles): Constitutional values, Credible elections, Inclusive participation, Stakeholder engagement, Public awareness, Human resources, Infrastructure, Technology, Innovation, Reinforcing democratic values: "Careful Commissioners Include States, Public, Human Ideas, Technology, Innovation, Results."

Also in the handout

  • The Commission’s supervisory role extends to issuing and enforcing the Model Code of Conduct that governs behaviour of parties and candidates during campaigns.
  • This vision emphasises elevating the credibility and reach of the electoral process so that democratic participation becomes more meaningful and resilient.
  • ## Core Principles Guiding Electoral Governance To translate its vision and mission into practice, the Commission follows a set of guiding principles that define its approach to electoral administration and reform: - Constitutional Values: Uphold constitutional values — equality, equity, impartiality, independence and the rule of law — in the exercise of superintendence, direction and control over electoral governance.

Which Article establishes the Election Commission's control of elections?
Article 324: superintendence, direction and control of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President.
Who conducts Panchayat and Municipality elections?
State Election Commissions (not under Article 324).
Who decides the number of Election Commissioners?

Who chairs the Commission when ECs are appointed?

Membership history of the ECI?
Single member 1950 to 15 Oct 1989; two ECs 16 Oct 1989; posts abolished January 1990; two ECs again October 1993, a three-member body since.
Why were two ECs appointed in 1989?
To handle the increased workload after the voting age fell from 21 to 18.

Six years or age 65, whichever is earlier. Salary and privileges equal to a Supreme Court Judge. Equal powers. Decisions by majority.
When is National Voter's Day?
25 January, declared in 2011 to mark the Commission's foundation day.
How is the CEC removed?
Same procedure and grounds as an SC Judge: resolution of both Houses by special majority on proven misbehaviour or incapacity, then the President removes.
How are ECs and Regional Commissioners removed?
Only on the recommendation of the CEC.
Three gaps in Article 324's design?
No specified qualifications, no definitive term of office, no bar on post-retirement appointments by the government.
Three kinds of ECI functions?
Administrative, advisory, quasi-judicial.
Administrative duties of the ECI?
Constituencies, electoral rolls, schedule, nomination scrutiny, cancelling polls, staff requisition, appointing officers, broadcast time, Model Code of Conduct.
Advisory duties of the ECI?
Advises the President on disqualification of MPs, Governors on state legislators, whether elections can be held under President's Rule, and recommends national or state party status.
Quasi-judicial duties of the ECI?
Disputes on party recognition and symbols, inquiry bodies for irregularities, enforcing election laws. Also manages the India International Institute of Democracy and Election Management (2011).
Which Order covers election symbols?
The Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968.
Symbol rule for national and state parties?
National party symbol cannot be used by another party anywhere in India. A state party symbol can be used in other states.
What did Gill vs. CEC (1977) establish?
The Commission has plenary power to use even powers not in the RPA to ensure free and fair elections. Example: Vellore 2019.
Field machinery of the ECI?
CEO at state, DEO at district, RO per constituency, Presiding Officers and polling staff at each booth. Headquarters in New Delhi.
What is the ECI's vision?
An Institution of Excellence enhancing citizen engagement and serving as a global model.
What do Articles 325 to 329 say?
325 no ineligibility for the roll on religion, race, caste or sex. 326 adult suffrage. 327 Parliament's power. 328 State Legislature's power. 329 bar to court interference.
What did the 73rd and 74th Amendments do for the ECI context?

What did the 84th and 87th Amendments do?

Traps#

Trap 1 · Local body elections Article 324 covers Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President only. Panchayat and Municipality polls go to State Election Commissions. Rule: Union and top offices = EC (Art. 324); local bodies = State Election Commission.
Trap 2 · Who gets judge-style removal Only the CEC is removed like an SC Judge. An Election Commissioner or Regional Commissioner is removed only on the CEC's recommendation. Rule: CEC = special-majority route in both Houses; other ECs = CEC must recommend.
Trap 3 · Single or three-member The EC was single-member from 1950 to 15 Oct 1989, had two extra ECs from 16 Oct 1989, went back to one in Jan 1990, and has been three-member since Oct 1993. Rule: answer three-member for today; remember the 1989-90 experiment was temporary.
Trap 4 · Gaps in the design The Constitution fixes no qualifications, no definite term, and no bar on later government posts for retiring ECs. Rule: three gaps = qualifications, term, post-retirement jobs.
